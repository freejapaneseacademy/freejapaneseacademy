## Scope and original lesson

- Objective: hear a simple spoken correction, distinguish short and long vowel timing in context, and identify immediately stated information. No scored question depends on seeing kana/kanji, script identification or written spelling.
- Track A: a man asks about a photograph of a place; a woman corrects him. The audible distinction between 家 (いえ, house) and いいえ (no) is explained **after** scoring.
- Track B: a woman guesses a relative in a photograph; a man corrects her and describes the person. The contrast おばさん (aunt) / おばあさん (grandmother) and long a are explained **after** scoring.
- Seven original single-choice questions: three for Track A, four for Track B. Every correct choice has a verbatim substring in the corresponding generated transcript. Each option has feedback rationale. The transcript and English meaning remain concealed until learner reveal or post-scoring.
- Independent Japanese naturalness/pronunciation and assessment-editor signoff are **pending**, not passed or invented. The automated Japanese screening found no identified blocker in the authored scripts, meanings or answer keys; this is not a human listening assessment.

## Internal screen — identified risks and mitigations

- **Vowel contrast:** いえ / いいえ (2 versus 3 morae); おばさん / おばあさん (4 versus 5 morae). The added あ is a held vowel, not an extra stressed syllable. Feedback uses these distinctions accurately.
- **Naturalness:** polite everyday photo conversations; the short responses いいえ and はい are contextually consistent. No unsupported claim that all real speakers pronounce exactly like the model.
- **Answer leakage:** default scene labels are neutral; full transcript and English translation appear only after learner reveal or scoring. Assisted mode never records blind mastery.
- **Storage:** existing `recordAttempt` uses a new `l0-m01-c02-l06-check-v1` quiz ID; `completeLesson` retains the stable canonical ID. No migration or destructive write.
