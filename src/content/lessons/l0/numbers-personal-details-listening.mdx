## Can-do: understand short introductions by ear

In this chapter you learn to state and recognize basic numbers, names and nationalities. Here you will **listen** to two new situations: a registration desk and a conversation between classmates. Your score is based on **what you hear**, not on your ability to read Japanese characters. The speakers use simple polite phrases and the number patterns from earlier lessons.

## Listening strategy

First, play each recording without opening its transcript. Listen once for the purpose of the exchange; listen again for **who**, **which number**, and **where someone came from**. You may replay as many times as needed. The answer choices are in English so this is a genuine listening task rather than a reading test.

### Track A: registration desk

A visitor checks in for a community Japanese class. Listen for the name the visitor gives, the number that follows, and how the desk worker ends the exchange. Do not assume the number is an age: in this scene it is a **registration number**.

### Track B: two new classmates

Two learners introduce themselves. Listen for the countries they say they came from and the class number. **〜からきました** means “came from ~” in this context; it does not by itself prove someone's nationality or citizenship. **〜ばん** identifies a number in a series, such as a class number.

## Practice in three passes

1. **First pass:** play both recordings for the situation and speaker changes.
2. **Second pass:** answer the six questions from the audio alone. Focus on spoken names, quantities and country names, not spelling.
3. **Third pass:** after submitting, reveal each transcript and English translation to check the exact spoken evidence. You may reveal it earlier for accessibility, but then the attempt is **assisted** and will not count as an unaided listening score.

## What to notice after checking

A polite introduction may use **〜です** (“I am …” in this context). The question **なんばんですか** asks which number; **わたしも** means “me too / I also.” Compare the two speakers' answers in the second conversation. Names and countries are context-dependent: never infer age, nationality or personal identity beyond what a speaker explicitly says.

## Optional speaking and writing follow-up

Replay one short sentence and repeat it aloud if you wish. Alternatively, after revealing the transcript, copy the two country names or write a fictional introduction. Neither activity is scored as pronunciation or handwriting. You do not need to provide any real personal information, microphone access or recordings.
