## Write a label someone else can read

Imagine a fictional classroom display with a mountain, a peach and a book. You are making the Japanese labels and one polite instruction for a classmate. Your goal is **recognizable written hiragana**; this is not a test of pronunciation, handwriting stroke order or unaided listening.

## 1. Retrieve the remaining basic kana

| Row | Hiragana | Romaji input cues |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M | ま み む め も | ma mi mu me mo |
| Y | や ゆ よ | ya yu yo |
| R | ら り る れ ろ | ra ri ru re ro |
| W and nasal | わ を ん | wa, wo (particle pronounced o), n |

There are no ordinary basic **yi, ye, wu** kana in this chart. **ん** counts as a separate mora, even at the end of a word. **を** is normally written for the direct-object particle; in contemporary standard Japanese it is pronounced *o*. An IME may accept *wo* to produce を, but the submitted answer must be the Japanese character.

If you use a Japanese IME, switch to hiragana mode, type the Latin input cue, and confirm the converted Japanese characters. Device methods differ. The on-screen palette in the workshop is an accessible alternative.

## 2. Labels and their meanings

| Fictional picture | Japanese label | Morae |
| --- | --- | --- |
| mountain | やま | や・ま (2) |
| peach | もも | も・も (2) |
| book | ほん | ほ・ん (2) |
| mandarin orange | みかん | み・か・ん (3) |

Write the whole word. **ほん** ends in ん, not the Latin letter n. The word **もも** repeats the same kana twice; **やま** has two different kana. Do not confuse the two-syllable English rendering of *hon* with the Japanese two-mora spelling ほ・ん.

## 3. Build a meaningful classroom request

A teacher points to a book and says **ほんをよみます。** (*Hon o yomimasu.* — “I read a book.”) The object is **ほん** and the object marker is **を**, written separately from the noun as a particle even though Japanese ordinarily uses no spaces here. The verb **よみます** means “read” in polite non-past form. The particle is not わ or お.

To politely ask a classmate to read a book, a useful model is **ほんをよんでください。** (“Please read the book.”) This is a supported model phrase from the previous listening lesson; you are not expected to derive the て-form rule independently yet. The ん in **よんで** is a nasal mora. For a different instruction, **ほんをみてください。** means “Please look at the book.” Do not mix up “look” and “read.”

## 4. Rehearse, check and revise

First hide the kana chart and try writing the M and Y rows. Then type the fifteen constrained entries below, including the special を and ん. You can use an IME or the accessible palette. Each answer has one explicit written target, so the constrained stage can be scored fairly.

Next write a **fictional** two-line label and instruction: label the peach as **もも**, then politely ask a classmate to read a book. You may use **ももです。** (“It is a peach.”) and **ほんをよんでください。** as models. Other natural expressions can be valid. Free composition is checked by **you** with the rubric, not automatically graded.

## 5. Typical corrections

- **もも** is peach, not **もの** (“thing”). Inspect the second kana.
- **ほん** includes a final ん. Typing *hon* without IME conversion is not a Japanese written answer.
- In **ほんをよみます**, the particle is **を**, not **お** even though the modern pronunciation is the same.
- A correctly typed sentence does not demonstrate correct spoken rhythm or pronunciation. Revisit [Lesson 06's real recordings](/learn/start/module-02/chapter-03/lesson-06/) for listening.
