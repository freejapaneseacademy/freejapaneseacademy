## The library word shelf

Mika, Ren and Nao return to the neighborhood library. Mika labels a display; Ren looks for a ticket to the reading event; Nao helps a visiting school class. Their words are useful beyond this scene, but the **meaning in context** matters. This is a written word-study lesson. Recorded pronunciation and dictation belong to Lesson 06; romanization is only a learning cue.

## Ten useful expressions

| Japanese | Meaning here | Reading or spelling clue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">きって</span> | postage stamp | small っ before て |
| <span lang="ja">きっぷ</span> | ticket | small っ before ぷ |
| <span lang="ja">ざっし</span> | magazine | small っ before し |
| <span lang="ja">がっこう</span> | school | small っ and こう |
| <span lang="ja">こうえん</span> | park | こう spells a long o |
| <span lang="ja">おおきい</span> | big | おお spells a long o |
| <span lang="ja">せんせい</span> | teacher | せい often spells a long e |
| <span lang="ja">とけい</span> | clock / watch | けい often spells a long e |
| <span lang="ja">つき</span> | moon | full-sized つ, not small っ |
| <span lang="ja">きて</span> | come (request form) | no small っ |

The small <span lang="ja">っ</span> marks a consonant hold and counts as one **mora**. It is not pronounced as a separate *tsu*. Full-sized <span lang="ja">つ</span> in <span lang="ja">つき</span> is different. The written <span lang="ja">きて</span> is a request form of “come” in an appropriate context; it is **not** an alternative spelling of <span lang="ja">きって</span> (postage stamp).

## Words in a real situation

- **<span lang="ja">きって</span>** — Mika puts a stamp label beside the envelope.
- **<span lang="ja">きっぷ</span>** — Ren checks a ticket for the library event.
- **<span lang="ja">ざっし</span>** — Nao places the magazine on the reading shelf.
- **<span lang="ja">がっこう</span>** — The school group visits the library.
- **<span lang="ja">こうえん</span>** — The park is marked on the neighborhood map.
- **<span lang="ja">おおきい</span>** — The big book needs a wide shelf.
- **<span lang="ja">せんせい</span>** — The teacher brings the class.
- **<span lang="ja">とけい</span>** — The clock is above the checkout desk.
- **<span lang="ja">つき</span>** — The moon appears in a picture book.
- **<span lang="ja">きて</span>** — This request is not the word for a postage stamp.

A word's dictionary gloss does not automatically tell you how to use it. For example, <span lang="ja">きっぷ</span> is a ticket, not the library's borrowing card; <span lang="ja">ざっし</span> is a magazine rather than any book; <span lang="ja">せんせい</span> refers to a teacher (or certain professionals in other contexts), not every adult in the room. <span lang="ja">おおきい</span> describes size and is not a noun meaning “book.”

## Spelling patterns are not interchangeable

The long-*o* spelling in <span lang="ja">こうえん</span> uses <span lang="ja">こう</span>, while <span lang="ja">おおきい</span> begins with <span lang="ja">おお</span>. Both can represent a long *o*, but copying the other word's pattern would be a spelling error. <span lang="ja">せんせい</span> and <span lang="ja">とけい</span> use <span lang="ja">えい</span> in their written forms; the actual phonetic realization varies by word and speaker. Do not infer that every <span lang="ja">えい</span> sequence is pronounced identically.

## Retrieval routine

Cover the English meanings and recall each word from its scene. Then cover the Japanese and write or type it from memory, checking the exact size of <span lang="ja">っ</span> and the original long-vowel spelling. Finally, contrast <span lang="ja">きって</span>, <span lang="ja">きて</span> and <span lang="ja">つき</span> aloud only as ungraded practice; no pronunciation is assessed here.
