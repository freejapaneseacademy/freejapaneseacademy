## A classroom word-card display

Mika has arranged eight **written** word cards for a classroom matching game. Each card has a letter, a hiragana label and a small drawing described below. The letters and English picture descriptions are study aids, not part of the Japanese words.

| Card | Printed label | Picture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | **ねこ** | cat |
| B | **いぬ** | dog |
| C | **くるま** | car |
| D | **やま** | mountain |
| E | **ほん** | book |
| F | **はな** | flower |
| G | **そら** | sky |
| H | **かわ** | river |

Read the **entire** kana sequence: **ねこ** (*neko*) is cat, while **いぬ** (*inu*) is dog. In **くるま** (*kuruma*), the middle kana is **る**, not **ろ**. In **ほん** (*hon*), **ん** is a separate mora: ほ・ん. The card **はな** (*hana*) shows a flower in this display; the same spoken word can also mean “nose” in another context.

## The printed instructions

Under the cards Mika writes three short classroom directions:

**Aのねこをみてください。** — Please look at the cat on card A.

**Eのほんをみてください。** — Please look at the book on card E.

**Hのかわをみてください。** — Please look at the river on card H.

Here **の** connects the card letter to its label, and **を** marks what the reader is asked to look at. The request **みてください** means “please look.” The object particle **を** is written differently from **お**, although it is normally pronounced *o*. These are written classroom directions, not recordings.

## How to find evidence

For a picture question, point to the card row containing the exact Japanese label. For an instruction question, quote the printed direction, such as **Eのほんをみてください。**. If asked for the object in that direction, the evidence is **ほん**, not the particle **を** or the request **みてください**.

First read rows A–H from left to right. Cover the English picture column and try matching the labels to the pictures. Next read the three directions without the English translations. Check which card letter and Japanese noun appear in each direction. This is untimed reading practice, not a test of speaking speed.

## Independent reading check

Answer ten original questions about the printed word cards and directions. Every answer can be checked against an **exact phrase** above. After submission, the explanations show the supporting Japanese text. Your score is a written comprehension score only; it does not certify oral fluency or an official examination level.
