## A small journey with katakana

**Can-do:** Read everyday loanwords, long-vowel marks and borrowed sound patterns. Today you will learn to recognize useful words from three places: a café menu, a stationery counter and a station sign. This is **written vocabulary study**; listening to a real announcement belongs to Lesson 06.

Imagine a traveler named Hana buying a snack, organizing her papers and finding her train. These fictional labels are original learning material, not photographs of real businesses.

### At the café: food and drink

| Japanese | Reading hint | Meaning | How to remember |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">コーヒー</span> | kōhī | coffee | Two long-vowel marks: コー and ヒー |
| <span lang="ja">ティー</span> | tī | tea | テ plus **small ィ**, followed by ー |
| <span lang="ja">ケーキ</span> | kēki | cake | The first vowel is long |
| <span lang="ja">チーズ</span> | chīzu | cheese | チー is long; ズ has dakuten |
| <span lang="ja">チェリー</span> | cherī | cherry | チ plus **small ェ**, ending リー |
| <span lang="ja">メニュー</span> | menyū | menu | ニュ uses small ュ; ユ is not interchangeable |

**Fictional menu:** <span lang="ja">コーヒー・ティー・ケーキ・チーズ</span>. Hana chooses <span lang="ja">ティー</span> and <span lang="ja">ケーキ</span>. The words are labels, not a complete spoken order. For an actual order, a learner needs an appropriate phrase and context, not just a noun.

### At the stationery counter: documents and technology

| Japanese | Reading hint | Meaning in context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">ファイル</span> | fairu | file, binder or folder |
| <span lang="ja">コピー</span> | kopī | copy; also a photocopy in a stationery setting |
| <span lang="ja">テキスト</span> | tekisuto | text or course textbook |
| <span lang="ja">メール</span> | mēru | email (often an electronic message) |

**Context matters:** <span lang="ja">ファイル</span> can mean a physical folder or a digital file. If Hana is buying a binder at a stationery shop, “folder” fits; if she is organizing computer documents, “file” fits. <span lang="ja">メール</span> is commonly email, not a physical letter simply because English *mail* can mean postal mail. Loanwords are **Japanese vocabulary**, not automatic English translations.

### At the station: getting around

| Japanese | Reading hint | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">タクシー</span> | takushī | taxi |
| <span lang="ja">チケット</span> | chiketto | ticket |
| <span lang="ja">ホーム</span> | hōmu | railway platform in station context |

**Fictional station board:** <span lang="ja">タクシー / チケット / ２ばんホーム</span>. The last label means platform 2 in this context. <span lang="ja">ホーム</span> does **not** necessarily mean a residence: Japanese railway usage is specialized. A real ticket machine or station may use other wording.

## Three spelling clues that change what you read

1. **ー stretches the preceding vowel.** <span lang="ja">コーヒー</span> has two bars, and <span lang="ja">ケーキ</span> has one. Do not silently drop them when copying or typing.
2. **Small vowels are part of the sound combination.** <span lang="ja">ティ</span> is テ + small ィ; <span lang="ja">ファ</span> is フ + small ァ; <span lang="ja">チェ</span> is チ + small ェ. Compare <span lang="ja">ティ</span> with <span lang="ja">テイ</span>: the second has a full-size イ.
3. **Borrowed spellings are adapted.** English *ticket* becomes <span lang="ja">チケット</span>, not a letter-by-letter English transcription. <span lang="ja">メニュー</span> uses small ュ. Read the printed kana rather than guessing from English spelling.

## Guided retrieval: read, cover, recall

First read the three tables slowly, following each character with your finger. Then cover the English column and recall the meaning of <span lang="ja">ティー</span>, <span lang="ja">ファイル</span> and <span lang="ja">タクシー</span>. Finally cover the Japanese column and type the three words using a Japanese IME. Compare your result character by character, checking small ィ/ァ and ー.

### A useful contrast

The café sells <span lang="ja">ケーキ</span>, not <span lang="ja">ケータイ</span> (a mobile phone). A stationery counter offers <span lang="ja">ファイル</span>, not <span lang="ja">ファン</span> (fan, an admirer or a device depending on context). Similar-looking katakana are not interchangeable; check the **whole word** and the **place where it appears**.

## Independent written practice

Answer ten multiple-choice questions. Some ask you to retrieve a meaning; others ask you to choose the correct Japanese label or infer a context-specific meaning. After submission, compare the rationale with the tables. Your result is a written vocabulary score, **not** a listening, pronunciation or certified-language score.

**Review checklist:** Can you recognize three food words, one document word and two transport words? Can you explain why <span lang="ja">ホーム</span> can mean a railway platform? Can you locate ー, small ィ, small ァ and small ェ in real words? The next lesson explains these spelling patterns in more detail.
