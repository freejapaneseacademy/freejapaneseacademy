## A small shelf in a neighborhood shop

**Chapter Can-do:** Read and write katakana from the N, H, M and Y rows. This first lesson establishes a setting and a purpose before more detailed shape and writing practice.

Yuna is a **customer** at a fictional neighborhood shop. The **clerk** points to a shelf of packaged foods. The printed words are original teaching examples, not a photograph or real product advertising. Yuna also notices a small name card for another visitor, Yumi.

| Printed text | What it identifies | Reading support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">ハム</span> | ham, a food | ハ (ha) + ム (mu) |
| <span lang="ja">バナナ</span> | banana, a food | バ (ba) + ナ (na) + ナ (na) |
| <span lang="ja">ミナ</span> | Mina, a person's name | ミ (mi) + ナ (na) |
| <span lang="ja">ユミ</span> | Yumi, a person's name | ユ (yu) + ミ (mi) |

**Important:** バ has a voiced mark (dakuten). You will study voiced katakana systematically later; for now use the word バナナ as a supported whole label. The name ミナ is an example, not another product on the shelf.

### Who does what?

Yuna wants to find a snack. The clerk shows her the product shelf. A label such as <span lang="ja">ハム</span> identifies a product, but **does not** by itself tell you its price, availability or ingredients. A person's name such as <span lang="ja">ユミ</span> is not a product merely because it is printed in katakana.

## Meet four rows

| Row | Katakana | Sound hints |
| --- | --- | --- |
| N | <span lang="ja">ナ ニ ヌ ネ ノ</span> | na ni nu ne no |
| H | <span lang="ja">ハ ヒ フ ヘ ホ</span> | ha hi fu he ho |
| M | <span lang="ja">マ ミ ム メ モ</span> | ma mi mu me mo |
| Y | <span lang="ja">ヤ ユ ヨ</span> | ya yu yo |

The modern standard Y row has **three** basic katakana, not five. The sound of フ is commonly romanized *fu*, though its Japanese pronunciation is not identical to English *f*. Romanization here is a temporary sound hint, not the script to memorize.

### Notice the shapes, then the meaning

- In <span lang="ja">ハム</span>, identify ハ and ム. Compare ム with マ; the shapes are different even though both belong to the M row.
- In <span lang="ja">ミナ</span>, identify ミ and ナ. The three strokes of ミ are a useful visual clue.
- In <span lang="ja">ユミ</span>, identify ユ and ミ. Katakana is used for some names as well as loanwords.
- In <span lang="ja">バナナ</span>, notice that ナ appears twice. Do not confuse ナ with メ, which has crossing strokes.

**Common misconception:** A katakana word does not automatically mean an English loanword. Names can be written in katakana, and product labels can contain information not visible in a short teaching card. Only infer what is actually printed or stated.

## Guided practice

Point to the two food labels, then the two personal names. Trace ナ and メ in the air and compare their crossing lines. Say which row each of ハ, ム, ミ, ナ and ユ belongs to. If typing with a Japanese IME, try `hamu` → ハム and `yumi` → ユミ, then check the actual kana rather than trusting the Latin letters.
