## Your mission: ask for help aloud

A classmate is learning to enter Japanese on a keyboard. Another classmate cannot read a word containing kanji. In this **optional** lesson you can rehearse useful polite classroom phrases using the two *unchanged, genuine synthetic-voice WAV recordings* from Chapter 04 Lesson 06. You can listen and shadow without a microphone. This is not an automated pronunciation examination.

**Can-do:** Enter Japanese using a keyboard and control reading aids. Speaking supports that goal when you need to ask a person for assistance.

## 1. Three classroom functions

| Japanese model | Reading aid | Meaning | When to use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">すみません。</span> | すみません | Excuse me. | Politely attract someone's attention. |
| <span lang="ja">どうすればいいですか。</span> | どうすればいいですか | What should I do? | Ask for guidance when unsure of a procedure. |
| <span lang="ja">日本語の入力を選んでください。</span> | にほんごの にゅうりょくを えらんでください | Please select Japanese input. | Give a polite input instruction. |
| <span lang="ja">読み方がわかりません。</span> | よみかたが わかりません | I don't know how to read it. | Explain a reading difficulty. |
| <span lang="ja">ふりがなを見てください。</span> | ふりがなを みてください | Please look at the furigana. | Point someone to a reading annotation. |
| <span lang="ja">ありがとうございます。</span> | ありがとうございます | Thank you. | Respond to assistance. |

**Language note:** <span lang="ja">ください</span> after a verb's て-form makes a polite request. It does not mean the same thing as the standalone noun-oriented <span lang="ja">おねがいします</span> in every context. <span lang="ja">ふりがな</span> are reading annotations; they are not the IME's candidate list and do not provide an English translation.

## 2. Scene A: getting Japanese input working

Listen to the whole recorded conversation first. The learner begins:

<span lang="ja">すみません。日本語を入力したいです。どうすればいいですか。</span>

“Excuse me. I want to type Japanese. What should I do?”

The helper answers:

<span lang="ja">まず、日本語の入力を選んでください。</span>

“First, please select Japanese input.”

**Shadowing task:** Use **Play this turn** for the helper's response. Listen, pause, then say the whole sentence. Notice the gentle request ending <span lang="ja">ください</span>. Do not over-emphasize each kana. When the learner says <span lang="ja">できました。ありがとうございます。</span> (“I did it. Thank you.”), practise that short reply too.

**Role-play:** Imagine you are the helper. After the question <span lang="ja">どうすればいいですか。</span>, respond with the recorded instruction, not a made-up keyboard shortcut. Actual IME menus and keys differ across devices.

## 3. Scene B: reading a word with kanji

The student says:

<span lang="ja">この言葉の読み方がわかりません。</span>

“I don't know how to read this word.”

The helper responds:

<span lang="ja">ふりがなを見てください。読み方がわかりますよ。</span>

“Please look at the furigana. It will show you the reading.”

Later the helper says:

<span lang="ja">候補を見て、意味を確認してください。</span>

“Look at the candidates and check the meaning.”

**Shadowing task:** Repeat the request to look at furigana, then repeat the instruction to check candidate meanings. These are different operations: furigana helps with reading; an IME candidate list helps choose text, including kanji. The final <span lang="ja">よ</span> in the helper's first response adds a reassuring tone; do not shout it.

**Role-play:** A classmate cannot read a word. Say <span lang="ja">ふりがなを見てください。</span> with an appropriate helpful tone. Then listen to the original turn again.

## 4. Five-step shadowing method

1. Listen to the entire exchange for its situation and meaning.
2. Choose one recorded turn and play it using its own button.
3. Pause and repeat the *whole phrase* aloud. Follow its natural pauses rather than spelling each kana separately.
4. Replay and compare. You may press **Start optional recording** and grant permission, but recording is never automatic, uploaded or saved to progress.
5. Apply the self-review checklist. If you cannot hear a difference confidently, leave that criterion unchecked and try again.

**Common mistakes:** using <span lang="ja">ふりがな</span> as if it meant an English translation; treating the IME candidate list as furigana; omitting the polite request ending; interpreting a written multiple-choice score as proof of spoken accuracy.
