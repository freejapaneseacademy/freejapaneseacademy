## The shelf label and the visitor card

**Can-do:** Read and write katakana N, H, M and Y rows. In this original fictional shop scene, a customer checks a shelf card reading <span lang="ja">ハム</span> (ham), while a visitor wears a card reading <span lang="ja">ミナ</span> (Mina). Both are katakana, but one is a product label and the other is a person's name. The printed letters alone do not identify their roles.

This lesson concerns **written forms and their functions**, not spoken-word identification. No recording is needed for the shape questions. Later L06 provides authentic listening practice.

## Notice before you guess

The N row is <span lang="ja">ナ ニ ヌ ネ ノ</span> (na, ni, nu, ne, no); the H row is <span lang="ja">ハ ヒ フ ヘ ホ</span> (ha, hi, fu, he, ho); the M row is <span lang="ja">マ ミ ム メ モ</span> (ma, mi, mu, me, mo). The basic Y row has only <span lang="ja">ヤ ユ ヨ</span> (ya, yu, yo). There are no modern basic *yi* or *ye* katakana in this row.

| Compare | What to inspect | Readings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">ナ / メ</span> | ナ has a vertical stroke crossed near its top; メ has two diagonal strokes | na / me |
| <span lang="ja">マ / ム</span> | マ has a top horizontal/angular structure; ム ends in a short rightward mark | ma / mu |
| <span lang="ja">ミ / ム</span> | ミ is three separate short strokes; ム is a connected angular form | mi / mu |
| <span lang="ja">ヌ / ス</span> | ヌ includes a crossing stroke that ス does not | nu / su |
| <span lang="ja">ユ / ヨ</span> | ヨ has three horizontal levels; ユ has a more open shape | yu / yo |
| <span lang="ja">ハ / ヘ</span> | ハ is two separated descending strokes; ヘ is a joined peaked stroke | ha / he |

Use a legible Japanese font and examine the **whole character**. Exact stroke appearance can vary slightly with typeface; do not rely on one pixel or a guessed handwriting style. Confusing <span lang="ja">ナ</span> with <span lang="ja">メ</span> changes the reading, not just the visual style.

## Read the shop labels

- <span lang="ja">ハム</span> = ハ (ha) + ム (mu): *ham*, a food on the shelf.
- <span lang="ja">ミナ</span> = ミ (mi) + ナ (na): *Mina*, the visitor's name in this scene.
- <span lang="ja">ユミ</span> = ユ (yu) + ミ (mi): *Yumi*, another possible name.
- <span lang="ja">バナナ</span> = バ (ba) + ナ (na) + ナ (na): *banana*. The two short marks on バ indicate voicing; systematic voiced rows are introduced later.

**Common error:** A learner sees ミナ and assumes it must be a product because it uses katakana. Katakana is also used for personal names, especially names written in katakana by choice or convention. Read the sign's context before deciding what the label does. A bare product name does **not** tell you its price or ingredients.

## Guided rehearsal

1. Cover the reading column above. Trace the difference between ナ and メ with your eyes, then read each aloud only as optional rehearsal.
2. Copy <span lang="ja">ハム</span> and <span lang="ja">ミナ</span> on paper or type them with a Japanese IME. Compare each character to the printed model; this is ungraded.
3. Look at <span lang="ja">ユミ</span>. Which first character distinguishes it from <span lang="ja">ミナ</span>? **ユ**, not ミ.
4. Read a fictional shelf card with only <span lang="ja">ハム</span>. Can you infer a price? **No**; no price is written.
