## What this checkpoint measures

**Chapter Can-do:** recognize long vowels, small *tsu* (っ), and nasal mora ん. You will first read original caption clues and choose written forms, then listen to two everyday conversations recorded for Chapter 03. These are different skills: a written question cannot prove listening comprehension, and a listening answer does not grade pronunciation.

Take about 30 minutes. Read the explanations below, answer all written questions, then play both audio scenes **without opening the transcripts** if you want a blind-listening result. Revealing a transcript is allowed, but the score is saved separately as *assisted*.

## 1. Three kinds of extra timing

Japanese rhythm is often described using **morae**, units of timing that do not always match English syllables. A long vowel occupies two morae; the small っ signals a brief consonant closure before the next consonant and occupies a mora; ん is its own nasal mora. The sound of ん can vary with the next consonant: do not force one fixed English “n” pronunciation.

| Printed form | Contextual meaning | Mora units | Count |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">おばさん</span> | aunt | お・ば・さ・ん | 4 |
| <span lang="ja">おばあさん</span> | grandmother | お・ば・あ・さ・ん | 5 |
| <span lang="ja">きて</span> | come (casual request) | き・て | 2 |
| <span lang="ja">きって</span> | stamp | き・っ・て | 3 |
| <span lang="ja">パン</span> | bread | パ・ン | 2 |
| <span lang="ja">かばん</span> | bag | か・ば・ん | 3 |

**Important distinction:** the *word* <span lang="ja">きって</span> can mean “stamp” in the post-office setting. <span lang="ja">きて</span> is a casual form of “come,” not a different spelling of “stamp.” Context determines the intended meaning. The small っ is not pronounced as an independent “tsu.”

## 2. Original caption reading: the shop and the picnic

Imagine two cards pinned to a classroom board.

**Card A — Post office:** <span lang="ja">切手を二枚ください。</span> “Two stamps, please.” The speaker is buying stamps. <span lang="ja">切手</span> is read <span lang="ja">きって</span>, with the timing units き・っ・て. The small っ matters. The counter <span lang="ja">二枚</span> (にまい) tells us **two flat items**.

**Card B — Picnic:** <span lang="ja">パンとお茶を持っていきます。</span> “I will bring bread and tea.” The word <span lang="ja">パン</span> has two morae, パ・ン. The expression <span lang="ja">持っていきます</span> means “will take/bring along” in this context; its small っ also matters for rhythm.

Read each caption again and point to the exact word supporting the English meaning. You are not required to memorize the kanji: the readings and translations are provided as support.

## 3. A small correction can change the message

A learner writes <span lang="ja">きてを二枚ください。</span> while intending to buy stamps. This does **not** express the intended request. The model is <span lang="ja">きってを二枚ください。</span> in kana, or naturally <span lang="ja">切手を二枚ください。</span> in mixed script. The sound contrast is between き・て and き・っ・て.

Another learner counts <span lang="ja">かばん</span> as two units because they hear two English-like syllables. In Japanese mora counting, か・ば・ん is **three**. Nasal ん can be pronounced differently before /b/, /p/ and other sounds; its timing role remains one mora.

**Do not overgeneralize:** long vowels and small っ can distinguish words, but not every long/short or doubled/undoubled sequence is a useful dictionary minimal pair. Learn meaning with a real context rather than inventing meanings from spelling.

## 4. Guided practice before scoring

Cover the answers, then uncover them to check your reasoning.

1. How many morae in <span lang="ja">きって</span>? **Three:** き・っ・て.
2. Which word on Card A means “stamps”? **切手**, read きって.
3. How many morae in <span lang="ja">パン</span>? **Two:** パ・ン.
4. What is the woman bringing in Card B? **Bread and tea:** パンとお茶.

## 5. Independent written production

Write or type one fictional post-office request using the supported model <span lang="ja">切手を二枚ください。</span>, then write its kana reading <span lang="ja">きってをにまいください。</span>. Compare your own work against these criteria: (1) きって includes the small っ; (2) the number is two; (3) the request ends with ください. This is **self-review**, not an automatically graded writing or pronunciation test. Do not enter personal information.

## 6. Listen for meaning, not spelling

The two existing Chapter 03 audio scenes involve a post-office purchase and planning a picnic. Listen to the **whole** conversations before choosing what the speakers request, where the letter is going, what food is coming, and what the bag is like. The transcript is deliberately withheld until scoring or until you choose assisted mode. No audio question asks you to identify printed characters from a sound.

After scoring, revisit incorrect answers and reread the exact evidence in the transcript. Record weak vocabulary such as <span lang="ja">切手</span>, <span lang="ja">二枚</span>, <span lang="ja">パン</span>, or <span lang="ja">かばん</span> in your own spaced-review notes. The browser stores assessment attempts locally; it does not certify mastery or automatically evaluate speech.
