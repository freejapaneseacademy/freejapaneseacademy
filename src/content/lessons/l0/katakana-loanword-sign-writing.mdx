## Can-do: make a readable sign

A fictional café beside a station needs a two-line sign. You are the sign writer. By the end, you can **type common loanwords accurately** and make a short, recognizable sign. This is a writing activity, not a listening or teacher-graded assessment.

### Observe the model

A café menu can be a list rather than a complete sentence:

> <span lang="ja">カフェ　メニュー</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">コーヒー　ケーキ　ティー</span>

This means “café menu / coffee, cake, tea.” In ordinary Japanese, tea is often **お茶（おちゃ）**; **ティー** is a loanword used in some menus and product names.

At a fictional station counter, you might see:

> <span lang="ja">チケット　タクシー</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">ファイル　サービス</span>

These are spelling-practice labels, **not** natural complete sentences or an authentic public notice. A real sign needs more specific context.

### Notice the important marks

| Word | Meaning | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">コーヒー</span> | coffee | Two long vowels: コー and ヒー |
| <span lang="ja">ケーキ</span> | cake | ー after ケ |
| <span lang="ja">ティー</span> | tea, loanword | Small ィ after テ, then ー |
| <span lang="ja">ファイル</span> | file | Small ァ, not full ア |
| <span lang="ja">チェーン</span> | chain | Small ェ and ー |
| <span lang="ja">タクシー</span> | taxi | Final ー |
| <span lang="ja">チケット</span> | ticket | Small ッ, not ー |
| <span lang="ja">メニュー</span> | menu | Small ュ and final ー |

**ー** lengthens the preceding vowel. **ッ** indicates a consonant closure; it is not a long-vowel mark. **ティ** and **テイ** are different sequences because ィ is small. Not all loanwords have a single spelling convention: **コンピュータ** and **コンピューター** both occur. This activity avoids treating legitimate alternatives as errors.

### Guided copying, then typing

Copy **コーヒー / ティー / ファイル / チェーン** into a Japanese-input field or onto paper. Circle each ー and underline the small vowel kana. Japanese IMEs vary: check the **resulting characters**, not just the romanized keystrokes. For example, `ko-hi-` may produce コーヒー.

Now complete the eight exact spelling prompts below. Surrounding whitespace and Unicode normalization are handled; missing ー or wrong-sized kana are not accepted. These are constrained **typing checks**, not grades for creative writing.

### Write two lines independently

Choose a **fictional café** or **station counter**. Make two short lines using at least three target words. Keep each ー and small kana clearly legible. You may reorder the words; other context-appropriate arrangements can be valid. Do not enter personal information. The writing panel saves a draft only when you press **Save draft**, locally in your browser.

Reveal the model when ready, then self-review using four criteria: **three suitable target words**, **correct ー**, **correct small kana**, and **two clear context-appropriate lines**. The checklist is self-review, not an automatic composition score or teacher feedback.

### Reading and optional speaking transfer

Read your sign silently, pointing to a drink, food, or transport label. If you wish, say a line aloud and compare it with the printed kana. No microphone, audio recording or pronunciation rating is used. The chapter's authentic listening activity belongs to L06.

**Exit check:** Can you distinguish ティ from テイ? Can you explain both ー marks in コーヒー? Can you create and self-review a two-line sign?
