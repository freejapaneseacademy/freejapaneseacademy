## The final basic-hiragana cards

**Chapter Can-do:** Read and write the remaining basic hiragana, including わ, を and ん. In this first lesson you will **recognize** the cards and understand why a learner uses them. Writing accuracy, grammar and listening will be practised in later lessons. The task here is visual reading, not a claim about your pronunciation.

In an original beginner classroom scene, learner **Mio** is helping tutor **Yuri** label a picture board. Mio reads a printed kana card, points to its row, and matches the card to a supported label. Yuri checks the placement. The purpose is to finish a readable board of familiar objects, not to guess the meaning of an isolated symbol.

| Participant | Role | Task |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mio | Learner | Sorts the remaining printed hiragana cards |
| Yuri | Tutor | Checks the row and models a short label |

## The remaining cards

| Group | Printed hiragana | Common beginner romanization |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M | <span lang="ja">ま み む め も</span> | ma, mi, mu, me, mo |
| Y | <span lang="ja">や ゆ よ</span> | ya, yu, yo |
| R | <span lang="ja">ら り る れ ろ</span> | ra, ri, ru, re, ro |
| W | <span lang="ja">わ を</span> | wa, o (wo in spelling-based romanization) |
| Nasal | <span lang="ja">ん</span> | n |

There are **16 remaining basic hiragana** here: five M, three Y, five R, two W and one nasal. The modern basic Y group does not have separate *yi* or *ye* cards. The W group has no modern basic *wi* or *we* cards. The full modern basic hiragana inventory contains 46 symbols.

The kana <span lang="ja">を</span> is commonly pronounced like *o* in modern standard Japanese and usually writes the direct-object particle. Its spelling is different from <span lang="ja">お</span>, the vowel kana, even when they sound alike. We will study particle use more carefully in Lesson 03. The kana <span lang="ja">ん</span> represents a separate moraic nasal; it does **not** form an ordinary five-vowel row.

## The picture board

Mio sees four original printed labels:

| Card | Printed label | Beginner reading | Picture meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | <span lang="ja">やま</span> | yama | mountain |
| B | <span lang="ja">ゆめ</span> | yume | dream |
| C | <span lang="ja">もも</span> | momo | peach |
| D | <span lang="ja">ほん</span> | hon | book |

The final label uses <span lang="ja">ほ</span> from the previous H row and ends in <span lang="ja">ん</span>. In <span lang="ja">ほん</span>, the nasal has its own rhythm unit, so it is not simply an English-style consonant attached to the preceding letter. Each label is a **whole word**; individual kana such as <span lang="ja">も</span> do not by themselves mean “peach.”

Yuri also writes <span lang="ja">ほん を よむ</span> (*hon o yomu*, “read a book”). Spaces are added here as a beginner aid; ordinary Japanese generally does not use them between words. In this supported example, <span lang="ja">を</span> marks the object <span lang="ja">ほん</span>. You are only noticing its printed form today, not being graded on sentence grammar.

## Controlled, guided and independent practice

First, point to <span lang="ja">め</span> in the M group, <span lang="ja">ゆ</span> in the Y group and <span lang="ja">れ</span> in the R group. Next, find the first kana of **やま** and the final kana of **ほん** in the chart. Finally, cover the romanization column and locate <span lang="ja">わ</span>, <span lang="ja">を</span> and <span lang="ja">ん</span> without a Latin-letter hint.

**Common mistakes:** Do not invent missing Y-row or W-row basic cards. Do not treat <span lang="ja">を</span> as a second spelling for every word containing the sound *o*. Do not assume <span lang="ja">ん</span> is interchangeable with <span lang="ja">な</span> or another N-row kana. The printed cards are enough for today's written recognition; sound discrimination requires actual recordings in the later listening lesson.

## Check your understanding

Answer eight original questions about Mio and Yuri, the printed chart, the labels and the role of <span lang="ja">を</span>. Each answer has an explanation. Results are saved locally when browser storage is available. The score is a **written recognition** score, not a listening, writing-quality or pronunciation grade.
