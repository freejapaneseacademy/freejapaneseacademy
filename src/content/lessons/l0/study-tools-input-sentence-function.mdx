## What can you ask a classmate to do?

**Chapter Can-do:** Enter Japanese using a keyboard and control reading aids.

Aya is preparing a short label on a computer. She can see a word but does not know its reading. Her classmate Ken distinguishes three tasks: typing Japanese, converting typed text to kanji, and displaying a reading aid. These are not interchangeable.

<blockquote lang="ja">
あや：すみません。にほんごで かきたいです。

けん：にほんごの にゅうりょくに してください。

あや：「ねこ」と かけました。かんじに へんかんできますか。

けん：「ねこ」は ふつう、ひらがなでも かけますよ。

あや：この かんじの よみかたを おしえて ください。

けん：ふりがなを みて ください。
</blockquote>

**Meaning in English:** Aya wants to write in Japanese. Ken asks her to switch to Japanese input. She types ねこ and asks whether she can convert it to kanji; Ken notes that ねこ is commonly written in hiragana too. Aya then asks how to read a kanji, and Ken points her toward furigana. A conversion candidate is not necessarily the best spelling for a word.

## 1. The grammar that changes the action

| Pattern | Example | What it accomplishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| N + で | <span lang="ja">にほんごで かきます。</span> | States the language or means used: “write in Japanese.” |
| Verb stem + たいです | <span lang="ja">かきたいです。</span> | States a wish: “I want to write.” This is **not** a direct instruction to someone else. |
| Te-form + ください | <span lang="ja">おしえて ください。</span> | Makes a polite request: “Please tell/show me.” |
| N + を + verb | <span lang="ja">よみかたを おしえて ください。</span> | Specifies *what* the listener should explain: the reading. |
| N + に + してください | <span lang="ja">にほんごの にゅうりょくに してください。</span> | Requests a change to a particular setting or state. |
| Potential + ますか | <span lang="ja">へんかんできますか。</span> | Asks whether conversion is possible, not whether it is always appropriate. |

The polite ending <span lang="ja">です</span> does not make every sentence a request. <span lang="ja">かきたいです</span> reports the speaker's wish. <span lang="ja">かいて ください</span> asks another person to write. Compare who is expected to act.

## 2. Input and furigana do different jobs

With a Japanese IME enabled and configured for romaji input, entering `neko` can produce <span lang="ja">ねこ</span>. An IME may then offer conversion candidates, depending on the word and context. Choosing a candidate is a separate step from entering the kana.

**Furigana is not an input mode.** In <ruby lang="ja">日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>, the small kana above the kanji provide its reading. Furigana can help someone understand written kanji without changing the underlying text.

A learner who says <span lang="ja">ふりがなを つけて ください。</span> (“Please add furigana”) is asking for a reading aid. A learner who says <span lang="ja">にほんごで にゅうりょくして ください。</span> (“Please enter it in Japanese”) is asking for a typing action.

**Device note:** IME controls and furigana availability vary by app and operating system. These examples explain the language and workflow, not a universal menu sequence.

## 3. Resolve an ambiguous classroom request

Aya points to <ruby lang="ja">学校<rt>がっこう</rt></ruby> on the screen and says:

<span lang="ja">おしえて ください。</span> — “Please tell me.”

This is polite but underspecified. Ken cannot tell whether Aya wants the meaning, the reading, or instructions for typing it. The listener may infer the intended task from the situation, but a clearer request reduces mistakes.

For the **reading**, say <span lang="ja">この かんじの よみかたを おしえて ください。</span> (“Please tell me how to read this kanji.”)

For the **meaning**, say <span lang="ja">この ことばの いみを おしえて ください。</span> (“Please tell me what this word means.”)

For **typing help**, say <span lang="ja">にほんごの にゅうりょくの しかたを おしえて ください。</span> (“Please show me how to enter Japanese.”)

These requests share <span lang="ja">おしえて ください</span> but the object before <span lang="ja">を</span> specifies the information sought.

## 4. Try it before the scored check

1. You want to write in Japanese, not ask a friend to write for you. Choose <span lang="ja">にほんごで かきたいです。</span>, not <span lang="ja">にほんごで かいて ください。</span>
2. You can see <ruby lang="ja">先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby> but cannot read it. Ask for <span lang="ja">よみかた</span>, not <span lang="ja">へんかん</span>.
3. A classmate speaks too quickly. <span lang="ja">ゆっくり おねがいします。</span> is more relevant than changing the IME.

**Model explanation:** <span lang="ja">かきたいです</span> expresses the learner's desire, while <span lang="ja">かいて ください</span> directs a request to another person. Furigana shows the reading of existing kanji; IME conversion changes a typed candidate. A specific noun phrase before <span lang="ja">を おしえて ください</span> makes the request less ambiguous.

## 5. Self-check

Without looking back, explain the difference between <span lang="ja">にほんごで かきたいです</span> and <span lang="ja">にほんごで かいて ください</span>. Then write a polite Japanese request for the reading of a kanji. Compare with the model <span lang="ja">この かんじの よみかたを おしえて ください。</span>.
