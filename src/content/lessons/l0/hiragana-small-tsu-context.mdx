## The library label-reading game

**Chapter Can-do:** Read words with small <span lang="ja">っ</span> and long-vowel spellings accurately. This first lesson introduces the **written context**; it does not grade pronunciation or listening. Later lessons teach more spelling contrasts and use real recordings.

At the neighborhood library, **Mika** prepares three labels for a display shelf. **Ren** matches each label to a picture. **Nao**, the volunteer librarian, checks the kana and explains why a small <span lang="ja">っ</span> is not the same as full-sized <span lang="ja">つ</span>. Their goal is to read the labels carefully enough to find the correct picture.

| Person | Role | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mika | Visitor | Prepares the written labels |
| Ren | Visitor | Matches labels with pictures |
| Nao | Volunteer librarian | Checks kana size and explains the spelling |

## The small っ on a label

The word <span lang="ja">きって</span> (*kitte*, postage stamp) contains a **small っ** before <span lang="ja">て</span>. It marks a held consonant timing, traditionally called a *geminate*. It counts as **one mora** (a Japanese rhythmic unit), even though it is not pronounced as a separate *tsu*. In contrast, full-sized <span lang="ja">つ</span> represents the syllable-like sound *tsu*, as in <span lang="ja">つき</span> (*tsuki*, moon).

| Written form | Meaning | What to notice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">きって</span> | postage stamp | small っ before て |
| <span lang="ja">つき</span> | moon | full-sized つ at the start |
| <span lang="ja">きっぷ</span> | ticket | small っ before ぷ |

Do not read <span lang="ja">きって</span> as *ki-tsu-te*. The small mark changes timing, not the word into three spoken syllables. The spelling <span lang="ja">きて</span> is a different form (the request “come,” in some contexts); it is **not** an alternative spelling of “stamp.”

## Long vowels on the other shelf

Mika also puts up a label for <span lang="ja">こうえん</span> (*kōen*, park). Its <span lang="ja">こう</span> has the common written long-*o* pattern <span lang="ja">おう</span> after a consonant: <span lang="ja">こ + う</span>. Other words can spell long *o* with <span lang="ja">おお</span>, as in <span lang="ja">おおきい</span> (*ōkii*, big). Long *e* is commonly written <span lang="ja">えい</span>, as in <span lang="ja">せんせい</span> (*sensei*, teacher). These patterns help you recognize writing, but they are **not freely interchangeable**: copy the spelling of each actual word.

| Label | Meaning | Spelling clue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">こうえん</span> | park | こう, a long-o spelling |
| <span lang="ja">おおきい</span> | big | おお, another long-o spelling |
| <span lang="ja">せんせい</span> | teacher | せい, a common long-e spelling |

The romanized macrons above are reading cues, not a claim that every written sequence sounds identical in all contexts. This is written recognition practice. Listen to the later recorded dictation before making pronunciation judgments.

## Match the library labels

Mika places a stamp picture next to <span lang="ja">きって</span>, a ticket picture next to <span lang="ja">きっぷ</span>, and a park picture next to <span lang="ja">こうえん</span>. Ren matches the labels; Nao checks that no one enlarged the small <span lang="ja">っ</span>. The label <span lang="ja">せんせい</span> is on a teacher's name card, while <span lang="ja">おおきい</span> describes a large book.

**Guided practice:** First locate the small <span lang="ja">っ</span> in <span lang="ja">きって</span> and <span lang="ja">きっぷ</span>. Next find the full-sized <span lang="ja">つ</span> in <span lang="ja">つき</span>. Then identify the written patterns <span lang="ja">こう・おお・せい</span>. Finally recall who wrote, matched and checked the labels.

**Common misconception:** The small <span lang="ja">っ</span> is not the full word “tsu,” and the spellings <span lang="ja">おう</span> and <span lang="ja">おお</span> cannot simply be swapped. These are spelling-and-timing distinctions, not decorative variations.

## Check your understanding

Answer eight written questions about the library setting, participants, small-<span lang="ja">っ</span> labels and long-vowel spellings. Every question has a keyed answer and an explanation. This score measures **written recognition**, not spoken accuracy, audio comprehension or official JLPT proficiency.
