## Your task at the station café

**Can-do:** Read and reproduce common katakana loanwords, including long vowels and borrowed sound combinations. A café clerk is preparing a menu beside the station ticket counter. You will check the written signs before they are printed. This lesson tests **written recognition and typing**, not listening or pronunciation. Spoken announcement comprehension comes in L06.

### Step 1 — Read the marks, not just the letters

The horizontal **ー** (chōonpu) lengthens the preceding vowel in katakana. It is not a hyphen, and leaving it out can change a word's conventional spelling.

| Printed label | Reading support | English in this scene |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">コーヒー</span> | kōhī | coffee |
| <span lang="ja">ケーキ</span> | kēki | cake |
| <span lang="ja">タクシー</span> | takushī | taxi |
| <span lang="ja">テーブル</span> | tēburu | table |
| <span lang="ja">ティー</span> | tī | tea (loanword label) |

For <span lang="ja">コーヒー</span>, both **コー** and **ヒー** contain a long vowel. Copy both marks. The English word *tea* is also often <span lang="ja">お茶</span> (おちゃ) in ordinary Japanese; <span lang="ja">ティー</span> appears in borrowed names and menu descriptions. Context matters.

### Step 2 — A small kana changes the sound unit

Compare the **small** kana with the full-size kana. A small vowel combines with the preceding katakana rather than standing as a separate full-sized syllable.

| Combination | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">ティ</span> (ti) | <span lang="ja">ティー</span> | tea, as a loanword |
| <span lang="ja">ファ</span> (fa) | <span lang="ja">ファイル</span> | file (paper or digital, by context) |
| <span lang="ja">チェ</span> (che) | <span lang="ja">チェーン</span> | chain |

In <span lang="ja">ファイル</span>, **ァ** is smaller than ordinary **ア**. In <span lang="ja">チェーン</span>, **ェ** is smaller than **エ**, and the following **ー** lengthens the vowel. These are written conventions for borrowed sounds; they are not new kanji.

### Step 3 — Compare whole words

| Correct label | Common learner slip | What to inspect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">コーヒー</span> | <span lang="ja">コヒー</span> | Missing ー after コ |
| <span lang="ja">ティー</span> | <span lang="ja">テイー</span> | Full イ instead of small ィ |
| <span lang="ja">ファイル</span> | <span lang="ja">フアイル</span> | Full ア instead of small ァ |
| <span lang="ja">チェーン</span> | <span lang="ja">チエーン</span> | Full エ instead of small ェ |
| <span lang="ja">タクシー</span> | <span lang="ja">タクシ</span> | Missing final ー |

A different spelling may sometimes exist as a recognized style variant. For example, <span lang="ja">コンピュータ</span> and <span lang="ja">コンピューター</span> both occur. Do not assume every omitted ー is always wrong in every loanword; check the word and editorial style. This practice uses conventional signs with an unambiguous target.

### Step 4 — Guided typing and optional tracing

Enable Japanese input on your device. Type *koohii* and convert to <span lang="ja">コーヒー</span>; some IMEs instead accept *ko-hi-* for the same katakana. Type *fairu* for <span lang="ja">ファイル</span>, *thi-* or your IME's supported *ti-* sequence for <span lang="ja">ティー</span>, and *che-n* for <span lang="ja">チェーン</span>. IME key sequences vary: inspect the **resulting Japanese characters** rather than trusting a single romanization recipe.

Copy these labels on paper: **ケーキ / ティー / ファイル / チェーン**. Circle every ー and underline the small vowel kana. Optional handwriting is self-reviewed, not automatically scored. Look for a clearly smaller ィ・ァ・ェ, a separate ー, and readable character spacing.

### Step 5 — Apply in a realistic scene

An original café sign reads: **コーヒー　ケーキ　ティー**. A station desk sign reads: **タクシー　チケット**. The first groups menu items; the second gives travel-related information. **チケット** has small **ッ**, which marks a consonant closure, not a long vowel; don't replace it with ー.

Try the scored written check below without looking at the comparison table. Then revisit each explanation and retry any missed spellings. The written score is local practice only, not an official JLPT result or a pronunciation assessment.
