## Hear the conversation before reading it

At a library, people often ask for a book, say where it is, or ask to borrow it. Listen to the two **original slow Japanese conversations** before opening the transcript. The six questions are about what is **said aloud**, not how kana looks on a page. Replay as often as you need.

## Track A — Finding a book

A visitor asks the librarian about a particular book. Listen for the topic of the book, whether the library has it, and the place the librarian points to. The answer is spoken directly in the dialogue. Do not open the transcript until you have attempted the three questions.

## Track B — Borrowing a book

A visitor wants to take a book home. The librarian gives one short instruction and repeats where to do it. Listen for the action the visitor requests, the information the librarian needs, and the location where the visitor should write it.

## Read after you listen

After your first listening attempt, open the transcript to check each line. Notice that **は** can mark the topic, **を** marks the object of an action, and **に** can mark a location where something is written. Japanese sentences do not normally insert spaces between every word. **。** closes a statement and **か** often signals a question. Those written observations support your reading practice; they are **not** scored as listening comprehension.

## Optional self-check dictation

Replay one line, write what you think you heard in hiragana, and then compare it with the transcript. For example, listen for the request before inspecting its particles. You can make a correction on paper or in your own notes. This is unscored practice, not a claim of automatic speech or handwriting assessment.
