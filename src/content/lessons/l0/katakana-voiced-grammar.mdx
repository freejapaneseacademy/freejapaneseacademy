## Shop labels are precise

**Can-do:** Complete basic katakana plus voiced sound families. At a fictional neighborhood shop, you see <span lang="ja">カバン</span> (bag), <span lang="ja">パン</span> (bread), <span lang="ja">ペン</span> (pen) and <span lang="ja">ソース</span> (sauce). A tiny mark can change which word you read. This lesson checks **written recognition**, not pronunciation or listening.

## 1. Two marks, two different changes

A **dakuten** (゛) consists of two short strokes at the upper right. It changes the represented consonant in a systematic series. Read across each row:

| Unvoiced | Voiced with dakuten | Sound pair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| カ キ ク ケ コ | ガ ギ グ ゲ ゴ | ka/ga, ki/gi, ku/gu, ke/ge, ko/go |
| サ シ ス セ ソ | ザ ジ ズ ゼ ゾ | sa/za, shi/ji, su/zu, se/ze, so/zo |
| タ チ ツ テ ト | ダ ヂ ヅ デ ド | ta/da, chi/ji, tsu/zu, te/de, to/do |
| ハ ヒ フ ヘ ホ | バ ビ ブ ベ ボ | ha/ba, hi/bi, fu/bu, he/be, ho/bo |

**Important spelling note:** ヂ and ヅ exist, but everyday Japanese usually writes the sounds commonly romanized *ji* and *zu* as ジ and ズ. The spellings are not freely interchangeable in every word. Learn the printed form of each actual word rather than replacing characters by sound alone.

A **handakuten** (゜) is a small circle. It attaches to the H row to make the P row:

| Base | With dakuten | With handakuten |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ハ (ha) | バ (ba) | パ (pa) |
| ヒ (hi) | ビ (bi) | ピ (pi) |
| フ (fu) | ブ (bu) | プ (pu) |
| ヘ (he) | ベ (be) | ペ (pe) |
| ホ (ho) | ボ (bo) | ポ (po) |

The **marks are not decoration**. Compare <span lang="ja">カバン</span> (*kaban*, bag), which has バ, and <span lang="ja">パン</span> (*pan*, bread), which has パ. Swapping the two marks changes the spelling and represented sound.

## 2. A shop counter comparison

Imagine the clerk places these four written labels on a counter:

| Label | Meaning | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">カバン</span> | bag | バ is ba (dakuten); final ン is n |
| <span lang="ja">パン</span> | bread | パ is pa (handakuten); final ン is n |
| <span lang="ja">ペン</span> | pen | ペ is pe (handakuten); final ン is n |
| <span lang="ja">ソース</span> | sauce | initial ソ is so; ー lengthens the preceding vowel |

**Guided check:** Point to the small circle in パン and ペン, then the two short marks in カバン. In ソース, locate the initial ソ. The long vowel bar ー is a separate symbol; it is not a dakuten.

## 3. Do not confuse ン and ソ

<span lang="ja">ン</span> represents a moraic *n*. <span lang="ja">ソ</span> represents *so*. Their printed strokes are similar in some fonts, but they are different characters. Compare them **side by side** in the same font: <span lang="ja">ン　ソ</span>. The direction of the short strokes and the longer finishing stroke differs in standard handwriting. Do not rely on a single stroke-direction mnemonic across all typefaces: use the **whole character** and the **word context**.

In <span lang="ja">ペン</span>, the final character is ン; in <span lang="ja">ソース</span>, the first character is ソ. These are written examples; they do not establish that the two sounds are interchangeable.

## 4. Common errors and repair

- Reading バ as パ: inspect **two strokes** versus a **small circle**.
- Reading ガ as カ: the added dakuten changes *ka* to *ga*.
- Treating ン and ソ as variants of the same letter: compare printed examples and check the word.
- Assuming every katakana word is English: パン means bread in Japanese, but it is historically derived from Portuguese.
