## Chapter checkpoint: read, compare, listen

**Can-do:** Read and write the N, H, M and Y katakana rows. This checkpoint combines script recognition, a new written shop display and comprehension of two original audio conversations. Complete the listening questions **by ear before opening transcripts**; using the transcript makes the attempt assisted practice, not an unaided listening result.

### A quick review of the rows

| Row | Katakana | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| N | <span lang="ja">ナ ニ ヌ ネ ノ</span> | na ni nu ne no |
| H | <span lang="ja">ハ ヒ フ ヘ ホ</span> | ha hi fu he ho |
| M | <span lang="ja">マ ミ ム メ モ</span> | ma mi mu me mo |
| Y | <span lang="ja">ヤ ユ ヨ</span> | ya yu yo |

The Y row has only **three standard modern basic katakana**. Do not invent ordinary yi/ye symbols in this beginner table. Compare ナ with メ (different crossing strokes), ミ with ム, and ユ with ヨ. Recognizing a shape does not by itself tell you whether a label is a name or a product.

### Original shop notice for the written section

This fictional counter has a **food shelf** marked <span lang="ja">ハム</span> (ham) and <span lang="ja">バナナ</span> (banana), a **fabric shelf** marked <span lang="ja">ヌノ</span> (cloth, a phonetic teaching label; ordinary spelling is often 布 or ぬの), and a **stationery table** headed <span lang="ja">メモ</span> (memo). Two **visitor cards** read <span lang="ja">ミナ</span> (Mina) and <span lang="ja">ユミ</span> (Yumi).

A product name and a visitor's name can both be written in katakana. The **placement** of the card provides its role. The voiced character バ in バナナ is a familiar whole-word reading; systematic voiced rows come later. You are not being asked to infer a price, brand, or quantity from the written labels.

### Listen without reading the scripts

Track A is a fruit-stall conversation. Track B takes place at a deli counter. Each is an original academy-authored dialogue recorded using licensed AivisSpeech voices. Use the playback controls to replay or slow the audio. Answer the listening items from the audio, not from the written shop notice: the scenes are distinct. After submitting, compare the revealed transcripts and rationales to your answers. No browser text-to-speech substitute is provided.

### How the checkpoint is scored

There are **12 scored single-choice questions**: five shape/reading items, four written evidence items and three listening items. Each has one correct answer, a stable ID and a post-submission explanation. The score and completion are stored locally on your device. If you reveal the transcript before submitting, your score is explicitly marked **assisted**. An automated score does not certify speaking ability, pronunciation or independent proficiency.

### Review after feedback

For a shape error, write the mistaken character next to the correct one and identify its strokes. For a reading error, quote the relevant printed label. For a listening error, replay the relevant turn before reading its transcript, then repeat it if comfortable. Keep the weak forms for spaced repetition. Independent qualified Japanese-language, pronunciation and assessment reviews have not been claimed.
