## Yuri's picture-label board

Yuri is preparing a classroom reading display. The cards are **written** clues: read each Japanese label and match it to its picture or instruction. You do not need to infer hidden information. For every answer, point to the **exact Japanese phrase** on the board that supports it.

| Card | Printed Japanese | Picture or classroom note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | **やま** | mountain |
| B | **ゆめ** | dream (a thought bubble) |
| C | **もも** | peach |
| D | **わに** | crocodile |
| E | **ほん** | book |
| F | **みかん** | mandarin orange |
| G | **むら** | village |
| H | **くるま** | car |
| I | **もり** | forest |
| J | **よる** | night |

All ten labels are ordinary beginner Japanese nouns written in hiragana for practice. Some normally appear in kanji in other contexts. A card's letter is an index, not part of its Japanese spelling.

## Two short reading notices

Yuri adds a note beneath the book card:

**ほんをよむ。** — “Read a book.” Here **ほん** means “book,” **を** marks the object, and **よむ** means “read.” The particle **を** is normally pronounced *o*, even though kana charts often romanize it as *wo*. It is **not** the same written character as **お**.

Under the mandarin orange picture, Yuri writes:

**みかんをみる。** — “Look at a mandarin orange.” Here **みかん** is the object of **みる** (“look at”), marked by **を**. The final **ん** in **みかん** is its own mora: み・か・ん. In **ほん**, the morae are ほ・ん.

These are short classroom practice notices, not a conversation. Do not treat the word **みかん** as “orange juice” or **もも** as “apple.”

## Guided evidence hunt

First locate **わに** in row D, then find **もり** in row I. Their initial characters are different: わ (*wa*) and も (*mo*). Next locate **よる** in row J and **ゆめ** in row B. Both begin with Y-row kana, but よ and ゆ are distinct.

Read the two notices without relying on their English glosses. The exact phrase **ほんをよむ。** supports the claim that the object is a book. The exact phrase **みかんをみる。** supports the claim that the object is a mandarin orange. You can cite the entire phrase or the shortest relevant word when answering a detail question.

## Independent reading check

Answer twelve scored questions using the printed board and notices. Each item has a stable ID, a correct answer and an evidence explanation. You may reread the source while answering: this is a **reading comprehension** check, not a memory-speed test. Feedback identifies the printed Japanese evidence after submission.

The questions use multiple-choice options. A score records comprehension of this specific printed passage; it does not certify fluent reading or pronunciation. Attempts and completion are stored separately on this device when storage is available.
