## Read whole words, not just isolated cards

**Chapter Can-do:** Read connected all-hiragana words with improving speed and accuracy. This first lesson introduces a realistic *written* word-card task. Later lessons practise confusable shapes, segmentation, fluency and recorded read-aloud comprehension. We do not assess speed or pronunciation here.

In an original after-school classroom scene, learner **Hana** prepares a picture-and-word board for younger learners. Tutor **Sora** checks the cards before they are displayed. Hana can already identify the 46 basic hiragana, but sometimes reads a character correctly without recognizing the whole word. The board is for a **beginner reading game**, not a shop order or an oral exam.

| Participant | Role | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hana | Learner | Arranges printed hiragana word cards beside picture clues |
| Sora | Tutor | Checks each complete word and its meaning |
| Visiting beginners | Readers | Use the finished board to practise reading |

## Four original word cards

The cards show ordinary words using basic hiragana. A picture clue gives the intended meaning; these are written examples, not audio recordings.

| Card | Printed word | Reading cue | Picture clue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | <span lang="ja">さかな</span> | sakana | fish |
| B | <span lang="ja">たまご</span> | tamago | egg |
| C | <span lang="ja">やま</span> | yama | mountain |
| D | <span lang="ja">くるま</span> | kuruma | car |

Notice that <span lang="ja">さかな</span> is **three** kana, not one symbol, and <span lang="ja">くるま</span> is also three. The printed word <span lang="ja">たまご</span> includes voiced <span lang="ja">ご</span>, which belongs to the later voiced-kana unit; for this first fluency scene, treat the complete word as a supported preview, not as a requirement to produce the voiced row independently. If that preview is unfamiliar, focus on the other three cards.

## The board's short message

Hana writes <span lang="ja">これはさかなです。</span> (*Kore wa sakana desu.* — “This is a fish.”) below card A. The sentence uses familiar <span lang="ja">これ</span> (“this”), topic particle <span lang="ja">は</span> (pronounced *wa* when used as a topic particle), <span lang="ja">さかな</span> (“fish”), and polite <span lang="ja">です</span>. Written Japanese does not normally insert spaces between those elements.

For supported segmentation, compare:

| With beginner spacing | Normal continuous writing |
| --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">これ は さかな です。</span> | <span lang="ja">これはさかなです。</span> |

The spaces in the first version are a teaching aid, **not** an alternative Japanese spelling rule. In the normal version, the same symbols appear in the same order. A word boundary is understood from vocabulary and sentence structure rather than a printed blank.

## Guided reading and independent practice

**First:** Point to each kana in <span lang="ja">やま</span> and <span lang="ja">くるま</span>. Then read each complete card as one word. **Next:** Find the fish card without looking at the romanization column. **Finally:** Cover the picture clues and match the four written words to their meanings again.

A common mistake is to treat each kana as a separate English word. Another is to guess that a long uninterrupted hiragana string has no word boundaries. The tutor checks the **whole label** and the clue, not merely whether a learner named one character.
