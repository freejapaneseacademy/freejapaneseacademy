## A new conversation at the library

**Chapter Can-do:** Decode short hiragana sentences without relying on romaji. This is an original **written reading** passage, not a recording. Yui and librarian Sora speak again, but this time Yui asks about a different book. Read the Japanese before checking the support. You will locate **exact Japanese words** that prove each answer, rather than guessing from the setting.

### First reading: no romaji

| Turn | Speaker | Japanese |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Yui | <span lang="ja">すみません。ねこのほんはありますか。</span> |
| 2 | Sora | <span lang="ja">はい、あります。</span> |
| 3 | Yui | <span lang="ja">どこですか。</span> |
| 4 | Sora | <span lang="ja">あそこです。あおいほんのとなりです。</span> |
| 5 | Yui | <span lang="ja">ありがとうございます。ねこのほんをみます。</span> |
| 6 | Sora | <span lang="ja">どうぞ。</span> |

**Reading task:** Cover the English support below. Identify the book Yui asks for, where Sora directs her, and what Yui says she will do. Underline the Japanese phrase for each answer. Japanese normally uses connected writing without spaces between words; the passage follows that convention.

## Read again with meaning support

| Japanese phrase | Meaning here |
| --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">すみません。</span> | Excuse me. |
| <span lang="ja">ねこのほんはありますか。</span> | Do you have a book about cats? |
| <span lang="ja">はい、あります。</span> | Yes, we do. |
| <span lang="ja">どこですか。</span> | Where is it? |
| <span lang="ja">あそこです。</span> | It is over there. |
| <span lang="ja">あおいほんのとなりです。</span> | It is next to the blue book. |
| <span lang="ja">ありがとうございます。</span> | Thank you very much. |
| <span lang="ja">ねこのほんをみます。</span> | I will look at the book about cats. |
| <span lang="ja">どうぞ。</span> | Go ahead / Here you are (inviting Yui to proceed). |

**New word support:** <span lang="ja">ねこ</span> = cat; <span lang="ja">ほん</span> = book; <span lang="ja">あそこ</span> = over there (away from both speakers); <span lang="ja">あおい</span> = blue; <span lang="ja">となり</span> = next to; <span lang="ja">どこ</span> = where. The phrase <span lang="ja">ねこのほん</span> means a book about cats in this scene, not a book owned by a cat. <span lang="ja">の</span> links the nouns; in <span lang="ja">あおいほんのとなり</span>, it relates the location to the blue book.

## Find evidence, not just a translation

**Who asks about a book?** Yui's turn 1 contains <span lang="ja">ねこのほんはありますか。</span>. The final <span lang="ja">か</span> marks a question; topic <span lang="ja">は</span> is written は even when pronounced *wa*.

**Is the book available?** Sora's turn 2 says <span lang="ja">はい、あります。</span>. That is the explicit yes-answer; the dialogue does not say whether Yui may borrow it.

**Where is the book?** Turn 4 contains two separate clues: <span lang="ja">あそこです。</span> gives a general location, and <span lang="ja">あおいほんのとなりです。</span> identifies the neighboring book. Neither sentence says the cat book itself is blue.

**What will Yui do?** Her turn 5 says <span lang="ja">ねこのほんをみます。</span>. The object particle <span lang="ja">を</span> follows the book phrase, and <span lang="ja">みます</span> means look at. Do not infer that she buys or borrows it.

### Punctuation and reading boundaries

A Japanese full stop <span lang="ja">。</span> closes a sentence, and <span lang="ja">、</span> separates a phrase within one sentence, as in <span lang="ja">はい、あります。</span>. A single speaker can say two sentences in one turn, as Sora does in turn 4. There are no teaching spaces in the dialogue; to decode <span lang="ja">ねこのほんをみます。</span>, temporarily group <span lang="ja">ねこのほん / を / みます / 。</span>, then read the connected version again.

## Three passes toward independent reading

1. **Guided:** Read turns 1–3 and point to <span lang="ja">か</span> in both questions. Identify the speaker who says <span lang="ja">はい</span>.
2. **Evidence practice:** Hide the English column. Copy the exact Japanese sentence that answers “Where is it relative to another book?” Check that your quotation includes <span lang="ja">となり</span>.
3. **Independent:** Read all six turns without English or romaji. Write a one-sentence summary in your preferred language and quote one Japanese phrase for the requested book, one for the location, and one for the action. The quotations, not an imagined detail, are the evidence.

**Common inference error:** <span lang="ja">あおいほんのとなりです。</span> says **next to** the blue book. It does not say “the cat book is blue.” Likewise <span lang="ja">みます</span> means look at, not buy. Keep explicit text separate from guesses.
