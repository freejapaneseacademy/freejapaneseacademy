## Listen for meaning at a station kiosk

**Chapter Can-do:** read and write the katakana vowels and K/S/T rows. This listening activity strengthens the connection between familiar katakana loanwords and their **spoken meanings**. The assessed skill is what you hear, not whether you can see or spell a character. You can replay both original conversations as often as you need.

Before playing either track, remember that a kiosk customer might ask for a drink or a snack, specify how many, and confirm a detail. These are general listening strategies; the answers to the six questions are intentionally **not disclosed** in the introduction.

## Track A — A drink order

Listen to the clerk and customer. Pay attention to the **item**, the **number ordered**, and the **customer's preference**. Answer the three Track A questions without opening the transcript if you want an unaided score.

## Track B — A different order

Listen to another short exchange. This time, pay attention to **what the customer asks about**, **whether the kiosk has it**, and **how many are requested**. The clerk's final line confirms the quantity.

## After your first attempt

Reveal the Japanese transcript and English translation **after submitting**, or reveal them earlier for accessible assisted practice. A transcript-assisted attempt can be checked for learning, but it is **not saved as an unaided listening score**.

After scoring, study the revealed transcripts for how a familiar loanword fits into a spoken request. Identify the particle that marks the requested item, the polite request ending, and the counter word for quantity. Compare the two exchanges and then return to your earlier written katakana vocabulary lesson. These are **post-listening study prompts**, not written-script listening questions.

## Optional unscored practice

Replay one request and try saying it aloud, then read the revealed transcript and compare the meaning. You may also write the katakana word you recognize on paper. No microphone, pronunciation grading or handwriting scoring is required.
