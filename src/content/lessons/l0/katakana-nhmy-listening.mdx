## Listen at two small shops

**Chapter Can-do:** read and write katakana N, H, M and Y rows. This lesson links familiar product words to **spoken Japanese**, but scores only what you understand by ear. Written character-shape discrimination is practised in the earlier script lesson, not in this listening quiz.

You will hear two original, short exchanges with a customer and shop clerk. The scenes use polite phrases such as **ありますか** (“Do you have…?”), **あります** (“We have…”), and **ください** (“Please give me…”). These expressions help you follow a shop request. The specific answers are deliberately not printed here before you listen.

## First listening: a fruit stall

Play Track A. Listen for the fruit, whether it is available, and how many the customer asks for. Try the three questions without revealing the transcript. You can replay the audio; listening more than once does not make the attempt assisted.

## Second listening: a deli counter

Play Track B. The speakers are in a different kind of shop. Listen for the item requested, the clerk's answer, and the quantity. Compare how the two customers ask for something after your first attempt.

## Check and learn from the transcript

After submitting, reveal the Japanese turns and English support to check each answer against what was spoken. You may reveal the transcript earlier as an accessibility aid; that attempt is explicitly treated as **assisted practice**, not an unaided listening score.

Notice how a katakana product name can be surrounded by hiragana grammar. In the follow-up reading activity, identify the item after **を** in a request and the phrase used to confirm availability. The difference between **ひとつ** and **ふたつ** changes the quantity. These are post-listening written observations, not clues for the scored audio questions.

## Optional practice

Replay one turn and repeat it aloud if comfortable. You can also copy the product name in katakana on paper and compare with the revealed transcript. No microphone, recording or automatic pronunciation judgment is required.
