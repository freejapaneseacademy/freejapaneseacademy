## A message beside the classroom door

A learner has written a label for a notebook on a table. Another learner asks about it. Read the original exchange:

> ミナ：これは ノートです。 — This is a notebook.
>
> リオ：これは ほんですか。 — Is this a book?
>
> ミナ：いいえ、ノートです。 — No, it's a notebook.

The scene is written practice, **not a recording**. A speaker may leave out a topic when it is clear from context; **ノートです** here refers to the object being discussed.

## 1. Look closely at the particles

| Written form | Read aloud | Job | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **は** | *wa* when it marks a topic | Introduces what the comment is about | わたし**は** がくせいです。 — I am a student. |
| **か** | *ka* | Marks a polite yes/no question after です | これ**は** ほんです**か**。 — Is this a book? |
| **を** | usually *o* as a particle | Marks the direct object of many verbs | ほん**を** よみます。 — I read a book. |

**を is only a spelling preview here**: よみます (“read”) is not tested as a new verb pattern. In **これは ほんです**, do not replace は with を. The topic particle is written **は** even though it is pronounced *wa*. The syllable **は** in **はな** (flower), by contrast, is pronounced *ha*.

## 2. Kana and word boundaries

| Japanese | Reading cue | Meaning | Notice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| わたし | wa-ta-shi | I / me | し, not ち |
| がくせい | ga-ku-se-i | student | が has two dots; せい ends せ + い |
| せんせい | se-n-se-i | teacher | ん comes before せ |
| ほん | ho-n | book | final ん |
| ノート | nō-to | notebook | long vowel mark ー |
| ペン | pe-n | pen | ペ has the handakuten circle |
| これ | ko-re | this (one) | こ, not ご |

**Long vowel caution:** ノート contains **ー**, not an extra オ. Japanese uses katakana for many loanwords; do not change **ノート** into hiragana when copying this classroom label. Spacing between phrases in this beginner lesson is for readability; ordinary Japanese is usually written without those spaces.

## 3. Type a sentence using a Japanese IME

Switch your keyboard to Japanese input. To produce **わたしは がくせいです。**, type the sounds *watashi wa gakusei desu*, then check the **written particle**: it must be **は**, not わ. Depending on the IME, typing *wa* produces わ, so enter *ha* for the particle or select the correct conversion. For **ノート**, use katakana conversion and verify the long mark **ー**.

Try these three controlled copies without looking at the model after the first attempt:

1. **これは ペンです。** — This is a pen.
2. **これは ノートですか。** — Is this a notebook?
3. **わたしは がくせいです。** — I am a student.

Compare character by character. Are **は** and **か** in the right positions? Does **ノート** have the long mark? The Japanese full stop **。** is preferred in the model; some keyboards may require conversion to enter it.

## 4. Guided correction: two different mistakes

**Incorrect:** これわ ノートです。  
**Correct:** これは ノートです。 — This is a notebook.

The topic particle sounds like *wa* but is spelled **は**.

**Incorrect:** これは ノトですか。  
**Correct:** これは ノートですか。 — Is this a notebook?

The missing **ー** changes the written word. When copying, treat the long mark as a real character.

**Incorrect:** これは ほんをです。  
**Correct:** これは ほんです。 — This is a book.

An object marker is not part of this noun-predicate frame.

## 5. Independent script check

Imagine a fictional desk with a pen and a notebook. Write one polite statement identifying the pen, and one polite question asking if the other object is a notebook. Model responses: **これは ペンです。** and **これは ノートですか。** Compare spelling, particle choice, katakana long mark, and final **か**. You can also trace **は・を・か・ん** slowly on paper; tracing is optional and not scored.

The interactive check below measures **written recognition and proofreading**, not handwriting quality, spoken fluency or audio comprehension. Optional speaking: read your sentences aloud to yourself, remembering particle は is pronounced *wa*. No recording or pronunciation score is collected.
