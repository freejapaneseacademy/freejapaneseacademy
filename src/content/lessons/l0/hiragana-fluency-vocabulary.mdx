## From kana cards to useful words

Hana and Sora are preparing the same **written classroom word-card game** from Lesson 01. Recognizing each kana is the first step; recognizing the **whole word** helps you find a matching picture or follow a simple instruction. This lesson uses the 46 basic hiragana and familiar words. There is no recorded audio, timed-reading score, or pronunciation assessment.

### Word bank: people, places and things

| Word | Reading support | Meaning | Example classroom clue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">さかな</span> | sakana | fish | A picture of a fish |
| <span lang="ja">やま</span> | yama | mountain | A mountain picture |
| <span lang="ja">くるま</span> | kuruma | car | A car picture |
| <span lang="ja">ねこ</span> | neko | cat | A cat picture |
| <span lang="ja">いぬ</span> | inu | dog | A dog picture |
| <span lang="ja">はな</span> | hana | flower; also nose in another context | A flower picture |
| <span lang="ja">みず</span> | mizu | water | A glass of water |
| <span lang="ja">ほん</span> | hon | book | A book picture |
| <span lang="ja">そら</span> | sora | sky | A sky picture |
| <span lang="ja">ともだち</span> | tomodachi | friend | Two friends |

**Reading boundary:** <span lang="ja">はな</span> can mean *flower* or *nose*. A picture and context resolve which meaning is intended; the spelling alone does not. <span lang="ja">みず</span> includes voiced <span lang="ja">ず</span>, and <span lang="ja">ともだち</span> includes voiced <span lang="ja">だ</span>. These are familiar whole-word previews; independent voiced-kana production comes later. All scored recognition can be answered using the supplied word bank.

### Three useful instructions in the game

Sora writes three short instructions beside the cards:

1. <span lang="ja">ねこをみてください。</span> — “Please look at the cat.”
2. <span lang="ja">ほんをみてください。</span> — “Please look at the book.”
3. <span lang="ja">やまはどれですか。</span> — “Which one is the mountain?”

Here <span lang="ja">ねこ</span> and <span lang="ja">ほん</span> name **what to look at**. The particle <span lang="ja">を</span> marks the object of <span lang="ja">みてください</span> (“please look”). The third sentence uses <span lang="ja">は</span> as a topic marker, pronounced *wa* in this grammatical use. <span lang="ja">どれ</span> asks “which one?” when choosing among visible cards.

| Teaching spaces (to see chunks) | Ordinary Japanese |
| --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">ねこ を みてください。</span> | <span lang="ja">ねこをみてください。</span> |
| <span lang="ja">やま は どれですか。</span> | <span lang="ja">やまはどれですか。</span> |

The spaces are **only a teaching aid**. Ordinary Japanese normally uses the continuous spellings at right. In a classroom game, <span lang="ja">ねこ</span> is not an instruction by itself; <span lang="ja">ねこをみてください。</span> is a request to look at the cat.

### Meaning versus use

A vocabulary label identifies a thing: <span lang="ja">ほん</span> means *book*. In the instruction <span lang="ja">ほんをみてください。</span>, that word identifies the object of the request; the entire sentence means *Please look at the book*, **not** “book please.” Similarly, <span lang="ja">はな</span> must be interpreted with a clue: on Hana's flower card it means *flower*, not *nose*.

Do not confuse the learner's name **Hana** with the common word <span lang="ja">はな</span>. They can share a pronunciation without referring to the same thing. Also, reading <span lang="ja">ほん</span> as <span lang="ja">ほ</span> plus <span lang="ja">ん</span> is useful for decoding, but its meaning is retrieved as a complete word.

### Guided recall

Cover the English meanings above. Find <span lang="ja">いぬ</span>, <span lang="ja">そら</span> and <span lang="ja">くるま</span>, and say or think of the matching picture. Then uncover the meanings. Next, read the three instructions and identify the **object** or the **choice question**. If you are unsure, use the word bank before taking the scored check.
