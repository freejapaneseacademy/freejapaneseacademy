## Your Can-do checkpoint

At the station kiosk you can recognize and write the katakana vowels and K/S/T rows, read familiar labels and identify what a customer actually orders. This is an original academy assessment, not an official JLPT score. It does **not** assess your accent, handwriting neatness or spoken fluency.

## 1. Read the shapes before answering

The five vowels are **ア イ ウ エ オ**. The K row is **カ キ ク ケ コ**, the S row **サ シ ス セ ソ**, and the T row **タ チ ツ テ ト**. Watch especially **シ** (*shi*) versus **ツ** (*tsu*): they are different characters even when their strokes look similar. **ス** (*su*) and **ソ** (*so*) also differ. The short quiz tests the shapes themselves, not a claim about spoken pronunciation.

A useful strategy is to identify each character in a word before guessing its meaning. For example, <span lang="ja">ココア</span> is **ko-ko-a**, the common Japanese word for cocoa. <span lang="ja">カカオ</span> (*ka-ka-o*) is cacao; the two are related but not interchangeable on a kiosk menu.

## 2. An original kiosk label card

This is a **fictional written card**, not an audio transcript. Read it once without the English hints and locate the exact label for each question.

| Kiosk card | Contextual English |
| --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">ココア</span> | cocoa drink |
| <span lang="ja">アイス</span> | ice cream at this kiosk |
| <span lang="ja">カカオ</span> | cacao ingredient |
| <span lang="ja">テキスト</span> | printed study text |

These words are written in katakana, but **アイス** can also refer to ice in other contexts. The kiosk context supports *ice cream* here; do not assume the word always has that meaning. **テキスト** is not a drink. Questions 5–8 require evidence from this card.
