## Your first morning in a Japanese classroom

**Chapter Can-do:** Greet someone appropriately and understand basic study instructions. In this first context lesson, focus on **who is speaking, when, and why**. Later lessons develop vocabulary, sentence patterns, writing, reading, listening and optional speaking.

Imagine a fictional beginner class at 9:00 a.m. The teacher, 田中先生（たなかせんせい, Tanaka-sensei）, enters. A learner, エマ (Emma), is seated near the door. The teacher displays a greeting card and then asks everyone to look at it.

| Speaker | Japanese | Reading support | Meaning and purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| エマ | <span lang="ja">おはようございます。</span> | ohayō gozaimasu | Good morning (polite greeting) |
| 田中先生 | <span lang="ja">おはようございます。</span> | ohayō gozaimasu | Good morning (reply) |
| 田中先生 | <span lang="ja">みてください。</span> | mite kudasai | Please look (at something) |
| エマ | <span lang="ja">ありがとうございます。</span> | arigatō gozaimasu | Thank you (polite) |

These are **original written teaching lines**, not recorded dialogue. The teacher's request is a classroom instruction; it is not a greeting. The last line is a polite expression of thanks, not a substitute for hello.

## A greeting depends on the situation

In the morning, **おはようございます** is a polite and safe greeting for a teacher or someone you do not know well. **おはよう** is a shorter, more casual version, often used with friends or family. A daytime greeting is **こんにちは** (*konnichiwa*). It is written with は at the end, even though the final particle is pronounced *wa*. At this stage, remember the whole expression rather than trying to infer its spelling from romanization.

| Situation | Suitable choice | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First meeting with your teacher at 9:00 a.m. | <span lang="ja">おはようございます。</span> | Polite morning greeting |
| Seeing a classmate at midday | <span lang="ja">こんにちは。</span> | General daytime greeting |
| Thanking someone who lends you a pen | <span lang="ja">ありがとうございます。</span> | Expresses gratitude, not hello |

**Context matters:** Japanese greeting habits vary by setting, relationship and workplace convention. Do not treat a clock as an absolute rule: for example, workplaces sometimes use おはようございます at the start of a shift later in the day. The table gives safe beginner choices for the specified situations.

## Read a tiny greeting card

A card by the classroom door says:

> <span lang="ja">あさ：おはようございます。</span>  
> <span lang="ja">ひる：こんにちは。</span>

Here **あさ** means morning and **ひる** means daytime/noon. The card is a study reminder, not a rigid rule for every social setting. Notice the colon: the text before it gives the context; the text after it gives a greeting.

### Follow the classroom request

The teacher points to the card and says <span lang="ja">みてください。</span> — “Please look.” The learner should **look at the card**. This expression is the polite request form of 見る（みる, to see/look), but you do not need to conjugate verbs yet. Later lessons will explain ください requests.

Compare:
- <span lang="ja">こんにちは。</span> — greeting another person.
- <span lang="ja">みてください。</span> — asking someone to look.
- <span lang="ja">ありがとうございます。</span> — thanking someone.

**Common mistake:** saying ありがとうございます when you first enter the room does not communicate “hello.” It communicates thanks. Similarly, みてください is a request, not a reply to a greeting.

## Guided rehearsal

1. Picture entering the classroom in the morning. Who speaks first? Say or silently read <span lang="ja">おはようございます。</span>
2. Picture the teacher pointing at a card. What action does <span lang="ja">みてください。</span> ask for? Point to the card.
3. Picture receiving a borrowed pencil. Choose <span lang="ja">ありがとうございます。</span>, not こんにちは.
4. Copy one greeting on paper or type it using a Japanese keyboard. This is optional ungraded writing practice; you are not being assessed on handwriting yet.

## Practice and review

Complete the eight written context questions below. All evidence comes from the classroom scene and greeting card. The quiz checks **written situational understanding**, not audio comprehension or spoken pronunciation. A later L06 will introduce actual licensed listening recordings; do not count reading these lines as listening.

After the quiz, cover the table and explain in English: who is the teacher, what does the learner say at 9:00, and what does the teacher ask everyone to do? Optionally rehearse the greeting aloud without claiming pronunciation feedback.

**Remember:** greet, request, and thank are three different communicative purposes. Choose an expression based on the situation and relationship, not on a one-word English translation.
