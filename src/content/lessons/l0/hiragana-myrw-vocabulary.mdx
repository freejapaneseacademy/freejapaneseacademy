## Mio and Yuri add everyday words

**Chapter Can-do:** Read and write the remaining basic hiragana, including わ, を and ん. You already identified the M/Y/R/W cards in Lesson 01. Today Mio is sorting **whole-word picture labels** on Yuri's classroom board. Some labels combine newly introduced kana with older vowels, K/S or T/N/H kana. This is written vocabulary practice: the romanization is a temporary reading cue, not a pronunciation test.

## Twelve word cards

| Printed Japanese | Reading aid | Picture or context | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">やま</span> | *yama* | A high mountain | mountain |
| <span lang="ja">ゆめ</span> | *yume* | A sleeping child dreaming | dream |
| <span lang="ja">もも</span> | *momo* | A peach in a fruit bowl | peach |
| <span lang="ja">みみ</span> | *mimi* | A face with ears indicated | ear / ears |
| <span lang="ja">むし</span> | *mushi* | A small insect on a leaf | insect / bug |
| <span lang="ja">まめ</span> | *mame* | Beans in a dish | bean / beans |
| <span lang="ja">よる</span> | *yoru* | A dark evening sky | night |
| <span lang="ja">そら</span> | *sora* | Blue sky above the mountain | sky |
| <span lang="ja">はる</span> | *haru* | A spring calendar | spring (season) |
| <span lang="ja">わに</span> | *wani* | A crocodile drawing | crocodile |
| <span lang="ja">ほん</span> | *hon* | A book on the desk | book |
| <span lang="ja">くるま</span> | *kuruma* | A car parked outside | car |

These are **whole words**, not translations of individual symbols. For example, <span lang="ja">も</span> is a kana read *mo*, while <span lang="ja">もも</span> is a word meaning peach in this picture. The word <span lang="ja">はる</span> names the spring season in the calendar scene; <span lang="ja">よる</span> names night, not a season.

## Look for the new kana inside familiar words

The **M group** appears in <span lang="ja">まめ</span> (ま and め), <span lang="ja">みみ</span> (み), <span lang="ja">むし</span> (む), <span lang="ja">もも</span> (も), and <span lang="ja">やま</span> (ま). The **Y group** appears in <span lang="ja">やま</span>, <span lang="ja">ゆめ</span> and <span lang="ja">よる</span>. The **R group** appears in <span lang="ja">そら</span>, <span lang="ja">はる</span>, <span lang="ja">よる</span> and <span lang="ja">くるま</span>. The **W group** appears in <span lang="ja">わに</span>. The final nasal <span lang="ja">ん</span> appears in <span lang="ja">ほん</span>; it has its own mora.

The W-row kana <span lang="ja">を</span> normally writes the **object particle**, not an everyday noun by itself. Yuri writes <span lang="ja">ほん を よむ</span> (*hon o yomu*, “read a book”) with spaces added only for beginner readability. Here <span lang="ja">ほん</span> means book, <span lang="ja">を</span> marks the object, and <span lang="ja">よむ</span> means read. We will study that grammar in Lesson 03. Do not substitute <span lang="ja">を</span> for <span lang="ja">お</span> wherever you hear the vowel *o*.

## Picture clues matter

Yuri holds up a peach picture and a fruit bowl labelled <span lang="ja">もも</span>. The written word also has other possible uses in Japanese, including “thigh” with different lexical meaning; **the fruit picture** establishes peach here. A single sound or spelling can have multiple meanings, so the picture and situation are evidence.

Yuri points to a calendar marked spring and writes <span lang="ja">はる</span>. A different picture of a nighttime sky is <span lang="ja">よる</span>. Both words end with <span lang="ja">る</span>, but the shared kana does not mean their meanings are related.

## Guided retrieval

1. Cover the English column and find the labels for a **car**, **book**, **crocodile** and **mountain**.
2. Circle <span lang="ja">ま</span> in <span lang="ja">やま</span> and <span lang="ja">くるま</span>. Locate <span lang="ja">ゆ</span> in <span lang="ja">ゆめ</span> and <span lang="ja">よ</span> in <span lang="ja">よる</span>.
3. Compare <span lang="ja">みみ</span> and <span lang="ja">もも</span>: the repeated-kana pattern is similar, but the words label **ears** and a **peach** respectively.
4. Cover the reading aids and retrieve the written labels for spring, night, beans and insect.
5. Find <span lang="ja">ん</span> at the end of <span lang="ja">ほん</span>. Compare the **whole word** with the separate particle <span lang="ja">を</span> in the supported sentence.

**Common mistakes:** Do not equate a single kana with a whole English word. Do not confuse <span lang="ja">よる</span> (night) with <span lang="ja">はる</span> (spring). Do not write <span lang="ja">を</span> as the final kana of <span lang="ja">もも</span>. Written word recognition is not evidence of listening or pronunciation mastery.
