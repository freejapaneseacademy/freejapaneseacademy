## A final foundation check: read, correct, repeat

This chapter helps you read kana more independently and build a sustainable review routine before N5 study. Today you will interpret a **fictional** diary by Mio (みお), a learner at a community class, and choose a specific practice goal. This context lesson is not a reading-speed test or an official JLPT assessment.

## 1. Mio's notebook

Mio is studying with Ken (けん) before tomorrow's class. Her headings are **よめた** (*yometa*, “I could read it”), **まちがえた** (*machigaeta*, “I made a mistake”) and **あした** (*ashita*, “tomorrow”). These are short diary labels; you do not need to conjugate their verbs yet.

> <span lang="ja">きょう、ひらがなを よみました。</span> — Today I read hiragana.
>
> <span lang="ja">「さくら」は よめました。</span> — I could read “sakura.”
>
> <span lang="ja">「きって」は 「きて」と まちがえました。</span> — I mistakenly read “kitte” as “kite.”
>
> <span lang="ja">あした、ちいさい「っ」を れんしゅうします。</span> — Tomorrow I will practise the small っ.

Spaces are added as beginner reading aids; normal Japanese writing usually does not insert these word spaces. The translations convey meaning, not new grammar to memorize.

**Evidence:** Mio could read さくら, but that does not establish mastery of all hiragana. Her exact mistake was omitting small っ in きって.

## 2. Small marks, real differences

<span lang="ja">きって</span> (*kitte*, postage stamp) has small **っ**, which marks a held consonant before て. <span lang="ja">きて</span> (*kite*, an informal “come” request in an appropriate context) does not. Their rhythm, spelling and meaning differ.

Ken's note reads <span lang="ja">「コーヒー」は よめました。</span> — “I could read coffee.” He adds <span lang="ja">「コヒ」は ちがいます。</span> — “コヒ is different; it is not the intended spelling.” The long-vowel mark **ー** matters in コーヒー.

Compare <span lang="ja">おばさん</span> (*obasan*, aunt or middle-aged woman depending on context) and <span lang="ja">おばあさん</span> (*obaasan*, grandmother or elderly woman). The extra あ lengthens the vowel and may change meaning. Do not infer a real person's age or relationship.

## 3. Make a review plan you can repeat

| Approach | Why it helps or does not |
| --- | --- |
| Read every chart for an hour | Large and hard to sustain; does not necessarily test recall |
| Read five cards without romaji, note two misses, retry tomorrow | Specific, manageable, and checks decoding |
| Read English translations only | Does not test kana recognition |

Try **5–10 minutes**: read three known cards, two weak cards and one short sentence. Hide romaji, retrieve the reading, check the model, note the precise error, and revisit it after a break or tomorrow. Adjust this suggestion to your circumstances; it is not a guarantee of fluency.

## 4. Controlled, guided and independent practice

**Controlled:** Locate small っ in きって and ー in コーヒー. Explain how each spelling differs when the mark is omitted.

**Guided:** Suppose you misread <span lang="ja">がっこう</span> (*gakkō*, school) as <span lang="ja">がこう</span>. Record “small っ omitted,” compare the forms, hide the answer and retry.

**Independent:** Pick three kana words from earlier lessons, read each silently or aloud, and record one pattern to revisit tomorrow. Keep your study note privately. Reading aloud is optional and not automatically graded.
