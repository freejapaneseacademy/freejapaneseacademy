## At the first study-group meeting

**Chapter Can-do:** Recognize simple topic-comment statements and questions. You are not expected to create long conversations yet. Today you will identify who is talking, what information is being given, and whether a sentence is asking or telling.

Imagine a small, fictional Japanese study group. Mina is introducing herself to Ken. A teacher points to a book on the table. The sentences below are original examples for study, not quotations from a real classroom.

| Speaker and situation | Japanese | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mina introduces herself | <span lang="ja">わたしは ミナです。</span> | I am Mina. |
| Ken asks about Mina | <span lang="ja">ミナさんは がくせいですか。</span> | Is Mina a student? |
| Mina answers | <span lang="ja">はい、がくせいです。</span> | Yes, (I am) a student. |
| Teacher points at a book | <span lang="ja">これは ほんです。</span> | This is a book. |

The spaces here are **learner-friendly reading breaks**; ordinary Japanese does not usually separate every word with spaces. ミナ and ケン are fictional names written in katakana. さん is a polite name suffix, not part of the name.

## Step 1: Notice what each sentence does

A **statement** gives information. <span lang="ja">わたしは ミナです。</span> identifies the speaker. <span lang="ja">これは ほんです。</span> identifies an object. A **question** requests information. <span lang="ja">ミナさんは がくせいですか。</span> asks whether Mina is a student. Until you hear the reply, do not assume the answer.

In a beginner-friendly model, **A は B です** presents A as the topic and gives information B about it. For example, <span lang="ja">ケンさんは がくせいです。</span> means “Ken is a student.” This is a useful starter frame, not a universal English-style equation: real Japanese often leaves an already-understood topic unstated.

Add **か** at the end of this polite pattern to form a yes/no question: <span lang="ja">ケンさんは がくせいですか。</span> means “Is Ken a student?” The punctuation **。** ends a written sentence; you may also see a question mark in informal writing. The question function comes from か, not from English word order.

## Step 2: Read the particles carefully

The topic particle **は** is written with the hiragana *ha* character but pronounced **wa** in this grammatical use. Do not replace it with わ when writing the topic marker. Compare <span lang="ja">わたし**は** ミナです。</span>: the first わ belongs to わたし; the later は marks the topic.

You may encounter **を**, pronounced **o** as an object particle. This is only a spelling-and-role preview: you do not need to produce を sentences today. **は** marks a topic; **を** normally marks a direct object. They are not interchangeable. Later lessons will teach verbs and を in full.

## Step 3: Small words with big jobs

| Form | Supported meaning here | Learning note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">わたし</span> | I / me | Often omitted when obvious from context |
| <span lang="ja">がくせい</span> | student | A person's role, not their age |
| <span lang="ja">せんせい</span> | teacher | Not the same as がくせい |
| <span lang="ja">ほん</span> | book | An everyday object |
| <span lang="ja">これ</span> | this (one) | Points to an item near the speaker |
| <span lang="ja">はい</span> | yes | Confirms a yes/no question in this model |
| <span lang="ja">です</span> | polite sentence ending | Helps form the beginner statement frame |
| <span lang="ja">か</span> | question marker | At the end of this polite question |

**Context matters:** <span lang="ja">がくせいです。</span> alone can mean “(I am / they are) a student” depending on the preceding question. Japanese frequently omits understood subjects. Do not assign an unstated identity to an isolated phrase.

## Guided reading: find the evidence

Read the four-line study-group scene again. Before looking at the English, identify (1) which line introduces a name, (2) which line asks a question, and (3) which line points to an object. Then check: the first, second and fourth lines respectively.

Try this controlled contrast:

- <span lang="ja">ミナさんは がくせいです。</span> — “Mina is a student.” (statement)
- <span lang="ja">ミナさんは がくせいですか。</span> — “Is Mina a student?” (question)
- <span lang="ja">ケンさんは せんせいです。</span> — “Ken is a teacher.” (different information; do not infer it from the earlier question)

## Independent practice and self-check

Complete the ten written questions below without consulting the table if you can. Each question has one best answer, with explanation after submission. The score measures **written recognition**, not listening, conversation or official JLPT proficiency. You may retry.

For an optional ungraded output task, type **わたしは [your name] です。** with your own name in suitable kana or katakana, then ask **[name] さんは がくせいですか。** Compare the position of は, です and か with the models. Do not submit real personal details to the site; this task is not collected.

Say in English what **は**, **です** and sentence-final **か** do in the examples. Revisit any missed question, then mark the lesson complete separately from your quiz score.
