## A new learner in a small classroom

Imagine a fictional community language class. You can see a teacher, a student, a book and a desk. You are not yet expected to make complex sentences: first, **recognize the words** that make simple identifications possible.

> これは ほんです。 — This is a book.
>
> わたしは がくせいです。 — I am a student.
>
> あのひとは せんせいです。 — That person over there is a teacher.

The first sentence identifies a nearby thing. The second identifies the speaker's role. The third identifies a person farther away. In a real conversation, a person's identity or role should not be assumed from appearance.

## 1. People: words and social roles

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning in this setting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ひと / 人 | *hito* | person |
| わたし / 私 | *watashi* | I, me (neutral/polite) |
| せんせい / 先生 | *sensei* | teacher; also a respectful address for certain professionals |
| がくせい / 学生 | *gakusei* | student, commonly a school or university student |
| ともだち / 友達 | *tomodachi* | friend |

**Important distinction:** せんせい is a role or respectful form of address, not a generic word for every adult. A person may refer to themselves as きょうし (teacher, as an occupation), but you do not need that distinction for this recognition lesson. がくせい refers to a student; in a language club, がくしゅうしゃ (learner) may be more precise for an adult participant.

**Guided check:** If the classroom volunteer says 「わたしは がくせいです」, the speaker identifies themself as a student. If they say 「せんせいです」, the understood person is a teacher. Japanese often leaves a subject unspoken when context makes it clear.

## 2. Objects: everyday nouns

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ほん / 本 | *hon* | book |
| つくえ / 机 | *tsukue* | desk |
| いす / 椅子 | *isu* | chair |
| かばん | *kaban* | bag |
| ペン | *pen* | pen |
| ノート | *nōto* | notebook |

The katakana words ペン and ノート are common loanwords. **ノート** normally means a notebook in this classroom context, not a musical note. **ほん** can be written 本; do not confuse it with a person's name merely because the same sound appears elsewhere.

Compare: これは **ほん**です。 (This is a book.) / これは **ノート**です。 (This is a notebook.) The sentence frame is unchanged, but the noun changes what you identify.

## 3. Words inside the sentence

| Japanese | English meaning | What to notice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| わたしは がくせいです。 | I am a student. | わたし identifies the speaker; がくせい identifies the role. |
| これは ペンです。 | This is a pen. | これ refers to an object near the speaker. |
| これは いすですか。 | Is this a chair? | か marks a polite question in this beginner frame. |
| あのひとは せんせいです。 | That person over there is a teacher. | あのひと refers to someone farther away. |

The particle **は** is written *ha* but pronounced *wa* when it marks the topic. It is not the same word as わたし. The particle **を** is written *wo* and generally pronounced *o*; it will be taught with verbs later, so do not insert it into these noun-identification sentences. **です** gives a polite style; **か** can make a question. L03 explains the grammar in more detail.

### Meaning depends on the scene

「せんせいです」 does not necessarily mean *I am a teacher*: with a visible person as the topic it can mean *That person is a teacher*. Similarly, 「ほんです」 can identify the object being discussed. Look for the topic and the shared context before translating.

## 4. Controlled retrieval → guided use → independent rehearsal

First cover the English meanings and retrieve せんせい, がくせい, ほん, つくえ, いす, かばん, ペン and ノート. Then point to fictional classroom objects and choose a sentence: 「これは ほんです」 or 「これは ペンです」. For an imagined teacher, try 「あのひとは せんせいです」. Do not label real people without knowing their role.

Finally, draw a simple classroom map with one desk, two chairs, a book and a bag. Write three labels in kana or katakana and compare them to the table. Reading aloud is optional, ungraded rehearsal; this written vocabulary lesson does **not** contain verified pronunciation audio. The separate L06 provides listening practice.
