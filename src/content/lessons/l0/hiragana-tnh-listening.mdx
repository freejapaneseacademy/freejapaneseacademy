## Hear the meaning first

You have practised reading the **た・ち・つ・て・と**, **な・に・ぬ・ね・の**, and **は・ひ・ふ・へ・ほ** rows. Listening is a different skill: when someone speaks, you need to understand their request and response, not identify how a word is written. In this lesson, two short fictional conversations use original Japanese dialogue performed by licensed synthetic AivisSpeech voices. You may hear a few words beyond the kana you have learned; use the surrounding conversation to understand the situation.

## How to listen

Listen to each recording at normal speed before looking at any written Japanese. On the first pass, ask who is speaking and what the speaker wants. On the second pass, listen for a detail such as a color or temperature. Replay as needed, then answer three questions per track. Only after submitting should you reveal the transcript and English translation to compare what you heard.

### Track A — At a hat shop

A customer asks a shopkeeper for an item. Listen for what is available, how the shopkeeper describes the choices, and what the customer selects. The scored questions concern **spoken objects and decisions**, not spelling or character shapes.

### Track B — Tea at a friend's home

A host offers a guest a drink. Listen for the guest's preference and the extra item the host serves. Try to identify the polite closing phrase as well.

## Your listening workspace

Use the two real WAV audio players below. Each track contains four alternating male/female turns. Each of the six questions has one best answer and an explanation grounded in an audible phrase. You can use replay, volume and playback speed controls. The Japanese transcript and English translation are hidden at first.

**Unaided listening:** keep the transcript hidden until after submitting. **Assisted practice:** revealing written support early is allowed, including when audio is inaccessible, but the attempt will not count as an unaided listening score. Completion and scores remain in your browser; no microphone or account is required.

## Review after scoring

Reveal the speaker-by-speaker transcript and listen again. For Track A, notice how the shopkeeper presents two options and the customer chooses one. For Track B, compare the host's offer with the guest's request and the final response. Listen for **ち**, **つ**, or **ふ** in future lessons, but do not confuse phoneme identification with reading kana from a page.
