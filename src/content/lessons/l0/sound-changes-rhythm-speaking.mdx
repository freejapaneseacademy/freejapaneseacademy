## Your mission: say something useful, not just isolated syllables

At a post office, you want **two stamps** to send a letter to a friend in Osaka. At a picnic, you explain that your bag is **small**, but the bread will fit. The original Chapter 03 Lesson 06 recordings contain these two everyday situations. You will listen to a complete exchange, imitate an actual recorded turn, then respond as a participant.

**Can-do:** recognize long vowels, small っ and nasal ん in meaningful Japanese speech, and rehearse those timing distinctions yourself. This is an **optional speaking lesson**. It does not measure your accent, and you do not need a microphone or a numerical speaking score.

## 1. Three timing cues

Japanese timing is often described in *morae*. A simple kana normally occupies one mora. A long vowel adds one mora, small <span lang="ja">っ</span> marks a consonant closure occupying a mora, and <span lang="ja">ん</span> occupies a mora. Do not interpret this as an instruction to stress every kana equally or to pause between every character.

| Word from the conversations | Meaning | Beginner timing guide | Speaking focus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">きって</span> | postage stamp | き・っ・て = 3 | Brief closure before the *t*; don't pronounce っ as *tsu*. |
| <span lang="ja">にまい</span> | two flat items | に・ま・い = 3 | Keep the counter phrase together naturally. |
| <span lang="ja">おおさか</span> | Osaka | お・お・さ・か = 4 | Hold the first vowel across two morae, not two separately stressed syllables. |
| <span lang="ja">にほん</span> | Japan | に・ほ・ん = 3 | Give the final nasal its own timing unit. |
| <span lang="ja">あした</span> | tomorrow | あ・し・た = 3 | Keep the whole phrase connected; vowels may weaken in natural speech. |
| <span lang="ja">かばん</span> | bag | か・ば・ん = 3 | Nasal ん is not an extra full *nu* syllable. |
| <span lang="ja">もって</span> | take/bring (て-form) | も・っ・て = 3 | The small っ is a brief consonant closure. |

The kana breakdowns are **written timing guides**, not separate prerecorded syllables. The actual models are full utterances, so follow their natural phrasing.

## 2. At the post office: ask clearly

**Recorded model, first turn:** <span lang="ja">すみません。切手を二枚ください。</span> — “Excuse me. Two stamps, please.”

**Recorded reply:** <span lang="ja">はい。二枚ですね。お手紙は日本に送りますか。</span> — “Certainly. Two, right? Are you sending the letter within Japan?”

**Recorded answer:** <span lang="ja">はい。大阪の友達に送ります。</span> — “Yes. I'm sending it to a friend in Osaka.”

First play the entire exchange. Then use **Play this turn** for the first line and repeat it. Keep the closure in <span lang="ja">切手（きって）</span> short and clear; a long vowel or an added *tsu* would change the sound. In <span lang="ja">大阪（おおさか）</span>, allow the first vowel its extra timing unit without stopping between two separate words.

**Guided role-play:** Imagine the counter worker asks <span lang="ja">お手紙は日本に送りますか。</span> Reply <span lang="ja">はい。大阪の友達に送ります。</span> Compare your answer to the actual woman's turn. The polite <span lang="ja">送ります</span> fits the service-counter context.

## 3. At the picnic: correct a detail politely

**Recorded question:** <span lang="ja">明日のピクニック、何を持っていきますか。</span> — “What will you bring to tomorrow's picnic?”

**Recorded answer:** <span lang="ja">パンとお茶を持っていきます。</span> — “I'll bring bread and tea.”

**Recorded follow-up:** <span lang="ja">いいですね。かばんは大きいですか。</span> — “Sounds good. Is your bag big?”

**Recorded correction:** <span lang="ja">いいえ、小さいです。でも、パンは入りますよ。</span> — “No, it's small. But the bread will fit.”

Shadow the question, then the answer. The closure in <span lang="ja">持って（もって）</span> matters, while <span lang="ja">かばん</span> ends in a nasal mora. Notice that <span lang="ja">いいえ</span> introduces a polite correction, followed by a reassuring contrast <span lang="ja">でも</span> (“but”).

**Guided role-play:** After hearing the question about the bag, say the recorded correction. You are not being asked to improvise an unfamiliar sentence. Begin slowly, then return to normal playback speed and keep the final <span lang="ja">よ</span> natural rather than shouting it.

## 4. How to shadow without guessing

1. Listen to the whole track for its meaning.
2. Select a real recorded turn, play it, then pause before repeating aloud.
3. Say the **whole phrase** rather than chanting individual kana. Listen for long-vowel duration, closure before a consonant and the nasal beat.
4. Replay the model. If you choose, record yourself locally **only after pressing the microphone button** and compare. No recording is uploaded or saved to your learning history.
5. Check the self-review criteria based on what you can hear. If unsure, leave a criterion unchecked and repeat the model later.

**Common mistakes:** pronouncing <span lang="ja">っ</span> as a full *tsu*; making <span lang="ja">ん</span> a full English *nuh* syllable; rushing through the first vowel in <span lang="ja">大阪</span>; over-stressing one English-like syllable; treating a written-answer quiz as proof of pronunciation.

## 5. What this practice can and cannot show

The optional five-question preparation check asks about **meaning, mora counts and appropriate shadowing methods**, not your voice. The self-review checklist is yours alone. Completing the lesson does not certify pronunciation, fluency or native-like speech. If your microphone is denied or unsupported, listen and speak aloud without recording.
