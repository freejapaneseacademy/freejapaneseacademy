## Ren's second table of picture cards

**Chapter Can-do:** Read, write and distinguish the T, N and H hiragana rows. In this vocabulary lesson, Aki returns to Ren's classroom table and matches **printed words** to pictures. You already met the fifteen new kana in Lesson 01. Now notice how familiar sounds combine into words with meanings.

Aki sees pictures of a cat, a dog, a boat, a star and a moon. Ren also has a summer calendar, a person icon, an octopus, a shelf, a cloth and a flower. Every label below is written in hiragana. A **kana** represents a sound unit (usually one mora); the **whole word** has a lexical meaning. Read the Japanese first and use the temporary romanization only if you need it.

The readings **chi** for <span lang="ja">ち</span>, **tsu** for <span lang="ja">つ</span> and **fu** for <span lang="ja">ふ</span> are conventional English-oriented cues, not separate extra kana. For this lesson you recognize **printed** words; actual pronunciation and listening are assessed with licensed recordings in a later chapter lesson.

## Use the classroom pictures as evidence

**Animals:** Ren points to the cat picture and labels it <span lang="ja">ねこ</span>. Next to it, the dog picture says <span lang="ja">いぬ</span>. Both are animals, but changing the kana changes the word.

**Sky and seasons:** A night-sky card shows a star labelled <span lang="ja">ほし</span> and the moon labelled <span lang="ja">つき</span>. The summer calendar is <span lang="ja">なつ</span>. A season is not a celestial object; these labels describe different things.

**People and objects:** The stick-figure card says <span lang="ja">ひと</span> (“person”). The boat card says <span lang="ja">ふね</span>. A classroom shelf is <span lang="ja">たな</span>, while the piece of fabric on the desk is <span lang="ja">ぬの</span>.

**Two pictures with possible ambiguity:** Aki sees <span lang="ja">たこ</span> under a sea-creature picture, so it means **octopus** here; the same pronunciation can also refer to a kite in a different scene. Ren labels a vase picture <span lang="ja">はな</span> (“flower”), but <span lang="ja">はな</span> can also mean **nose** in a face diagram. Kana alone does not determine which meaning is intended.

## Cover, retrieve, and compare

1. Cover the English column. Point to <span lang="ja">ねこ</span>, <span lang="ja">いぬ</span> and <span lang="ja">ふね</span>; name the pictured object for each.
2. Read <span lang="ja">ほし</span> and <span lang="ja">つき</span> aloud only as optional self-rehearsal. Which card shows a star? Which shows the moon? This activity does **not** provide or grade pronunciation.
3. Compare <span lang="ja">たな</span> (shelf) and <span lang="ja">なつ</span> (summer). Both contain <span lang="ja">な</span>, but sharing a kana does not make their meanings related.
4. Imagine the two <span lang="ja">はな</span> pictures: one vase, one face. Say which English meaning each picture supports, and explain why the word needs context.
5. Find <span lang="ja">ち</span> in the T-row chart from Lesson 01. It is part of the chapter's character set even though none of today's eleven picture labels requires it. Lesson 03 will focus on exceptional sound cues and confusions.

## Common beginner errors

**Do not translate individual kana as words.** The character <span lang="ja">ね</span> does not independently mean “cat”; the complete word <span lang="ja">ねこ</span> means cat.

**Do not treat romaji as the target spelling.** Write <span lang="ja">ふね</span> rather than *fune* when the task asks for hiragana. The *fu* cue helps you read <span lang="ja">ふ</span>, but it is not a separate character.

**Do not ignore the picture.** A sea-creature card supports octopus for <span lang="ja">たこ</span>; a kite picture supports another meaning. A vase supports flower for <span lang="ja">はな</span>; a face supports nose.

## Independent word check

Answer ten written multiple-choice questions without looking at the table. Every item has a stable ID and an explanation. Your result is a **written vocabulary score**, not a listening, speaking, pronunciation or handwriting grade. The attempt is saved on your device when local storage is available.

**Review:** Return to the picture-card table and recall the Japanese for cat, dog, moon, star and flower. In Lesson 03 you will study the exceptional sound cues and easily confused kana more closely.
