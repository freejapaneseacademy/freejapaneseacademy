## A tiny mark changes the message

Mina and Ken are labeling pictures for a classroom display. One card shows a writer, another a slope; one shows a grandmother, another an aunt. Their job is to **copy the right Japanese spelling**, not guess a pronunciation from English letters.

Today's Can-do is to **recognize long vowels, small tsu and the nasal mora** in writing. This is script and typing practice: the evidence is visible kana. You are not being tested on listening or automatically graded on pronunciation.

## 1. Three ways a word can gain a mora

A **mora** is a Japanese rhythm unit. A basic kana such as <span lang="ja">か</span> generally counts as one. A small <span lang="ja">っ</span>, a standalone <span lang="ja">ん</span>, and the second unit of a long vowel also occupy one mora each. They do different jobs.

| Written contrast | Mora breakdown | Meaning in our picture-card context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">おばさん</span> / <span lang="ja">おばあさん</span> | お・ば・さ・ん (4) / お・ば・あ・さ・ん (5) | aunt / grandmother |
| <span lang="ja">さか</span> / <span lang="ja">さっか</span> | さ・か (2) / さ・っ・か (3) | slope / writer |
| <span lang="ja">ほ</span> / <span lang="ja">ほん</span> | ほ (1) / ほ・ん (2) | the syllable *ho* / book (ほん) |

The last row contrasts a single **syllable label** with a complete word, not two equivalent vocabulary words. It helps you see that <span lang="ja">ん</span> is an independent written mora. Unlike an ordinary vowel kana, <span lang="ja">ん</span> does not start a standard native Japanese word.

**Guided check:** the picture says “grandmother.” Which spelling fits? <span lang="ja">おばあさん</span>. Point to the extra <span lang="ja">あ</span>. If you leave it out, you have written the different word <span lang="ja">おばさん</span>.

## 2. Long vowels: do not just double the visible letter

A long vowel lasts two morae, but its conventional spelling depends on the word and script. In hiragana, <span lang="ja">おばあさん</span> adds <span lang="ja">あ</span> for a long *a*. Many long *o* vowels are written with <span lang="ja">う</span>: <span lang="ja">こうこう</span> (*kōkō*, high school) is こ・う・こ・う, **four** morae. Do not replace it with <span lang="ja">こおこお</span> just because the sound is long.

Katakana frequently uses the long-vowel mark <span lang="ja">ー</span>, as in <span lang="ja">コーヒー</span> (*kōhī*, coffee): コ・ー・ヒ・ー, four morae. The mark extends the previous vowel; it is not a hyphen or a standalone vowel with its own fixed sound.

**Common mistake:** treating every two-letter romaji sequence as two morae. <span lang="ja">か</span> (*ka*) is one mora, while <span lang="ja">かあ</span> is two. Count the Japanese units, not the Latin letters.

## 3. Small っ is not full-size つ

Compare the shapes <span lang="ja">っ</span> (small) and <span lang="ja">つ</span> (full-size). They are different characters. The small form marks a held consonant interval before the following consonant in words such as <span lang="ja">さっか</span> (writer). It counts as a mora, although it does **not** have an independent *tsu* vowel.

| Picture label | Spelling | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Slope | <span lang="ja">さか</span> | No small っ: さ・か |
| Writer | <span lang="ja">さっか</span> | Small っ before か: さ・っ・か |
| Stamp | <span lang="ja">きって</span> | き・っ・て: the small っ is between き and て |
| Come (plain request) | <span lang="ja">きて</span> | き・て: without っ, a different word |

The word <span lang="ja">きて</span> is the *te*-form of “come,” used in requests in context; <span lang="ja">きって</span> is a noun meaning “postage stamp.” A picture of a stamp is evidence for <span lang="ja">きって</span>, not <span lang="ja">きて</span>.

**Guided check:** the caption is “a writer.” Copy <span lang="ja">さっか</span>, then compare it against <span lang="ja">さつか</span>. The second spelling uses full-size つ and is not the target word.

## 4. Nasal ん has its own place

In <span lang="ja">ほん</span> (book), write <span lang="ja">ほ</span> followed by <span lang="ja">ん</span>. In <span lang="ja">さんぽ</span> (walk/stroll), the written breakdown is さ・ん・ぽ, **three** morae. The pronunciation of ん varies with neighboring sounds; the spelling remains <span lang="ja">ん</span>. Do not turn it into <span lang="ja">な</span>, <span lang="ja">に</span>, or <span lang="ja">む</span> merely because of what you think you hear.

**Common mistake:** treating the Latin letter *n* as a Japanese kana. A romaji-input keyboard may require a specific conversion sequence to enter <span lang="ja">ん</span>. Always inspect the converted output.

## 5. Type and check the real characters

Enable a Japanese hiragana IME on your device, or use a supported kana input method. Keyboard shortcuts vary by operating system. Copy the following picture-card labels, compare **character by character**, and correct any differences:

1. “Aunt” — <span lang="ja">おばさん</span>; “grandmother” — <span lang="ja">おばあさん</span>. Find the additional あ.
2. “Slope” — <span lang="ja">さか</span>; “writer” — <span lang="ja">さっか</span>. Find the small っ.
3. “Stamp” — <span lang="ja">きって</span>; “come” — <span lang="ja">きて</span>. Check which has っ.
4. “Book” — <span lang="ja">ほん</span>; “stroll” — <span lang="ja">さんぽ</span>. Underline each ん.
5. “High school” — <span lang="ja">こうこう</span>. Circle each う that extends the preceding o vowel.

If your keyboard cannot enter kana yet, select and copy the printed models. Do not mark a Latin-letter answer as correct Japanese script.

### Optional tracing

On paper, enlarge the printed <span lang="ja">っ</span>, <span lang="ja">つ</span>, and <span lang="ja">ん</span>. Copy each three times, paying attention to **relative size**: <span lang="ja">っ</span> is visibly smaller than <span lang="ja">つ</span>. Compare the final shapes to the model. This self-check does not certify stroke order or handwriting proficiency.

## 6. Independent retrieval with feedback

Cover the tables and answer these before revealing the model.

**A.** Which word means “writer,” <span lang="ja">さか</span> or <span lang="ja">さっか</span>? **Answer:** <span lang="ja">さっか</span>. The small っ adds a mora and distinguishes it from “slope.”

**B.** The picture shows a postage stamp. Type the label. **Answer:** <span lang="ja">きって</span>. <span lang="ja">きて</span> lacks the small っ and means “come” in its verb context.

**C.** How many morae are in <span lang="ja">さんぽ</span>? **Answer:** three, さ・ん・ぽ. The ん counts even though it is not an ordinary consonant-vowel syllable.

**D.** How many morae are in <span lang="ja">コーヒー</span>? **Answer:** four, コ・ー・ヒ・ー. Each ー lengthens the preceding vowel by one mora.

**E.** A card says “grandmother,” but you typed <span lang="ja">おばさん</span>. What should you change? **Answer:** insert あ after ば: <span lang="ja">おばあさん</span>. The extra vowel is meaning-bearing.

## Exit self-check

Can you locate the additional vowel in <span lang="ja">おばあさん</span>, the small <span lang="ja">っ</span> in <span lang="ja">さっか</span>, and the nasal <span lang="ja">ん</span> in <span lang="ja">さんぽ</span>? Can you type each without leaving romaji in the final answer? If not, revisit the contrast tables and try again.
