## Words that make a conversation work

**Chapter Can-do:** Decode short hiragana sentences without relying on romaji. In this vocabulary lesson, you will learn to recognize eight useful words and expressions from the library conversation. Read the Japanese first; use the English meanings to check your understanding. No audio is required for this written word-study activity.

**Setting:** Yui asks Sora, a librarian, about picture books. They speak politely.

| Japanese | Meaning in this scene | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">こんにちは</span> | Hello | A daytime greeting |
| <span lang="ja">えほん</span> | Picture book | A kind of book, not a notebook |
| <span lang="ja">あります</span> | There is / there are; we have | Availability of an inanimate thing |
| <span lang="ja">はい</span> | Yes | Confirms the answer |
| <span lang="ja">ここ</span> | Here | A location near the speaker |
| <span lang="ja">みます</span> | Look at / see | What Yui plans to do with the book |
| <span lang="ja">ありがとうございます</span> | Thank you very much | A polite expression of thanks |
| <span lang="ja">か</span> | Question-ending particle | Turns <span lang="ja">あります</span> into a question here |

**Reading reminder:** Japanese text ordinarily has no spaces between these word chunks. In the supported examples below, spaces are added only to help a beginner notice boundaries.

## Revisit the library exchange

| Speaker | Japanese | Natural English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yui | <span lang="ja">こんにちは。</span> | Hello. |
| Yui | <span lang="ja">えほんは ありますか。</span> | Do you have picture books? |
| Sora | <span lang="ja">はい、あります。</span> | Yes, we do. |
| Sora | <span lang="ja">えほんは ここです。</span> | The picture books are here. |
| Yui | <span lang="ja">えほんを みます。</span> | I'll look at a picture book / picture books. |
| Yui | <span lang="ja">ありがとうございます。</span> | Thank you very much. |

## Learn the words in context

### Finding something

<span lang="ja">えほん</span> is a **picture book**. <span lang="ja">ここ</span> means **here**, not “there.” When Sora says <span lang="ja">えほんは ここです。</span>, the sentence points to the books' location. The particle <span lang="ja">は</span> marks the topic; as a particle it is pronounced *wa*, even though the character is written は.

### Asking about availability

<span lang="ja">あります</span> can say that an inanimate thing exists or is available. In this library, <span lang="ja">えほんは ありますか。</span> naturally asks “Do you have picture books?” The final <span lang="ja">か</span> marks a question. <span lang="ja">はい、あります。</span> is a polite affirmative response. This does **not** mean “I am a picture book.”

### Looking, not buying

<span lang="ja">みます</span> means “look at” or “see” in this context. <span lang="ja">えほんを みます。</span> says that Yui will look at picture books; it does not say she will buy or borrow them. The particle <span lang="ja">を</span> marks the thing being looked at. Neither <span lang="ja">は</span> nor <span lang="ja">を</span> is an English word that can be translated on its own in every sentence.

### Polite social expressions

<span lang="ja">こんにちは。</span> is a greeting, not a request for a book. <span lang="ja">ありがとうございます。</span> is a polite way to thank someone for help. It is more formal than the casual <span lang="ja">ありがとう。</span>.

## Guided retrieval

1. Cover the English column above. Point to the word meaning “picture book.”
2. Find the word meaning “here,” then find it in Sora's line.
3. Compare <span lang="ja">あります。</span> and <span lang="ja">ありますか。</span>. What changes when か is added?
4. Find the action word in <span lang="ja">えほんを みます。</span>. Does Yui look at, buy or write a book?
5. Read the final line and decide whether Yui is greeting Sora or thanking Sora.

**Common mistakes:** <span lang="ja">ここ</span> is “here,” not “this”; <span lang="ja">えほん</span> is a picture book, not a generic pencil case; <span lang="ja">みます</span> does not mean “buy.” A single Japanese verb can need a different natural English translation depending on context.
