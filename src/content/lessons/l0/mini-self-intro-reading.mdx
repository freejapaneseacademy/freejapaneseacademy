## Your mission: read what the card actually says

**Can-do:** Recognize simple topic–comment statements and questions. Two learners meet at a beginner Japanese class. Each card is an **original fictional text** written for this lesson. Read each one, find the stated information, and point to the Japanese phrase that proves it. You are not expected to infer anything about the speakers beyond their words.

## Sentence-reading toolkit

| Japanese | Reading / meaning | What it tells you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">わたしは</span> | *watashi wa*, as for me | The topic is the speaker; the particle は is pronounced *wa*. |
| <span lang="ja">がくせいです</span> | *gakusei desu*, am a student | A polite statement about role. |
| <span lang="ja">せんせいです</span> | *sensei desu*, am a teacher | A different role, not a name. |
| <span lang="ja">これは</span> | *kore wa*, as for this | Points to something near the speaker. |
| <span lang="ja">それは</span> | *sore wa*, as for that | Points to something near the listener or previously mentioned. |
| <span lang="ja">わたしの</span> | *watashi no*, my | の connects a possessor to an object. |
| <span lang="ja">ですか</span> | *desu ka*, polite question ending | The final か signals a yes/no question. |
| <span lang="ja">はい / いいえ</span> | yes / no | Listen for or read the answer, not just the question. |

**Reading note:** In the spelling preview <span lang="ja">は</span> is written *ha* but pronounced *wa* when it is a topic particle. <span lang="ja">を</span> is generally pronounced *o* as an object particle, but these cards do not yet use it. Do not insert を into the model <span lang="ja">AはBです</span> pattern.

## Original mini self-introduction A — Mika

> <span lang="ja">わたしは　ミカです。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">わたしは　がくせいです。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">これは　わたしの　ほんです。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">それは　ペンですか。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">はい、ペンです。</span>

**English meaning:** “I am Mika. I am a student. This is my book. Is that a pen? Yes, it is a pen.”

Mika identifies herself, gives her role, names her book, then asks a question about a pen. The last sentence answers that question. The speaker of the answer is not named, so do not invent a second person's identity.

## Original mini self-introduction B — Ken

> <span lang="ja">わたしは　ケンです。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">わたしは　せんせいです。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">これは　わたしの　ペンです。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">それは　ほんですか。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">いいえ、ノートです。</span>

**English meaning:** “I am Ken. I am a teacher. This is my pen. Is that a book? No, it is a notebook.”

Here the question is about a book, but the reply says **no** and identifies a notebook. A question does not prove that the guessed object is correct.

## How to cite evidence

**Question:** Who is a student? **Evidence:** Card A, <span lang="ja">わたしは　がくせいです。</span> **Answer:** Mika. The phrase <span lang="ja">がくせいです</span> supplies the role, and the preceding <span lang="ja">わたしは　ミカです</span> identifies the speaker.

**Question:** Is the object in Ken's question really a book? **Evidence:** <span lang="ja">それは　ほんですか。</span> followed by <span lang="ja">いいえ、ノートです。</span> **Answer:** No; the reply identifies a notebook. Read the reply before deciding.

## Guided practice: point, then answer

1. Find both occurrences of <span lang="ja">わたしの</span>. Which objects follow? **Answer:** <span lang="ja">ほん</span> in A and <span lang="ja">ペン</span> in B.
2. Circle the two lines ending in <span lang="ja">か。</span>. **Answer:** <span lang="ja">それは　ペンですか。</span> and <span lang="ja">それは　ほんですか。</span>
3. Which reply starts with <span lang="ja">いいえ</span>? **Answer:** Card B; it rejects the guess “book” and names <span lang="ja">ノート</span>.

**Common trap:** <span lang="ja">これは　わたしの　ほんです。</span> is a statement; <span lang="ja">それは　ほんですか。</span> is a question. The particle か changes the function of the sentence. Another trap is mixing up ほん (book), ペン (pen), and ノート (notebook) because all three are classroom objects.

## Optional writing transfer

Without looking, type one true sentence about Mika and one question about Ken's pen. Compare against these models: <span lang="ja">ミカは　がくせいです。</span> (“Mika is a student”) and <span lang="ja">それは　ケンの　ペンですか。</span> (“Is that Ken's pen?”). The latter is a **new practice question**, not a line stated on either card. This open writing practice is unscored.
