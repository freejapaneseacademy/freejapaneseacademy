## Make a reading-club label

Yui and Sora are preparing labels for their reading club. The first card says **おちゃ** (“tea”). It contains **ちゃ**, made from ち plus a **small ゃ**. Another card says **じしょ** (“dictionary”); its final **しょ** uses a small ょ. These are ordinary written Japanese words, not sound recordings.

The small kana is part of the contracted combination (*yōon*, 拗音). In the standard beginner pattern, an i-row kana such as **き・し・ち・に・ひ・み・り** combines with small **ゃ・ゅ・ょ** to make **one mora**. The two printed characters do not make two separate rhythmic beats. Voiced bases such as **ぎ・じ・び** can also combine with small kana.

| Base | Small ゃ | Small ゅ | Small ょ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| き | **きゃ** (kya) | **きゅ** (kyu) | **きょ** (kyo) |
| し | **しゃ** (sha) | **しゅ** (shu) | **しょ** (sho) |
| ち | **ちゃ** (cha) | **ちゅ** (chu) | **ちょ** (cho) |
| に | **にゃ** (nya) | **にゅ** (nyu) | **にょ** (nyo) |
| り | **りゃ** (rya) | **りゅ** (ryu) | **りょ** (ryo) |
| ぎ | **ぎゃ** (gya) | **ぎゅ** (gyu) | **ぎょ** (gyo) |

The right-hand kana in each pair must be **visibly smaller**. Compare **きゃ** (one mora) with **きや** (two full-sized kana, two morae). The latter is a constructed decoding contrast, not a word to memorize. Likewise **しゅ** is one mora but **しゆ** has two; do not mistake **しゅ** for *shi-yu*.

## Inspect the labels before typing

| Card | Meaning | Written clue | Morae |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **おちゃ** | tea | ち + small ゃ = ちゃ | お・ちゃ (2) |
| **しゃしん** | photograph | し + small ゃ = しゃ | しゃ・し・ん (3) |
| **じしょ** | dictionary | し + small ょ = しょ | じ・しょ (2) |
| **きゃく** | guest | き + small ゃ = きゃ | きゃ・く (2) |
| **しゅくだい** | homework | し + small ゅ = しゅ | しゅ・く・だ・い (4) |
| **りょこう** | trip | り + small ょ = りょ | りょ・こ・う (3) |
| **ぎゅうにゅう** | milk | ぎゅ and にゅ are two pairs | ぎゅ・う・にゅ・う (4) |
| **びょういん** | hospital | び + small ょ = びょ | びょ・う・い・ん (4) |

A following **う** is a **separate mora** even when it extends the preceding vowel: **きょう** = **きょ・う** (two morae), not one. The letter count alone does not tell you the mora count.

## Type the exact printed form

With a Japanese keyboard/IME in **romaji-to-hiragana mode**, common input sequences are **kya → きゃ**, **shu → しゅ**, **cho → ちょ**, **nya → にゃ**, **ryo → りょ**, **gyu → ぎゅ** and **sho → しょ**. Type **ocha → おちゃ**, **jisho → じしょ**, **shashin → しゃしん** and **ryokou → りょこう** in a scratch field or on your own device. Actual conversion steps depend on your OS, keyboard and IME; verify the *resulting Japanese text*, not just the Latin keys. The scored recognition quiz below is an accessible alternative when an IME is unavailable.

For a controlled exercise, copy **きゃ・しゅ・ちょ** and circle the **small** kana. For guided practice, compare **きゃ / きや**, then copy **おちゃ** and **じしょ** from the table. Independently type **しゃしん** and **ぎゅうにゅう**, check each small kana and count the morae. Optional handwriting: trace **きゃ**, **しゅ** and **ちょ** on paper, preserving the size difference. This is self-review only; no stroke correctness is automatically graded.

## A label in a sentence

Yui writes **しゃしんをみてください。** — “Please look at the photograph.” Here **しゃ** is part of the noun **しゃしん** (“photograph”); **を** marks the object, and **みてください** is a polite request. The contracted pair is still one mora. Do not replace small **ゃ** with full-sized **や**.
