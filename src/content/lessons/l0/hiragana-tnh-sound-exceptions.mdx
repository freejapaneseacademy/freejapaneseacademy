## The picture cards have a sound key

Ren asks Aki to read the kana labels from Lesson 02. Aki knows that each row is arranged by the five vowel positions **a, i, u, e, o**, but she cannot simply attach the letter *t* or *h* to every position. In the familiar Hepburn-style reading aid, three kana use special spellings.

| Row | a | i | u | e | o |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T | <span lang="ja">た</span> ta | <span lang="ja">ち</span> **chi** | <span lang="ja">つ</span> **tsu** | <span lang="ja">て</span> te | <span lang="ja">と</span> to |
| N | <span lang="ja">な</span> na | <span lang="ja">に</span> ni | <span lang="ja">ぬ</span> nu | <span lang="ja">ね</span> ne | <span lang="ja">の</span> no |
| H | <span lang="ja">は</span> ha | <span lang="ja">ひ</span> hi | <span lang="ja">ふ</span> **fu** | <span lang="ja">へ</span> he | <span lang="ja">ほ</span> ho |

The spelling *chi* for <span lang="ja">ち</span>, *tsu* for <span lang="ja">つ</span>, and *fu* for <span lang="ja">ふ</span> are **romanization aids**, not extra kana and not English pronunciation guarantees. In some Japanese input systems, alternate key sequences such as *ti*, *tu*, or *hu* also produce these characters. This is about the conventional **reading**, not a claim that a keyboard accepts only one spelling. Actual listening discrimination comes in Lesson 06 with licensed recordings.

## Apply the exceptions to meaningful words

Ren puts four picture cards on the desk:

| Japanese label | Reading aid | Picture / meaning | What to notice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">つき</span> | *tsuki* | moon | The first kana is **つ**, not *tu* in the reading aid |
| <span lang="ja">なつ</span> | *natsu* | summer | The second kana is **つ**, so *natsu* |
| <span lang="ja">ふね</span> | *fune* | boat / ship | The first kana is **ふ**, conventionally *fu* |
| <span lang="ja">ちか</span> | *chika* | underground / basement (context-dependent) | The first kana is **ち**, conventionally *chi* |

<span lang="ja">ちか</span> is introduced here only as a reading example. It can refer to an underground area or basement, depending on context; do not assume that an isolated label is a complete sentence.

Try a controlled rehearsal: cover the reading-aid column. Point to each kana in <span lang="ja">なつ</span>, then recover *na-tsu*. Next compare <span lang="ja">ふね</span> (*fu-ne*) with <span lang="ja">ほし</span> (*ho-shi*, star). The first character changes both the written word and its meaning.

## Compare shapes before guessing

Aki briefly confuses <span lang="ja">ぬ</span> (*nu*) with <span lang="ja">ね</span> (*ne*). Both start with a similar left-side stroke, but their **right-side endings differ**: <span lang="ja">ぬ</span> has a looped lower-right ending; <span lang="ja">ね</span> has a more upright right-side shape. Compare the printed forms at a readable size, not only their romaji.

Also compare <span lang="ja">は</span> (*ha*) and <span lang="ja">ほ</span> (*ho*). <span lang="ja">ほ</span> has an additional short horizontal stroke in its right-hand part. In the same way, <span lang="ja">た</span> (*ta*) and <span lang="ja">な</span> (*na*) are not interchangeable: inspect the strokes to the right of the crossing, rather than relying on their shared first impression.

These are **visual recognition cues**, not stroke-order instructions. Writing and tracing will be practised in Lesson 04.

## A common misuse: row names are not a reading algorithm

An English-speaking learner might predict *ti* for <span lang="ja">ち</span> because it sits in the T row, or *hu* for <span lang="ja">ふ</span> because it sits in the H row. Those spellings are not the conventional reading aids used in this course. Likewise, *tsu* belongs to **one** kana, <span lang="ja">つ</span>; it is not the sequence <span lang="ja">とす</span>. A romanization aid is useful for beginning readers, but the goal is to recognize the kana directly.

Another distinction: a kana's sound is not its word's meaning. <span lang="ja">つ</span> alone does not mean moon; <span lang="ja">つき</span> is the word for moon in Ren's picture-card context.

## Guided practice

1. Read <span lang="ja">た・ち・つ・て・と</span> from left to right. Identify the two exceptional reading aids.
2. In <span lang="ja">なつ</span>, point to the character that represents *tsu*. In <span lang="ja">ふね</span>, point to *fu*.
3. Compare <span lang="ja">は</span> and <span lang="ja">ほ</span> side by side. Which one has the extra short horizontal stroke?
4. Read <span lang="ja">ねこ</span> (*neko*, cat) and <span lang="ja">ぬの</span> (*nuno*, cloth). Check the second shape in each first kana before choosing the reading.
5. Explain why *ti* is not the conventional reading aid for <span lang="ja">ち</span> in this course, even though some keyboard methods can accept *ti* as an input sequence.
