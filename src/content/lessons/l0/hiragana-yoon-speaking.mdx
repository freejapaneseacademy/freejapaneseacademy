## Optional speaking: keep the sounds together

In contracted hiragana, a full-sized *i*-row kana followed by small **ゃ**, **ゅ** or **ょ** represents one blended mora. Compare **ぎゅ** (*gyu*) with **ぎゆ** (*gi-yu*): the latter is two kana-sized morae and is not the spelling of the first sound. This practice is optional. You do not need a microphone or a speaking score to continue.

Listen to the **two unchanged original synthetic AivisSpeech recordings from Lesson 06**, first as complete scenes and then one turn at a time. The fictional café and library conversations are simple practice settings, not authentic field recordings. Their transcripts are visible for supported shadowing, which differs from blind listening comprehension.

## Scene A — ordering a drink

The staff member asks **きょうは、なにをのみますか。** (“What will you drink today?”). The customer responds **ぎゅうにゅうをください。** (“Milk, please.”). Hear **ぎゅ** as one blended sound, then the long vowel **う**, then **にゅう**. The word has **four morae**: **ぎゅ・う・にゅ・う**. Count each contracted pair as one unit and each following う as one.

The staff member asks **つめたいぎゅうにゅうですか。** (“Cold milk?”). Repeat the request and the question without changing **ぎゅ** into **ぎゆ**. Later **しょうしょうおまちください。** (“Please wait a moment.”) contains two **しょ** combinations. Do not stretch each small ょ into a separate *yo*.

## Scene B — going to the library

A man asks **にちようびは、どこへいきますか。** (“Where are you going on Sunday?”). The woman answers **としょかんへいきます。** (“I'm going to the library.”). The **しょ** in **としょかん** is one blended mora. Try **と・しょ・か・ん** with four rhythmic units.

Next she says **いっしょにほんをよみましょう。** (“Let's read books together.”). The small **っ** in **いっしょ** marks a consonant hold, while **しょ** remains blended. Repeat the whole invitation, then compare with the recording at normal speed. **よみましょう** is a polite suggestion to read together.
