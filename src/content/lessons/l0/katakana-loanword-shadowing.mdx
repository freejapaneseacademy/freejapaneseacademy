## Your optional station voice practice

**Chapter Can-do:** Read everyday loanwords, the long vowel bar and borrowed sound patterns. You are rehearsing two **fictional station announcements**: one for a café, one for travel assistance. The model recordings belong to **Chapter 04 Lesson 06**. This optional speaking lesson must not be published before its original WAVs, rights and checksum manifest are verified. There is no new synthetic voice, no pronunciation grade and no requirement to record yourself.

**Warm-up:** Read コーヒー (*coffee*), ティー (*tea*), タクシー (*taxi*) and ファイル (*file/folder*). The long bar **ー** extends the previous vowel. In **ティ**, the ィ is small; in **ファ**, the ァ is small. A katakana loanword does not necessarily sound exactly like its English source. The lesson's purpose is careful listening and supported rehearsal, not perfect imitation of an English accent.

## Model A — café announcement

1. Announcer: <span lang="ja">駅のカフェからお知らせです。コーヒーとティーがあります。ケーキもあります。</span> — An announcement from the station café. Coffee and tea are available. Cake is also available.
2. Announcer: <span lang="ja">ティーはカウンターでご注文ください。</span> — Please order tea at the counter.

**Guided shadowing:** Play the entire recording once. Replay the first turn; pause after お知らせです, あります and あります. Repeat each chunk, then say the whole turn without looking. In the second turn, keep **ティー** and the polite ending **ください** complete. This is an announcement, not a conversation with a cashier; the learner is imitating a public-information voice.

## Model B — travel information

1. Announcer: <span lang="ja">駅からのお知らせです。タクシーは駅の前にいます。</span> — A station announcement. Taxis are in front of the station.
2. Announcer: <span lang="ja">チケットについては、スタッフにおたずねください。</span> — For questions about tickets, please ask the staff.
3. Announcer: <span lang="ja">ファイルのわすれものがありました。</span> — A file folder has been found as a lost item.

**Independent rehearsal:** Play the track, then repeat the first sentence in one smooth phrase. Practice **タクシー** with the final long vowel and **ファイル** with the small ァ. Keep the courteous request **おたずねください** intact. You may read the model before speaking; this is supported practice, not an unaided listening test.

## Useful distinctions

| Written form | Meaning | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">コーヒー</span> | coffee | Two long-vowel bars, not コヒ |
| <span lang="ja">ティー</span> | tea | Small ィ followed by ー |
| <span lang="ja">ケーキ</span> | cake | One long-vowel bar |
| <span lang="ja">タクシー</span> | taxi | Final ー |
| <span lang="ja">チケット</span> | ticket | Small ッ, not a full-sized ツ |
| <span lang="ja">ファイル</span> | file/folder | Small ァ after フ |

**Common pitfall:** Do not add a long bar just because an English word has a long-sounding vowel. Learn each Japanese spelling. The station announcements do not give prices or departure times; avoid inventing either when summarizing.

## Self-review, not a certified speaking assessment

Rehearse both recordings. Then compare your own speech against the models: (1) Did you sustain the long vowel in コーヒー and タクシー? (2) Did you keep the small-kana combinations in ティー and ファイル? (3) Did you keep ください at the end of the polite request? (4) Did you pause at sentence boundaries rather than stopping mid-word? Mark observations in the activity below. These are **personal observations**, not a machine or native-speaker pronunciation score.

The microphone is **off by default**. If you explicitly start recording, audio is held only in a temporary local tab URL and can be discarded; it is not uploaded or stored in the learning database. You can skip the microphone, speak privately, or silently rehearse. Six optional scored questions check **written understanding only**, not speech quality. Results are local practice data, never official JLPT scores.
