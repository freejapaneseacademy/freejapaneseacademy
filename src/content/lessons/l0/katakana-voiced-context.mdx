## A small stationery and snack shop

**Chapter Can-do:** Complete basic katakana and begin recognising voiced sound families. Today you will understand **who**, **where** and **why** before later lessons teach all the forms systematically. This is a written context lesson; it does **not** assess listening or pronunciation.

At a fictional neighborhood shop, **Ren** is a customer looking for a pen and a bag. **Aya** is the shop assistant. The signs are short labels, not a recording or a complete Japanese conversation.

| Person | Role | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ren | Customer | Find a pen and a bag |
| Aya | Shop assistant | Show where the products are |

### Read the shop signs

| Printed label | English meaning | What to notice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">ペン</span> | pen | The last character is **ン** |
| <span lang="ja">カバン</span> | bag | **バ** has two short marks (dakuten); the last character is **ン** |
| <span lang="ja">パン</span> | bread | **パ** has a small circle (handakuten) |
| <span lang="ja">コップ</span> | cup | **プ** has a small circle; the small **ッ** is not the full-sized ツ |
| <span lang="ja">ソース</span> | sauce | The first character is **ソ**, not ン |

These are real Japanese words used here as **original teaching labels**. The shop and people are fictional. A stationery-and-snack shop may carry different goods; this scene is an instructional setting, not a claim about every shop in Japan.

## Notice the sound marks

A **dakuten** looks like two short strokes: <span lang="ja">カ</span> (*ka*) becomes <span lang="ja">ガ</span> (*ga*), and <span lang="ja">ハ</span> (*ha*) becomes <span lang="ja">バ</span> (*ba*). A **handakuten** is a small circle: <span lang="ja">ハ</span> (*ha*) becomes <span lang="ja">パ</span> (*pa*). In <span lang="ja">カバン</span>, **バ** is *ba*; in <span lang="ja">パン</span>, **パ** is *pa*. Do not treat the marks as decoration: they change the represented sound.

You will practise the full families in Lesson 03. For now, find the marks visually and connect each label to its shop meaning. Katakana can write ordinary Japanese product names as well as loanwords; **パン** comes historically from Portuguese, but its modern Japanese meaning is simply “bread.”

## A useful shape contrast: ン and ソ

<span lang="ja">ペン</span> ends in **ン** (*n*). <span lang="ja">ソース</span> begins with **ソ** (*so*). These two printed characters can look similar, especially in small fonts. Look at the direction and placement of their strokes rather than guessing from an English translation. The shape distinction is a **reading** skill, not a sound-identification test. The next lessons give more guided comparison.

**Common mistake:** Do not read <span lang="ja">カバン</span> as *kahan* or <span lang="ja">パン</span> as *han*: the marks on バ and パ matter. Also, **ン** and **ソ** are different kana even when a particular font makes them appear close.

## Guided shop task

Imagine Ren points at a printed <span lang="ja">ペン</span> label. Aya shows the shelf with <span lang="ja">ペン</span> and <span lang="ja">カバン</span>. Ren also notices <span lang="ja">パン</span> and <span lang="ja">ソース</span> near the snacks. Read the five labels again, then answer:

1. Which two items does Ren want?
2. Which label has **バ** with dakuten?
3. Which label has **パ** with handakuten?
4. Which label begins with **ソ**, and which ends with **ン**?
