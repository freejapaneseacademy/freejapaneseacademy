## Aki's label-reading mission

Ren has prepared a **printed classroom display** for Aki. Each card has a letter, a hiragana word, a beginner reading aid and an English meaning. The task is to find what is **on the page** and quote it as evidence, not to guess a meaning from individual kana. This lesson assesses **reading only**. It does not claim audio, pronunciation assessment or stroke-order instruction.

## Source A — the classroom display

| Card | Printed Japanese label | Reading aid | Meaning on Ren's card |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | <span lang="ja">はな</span> | hana | flower |
| B | <span lang="ja">ふね</span> | fune | boat |
| C | <span lang="ja">ほし</span> | hoshi | star |
| D | <span lang="ja">なつ</span> | natsu | summer |
| E | <span lang="ja">ねこ</span> | neko | cat |
| F | <span lang="ja">たこ</span> | tako | octopus |
| G | <span lang="ja">つき</span> | tsuki | moon |
| H | <span lang="ja">ひと</span> | hito | person |

These are **isolated labels**, not complete Japanese sentences. The meanings are supplied by Ren's display. In another context <span lang="ja">はな</span> can mean “nose”; on **Card A**, Ren has explicitly labeled it “flower.” This is why reading the source context matters.

## Source B — a printed sorting note

Ren's note says:

> **Shelf 1 — nature:** A <span lang="ja">はな</span> (flower), C <span lang="ja">ほし</span> (star), D <span lang="ja">なつ</span> (summer), G <span lang="ja">つき</span> (moon).
>
> **Shelf 2 — living things:** E <span lang="ja">ねこ</span> (cat), F <span lang="ja">たこ</span> (octopus), H <span lang="ja">ひと</span> (person).
>
> **Shelf 3 — transport:** B <span lang="ja">ふね</span> (boat).

The shelves are **Ren's classroom categories**, not a universal taxonomy. In particular, the “nature” category includes a season, and the “living things” category includes a person. Read the actual note rather than assuming the groups from your own classification.

## Source C — kana reference strip

| Vowel position | T row | N row | H row |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| a | <span lang="ja">た</span> ta | <span lang="ja">な</span> na | <span lang="ja">は</span> ha |
| i | <span lang="ja">ち</span> chi | <span lang="ja">に</span> ni | <span lang="ja">ひ</span> hi |
| u | <span lang="ja">つ</span> tsu | <span lang="ja">ぬ</span> nu | <span lang="ja">ふ</span> fu |
| e | <span lang="ja">て</span> te | <span lang="ja">ね</span> ne | <span lang="ja">へ</span> he |
| o | <span lang="ja">と</span> to | <span lang="ja">の</span> no | <span lang="ja">ほ</span> ho |

The conventional reading aids for <span lang="ja">ち</span>, <span lang="ja">つ</span> and <span lang="ja">ふ</span> are *chi*, *tsu* and *fu*. A romaji input method may also accept alternative typing sequences. In this reading lesson, focus on the actual hiragana.

## Guided evidence reading

**Example 1.** “Which card means boat?” Find **boat** in Source A. It is **Card B: <span lang="ja">ふね</span>**. Source B independently places **B ふね** on Shelf 3. The first kana <span lang="ja">ふ</span> is *fu*, not <span lang="ja">ほ</span> (*ho*).

**Example 2.** “Which label starts with <span lang="ja">ほ</span>?” Scan the first character of every printed word. **Card C: <span lang="ja">ほし</span>** starts with <span lang="ja">ほ</span>; Card A <span lang="ja">はな</span> begins with <span lang="ja">は</span>. Quote the first character, not just the English meaning.

**Example 3.** “Which label ends with <span lang="ja">つ</span>?” Source A prints **Card D: <span lang="ja">なつ</span>**. It is two kana, <span lang="ja">な</span> + <span lang="ja">つ</span>, with the conventional reading aid *natsu*.

**Example 4.** “Which shelf holds <span lang="ja">ひと</span>?” Source B explicitly prints **Shelf 2 — living things: H ひと**. Source A gives the English meaning “person.” The evidence is the printed shelf note.

## Check before answering

Avoid three common errors. First, **は** (*ha*) and **ほ** (*ho*) are distinct: compare <span lang="ja">はな</span> with <span lang="ja">ほし</span>. Second, **ふ** (*fu*) is not **ほ** (*ho*): <span lang="ja">ふね</span> is the boat card. Third, the English meaning belongs to the **whole word** in context: <span lang="ja">つ</span> alone does not mean summer or moon.

Read Sources A–C again, cover the English column and recover the printed words. Then complete eight scored questions below. Each explanation names the exact source phrase or card. Quiz attempts and lesson completion are saved separately on this device when local storage is available.
