## Optional speaking: hear the mark, say the word

A small mark on hiragana changes a consonant: **ふ** is different from **ぶ**, and **は** is different from **ぱ**. This chapter's Can-do is to read and write voiced and p-series hiragana. Here you can optionally practise saying those sounds in familiar spoken words. No microphone is required, and neither your accent nor your pronunciation is automatically graded.

The two original **synthetic AivisSpeech recordings from Lesson 06** are reused unchanged. The bakery and zoo are fictional teaching scenes. Start by listening to the entire conversation at normal speed; then play individual turns, pause and repeat. Read the transcript while practising, but remember that seeing text is different from understanding speech unaided.

## Track A — asking for bread

A customer enters a bakery. The exchange starts with **こんにちは。パンはありますか。** (“Hello. Do you have bread?”). The shopkeeper answers **はい。たまごパンとぶどうパンがあります。** (“Yes. We have egg bread and grape bread.”)

Notice the contrast between **パン** (*bread*, pronounced with a p sound) and **ぶどう** (*grapes*, beginning with a voiced b sound). The word ぶどう has three written mora units, ぶ・ど・う; keep the final vowel sequence instead of stopping after ど. The shopkeeper's word **たまご** (*egg*) ends with voiced ご.

Repeat the customer's whole request **ぶどうパンをひとつください。** (“One grape bread, please.”). Then repeat **みずをください。** (“Water, please.”). The particle を is normally pronounced *o* here. Practise the request as a connected phrase, not as unrelated word fragments. You do not need to mimic a particular actor's accent.

## Track B — animals at the zoo

The man points out **あそこに、パンダがいますよ。** (“There's a panda over there.”). The word **パンダ** uses a p-series パ and a voiced ダ in the same word. Pause after the model and say パン・ダ with the final ダ clearly, but remember that パンダ is normally spoken as one word.

Next he says **ぞうもいます。** (“There's an elephant too.”). The voiced ぞ begins ぞう; its vowel sequence takes two morae. The woman replies **ぞうはおおきいですね。** (“The elephant is big, isn't it?”). Repeat that complete sentence, including the topic particle は, pronounced *wa*.

Finally the woman suggests **ぶたをみにいきましょう。** (“Let's go see the pigs.”). Compare ぶた with パンダ: they are different animal words, and the opening consonants belong to different marked-kana families. Do not replace one word with the other just because both occur at the zoo.

## A safe, repeatable rehearsal

Play the complete track, then select one turn to hear its model. Repeat immediately, then try again without reading. If the audio does not play, use the Japanese and English transcript for reading practice; do not claim to have heard the model. Slower playback is available but can change the perceived voice quality.
