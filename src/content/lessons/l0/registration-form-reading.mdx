## Your reading mission

**Can-do:** State and recognize basic numbers, names and nationalities. In this reading lesson, you will **recognize personal details** in an original registration form. A form is not a conversation: its field labels tell you what each value means. You do not need to translate every word to find a correct answer.

The cards below are **fictional teaching examples**. They contain no real person's personal information. Reading is assessed here; listening and spoken introductions belong to the later chapter lessons.

## Decode the labels first

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">なまえ</span> | namae | name | A person's written name |
| <span lang="ja">くに</span> | kuni | country | Country name, not a person's name |
| <span lang="ja">ねんれい</span> | nenrei | age | A numeral with <span lang="ja">さい</span> |
| <span lang="ja">ばんごう</span> | bangō | number / identifier | A participant number, **not** an age |
| <span lang="ja">さい</span> | sai | years old (age suffix) | Appears after the age numeral |

Numbers such as **18**, **27**, and **31** are Arabic numerals in these forms. They are common in practical Japanese paperwork. You may read 18 as <span lang="ja">じゅうはち</span> (*jūhachi*) and 27 as <span lang="ja">にじゅうなな</span> (*nijūnana*), but the task here is to **find the printed digits accurately**. <span lang="ja">にほん</span> means Japan; <span lang="ja">カナダ</span> means Canada. These cards do not ask you to infer nationality from a person's name.

## How to find evidence

Suppose someone asks, “How old is Mina?” Find Card A, then locate <span lang="ja">ねんれい</span>. The value is <span lang="ja">18さい</span>. That is your **exact evidence**. Do **not** answer 27: that number belongs to <span lang="ja">ばんごう</span> and identifies the participant, not the age.

For “Where is Yumi's country field?”, find Card B and the label <span lang="ja">くに</span>. The entry is <span lang="ja">にほん</span>. The name <span lang="ja">ユミ</span> alone is **not evidence** of nationality. The form gives a country, not an explicit nationality claim.

## Connect the form to a simple sentence

The supported model <span lang="ja">わたしは ミナです。</span> means “I am Mina.” Here <span lang="ja">わたし</span> is “I,” <span lang="ja">は</span> marks the topic (pronounced *wa* as a particle), and <span lang="ja">です</span> is a polite sentence ending. This is a beginner model, not a claim that every registration form uses complete sentences.

You may also read <span lang="ja">わたしは ユミです。</span> — “I am Yumi.” The name changes; the polite frame stays the same. Avoid the common error of treating <span lang="ja">です</span> as part of the name.

## Guided reading

Try these without looking at the English glosses: (1) Find the two <span lang="ja">ばんごう</span> values; (2) point to the field containing <span lang="ja">カナダ</span>; (3) copy the age from Card B exactly, including <span lang="ja">さい</span>. Check: **27 and 31**, **くに**, and **20さい**.

For optional writing practice, copy <span lang="ja">なまえ：ミナ</span> and <span lang="ja">ねんれい：18さい</span> using your Japanese keyboard. Compare character by character; copying is not a graded handwriting or speaking assessment.

## Review

Read each field label before its value. A participant number is not an age; a name does not prove a nationality; and an age suffix <span lang="ja">さい</span> belongs with the age.
