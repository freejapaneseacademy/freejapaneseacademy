## Two conversations, two listening goals

**Chapter Can-do:** complete the basic katakana families, including R/W and voiced sounds. Here you connect familiar loanwords to natural **spoken** shop exchanges. You are scored on information you hear, not on recognizing the written shapes of ン and ソ or deciding which printed kana has dakuten.

In the first scene, a customer places an order at a bakery. Listen for **how many** items are requested and what extra item is offered. In the second, two people discuss a bag and a gift. Listen for **who** the bag belongs to and **who** will receive the present.

## A useful listening strategy

On the first play, focus on the general situation. On the second, listen for number words such as *hitotsu* (one) and *futatsu* (two), or for the ownership marker *no* (“of / belonging to”). These are listening strategies, not the answers to the questions. Do not read a transcript until you have tried the audio.

Japanese requests often use **〜をください** (“please give me …”). A clerk might add **〜もあります** (“we also have …”). In a conversation about belongings, **〜さんの** can tell you whose object it is. The expressions are introduced here as comprehension support, not as a separate grammar exam.

## Track A — Bakery counter

Listen to the original bakery conversation. First identify the order, then listen again for quantities. Answer the three Track A questions without written hints. Replay as often as you need.

## Track B — Gift shop

Listen to the original gift-shop conversation. Listen for the name associated with the bag and whether the item is a present. Answer the three Track B questions.

## Check your evidence after answering

After submission, reveal the Japanese transcript and its English translation. Point to the spoken turn that supports each answer. Revealing a transcript before submitting is allowed for accessibility, but the result is clearly **assisted practice** and is not saved as a blind listening score.

For optional speaking, repeat one polite request aloud or silently mouth the rhythm. No microphone is activated, and no pronunciation grade is assigned. For optional writing, copy a loanword from the revealed transcript and compare the voiced marks with the printed model; writing is not part of the scored listening assessment.
