## At the neighborhood shop

**Can-do:** Recognize useful katakana words and names in a shop scene. Yuna is the customer, the clerk points to food labels, and Yumi is a visitor whose name appears on a card. These are original fictional teaching labels, not real advertisements. This lesson tests **written vocabulary**, not audio pronunciation.

| Printed label | Reading support | Meaning and use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">ハム</span> | ha-mu | ham; a food |
| <span lang="ja">バナナ</span> | ba-na-na | banana; a food |
| <span lang="ja">ミナ</span> | mi-na | Mina; a person's name |
| <span lang="ja">ユミ</span> | yu-mi | Yumi; a person's name |

**Important:** バ is バ (ba), made from ハ with a voiced mark. This word is presented as a whole familiar label; systematic voiced katakana comes later. The word ハム is a Japanese loanword from English *ham*, but not every katakana string is an English loanword. ミナ and ユミ are possible names.

## Learn the shape-to-word connections

The N row is <span lang="ja">ナ ニ ヌ ネ ノ</span> (na, ni, nu, ne, no). The H row is <span lang="ja">ハ ヒ フ ヘ ホ</span> (ha, hi, fu, he, ho). The M row is <span lang="ja">マ ミ ム メ モ</span> (ma, mi, mu, me, mo). The Y row is <span lang="ja">ヤ ユ ヨ</span> (ya, yu, yo). Modern basic Y-row katakana has **three**, not five, symbols.

Read <span lang="ja">ハム</span> from left to right: ハ (ha) + ム (mu). Read <span lang="ja">ミナ</span>: ミ (mi) + ナ (na). Read <span lang="ja">ユミ</span>: ユ (yu) + ミ (mi). In <span lang="ja">バナナ</span>, ナ occurs twice. Use the entire character shape: ナ is not メ, ム is not マ, and ミ has three short strokes.

## Meaning depends on context

A printed label <span lang="ja">ハム</span> tells you the **product name**. It does not state the price, ingredients, or whether someone bought it. A card with <span lang="ja">ユミ</span> is a **person's name** in this story, not a food. Katakana can represent loanwords and names; script alone does not determine a word's role.

### Guided retrieval

1. Cover the meaning column. Point to ハム and say whether it is a product or a name.
2. Find the two printed names, ミナ and ユミ. Identify the first symbol of each.
3. Compare ム and マ; then compare ナ and メ. Name one visual difference without guessing the whole word.
4. Imagine a new shelf card that says ハム only. Can you determine its price? **No**: the card does not provide one.
