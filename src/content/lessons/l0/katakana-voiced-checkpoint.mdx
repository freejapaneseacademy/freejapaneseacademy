## Chapter 03 checkpoint: the right marks matter

**Can-do:** Complete basic katakana and recognize common voiced sound families. This checkpoint mixes visual reading, a new fictional shop display, and listening to two original spoken dialogues. Try the listening questions **before revealing the transcripts**; viewing them early marks your attempt as assisted.

### 1. Read the remaining forms

| Form | Reading | What to notice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">ラ リ ル レ ロ</span> | ra ri ru re ro | The R row has five basic symbols. |
| <span lang="ja">ワ ヲ ン</span> | wa, o (historically wo), n | ヲ is mainly a written particle in modern Japanese; ン is the moraic nasal. |
| <span lang="ja">ソ / ン</span> | so / n | Their short strokes and longer sweeping strokes have different orientation. Check the whole shape rather than a single stroke. |
| <span lang="ja">ハ / バ / パ</span> | ha / ba / pa | No mark / two short strokes (dakuten) / small circle (handakuten). |
| <span lang="ja">シ / ジ</span> | shi / ji | ジ adds dakuten; <span lang="ja">ジュ</span> uses a **small** ュ. |

The dakuten changes sounds in several rows, such as カ → ガ and ハ → バ. The handakuten changes H-row forms to P-row sounds: ハ → パ, ヒ → ピ, フ → プ, ヘ → ペ, ホ → ポ. These are **spelling and sound distinctions**, not interchangeable decoration. Some loanwords use other combinations, which you will study later.

### 2. Read the fictional counter display

At a small fictional bakery, a shelf card reads **<span lang="ja">パン</span>** (bread) and **<span lang="ja">ジュース</span>** (juice). A quantity note below it says **<span lang="ja">パン ふたつ / ジュース ひとつ</span>** (two breads / one juice). At the adjacent gift counter, a name card reads **<span lang="ja">ミナさんのバッグ</span>** (Mina's bag). The word **<span lang="ja">バッグ</span>** means bag; the small ッ marks the consonant timing. Do not infer prices or ownership from an unlabeled product alone: the printed phrase with の provides the ownership evidence.

Compare <span lang="ja">パン</span> with the deliberately incorrect spelling <span lang="ja">バン</span>. A circle versus two marks changes the initial sound. The display is a **new written stimulus**; it is not the transcript of the listening scenes.

### 3. Listen to the two shop scenes

Track A is an original bakery conversation: the clerk offers bread, the customer requests two, and the clerk mentions juice. Track B is an original gift-shop exchange about Mina's bag and a present. The clips were synthesized with approved AivisSpeech voices in the predecessor L06 lesson; their versioned WAVs and rights manifest must be present and integrity-checked before publication.

Listen once for the situation, again for a key word, and a third time for quantities or recipients. **Do not open the transcript until you have attempted the listening items** unless you deliberately choose assisted practice. A written answer key cannot verify pronunciation; independent human pronunciation and assessment reviews remain pending.

### 4. How the checkpoint works

There are **12 scored questions**: four script/shape questions, five written-label evidence questions and three listening questions. Select one answer per item. Feedback after submission identifies the supporting printed label or spoken line. Your score is a local practice result, not an official proficiency rating. A transcript-assisted attempt is stored under a different progress identifier.

After feedback, write the mistaken kana beside its correct counterpart, explain the mark or stroke difference, and try the written display again. For listening errors, replay the relevant utterance and compare it with the transcript only afterward. You may complete the chapter regardless of score; under 80%, review the forms you missed before moving on.
