## Mio's first word cards

**Chapter Can-do:** Read, write and identify <span lang="ja">あ–お・か–こ・さ–そ</span>. In this vocabulary step, you will connect short written words to objects, times and places. Later lessons teach writing strokes and provide actual syllable audio.

Tutor Sora sets ten illustrated labels on Mio's desk. Mio already knows the vowel, K and S rows from Lesson 01, but recognizing <span lang="ja">か</span> and <span lang="ja">さ</span> is not enough to know what <span lang="ja">かさ</span> means. A **kana** represents a sound unit; a **word** has a meaning learned through use.

## Ten useful first words

| Hiragana | Reading aid | Meaning | Scene |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">かさ</span> | *kasa* | umbrella | An object that keeps rain off you |
| <span lang="ja">すし</span> | *sushi* | sushi | A Japanese food made with seasoned rice |
| <span lang="ja">あさ</span> | *asa* | morning | A time of day |
| <span lang="ja">いす</span> | *isu* | chair | Something you sit on |
| <span lang="ja">かお</span> | *kao* | face | A person's face |
| <span lang="ja">あし</span> | *ashi* | foot or leg | A body part; context decides foot versus leg |
| <span lang="ja">さけ</span> | *sake* | salmon (or alcoholic drink) | Written さけ is ambiguous without context; in a fish-shop label it can mean salmon |
| <span lang="ja">そこ</span> | *soko* | there, near you | A location near the listener |
| <span lang="ja">ここ</span> | *koko* | here | A location near the speaker |
| <span lang="ja">せかい</span> | *sekai* | world | The whole world |

The romanization is temporary support. Look at the hiragana first, then check the reading aid. For <span lang="ja">し</span>, common English-oriented romanization uses *shi*, not *si*.

## Put the words in context

**At the door on a rainy morning:** Sora points to an umbrella and says <span lang="ja">かさ</span> (*kasa*, umbrella). Mio sees a morning picture marked <span lang="ja">あさ</span> (*asa*, morning). The similar ending <span lang="ja">さ</span> does **not** make these two words mean the same thing.

**At the classroom table:** An empty chair is labelled <span lang="ja">いす</span> (*isu*, chair). A portrait has the label <span lang="ja">かお</span> (*kao*, face). A body-parts picture has <span lang="ja">あし</span> (*ashi*, foot or leg). The latter English translation depends on which part is indicated.

**At the food counter:** <span lang="ja">すし</span> (*sushi*) names a food with seasoned rice. At a **fish** shop, <span lang="ja">さけ</span> (*sake*) can mean salmon. The same kana spelling can also refer to the alcoholic drink *sake* in another setting. **Do not guess an English meaning from the sounds alone.** Context disambiguates the word.

**Pointing to places:** <span lang="ja">ここ</span> (*koko*) means “here,” near the speaker. <span lang="ja">そこ</span> (*soko*) means “there,” near the listener. <span lang="ja">せかい</span> (*sekai*) means “world.” These are lexical meanings, not translations of each kana.

## Read, cover, retrieve

1. Read <span lang="ja">かさ・すし・いす</span> from left to right, one kana at a time. Say their meanings, then cover the English column and recall them.
2. Compare <span lang="ja">あさ</span> (morning) and <span lang="ja">かさ</span> (umbrella). Which kana is shared? Which word begins with the vowel <span lang="ja">あ</span>?
3. Point to your chair and retrieve <span lang="ja">いす</span>. Point to your face and retrieve <span lang="ja">かお</span>. For <span lang="ja">あし</span>, identify whether your context is foot or leg.
4. Imagine two people standing apart. From the speaker's viewpoint, use <span lang="ja">ここ</span> for “here” and <span lang="ja">そこ</span> for “there near you.”

These are written practice prompts, not recorded pronunciation tests. For audio-based pronunciation models, continue to Lesson 06 when it becomes available.

## Avoid three common mistakes

**Mistake 1 — translating kana separately.** <span lang="ja">か</span> and <span lang="ja">さ</span> are sound symbols in <span lang="ja">かさ</span>; the complete word means umbrella.

**Mistake 2 — treating a reading aid as Japanese spelling.** *Sushi* helps you remember <span lang="ja">すし</span>, but the target script is the hiragana.

**Mistake 3 — ignoring context.** <span lang="ja">さけ</span> can mean salmon or an alcoholic drink. A fish-counter label supports salmon; the kana alone do not settle the meaning.
