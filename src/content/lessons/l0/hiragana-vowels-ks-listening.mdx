## Listen for meaning, not letters

You have learned to recognize fifteen hiragana: the five vowels and the K and S rows. Now hear **real spoken Japanese**. Written kana can help you study later, but a listening answer must come from what you hear. You will not be asked to identify a printed character by sound.

These are **original fictional dialogues performed by licensed synthetic Japanese voices**, not recordings of real people. Track A is a short request and reply. Track B is a conversation about a daily plan. Both contain useful expressions beyond the fifteen kana already studied. You do **not** need to read or memorize those new expressions before listening.

## Strategy: listen in three passes

First, listen once without the transcript. Try to catch one familiar word without looking at any written hints. Next, replay and notice whether the reply is affirmative or negative and what the speakers plan to do. Finally, answer the questions **before** revealing written support. This is a comprehension exercise, not a spelling test.

### Track A — A short request

Listen for the object requested, the answer, and a location. After submitting, reveal the transcript to study the expressions and polite closing.

### Track B — An everyday plan

Listen for the activity, the items mentioned, and whether the second speaker agrees. Study the verbs and particles after submitting your first listening attempt.

## Your listening check

Use the two actual WAV players below. Each recording has four spoken turns with different male and female voices. Replay freely at normal speed or use the optional speed control. Answer three questions per track; each question has a single best answer and a spoken-evidence explanation shown after submission.

**Blind attempt:** keep both transcripts hidden until you submit. **Assisted practice:** revealing a transcript early is allowed for accessibility and study, but it will not be saved as an unaided listening score. If audio fails, use assisted mode rather than guessing what an unheard recording contains.

## After your attempt

Reveal the Japanese and English transcripts and replay each turn. Check whether you heard the **request**, **answer**, and **detail**. Compare familiar spoken words with the word cards from Lesson 05; written and spoken recognition reinforce one another, but they are separate skills.
