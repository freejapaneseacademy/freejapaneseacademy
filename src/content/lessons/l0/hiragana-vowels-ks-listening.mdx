## Listen for meaning, not letters

You have learned to recognize fifteen hiragana: the five vowels and the K and S rows. Now hear **real spoken Japanese**. Written kana can help you study later, but a listening answer must come from what you hear. You will not be asked to identify a printed character by sound.

These are **original fictional dialogues performed by licensed synthetic Japanese voices**, not recordings of real people. Track A is a brief request about an umbrella. Track B is a conversation about lunch. Both contain useful expressions beyond the fifteen kana already studied. You do **not** need to read or memorize those new expressions before listening.

## Strategy: listen in three passes

First, listen once without the transcript. Try to catch just one familiar word, such as *kasa* (umbrella), *sushi*, or *ocha* (tea). Next, replay and notice whether the reply is affirmative (*hai*, yes) and what the speaker says they will do. Finally, answer the questions **before** revealing written support. This is a comprehension exercise, not a spelling test.

### Track A — Looking for an umbrella

A woman politely asks a man whether an umbrella is available. Listen for the object, his yes-or-no response, and the location he mentions. A useful pattern to notice **after listening** is *arimasu* (there is / exists), used here for an inanimate object. The expression *koko ni* means “here.” The polite closing is a thank-you and reply.

### Track B — Choosing lunch

A man asks what someone will eat for lunch. Listen for the food she names, the drink he offers, and her response. The verb *tabemasu* means “eat,” while *nomimasu* means “drink.” You can understand the basic exchange without analyzing all the particles yet. The particle *mo* in *ocha mo* adds “also.”

## Your listening check

Use the two actual WAV players below. Each recording has four spoken turns with different male and female voices. Replay freely at normal speed or use the optional speed control. Answer three questions per track; each question has a single best answer and a spoken-evidence explanation shown after submission.

**Blind attempt:** keep both transcripts hidden until you submit. **Assisted practice:** revealing a transcript early is allowed for accessibility and study, but it will not be saved as an unaided listening score. If audio fails, use assisted mode rather than guessing what an unheard recording contains.

## After your attempt

Reveal the Japanese and English transcripts and replay each turn. Check whether you heard the **request**, **answer**, and **detail**. Compare the sound of *kasa* with the word card from Lesson 05; written and spoken recognition reinforce one another, but they are separate skills.
