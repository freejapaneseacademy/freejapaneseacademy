## The library's two-column label list

At a neighborhood library, Hana and Ken are checking printed labels for a display. The left column gives a **shorter or contrasting spelling**; the right column gives the **target label**. Read both before answering. These are written examples, **not audio**. A contrast may be a different word or simply a spelling comparison; do not assume every pair is a minimal pair.

| Pair | Left label | Right label | Evidence in print |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | **きて** — “come” (request form) | **きって** — postage stamp | Right adds small **っ** before て |
| B | **つき** — moon | **きっぷ** — ticket | Left begins with full-sized **つ**; right has small **っ** before ぷ |
| C | **さし** — ruler | **ざっし** — magazine | Right has voiced **ざ** and small **っ**; these are different words |
| D | **こうえん** — park | **おおきい** — big | Left contains **こう** (おう pattern); right begins **おお** |
| E | **とけい** — clock | **せんせい** — teacher | Both have written **えい** |
| F | **とおい** — far | **がっこう** — school | Left has **おお**; right has **っ** and **こう** |
| G | **ここ** — here | **こうこう** — high school | Right has **こう** twice, with two separate う kana |

The English gloss for **きて** describes the request form of 来る (“to come”); the word needs context. **きって** means “postage stamp.” Small **っ** is one mora, a consonant hold before the following consonant; it is **not** the same as the full-sized **つ** in **つき**. The table is a spelling-and-reading task, not a pronunciation assessment.

## Mora evidence strip

| Printed word | Written mora division | Total |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **きて** | **き・て** | 2 |
| **きって** | **き・っ・て** | 3 |
| **きっぷ** | **き・っ・ぷ** | 3 |
| **ざっし** | **ざ・っ・し** | 3 |
| **こうえん** | **こ・う・え・ん** | 4 |
| **おおきい** | **お・お・き・い** | 4 |
| **せんせい** | **せ・ん・せ・い** | 4 |
| **とおい** | **と・お・い** | 3 |
| **がっこう** | **が・っ・こ・う** | 4 |
| **こうこう** | **こ・う・こ・う** | 4 |

Each kana in the displayed divisions contributes one mora, including **っ**, **ん** and a written vowel extending a long vowel. In standard Japanese, **こう** and **おお** commonly represent long *o*, but **the spelling belongs to the word**: do not change **こうえん** to こおえん or **おおきい** to おうきい. The **えい** of **せんせい** and **とけい** often corresponds to a long *e* in ordinary speech, with contextual variation; here you must identify **written letters**.

## A note attached to the labels

Hana writes **きってをみてください。** (“Please look at the postage stamp.”) Ken writes **こうえんはとおいです。** (“The park is far.”) In the first note, **きって** comes before the object particle **を**. In the second, **こうえん** is the topic before **は**, while **とおい** describes the distance. Point to the exact printed phrase that supports your answer rather than guessing from English alone.

## Read, locate and explain

**Controlled:** Find pair A and underline the small **っ** in **きって**. Then use the mora strip to show why **きて** has two morae but **きって** has three.

**Guided:** Compare pair D and pair F. Circle the **おう** pattern in **こうえん** and **がっこう**, then the **おお** pattern in **おおきい** and **とおい**. Both patterns can represent long *o*; they cannot be freely substituted in spelling.

**Independent:** Without looking at the explanations, find the two labels with **えい**, identify the pair with two **こう** sequences, and explain the difference between **つき** and **きっぷ**. Reopen the chart and cite the row that proves each answer.
