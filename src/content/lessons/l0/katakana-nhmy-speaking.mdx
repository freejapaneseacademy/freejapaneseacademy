## A shop conversation you can rehearse

**Can-do:** Read and write the N, H, M and Y katakana rows, and optionally say a short shop request with support. Speaking here is **optional**. You can finish the lesson without using a microphone or saying anything aloud. The written quiz checks only written forms; it is **not** a pronunciation test.

Our fictional fruit stall and deli counter have printed product labels: <span lang="ja">バナナ</span> (banana) and <span lang="ja">ハム</span> (ham). Two visitor name cards read <span lang="ja">ミナ</span> (Mina) and <span lang="ja">ユミ</span> (Yumi). Katakana is used for these labels and names in this scenario; names can be written in other scripts in real life.

## Listen, pause, and optionally shadow

Use the **two genuine L06 AivisSpeech WAV recordings** in the practice panel below. The same recordings contain short original shop conversations. They are not generated by your browser. Play a whole dialogue once, then pause after a customer turn and repeat it only if you wish. You can instead read the Japanese silently or tap along to the mora rhythm.

**Track A, fruit stall (customer):** <span lang="ja">バナナはありますか。</span> — “Do you have bananas?” Later: <span lang="ja">バナナをふたつください。</span> — “Two bananas, please.”

**Track B, deli counter (customer):** <span lang="ja">ハムはありますか。</span> — “Do you have ham?” Later: <span lang="ja">では、ハムをひとつください。</span> — “Then one ham, please.”

These are polite shop requests, not generic translations for every situation. <span lang="ja">は</span> marks the topic (pronounced *wa* as a particle); <span lang="ja">を</span> marks the requested object. <span lang="ja">ひとつ</span> means one item and <span lang="ja">ふたつ</span> means two items in this simple count. English glosses support comprehension; do not force every Japanese word into a one-to-one English slot.

## Controlled → guided → independent practice

1. **Notice:** Find <span lang="ja">ナ</span> in <span lang="ja">バナナ</span>, and <span lang="ja">ハ</span> and <span lang="ja">ム</span> in <span lang="ja">ハム</span>. Compare <span lang="ja">ミ</span> with <span lang="ja">メ</span>, and <span lang="ja">ユ</span> with <span lang="ja">ヨ</span>.
2. **Rehearse:** Listen again. Pause after <span lang="ja">ありますか</span> and <span lang="ja">ください</span>. Say the phrase, mouth it silently, or read it without speaking.
3. **Use independently:** Imagine asking for a banana at the stall. Read <span lang="ja">バナナをひとつください。</span> — “One banana, please.” This is a supported variation for practice, **not** a claim that this new sentence occurs in the recording.

## Self-review, not a speech grade

After rehearsal, use the panel checklist to reflect on **(a)** whether you found the product name, **(b)** whether you followed the model at a comfortable pace, and **(c)** whether you distinguished one from two. Marking boxes records no pronunciation score or verified speaking proficiency. No microphone access is requested. If audio is unavailable, the written scripts and silent rehearsal remain accessible; do not claim to have heard a recording you could not play.

The two model WAVs belong to the Chapter 02 L06 lesson, with its manifest, original AivisSpeech attribution and checksum verification required before this lesson can be published. Independent native pronunciation and assessment reviews remain pending.
