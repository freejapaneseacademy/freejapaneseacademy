## From word cards to a written message

You have already read classroom word cards and listened to short spoken situations. Now you will **write** words and two short messages in basic hiragana. Accuracy matters more than speed. This is a written practice task, not a pronunciation test.

### Step 1 — retrieve the whole word

| Meaning | Model word | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| cat | **ねこ** | ね has a different final shape from れ |
| dog | **いぬ** | ぬ is not め |
| car | **くるま** | る is not ろ |
| mountain | **やま** | や and ま |
| book | **ほん** | ん is a full mora |
| river | **かわ** | か and わ |

Cover the Japanese column, type the word from its meaning, and uncover the model. Read each character in sequence rather than guessing from the first shape. Printed and handwritten kana may differ slightly between fonts and people; do not rely on one isolated stroke.

### Step 2 — connect two nouns

The particle **と** means *and* when joining these nouns. The phrase **ねこといぬ** means *cat and dog*. The phrase **やまとかわ** means *mountain and river*. These are noun phrases, not complete sentences. The full models are **ねこといぬ** and **やまとかわ**. In ordinary Japanese writing, spaces are generally not inserted between these kana.

### Step 3 — write a classroom request

The model **ねこをみてください。** means *Please look at the cat.* The model **ほんをみてください。** means *Please look at the book.* The object particle **を** is written with を, even though it is normally pronounced like お. The verb request **みてください** means *please look*. The punctuation **。** is the Japanese full stop.

Notice that **ねこをみてください。** is a request, not the assertion *I see a cat*. Use the exact written pattern **[noun] + を + みてください。** when asking someone to look at a classroom picture card.

### Step 4 — use your Japanese keyboard

Switch to a Japanese IME and select hiragana output. Check the actual kana in the input box before submitting; typing Latin letters without conversion does not count. To finish **ん**, some IMEs require an extra *n* or explicit confirmation. The scored check accepts the taught kana models, ignores outer whitespace, and compares Unicode-normalized characters. It does not accept romaji, and it does not score penmanship.

### Independent practice and review

Complete ten independent typing items. The answer models and reasons appear after you submit. Then use the optional composition box to write your own two-line picture-card message, for example:

**ねこといぬ**

**ねこをみてください。**

Compare it against four criteria: (1) every kana is recognizable, (2) the intended word is complete, (3) **と** joins nouns and **を** marks the request's object correctly, and (4) the request ends with **みてください。**. Self-review is guidance, not an automatic human writing grade. Your scored typing attempt is stored locally; your optional free composition is not uploaded or automatically assessed.
