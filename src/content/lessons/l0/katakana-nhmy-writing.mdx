## Your task: prepare labels for a small shop

**Can-do:** Read and write katakana N, H, M and Y rows. At a fictional neighborhood shop, a helper is copying product cards and visitor name cards. You will create recognizable written forms, compare them with a model and correct errors. This is **written practice**, not a listening test or an automatic handwriting assessment.

## First review the characters

| Row | a | i | u | e | o |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| N | ナ | ニ | ヌ | ネ | ノ |
| H | ハ | ヒ | フ | ヘ | ホ |
| M | マ | ミ | ム | メ | モ |
| Y | ヤ | — | ユ | — | ヨ |

The Y row has **three** basic modern katakana: ヤ (*ya*), ユ (*yu*), ヨ (*yo*). Do not invent a basic **yi** or **ye** cell. In the H row, フ is *fu* in standard beginner romanization. These are sound-to-script reference cues; handwriting quality is assessed by comparing actual written shapes.

## Model labels in context

| Card | Japanese model | Reading and purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Food shelf | <span lang="ja">ハム</span> | *hamu*, ham |
| Note pad | <span lang="ja">メモ</span> | *memo*, memo |
| Visitor card | <span lang="ja">ユミ</span> | *Yumi*, a given name |
| Visitor card | <span lang="ja">ミナ</span> | *Mina*, a given name |
| Fabric practice card | <span lang="ja">ヌノ</span> | *nuno*, cloth; ordinarily written 布 or ぬの |

The scene tells you whether the card names a product, note or person. Katakana itself does not determine that role. For the fabric card, ヌノ is a **phonetic copying drill**, not a recommendation to write cloth in katakana in normal Japanese.

## Guided copy–cover–write cycle

1. Look at **ハム**. Read the two characters separately: ハ (*ha*) + ム (*mu*). Copy the model slowly on paper.
2. Cover the model and write **ハム** from memory. Uncover it and compare the character count, left-to-right order and distinguishability of each form.
3. Repeat with **メモ**, **ユミ**, and **ミナ**. On the name cards, check whether you wrote ユ (*yu*) or ヨ (*yo*) and whether ミ (*mi*) accidentally became ム (*mu*).
4. For an extra challenge, copy **ナ ニ ヌ ネ ノ** in that order, then **ヤ ユ ヨ**. Do not add imaginary Y-row vowels.

## Shape distinctions that change the label

**ナ vs メ:** Both have crossing strokes, but the overall angle and placement differ. Do not identify a glyph from one stroke alone. **マ vs ム:** Compare the upper shape and lower strokes. **ミ vs ム:** ミ has three separate marks; ム does not. **ユ vs ヨ:** ヨ has a middle horizontal mark; ユ does not. **ヌ vs ノ:** ヌ has additional strokes; ノ is a single slanted stroke. Printed font details vary, so use a clear Japanese font and compare complete forms.

## Typing with a Japanese IME

If your device supports Japanese input, enter `hamu`, `memo`, `yumi` and `mina`, then select **katakana** conversion. Check each output against the printed model. A keyboard may initially offer hiragana, kanji or a different word: **conversion is not proof of correctness**. If no IME is available, the paper-copy task and scored multiple-choice check remain fully accessible.

## Your personal writing rubric

For each copied label, award **0–2** points on each criterion: **recognizability** (0 illegible, 1 partly recognizable, 2 every character clear); **sequence** (0 incorrect, 1 one error, 2 exact order); and **confusable distinctions** (0 shapes ambiguous, 1 one unclear pair, 2 all shapes distinct). Total: **0–6 per label**. The rubric is learner self-review only; no computer or human has inspected your handwriting. Write one specific correction and try the label again.

## Independent written check

Answer the eight scored questions below without relying on an IME. Feedback and the answer explanations appear **after** submission. The scored questions test written recognition and spelling; your optional paper writing and typing self-check are separate, ungraded tasks. Progress is saved locally when available.
