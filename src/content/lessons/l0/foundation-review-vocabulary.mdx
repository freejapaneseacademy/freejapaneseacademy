## Bring your foundation words into one day

**Can-do:** Read kana fluently enough for N5 and set a sustainable review routine. In this written word-study lesson, you will use earlier foundation vocabulary in three places: a morning classroom, a café, and a short study session. **No listening or pronunciation proficiency is scored here**; the chapter's original listening review belongs to Lesson 06.

Imagine that a learner, みな, enters a classroom in the morning, studies with a teacher, then stops at a café. These are fictional teaching situations, not a transcript of real speech.

## 1. Greetings and relationships

| Japanese | Meaning | When to use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">あいさつ</span> | greeting | A greeting such as おはようございます or こんにちは. |
| <span lang="ja">おはようございます</span> | good morning (polite) | A polite greeting used in the morning. |
| <span lang="ja">こんにちは</span> | hello / good afternoon | A daytime greeting; write the final particle-like は, not わ. |
| <span lang="ja">ありがとう</span> | thank you (familiar) | Use with people you know well; ありがとうございます is more polite. |
| <span lang="ja">なまえ</span> | name | Use なまえ to identify a person's name; おなまえ is polite in questions. |
| <span lang="ja">せんせい</span> | teacher | A teacher or a title used when addressing a teacher. |
| <span lang="ja">がくせい</span> | student | A student, often in school or university. |

At the classroom door, みな says <span lang="ja">おはようございます。</span> to the teacher. Later, she says <span lang="ja">ありがとうございます。</span> after receiving help. Both are polite expressions, but they serve different purposes: greeting versus thanking.

**Do not equate a dictionary translation with a complete usage rule.** <span lang="ja">ありがとう</span> is familiar, while <span lang="ja">ありがとうございます</span> is a more polite choice. <span lang="ja">こんにちは</span> is normally written with final **は**, despite its *wa* pronunciation. It is not spelled こんにちわ in standard instructional writing.

**Check your recall:** Cover the English meanings. Which word is a teacher? Which is a student? Which expression thanks someone politely? Then reverse the direction: cover the Japanese and write the three answers in kana.

## 2. Objects and a café label

| Japanese | Meaning | Notice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">ほん</span> | book | A physical book; ん is a full mora. |
| <span lang="ja">ノート</span> | notebook | A katakana loanword with the long-vowel mark ー. |
| <span lang="ja">コーヒー</span> | coffee | Katakana with two long-vowel marks. |

A fictional classroom table has a <span lang="ja">ほん</span> and a <span lang="ja">ノート</span>. A nearby café menu lists <span lang="ja">コーヒー</span>. These words belong to different settings, even though you can recognize all three without translating an entire sentence.

**Spelling check:** ノート is not ノト: the mark **ー** extends the preceding vowel. コーヒー has **two** long-vowel marks. The hiragana ん in ほん counts as a mora; do not silently omit it when typing.

## 3. Words for your study routine

| Japanese | Meaning | Notice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">きょう</span> | today | きょ is a contracted sound; う lengthens the vowel. |
| <span lang="ja">ふくしゅう</span> | review / revision | Review previously learned material; しゅ is a contracted sound. |

Use <span lang="ja">きょう</span> (*today*) and <span lang="ja">ふくしゅう</span> (*review*) as labels in your own study notes. In きょう, **きょ** uses a small ょ; in ふくしゅう, **しゅ** uses a small ゅ. Small kana and vowel length are part of the written word, not optional decorations.

### A sustainable three-step correction loop

1. **Notice:** Compare your typed answer with the model and circle the exact difference, such as a missing ー or the wrong kana size.
2. **Repair:** Copy the correct form once while saying or thinking its parts. Then hide it and type it again from memory.
3. **Return:** Put the difficult item into your personal review queue. Revisit it later in a short session rather than repeating it endlessly now.

A practical starting routine is five to ten minutes of review on most study days, adjusting to your energy and recall. Spaced repetition can help schedule later attempts; a single correct response is not proof of lasting mastery.

## 5. Independent practice and self-check

Complete the twelve written questions below. Choose one best answer each time; after submitting, inspect the explanations and retry any missed words. For productive practice, type <span lang="ja">こんにちは・ノート・きょう・ふくしゅう</span> with a Japanese IME, then compare small kana, ー, and final は carefully.

**Review plan:** Select just three difficult items for your next session. For each, record the model form, your earlier error, and the specific repair cue. You will use these habits when reading the chapter's short kana story and hearing its listening review.
