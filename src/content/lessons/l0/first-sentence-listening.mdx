## Can-do: follow simple spoken questions and answers

You are listening at a study table and at a classroom door. One speaker asks about an object; another asks whether a person is a teacher or student. You do not need to recognize printed kana to answer: **listen for the information in the voices**.

These two original dialogues are awaiting approved AivisSpeech generation and waveform/rights checks. The listening quiz should only be used when both real recordings play successfully. Do not use a transcript as a substitute for audio.

## 1. Hear the shape of a question

A polite beginner question often ends in **ですか**. In a statement, **です** gives a polite ending; adding **か** turns it into a question in these examples. A question about identity can sound like **せんせいですか** (“Is [someone] a teacher?”). Listen to the *whole* exchange: the response **はい** (“yes”) or **いいえ** (“no”) changes what you can conclude.

| Heard expression | Meaning | Listening clue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| これは、なんですか。 | What is this? | **なん** asks “what” |
| それは、ほんです。 | That is a book. | **ほん** identifies the object |
| せんせいですか。 | Is [the person] a teacher? | **か** signals a question |
| はい。 | Yes. | Confirms the proposition |
| いいえ。 | No. | Rejects the proposition |

In **たなかさんは、せんせいです**, the topic particle **は** is written *ha* but pronounced *wa*. Listening tests the spoken topic-and-comment pattern, **not** whether you can see the correct character. The particle **を** is useful in later object sentences but cannot be identified as a printed symbol by ear.

## 2. Scene A — at the study table

A learner points to something and asks what it is. The other speaker identifies it. Next, the learner asks about Tanaka's role. On your first play, listen only for **ほん**, **せんせい**, and **はい**. On the second, try to notice which question comes first.

**Guided check:** Is the first answer about a person or an object? Do not reveal the transcript yet.

## 3. Scene B — at the classroom door

Someone asks if an item is a notebook. Then the speakers discuss another person. Listen for **ノート** and the contrast between **がくせい** (student) and **せんせい** (teacher). The final answer starts with **いいえ**; that matters even if you miss one word.

**Common mistake:** hearing **がくせいですか** and concluding that the person *is* a student. It is only the question. The answer, not the question, supplies the fact.

## 4. Listen, decide, and check

Play both clips without showing the transcripts. Answer three questions for each track. Then submit for evidence-based feedback. Revealing a transcript before submitting changes the attempt to **assisted practice** and records it separately from unaided listening. Replay freely; no speed or listening score can certify independent pronunciation proficiency.

## 5. Verify the language after your attempt

After submitting, open each transcript and compare the spoken turns with the English meanings. Notice **これは** (“this,” near the speaker) and **それは** (“that,” near the listener). In the second dialogue **あのひと** refers to “that person” farther away. These are context-sensitive pointing expressions; their English glosses are not interchangeable in every situation.

**Reading and writing follow-up (self-check, not scored as listening):** Type **これはなんですか**, **それはほんです**, and **あのひとはせんせいです** using a Japanese keyboard. Check that the topic particle is written **は**, not **わ**, and that a polite question ends with **か**.

**Optional speaking:** Once audio is available, shadow one short question and one response at your own pace. No microphone starts automatically and no pronunciation rating is given.

## 6. Review and next step

If you missed an object, replay its answer. If you missed a person's role, replay both the **ですか** question and the **はい / いいえ** response. You can return to the previous mini self-introduction reading lesson to strengthen the same noun vocabulary, then continue to guided sentence writing in L07.
