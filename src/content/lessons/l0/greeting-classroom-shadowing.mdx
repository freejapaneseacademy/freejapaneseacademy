## Today's Can-do

**Greet someone appropriately and understand basic study instructions.** This optional speaking lesson rehearses the same two original conversations used for listening in L06. It does not test JLPT speaking, certify pronunciation or require a microphone. First listen to the genuine recordings; if they are not yet available, read the transcript for meaning but **do not claim audio shadowing completed**.

## 1. Arrive at the classroom in the morning

In the first recording a learner greets a teacher by name:

> <span lang="ja">おはようございます。田中先生。</span> — Good morning, Tanaka-sensei.

The polite **ございます** form is suitable for a teacher. The teacher replies:

> <span lang="ja">おはようございます。どうぞ、入ってください。</span> — Good morning. Please come in.

The learner answers <span lang="ja">ありがとうございます。</span> — Thank you. The expression **入ってください** (はいってください) is a polite request to enter. The っ is a small character that marks a brief closure before the following consonant; do not read it as a full independent vowel.

**Shadowing sequence:** listen to the whole track once, pause after the learner's greeting, repeat at a comfortable pace, then listen to the teacher's invitation and practise the thankful response. Do not substitute a made-up voice for the recording.

## 2. Respond to a daytime instruction

In the second recording the teacher says <span lang="ja">こんにちは。みなさん。</span> — Hello, everyone. The class replies <span lang="ja">こんにちは。</span> The teacher then gives a concrete instruction:

> <span lang="ja">本を開いてください。</span> — Please open your book.

本 is read **ほん** (*hon*) in this sentence; no kanji writing mastery is required at Level 0. The action is to **open**, not close, the book. If you missed the instruction, a learner can say:

> <span lang="ja">すみません。もう一度お願いします。</span> — Excuse me. One more time, please.

The teacher replies <span lang="ja">はい。本を開いてください。</span> — Certainly. Please open your book. The phrase **もう一度** (もういちど) means one more time; it is not a greeting. Notice that こんにちは ends with the particle spelling は even though the ending is pronounced *wa*.

## 3. Your two-person rehearsal

Play each complete model using the players below. Then practise the **learner role** in Track A: greet, listen for the invitation, and thank the teacher. Switch roles and say the teacher's line. For Track B, greet the class, give the book-opening instruction, then ask for repetition politely as the learner. Use pauses; clarity and appropriate timing matter more than speed.

The printed cues match the original L06 job scripts. They are not independent edited audio clips, and exact phrase timestamps are not promised. Replay and seek within the full recordings. A slower playback rate is a study aid, not a substitute for listening at natural speed.

## 4. Compare meaning and speaking

Before your optional microphone rehearsal, try the six **written** choices in the workshop. Their score measures contextual phrase selection, **not** pronunciation or listening performance. In your self-review, ask: Did I use the correct time-of-day greeting? Did I distinguish a request from a thank-you? Did I leave a pause for the other speaker? Did I try the polite repair phrase when needed?

You can complete this lesson without granting microphone permission. Any recording is opt-in, stays in your browser tab, and can be discarded. A qualified Japanese-language reviewer has **not** independently assessed these model recordings or your speech.

## 5. Continue

If a phrase remains difficult, revisit L06 for comprehension and L07 for supported writing. The chapter checkpoint combines skills; optional speaking is not a prerequisite for completing it.
