## Optional speaking: listen, pause, repeat

A learner is shopping for a hat, then visiting a friend who offers tea. The two original conversations were already recorded for Lesson 06 with licensed synthetic Japanese voices. Here we **reuse the unchanged WAVs** as speaking models rather than pretending they are new recordings. You may read the transcripts while shadowing; this is not a blind listening exam. You can complete this lesson without a microphone.

You have studied the T row **た・ち・つ・て・と**, N row **な・に・ぬ・ね・の**, and H row **は・ひ・ふ・へ・ほ**. In common romanization, ち is *chi*, つ is *tsu*, and ふ is *fu*. They are each **one kana**, not two syllables. The dialogues also contain other kana and polite expressions, offered as supported extensions rather than new writing requirements.

## Step 1 — A hat shop: shadow a request

Listen to Track A all the way through. Then use the **Play this turn** controls and repeat after the woman:

- <span lang="ja">すみません。ぼうしはありますか。</span> — “Excuse me. Do you have hats?”
- <span lang="ja">あかいぼうしをください。</span> — “The red hat, please.”

The important familiar sound in <span lang="ja">ぼうし</span> is the H-row base sound **ほ** (*ho*), here voiced as **ぼ** (*bo*) with dakuten. The extra voiced mark is a preview, not a new kana-writing target. Listen for the long **ō** sound: <span lang="ja">ぼうし</span> is not just *boshi*. The shopkeeper says there are red and white hats and finishes with <span lang="ja">はい。どうぞ。</span> (“Certainly. Here you are.”)

**Guided practice:** listen, pause, repeat the request once slowly and once at a comfortable conversational pace. Do not imitate an English stressed syllable; follow the model's rhythm. Practise the shorter chunk <span lang="ja">ぼうし</span> before repeating the full sentence.

## Step 2 — Tea with a friend: shadow a polite reply

Listen to Track B. The host asks <span lang="ja">おちゃをのみますか。</span> (“Would you like some tea?”). The guest answers:

- <span lang="ja">はい。あたたかいおちゃをおねがいします。</span> — “Yes. Warm tea, please.”
- <span lang="ja">ありがとうございます。</span> — “Thank you very much.”

In <span lang="ja">おちゃ</span>, the small ゃ combines with ち to form *cha*. This contracted sound is a preview; the goal here is to copy the **recorded** pronunciation rather than deduce it from romanization alone. The guest's word <span lang="ja">あたたかい</span> contains repeated **た** sounds. Listen to the real model, then repeat just that word and the whole request.

## Step 3 — Supported role-play

Imagine you are the customer in the hat shop. Ask for the red hat, pause for the shopkeeper's <span lang="ja">はい。どうぞ。</span>, and answer politely. Then switch to the tea scene and practise asking for warm tea. The recordings provide the original spoken turns and English translations; no synthetic browser speech is generated.

Use the audio players below for full tracks or individual turns. The optional speed selector can help, but slower playback may change sound quality. If playback fails, the written transcript remains an accessibility alternative; do not claim to have heard or imitated the model.

## Step 4 — Your own reflection

Speak aloud without recording, or choose **Start optional recording** and grant microphone permission. A recording is held only in this tab, can be discarded, and is never uploaded or saved as progress. Compare your attempt with the model: Were the target words recognizable? Did you pause between turns? Did you try the full phrase after the shorter chunk? Did you keep the T/N/H sound contrasts clear?
