## Label the library desk

Mika and Ren are preparing a display at a library. One label reads **きって** (postage stamp); another reads **きっぷ** (ticket). Both use a **small っ**. The label **つき** (moon) begins with a **full-sized つ** instead. You are inspecting printed characters and writing, not taking a listening or pronunciation test.

| Label | Meaning | Mora breakdown | What to notice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **きて** | “come” (request form, depending on context) | き・て (2) | no small っ |
| **きって** | postage stamp | き・っ・て (3) | small っ before て |
| **きっぷ** | ticket | き・っ・ぷ (3) | small っ before ぷ |
| **ざっし** | magazine | ざ・っ・し (3) | small っ before し |
| **がっこう** | school | が・っ・こ・う (4) | both small っ and long-o spelling |
| **つき** | moon | つ・き (2) | full-sized つ is a separate syllable |

Small **っ** marks a consonant hold before the next consonant in these examples and occupies **one mora**. It is not pronounced as a separate “tsu.” Full-sized **つ**, as in **つき**, is a regular kana. Compare the printed forms **きて / きって** carefully: adding small っ changes the meaning and the count from two to three morae.

## Long-vowel spelling cards

A long vowel spans two timing units, but its **written form depends on the word**. Do not replace おお with おう or vice versa simply because both often represent long *o*.

| Printed word | Meaning | Written pattern | Morae |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **こうえん** | park | こ + う (おう-pattern long o) | こ・う・え・ん (4) |
| **おおきい** | big | お + お (おお-pattern long o) | お・お・き・い (4) |
| **とおい** | far | と + お (おお-pattern long o) | と・お・い (3) |
| **せんせい** | teacher | せ + い (えい-pattern) | せ・ん・せ・い (4) |
| **とけい** | clock or watch | け + い (えい-pattern) | と・け・い (3) |
| **がっこう** | school | っ plus こ + う | が・っ・こ・う (4) |

The **えい** spelling in words such as **せんせい** often corresponds to a long *e* in ordinary speech, though realization varies by context and speaking style. Here you must recognize the **written sequence**, not infer a sound from a recording.

## Type the labels with a Japanese IME

Switch your keyboard to **Japanese romaji-to-hiragana input**. Common sequences include **kitte → きって**, **kippu → きっぷ**, **zasshi → ざっし**, **gakkou → がっこう**, **kouen → こうえん**, **ookii → おおきい**, **tooi → とおい**, **sensei → せんせい** and **tokei → とけい**. IME behavior varies by operating system and settings: always inspect the resulting kana. Typing two consonant letters (such as **tt** in kitte) commonly creates small っ, but this convention is not a rule about Japanese pronunciation.

Try three stages. **Controlled:** Copy **きって** and **つき** and circle the different-sized kana. **Guided:** Type **がっこう** and **おおきい**, then compare every kana against the table. **Independent:** Without looking, type **こうえん**, **ざっし** and **せんせい**, reveal the table, and correct any mismatches. If an IME is unavailable, copy the words on paper or use the accessible written quiz below.

## Optional tracing and self-check

On paper, trace **っ** beside **つ**, making っ visibly smaller. Copy **きっぷ** and **がっこう**, preserving the small っ; then copy **こうえん** and **おおきい**, checking the exact vowel spelling. Use this self-check rubric: (1) the small っ is clearly smaller than つ; (2) all kana appear in the model's order; (3) おう, おお and えい match the target word; (4) the mora count agrees with the table. This is **optional ungraded handwriting practice**. No camera, stroke recognition, pronunciation or listening assessment is performed.

## A short written message

Mika writes **きってをみてください。** (“Please look at the postage stamp.”) The object particle **を** follows **きって**. Ren writes **こうえんはとおいです。** (“The park is far.”) The particle **は** marks the topic; **こうえん** uses おう, while **とおい** uses おお. The two long-o spellings are not interchangeable. Read and copy both sentences, checking each word's exact spelling.
