## At a community classroom

In this fictional scene, Mina meets a new learner and points to objects on a table. You already know **がくせい** (student), **せんせい** (teacher), **ほん** (book) and **ノート** (notebook). Today the goal is to **connect** those words in meaningful polite sentences, not memorize an English word order.

> ミナ：わたしは ミナです。 — I'm Mina.
>
> リオ：わたしは リオです。がくせいです。 — I'm Rio. I'm a student.
>
> ミナ：これは ノートですか。 — Is this a notebook?
>
> リオ：はい、ノートです。 — Yes, it is a notebook.

These are original teaching examples. The Japanese is polite and suitable for a first meeting. Names are written in katakana here; no kanji mastery is expected.

## 1. A は B です: a topic and a comment

**AはBです。** can identify or describe a topic. The part before **は** is the topic, and **Bです** says something about it. In these beginner examples, B is a noun or noun phrase.

| Japanese | Natural English | Topic | Comment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| わたしは がくせいです。 | I am a student. | わたし | がくせいです |
| あのひとは せんせいです。 | That person over there is a teacher. | あのひと | せんせいです |
| これは ほんです。 | This is a book. | これ | ほんです |
| これは ペンです。 | This is a pen. | これ | ペンです |

**は is written は but pronounced *wa* when used as the topic particle.** The same kana can represent *ha* inside ordinary words, such as はな (flower). The particle marks a topic, not automatically the grammatical subject of every sentence. In a simple identification like these, it is useful to think “as for A, it is B.”

**です** gives a polite sentence ending. It does not literally correspond to a separate English “is” in every possible sentence, and Japanese does not always state an English-style subject.

## 2. Turn a statement into a confirmation question

In this polite noun-sentence pattern, add **か** after **です**.

| Statement | Question |
| --- | --- |
| これは ほんです。 — This is a book. | これは ほんですか。 — Is this a book? |
| あのひとは せんせいです。 — That person is a teacher. | あのひとは せんせいですか。 — Is that person a teacher? |
| リオさんは がくせいです。 — Rio is a student. | リオさんは がくせいですか。 — Is Rio a student? |

The question particle is **at the end**, not between the noun and です. Japanese questions are often written with **。** rather than a question mark, especially in instructional examples. In speech, intonation helps; no audio is assessed here.

**Guided response:** これは ノートですか。 — “Is this a notebook?” If yes, reply **はい、ノートです。** (Yes, it is.) If no, **いいえ、ノートではありません。** (No, it isn't.) The negative form is a preview only; you do not need to produce it in this lesson.

## 3. The spelling preview: は versus を

These two particles are easy to mix up when learning kana.

- **は**, read *wa* as a particle, introduces a topic: **わたしは がくせいです。**
- **を**, generally read *o* as a particle, marks the direct object of many verbs: **ほんを よみます。** — “I read a book.” The verb よみます is only a preview, not a new production target.

**Incorrect:** これは ほんをです。  
**Correct:** これは ほんです。

Why? The first sentence wrongly places an object marker before the polite noun predicate. There is no object-taking verb. Likewise, **これを ほんです** is not a correct substitute for **これは ほんです**. Save を for a later verb lesson.

## 4. Meaning depends on the understood topic

Japanese often omits information that speakers already share. **せんせいです。** can mean “(That person) is a teacher,” “(I am) a teacher,” or another contextually understood identification. Do not force “I” into every translation.

At the table, Rio points to a book and says **ほんです。** The topic is understood from the pointing gesture. Mina might then ask **これは ほんですか。** to confirm it.

**Common misuse:** treating **は** as a universal English “is” or assuming **を** is interchangeable because both have special particle pronunciations. They have different grammatical functions.

## 5. Practice: controlled → guided → independent

**Controlled:** Cover the English translations in the tables. Find the topic in each Japanese sentence and circle **は**. Underline **です**. Add **か** to change one statement into a question.

**Guided:** Imagine a book and a notebook on a classroom table. Write **これは ほんです。** and **これは ノートですか。** Check that the question ends **ですか。** and the topic uses **は**, not **を**.

**Independent:** Write two short original polite statements about a fictional student or classroom object and one yes/no question. You can use **わたし**, **あのひと**, **これ**, **がくせい**, **せんせい**, **ほん**, **ペン**, **ノート**. Compare your sentences with the patterns above. This open-ended writing is self-reviewed, **not automatically graded**.
