## At the shop counter

**Can-do:** Retrieve useful katakana words from fictional shop signs. Ren (レン), the customer, wants a pen and a bag. Aya, the clerk, points to product shelves. This lesson uses **written** signs, not audio.

| Printed label | Reading support | Meaning in the scene |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">ペン</span> | pen | pen; product |
| <span lang="ja">カバン</span> | ka-ban | bag; product |
| <span lang="ja">パン</span> | pan | bread; product |
| <span lang="ja">ソース</span> | sō-su | sauce; product |
| <span lang="ja">コップ</span> | kop-pu | cup; product |
| <span lang="ja">レン</span> | ren | Ren; person's name |

パン was historically borrowed from Portuguese *pão*. Katakana writes borrowed words **and** names; it does not mean every word is English.

## Sound and shape clues

**Dakuten** are two short marks: ハ (ha) becomes バ (ba) in カバン. **Handakuten** is a small circle: ハ becomes パ (pa) in パン; ヘ (he) becomes ペ (pe) in ペン. These marks are part of spelling and change the represented sound.

**ン** (n) ends ペン and カバン. **ソ** (so) begins ソース. They look similar but are different characters. The bar **ー** in ソース lengthens the preceding vowel. Small **ッ** in コップ signals a held consonant. Here these are whole-word reading clues, not separate mastery targets.

## Context matters

A shelf tag **カバン** names a bag. It does not state the price or prove Ren purchased one. A name card **レン** identifies Ren in this story, not a product. The script alone cannot tell you the role of a word.

## Guided retrieval

1. Cover the meanings and locate ペン, パン and カバン. Which has dakuten? Which have handakuten?
2. Compare the final ン in ペン with the first ソ in ソース. Read the entire word before deciding.
3. Read ソース and コップ using the printed supports. Notice ー and small ッ.
4. Imagine a shelf tag that says only カバン. Explain why its price is unknown.
