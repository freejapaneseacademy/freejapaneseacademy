## At the kana-card table

**Chapter Can-do:** Read, write and identify あ–お, か–こ and さ–そ. In this first step, identify the classroom setting, people and purpose, and begin recognizing the written forms. Writing and listening are taught in later lessons.

Learner **Mio** has fifteen hiragana cards. Tutor **Sora** helps her arrange them by sound family. Mio wants to read Japanese labels without relying on Latin letters. This is an original written scene, not recorded audio.

| Speaker | Japanese | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mio | <span lang="ja">これは あ ですか。</span> | Is this *a*? |
| Sora | <span lang="ja">はい、あ です。</span> | Yes, it's *a*. |
| Mio | <span lang="ja">これは し ですか。</span> | Is this *shi*? |
| Sora | <span lang="ja">はい、し です。</span> | Yes, it's *shi*. |

Spaces are beginner reading supports; ordinary Japanese usually has no spaces between words.

## Meet the fifteen cards

| Row | a | i | u | e | o |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vowels | <span lang="ja">あ</span> a | <span lang="ja">い</span> i | <span lang="ja">う</span> u | <span lang="ja">え</span> e | <span lang="ja">お</span> o |
| K | <span lang="ja">か</span> ka | <span lang="ja">き</span> ki | <span lang="ja">く</span> ku | <span lang="ja">け</span> ke | <span lang="ja">こ</span> ko |
| S | <span lang="ja">さ</span> sa | <span lang="ja">し</span> shi | <span lang="ja">す</span> su | <span lang="ja">せ</span> se | <span lang="ja">そ</span> so |

Each kana represents a Japanese mora (a unit of timing), not an English alphabet letter. The five vowel cards are <span lang="ja">あ・い・う・え・お</span>. The next five form the K row, and the final five form the S row.

**Guided rehearsal:** Find <span lang="ja">く</span> in the K row, u column. Find <span lang="ja">せ</span> in the S row, e column. Then point to <span lang="ja">し</span> in the S row, i column. In common English-oriented romanization it is written *shi*, not *si*. The row is still called S.

## First words with familiar cards

<span lang="ja">かさ</span> (*kasa*) means “umbrella”: <span lang="ja">か</span> is in K and <span lang="ja">さ</span> is in S. <span lang="ja">すし</span> (*sushi*) means “sushi”: both characters are in S. These are short meaningful examples, but not every combination of kana makes a Japanese word.

Mio is learning **written recognition**. Sora is the tutor who confirms her choices. They are not translating a kanji dictionary, recording audio or grading pronunciation.

## Common confusions

**Romaji is a reading aid, not the target.** Recognizing *ka* in Latin letters is not the same as recognizing <span lang="ja">か</span>. **し is not い.** The former belongs to the S row and is read *shi*; the latter is the vowel *i*. **Row names are not translations.** The word <span lang="ja">かさ</span> means umbrella, not two separate English words.
