## Can-do: state and recognize basic numbers, names and nationalities

At a fictional community Japanese class, a receptionist checks a registration number and two students introduce themselves. This checkpoint asks you to connect **written forms**, **number expressions** and **what speakers actually say**. It does not assess free conversation or pronunciation.

### 1. Rebuild numbers from parts

Japanese commonly builds two-digit numbers as tens followed by units. <span lang="ja">じゅうに</span> = 10 + 2 = **12**; <span lang="ja">さんじゅうよん</span> = 3 × 10 + 4 = **34**; <span lang="ja">ななじゅう</span> = **70**. The order matters: <span lang="ja">にじゅう</span> means 20, not 12. For zero, <span lang="ja">ゼロ</span> is common; <span lang="ja">れい</span> is also used in some settings. Number readings can vary with context; this checkpoint uses simple standalone numbers.

### 2. Give a polite name or country

| Japanese | Meaning | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">わたしはミカです。</span> | I am Mika. | The topic particle は is written *ha* but pronounced *wa* here. |
| <span lang="ja">カナダです。</span> | (It) is Canada. | The subject must be understood from context; it does **not** by itself mean “I am Canadian.” |
| <span lang="ja">カナダじんです。</span> | (I/they) am/are Canadian. | じん marks nationality in this example. |
| <span lang="ja">にほんじんです。</span> | (I/they) am/are Japanese. | Country にほん + nationality suffix じん. |

<span lang="ja">です</span> is a polite copular ending; it is **not** an all-purpose English “am” that can be attached to any word. In an introduction, <span lang="ja">はじめまして</span> is a conventional opening when meeting someone for the first time. <span lang="ja">よろしくおねがいします</span> is a conventional closing, not a literal statement of nationality.

### 3. Read three original registration records

These records are fictional; numbers are classroom registration identifiers, not ages or phone numbers.

- **Form A:** <span lang="ja">なまえ：たなか　／　ばんごう：12　／　くに：にほん</span>
- **Form B:** <span lang="ja">なまえ：ミカ　／　クラス：3　／　くに：にほん</span>
- **Form C:** <span lang="ja">なまえ：ケン　／　クラス：3　／　くに：カナダ</span>

Read the **field label** before the value. <span lang="ja">ばんごう</span> means number, <span lang="ja">クラス</span> means class, <span lang="ja">なまえ</span> means name, and <span lang="ja">くに</span> means country. A form listing <span lang="ja">くに：カナダ</span> records a country; it does not by itself prove someone's citizenship.

### 4. Listen to two original introduction clips

**Track A — Registration desk:** a clerk asks for a name and number. The visitor says <span lang="ja">たなかです</span> and <span lang="ja">じゅうにです</span>. **Track B — Two classmates:** Ken says he came from Canada, Mika says she came from Japan, and both say they are in class three. The phrase <span lang="ja">カナダからきました</span> describes where Ken came **from**; it does not explicitly establish his nationality.

### 5. Check, type and review

Answer fourteen questions: six number/grammar/script items, four registration-form reading items and four audio-only comprehension items. After submitting, use the feedback and Japanese evidence to correct your errors.

For optional ungraded writing, type <span lang="ja">わたしはミカです。</span> and <span lang="ja">さんじゅうよん</span> with a Japanese IME. Check: (1) the conventional particle は, (2) correct です, (3) the tens-before-units order and (4) readable kana.
