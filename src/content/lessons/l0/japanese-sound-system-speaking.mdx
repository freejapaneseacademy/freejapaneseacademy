## Turn sound knowledge into a conversation

Imagine showing a classmate two photographs. One is of a shop, which they mistake for a house. The other is of a grandmother, whom they mistake for an aunt. You need to correct each mistake politely and clearly. You have already heard these situations in Lesson 06, and you typed their vowel contrasts in Lesson 07. Now your goal is to **say** the important differences.

**Chapter Can-do:** distinguish the five Japanese vowel qualities and the timing of morae. **Today you are practising production**: imitate a meaningful recorded line, hear your own timing, and improve it. You are not taking an official JLPT speaking examination, and a multiple-choice preparation score cannot prove pronunciation quality. You can complete this optional lesson without a microphone.

The workshop uses two **real, original Japanese conversations**, already published in Chapter 02 Lesson 06. They were generated with the approved AivisSpeech Japanese voices (male Aida Shigeru Calm and female morioki Normal), with documented model attribution. No new voice or fake audio is being substituted. Automated checks validate the media files and scripts, but **independent human pronunciation review remains pending**. Treat a sound as a learning model, not a certified accent reference.

## 1. Find five steady vowels

Japanese has five basic vowel categories. Use these as a rough starting point; descriptions using English words are approximations, not exact matches.

| Vowel | Hiragana | Useful articulation cue | What to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| a | <span lang="ja">あ</span> | Open mouth and relax the jaw, keeping the vowel steady. | Turning it into the moving vowel of some English pronunciations of *eye*. |
| i | <span lang="ja">い</span> | Keep the tongue high and forward; hold a steady sound. | Pronouncing the English letter name *I*. |
| u | <span lang="ja">う</span> | Tongue high and toward the back; lips are not as rounded as an English *oo*. | Exaggerated lip rounding, or replacing it with an English letter name. |
| e | <span lang="ja">え</span> | Mid-front, without an added off-glide. | Turning it into an English letter-name *A*. |
| o | <span lang="ja">お</span> | Rounded, steady mid-back vowel. | Adding the glide common in English *go*. |

**First speaking warm-up:** try <span lang="ja">あ・い・う・え・お</span>, one short beat each, then repeat softly while noticing whether the vowel stays stable. This sequence is **your own articulation exercise**; it is not represented as an isolated recording in the two conversation tracks. If the movement feels forced, relax and go slower rather than speaking louder.

A **mora** is a rhythmic timing unit of Japanese. It is not the same thing as an English syllable or a written Latin letter. As a beginner, you can tap once per ordinary kana and once more for the extra vowel in the length contrasts below:

| Expression | Meaning here | Mora count | Tap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">いえ</span> (*ie*) | house | 2 | い · え |
| <span lang="ja">いいえ</span> (*iie*) | no | 3 | い · い · え |
| <span lang="ja">おばさん</span> (*obasan*) | aunt | 4 | お · ば · さ · ん |
| <span lang="ja">おばあさん</span> (*obaasan*) | grandmother | 5 | お · ば · あ · さ · ん |

The small difference between these pairs can change the intended meaning. In this lesson's family-photo context, <span lang="ja">おばさん</span> means *aunt* and <span lang="ja">おばあさん</span> means *grandmother*. In other contexts they may also be used as age-related forms of address. Keep the extra vowel length clear **without artificially separating the word into isolated, equally stressed English syllables**.

## 2. The model conversations: what you can actually hear

The two recordings below are identical to the licensed Chapter 02 Lesson 06 WAVs. You can first play the **whole** track to understand the exchange, then play the short segments in the workshop to practise a particular turn. The workshop uses actual turn start/end timings stored in the versioned media manifest; those times are not invented.

### Track A — Is this a house?

A man asks whether a photograph shows a house. A woman says no; it is a shop. The initial short word <span lang="ja">家</span> is pronounced <span lang="ja">いえ</span>, while the corrective <span lang="ja">いいえ</span> has an extra *i* mora:

> <span lang="ja">男性：これ、家の写真ですか。</span>
>
> Man: “Is this a photo of a house?”
>
> <span lang="ja">女性：いいえ、これはお店の写真です。</span>
>
> Woman: “No, this is a photo of a shop.”

Read the meaning before practising. A helpful two-pass drill is: listen once and identify the contrast; then repeat the woman's <span lang="ja">いいえ</span> at a comfortable speed. Keep the longer *i* clear but do not insert a complete silence between its two timing units. The particle <span lang="ja">は</span> is pronounced *wa* in <span lang="ja">これは</span>; English letter names are not good pronunciation models.

### Track B — Who is in the photograph?

A woman asks whether a person in a photo is the man's aunt. The man replies that she is his grandmother:

> <span lang="ja">女性：この写真の人は、あなたのおばさんですか。</span>
>
> Woman: “Is the person in this photo your aunt?”
>
> <span lang="ja">男性：いいえ、私のおばあさんです。</span>
>
> Man: “No, she's my grandmother.”

The key difference in this setting is the held *a* in <span lang="ja">おばあさん</span>. If you shorten it, the family relationship may be misunderstood. Also notice <span lang="ja">ん</span> in both words: it has its own mora, even when it is not followed by a vowel.

## 3. Shadowing is listen → pause → imitate → compare

Try a practical three-pass routine on each track:

1. **Understand first.** Play the complete audio at normal speed; follow the English situation summary without rushing to speak.
2. **Isolate a turn.** Use the **Play this turn** buttons to listen to a recorded utterance, including the actual question and correction. If needed, use 0.85× or 0.95× playback for a first attempt; return to 1× for natural pacing.
3. **Shadow it.** After hearing the full turn, speak aloud once. Aim for a steady vowel, a clear extra mora where needed, and natural phrase boundaries. Replay the turn and try again.
4. **Compare meaning.** Did you say the expression for *house* or *no*? Did the family-photo correction refer to *aunt* or *grandmother*? Real communication depends on that meaning.
5. **Self-review honestly.** Tick a criterion only if you think you met it. The checkboxes are **not** voice recognition and do not grade your accent. You may record locally if you wish, but that is never required.

**If your microphone is unavailable or permission is denied:** speak aloud and use the written self-review instead. You can still complete the lesson. The academy does not need to receive a recording to help you practise, and this workshop does not upload, store, or send one.

## 4. How to practise the complete interaction

In a classroom role-play, imagine you are answering the first question as the woman:

- Listen to the man's line, then reply: <span lang="ja">いいえ、これはお店の写真です。</span> — “No, this is a photo of a shop.”
- Listen to the family-photo question, then reply as the man: <span lang="ja">いいえ、私のおばあさんです。</span> — “No, she's my grandmother.”
- Work on only **one correction** at a time. The words around the contrast should be understandable; avoid isolating the vowel so much that you lose the situation.
- If you practise from the displayed script, use it as support. Sound production is the skill today, not how fast you read Japanese characters.

**Common traps:** English-like stress on just one syllable; turning a long vowel into two separately stressed English vowels; confusing a long vowel with a whole-word pause; assuming that a written long-vowel contrast automatically guarantees you pronounced it correctly; treating a microphone recording or theory quiz as an automatic pronunciation assessment.

The five short preparation questions in the workshop assess **knowledge about meaning, models and mora timing**. They can be scored in your browser, but they are **not** an automated test of your voice.
