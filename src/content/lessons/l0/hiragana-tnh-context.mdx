## A second table of kana cards

**Chapter Can-do:** Read, write and distinguish the hiragana in the T, N and H rows. **This first lesson** introduces the setting, roles and purpose. Later lessons teach detailed sound contrasts, handwriting and listening.

In a beginner classroom, learner **Aki** joins tutor **Ren** at a table. Aki already knows the vowel, K and S rows. Ren has prepared fifteen new hiragana cards and a chart with five vowel columns. Aki's job is to **recognize and sort the printed symbols**, not to guess a word's meaning from its shape.

| Person | Role | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aki | Learner | Points to a card, checks its row and column |
| Ren | Tutor | Names the row and confirms the choice |

This is an original **written classroom scenario**, not a recording. In a short supported exchange, Aki asks <span lang="ja">これは た ですか。</span> (*Kore wa ta desu ka?* — “Is this *ta*?”). Ren replies <span lang="ja">はい、た です。</span> (*Hai, ta desu.* — “Yes, it's *ta*.”). The spaces are beginner reading supports; ordinary Japanese does not normally insert spaces like this.

## Meet the fifteen new cards

| Sound column | a | i | u | e | o |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T row | <span lang="ja">た</span> ta | <span lang="ja">ち</span> chi | <span lang="ja">つ</span> tsu | <span lang="ja">て</span> te | <span lang="ja">と</span> to |
| N row | <span lang="ja">な</span> na | <span lang="ja">に</span> ni | <span lang="ja">ぬ</span> nu | <span lang="ja">ね</span> ne | <span lang="ja">の</span> no |
| H row | <span lang="ja">は</span> ha | <span lang="ja">ひ</span> hi | <span lang="ja">ふ</span> fu | <span lang="ja">へ</span> he | <span lang="ja">ほ</span> ho |

Read across each row from the a column to the o column. These symbols are hiragana; they are not Latin alphabet letters. The chart organizes **sounds and written forms**, not translations.

**Guided check:** Find <span lang="ja">ね</span> at N + e, <span lang="ja">と</span> at T + o, and <span lang="ja">ふ</span> at H + u. Point to each before looking at its romanization. Try locating <span lang="ja">ち</span> without using its Latin-letter cue.

## Why the T and H rows look unusual in romaji

In common English-oriented romanization, <span lang="ja">ち</span> is **chi**, <span lang="ja">つ</span> is **tsu**, and <span lang="ja">ふ</span> is **fu**. These are still the i and u positions in the T row, and the u position in the H row. Some other romanization systems write these sounds differently. Do not assume that *ti*, *tu* or *hu* are the standard pronunciation cues used in this course.

These are **not** three extra kana: they are part of the fifteen-card chart. Their actual pronunciation and listening distinctions are taught with genuine audio in a later lesson; a printed spelling is not a sound recording.

## Recognize context, not just shapes

Ren shows Aki a label with <span lang="ja">た</span> (*ta*) and asks Aki to locate it in the T row. Aki also sees <span lang="ja">はな</span> (*hana*, “flower” or “nose” depending on context). The word combines <span lang="ja">は</span> (H row) and <span lang="ja">な</span> (N row). Without context, the same kana spelling can have different meanings; a kana card alone does not tell you which one is intended.

Aki's immediate goal is to sort **written kana**, not to translate a whole sentence, assess pronunciation, or master stroke order. Ren's job is to guide and check the sorting task.

## Avoid three beginner mistakes

**Do not confuse the row with its English meaning.** The T row is a sound grouping, not a word meaning “T.” **Do not mistake the exception spellings for additional characters.** <span lang="ja">つ</span> is in the T row even though the cue is *tsu*. **Do not assume all kana combinations form words.** The fifteen characters can be combined, but meaning requires real vocabulary and context.
