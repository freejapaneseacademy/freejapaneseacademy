## Can-do: greet and follow simple classroom instructions

In this chapter, you learn to **greet someone appropriately and understand basic study instructions**. This vocabulary lesson gives you expressions to recognize and retrieve. The later listening lesson will supply actual recorded conversation; the written examples here are not audio.

### A small set of high-value phrases

| Japanese | Reading support | Meaning in context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">おはよう</span> | ohayō | Morning greeting, casual |
| <span lang="ja">おはようございます</span> | ohayō gozaimasu | Morning greeting, polite |
| <span lang="ja">こんにちは</span> | konnichiwa | Daytime greeting |
| <span lang="ja">ありがとう</span> | arigatō | Thanks, casual |
| <span lang="ja">ありがとうございます</span> | arigatō gozaimasu | Thank you, polite |
| <span lang="ja">すみません</span> | sumimasen | Excuse me / sorry / getting attention, depending on context |
| <span lang="ja">おねがいします</span> | onegai shimasu | Polite request formula, not a literal substitute for every “please” |

The long vowel in **おはよう** and **ありがとう** matters: the final よう and とう are held longer than a short vowel. Romanization is only a reading aid. In **こんにちは**, the final は is pronounced *wa*: this is the conventional spelling of the greeting. Do not write こんにちわ in standard beginner writing.

### Classroom action words

A teacher can say the following phrases. They are **requests** rather than labels or greetings.

| Japanese | Reading support | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">みてください。</span> | mite kudasai | Look, please |
| <span lang="ja">きいてください。</span> | kiite kudasai | Listen, please |
| <span lang="ja">よんでください。</span> | yonde kudasai | Read, please |
| <span lang="ja">かいてください。</span> | kaite kudasai | Write, please |
| <span lang="ja">いってください。</span> | itte kudasai | Say it, please |

**ください** makes these verb forms into polite requests. You do not need to conjugate the verbs yet. Notice the distinct actions: **みる** (look), **きく** (listen/ask), **よむ** (read), **かく** (write), and **いう** (say). The forms in the table are useful chunks to memorize. Do not assume that ください can simply be attached to any dictionary-form verb.

### Original classroom scene

At a fictional language school, teacher 田中先生 welcomes new student ミナ at 9 a.m.:

> ミナ：おはようございます。  
> 田中先生：おはようございます。みてください。  
> 田中先生：つぎに、よんでください。  
> ミナ：はい。ありがとうございます。

*Meaning:* Mina greets the teacher politely. The teacher asks her to look, then to read. Mina acknowledges and thanks the teacher. **はい** means yes / understood here; it is not a greeting. **つぎに** means next.

At midday Mina meets a friend:

> ミナ：こんにちは。  
> 友だち：こんにちは。  
> ミナ：すみません。もういちど おねがいします。

The final line can mean “Excuse me. Once more, please.” **もういちど** means once more. It is a useful repair request when you miss something, not an expression of thanks.

## Choose meaning by purpose and relationship

A phrase may have more than one natural translation. **すみません** can attract attention, apologize, or express a kind of gratitude for someone's trouble. It does not always mean the speaker committed a serious mistake. **おねがいします** is a conventional polite request, often used when asking someone to do something or handing something over for attention. The situation supplies its meaning.

**Common misuse:** saying ありがとうございます when you first see someone does not mean “hello.” It means “thank you.” Similarly, **きいてください** asks for listening, whereas **いってください** asks for speaking. The small spelling differences represent different actions.

## Retrieval routine: look, cover, recall, check

1. Read the Japanese phrase and identify its function: greeting, thanks, or instruction.
2. Cover the English column and retrieve the action for みて, きいて, よんで, かいて, and いって.
3. In a notebook, write two original micro-lines: one greeting to a teacher, one polite request for repetition. Compare with おはようございます and もういちど おねがいします. This is optional self-checked writing, not a handwriting score.
4. Optionally say the phrases aloud slowly, keeping long vowels intact. No pronunciation assessment or authentic recording is provided in this written lesson.
