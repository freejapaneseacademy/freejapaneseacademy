## Read the word before you sort the picture

Mika and Ren are preparing another **picture-card sorting game**. Mika has printed ten Japanese labels; Ren reads them and places each one beside a matching drawing. Today the focus is on **whole-word recognition**: a small mark on a kana can change which word you see. You can use the reading cues at first, then cover them to practise retrieval. This is written vocabulary study; pronunciation and listening discrimination will be tested later with real recordings.

A **dakuten** looks like two short strokes, as in <span lang="ja">か → が</span> (*ka → ga*) and <span lang="ja">は → ば</span> (*ha → ba*). A **handakuten** looks like a small circle, as in <span lang="ja">は → ぱ</span> (*ha → pa*). The mark belongs to the kana, not to the entire word.

## Notice the mark and retrieve the meaning

The voiced sound may appear at the beginning, middle, or end of a word. In <span lang="ja">かぎ</span>, the second kana <span lang="ja">ぎ</span> carries dakuten. In <span lang="ja">みず</span>, <span lang="ja">ず</span> carries dakuten. In <span lang="ja">ぶた</span>, the first kana <span lang="ja">ぶ</span> carries dakuten. In <span lang="ja">ぱん</span>, the first kana <span lang="ja">ぱ</span> carries the small-circle handakuten. The mark does **not** tell you the meaning by itself: you still need the whole word and the picture clue.

A useful contrast is <span lang="ja">かぎ</span> (*key*) versus <span lang="ja">かき</span> (*persimmon* or *oyster*, depending on context). The second kana is different, so the words are different. Do not guess the meaning of an unfamiliar word solely from the dots.

## Labels versus classroom instructions

Mika writes <span lang="ja">かぎ</span> under a picture of a key. This **label** names an object. Ren then writes <span lang="ja">かぎをみてください。</span> — “Please look at the key.” This is an **instruction**: the word <span lang="ja">かぎ</span> names the object to look at, while <span lang="ja">をみてください</span> supplies the request. The particle <span lang="ja">を</span> marks the object here.

A second instruction is <span lang="ja">ぼうしをみてください。</span> — “Please look at the hat.” Ren also asks <span lang="ja">ぱんはどれですか。</span> — “Which one is the bread?” Here <span lang="ja">は</span> marks the topic and is pronounced *wa* as a particle. The spelling <span lang="ja">ぱん</span> remains a practice spelling; a real shop sign would ordinarily use <span lang="ja">パン</span>.

The spaces on the left are a learning aid, not ordinary Japanese punctuation. A word's dictionary meaning and its **function in a sentence** are related but not identical.
