## A new kind of picture-card challenge

**Chapter Can-do:** Read and write voiced hiragana (が, ざ, だ, ば rows) and the p-series (ぱ row). You already know the basic 46 hiragana. In this opening *written context* lesson, you will identify why the new marks matter; later lessons will teach the complete rows and their sound contrasts. This is not a listening or pronunciation test.

At an after-school reading club, learner **Mika** and tutor **Ren** prepare a sorting game for classmates. Mika prints pairs of kana cards. Ren checks the marks before the cards are placed next to picture clues. Classmates must match each printed word with the right picture. The game is about **reading changed kana accurately**, not choosing an English spelling.

| Person | Role | What they do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mika | Learner | Prints kana and word cards |
| Ren | Tutor | Checks each mark and picture clue |
| Classmates | Readers | Sort the finished cards into matching pairs |

## Two marks that matter

A **dakuten** is the pair of small strokes at the upper right: <span lang="ja">か → が</span> (*ka → ga*). The same mark appears in <span lang="ja">さ → ざ</span> (*sa → za*), <span lang="ja">た → だ</span> (*ta → da*), and <span lang="ja">は → ば</span> (*ha → ba*). These are *examples*, not yet a demand to memorize every member of the rows.

A **handakuten** is the small circle: <span lang="ja">は → ぱ</span> (*ha → pa*). The circle is used on the h-row to form the p-series. Compare <span lang="ja">は</span> (*ha*), <span lang="ja">ば</span> (*ba*) and <span lang="ja">ぱ</span> (*pa*). The underlying character shape is similar, but the mark changes the sound. Do not confuse the two dots with the circle.

## The first word cards

Mika's board uses short, concrete labels. The words below are natural Japanese nouns, written in hiragana for beginning readers.

| Printed card | Reading cue | Picture clue | Important detail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">かぎ</span> | kagi | key | <span lang="ja">ぎ</span> has dakuten |
| <span lang="ja">かさ</span> | kasa | umbrella | <span lang="ja">さ</span> has no mark |
| <span lang="ja">ぱん</span> | pan | bread | <span lang="ja">ぱ</span> has handakuten |
| <span lang="ja">ばす</span> | basu | bus | <span lang="ja">ば</span> has dakuten |

The word <span lang="ja">ぱん</span> is normally written パン in everyday Japanese because it is a loanword. The hiragana <span lang="ja">ぱん</span> here is **intentional sound-and-script practice**, not a claim about standard everyday spelling. Similarly, ばす is commonly バス. A word card can help you study sounds without replacing the usual written form.

## Guided practice: look closely

**First:** Find the two dots in <span lang="ja">ぎ</span> and <span lang="ja">ば</span>. **Next:** Find the small circle in <span lang="ja">ぱ</span>. **Then:** Compare <span lang="ja">は・ば・ぱ</span> without relying on romanization. **Finally:** Say which person printed the cards and which person checks them.

Ren notices that one card reads <span lang="ja">かぎ</span> (“key”), while another reads <span lang="ja">かき</span> (“persimmon” or “oyster,” depending on context). The difference between <span lang="ja">ぎ</span> and <span lang="ja">き</span> is not decoration: the dakuten changes the reading, and these are different words. The picture clue resolves which meaning is intended. You cannot determine every word's meaning from a kana mark alone.

## Check your understanding

Answer eight written questions about the sorting game, its participants, the marks and the picture clues. Feedback explains each answer. Your local score is a *written recognition* result, not a measurement of spoken accuracy or an official exam score.
