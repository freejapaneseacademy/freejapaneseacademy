## Write labels for a fictional shop

**Can-do:** Complete basic katakana plus voiced sound families. A shop worker is preparing a small display with bags, pens and bread. Your job is to type the labels clearly enough that another learner can recognize them. All names and situations here are invented; do not enter personal details.

This is a **written** lesson. It does not assess listening, handwriting accuracy or pronunciation. Use a Japanese IME: type the romanized syllables, then select the **katakana** conversion. On mobile, choose the katakana keyboard or convert from hiragana. Confirm the displayed characters before submitting.

## Observe: the marks carry meaning

| Word | Meaning | What to inspect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">パン</span> | bread | パ has a **small circle** (handakuten), final ン is *n* |
| <span lang="ja">カバン</span> | bag | バ has **two short marks** (dakuten), final ン |
| <span lang="ja">ペン</span> | pen | ペ uses the circle; ベ would be *be* |
| <span lang="ja">ソース</span> | sauce | initial ソ is *so*, ー extends the preceding vowel |
| <span lang="ja">リンゴ</span> | apple | ン is *n*, ゴ uses dakuten |
| <span lang="ja">ラジオ</span> | radio | ジ uses dakuten, not シ |
| <span lang="ja">ピアノ</span> | piano | ピ uses the small circle |
| <span lang="ja">パンダ</span> | panda | パ uses the circle, ダ uses dakuten |

The long vowel bar ー is not a dakuten. The similar-looking ン (*n*) and ソ (*so*) are different characters. Compare them side by side: <span lang="ja">ン　ソ</span>. Inspect the whole form and the known word, not just a single stroke.

## Recombine and repair

A learner wrote <span lang="ja">カパン</span> for *kaban* (bag). Compare the model <span lang="ja">カバン</span>: change **パ** to **バ** because *ba* requires dakuten. Another learner wrote <span lang="ja">ンース</span> for *sōsu* (sauce). Replace the first character with **ソ** to write <span lang="ja">ソース</span>. These are purposeful spelling corrections, not interchangeable variants.

For a writing warm-up, copy <span lang="ja">パ・バ・ピ・ビ・ペ・ベ</span> on paper or type them with your IME. Optional handwriting is self-observation only; no handwriting score is generated.

## Eight guided typing checks

Type the exact **katakana** word requested in each question. Leading/trailing spaces and Unicode composition differences are normalized; other spellings are not automatically accepted in these constrained script-conversion checks. The exercise reveals the model answer and a reason after submission. It is not a teacher grade. You can retry after reviewing.

## Your own two-line shop note

Imagine you are labeling two shelves in a **fictional** shop. Type two lines such as:

> <span lang="ja">パン　ペン</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">カバン　ソース</span>

You may choose any two or more of the taught words, in any order, and separate labels with spaces or Japanese punctuation. These are **labels**, not full grammatical sentences. Compare your draft to the models and use the self-review checklist in the activity. Your creative note is **not automatically graded**; the eight constrained typing items have a separate written score.

## Self-review criteria

1. **Script:** Each chosen word is written in katakana rather than Latin letters or hiragana.
2. **Marks:** Dakuten (゛) and handakuten (゜) appear on the correct characters.
3. **Shape:** The intended ン or ソ is legible and matches the chosen word.
4. **Clarity:** The two lines are separated and another reader could identify the labels.

If a criterion is not met, revise your note. You can keep your draft in this browser only; nothing is sent to a teacher or external grading service. Independent Japanese-language and assessment reviews of this lesson are pending.
