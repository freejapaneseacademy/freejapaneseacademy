## Aki's classroom label workshop

Aki is preparing new **hiragana-only** cards for Ren's classroom. Your task is to write the fifteen T/N/H kana, combine familiar characters into short words, then prepare two labels. You can type with a Japanese IME or use a kana/flick keyboard. Optional paper handwriting is self-reviewed, never automatically graded.

## Observe the complete model chart

| Vowel | T | N | H |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| a | **た** ta | **な** na | **は** ha |
| i | **ち** chi | **に** ni | **ひ** hi |
| u | **つ** tsu | **ぬ** nu | **ふ** fu |
| e | **て** te | **ね** ne | **へ** he |
| o | **と** to | **の** no | **ほ** ho |

*Chi*, *tsu* and *fu* are conventional romanizations of **one character each**. Some romaji IMEs also accept `ti`, `tu` or `hu`; only the final Japanese-script character is assessed.

## Guided production ladder

**Observe:** Read each row aloud if comfortable, but this lesson does not assess speech. **Copy:** Type <span lang="ja">たちつてと</span>, then <span lang="ja">なにぬねの</span>, then <span lang="ja">はひふへほ</span>. **Contrast:** Type <span lang="ja">ぬねぬね</span> and <span lang="ja">はほはほ</span> while looking at the model. **Produce:** Cover the chart and type the requested character from its reading cue.

<span lang="ja">ぬ</span> (*nu*) has a lower-right loop; <span lang="ja">ね</span> (*ne*) has a more upright ending. <span lang="ja">ほ</span> (*ho*) has an extra short horizontal line compared with <span lang="ja">は</span> (*ha*). These are visual comparisons, **not stroke-order diagrams**. Printed fonts and handwriting can differ.

## Recombine characters into useful words

| Meaning on the classroom card | Japanese model | Character breakdown |
| --- | --- | --- |
| summer | <span lang="ja">なつ</span> | な + つ |
| boat | <span lang="ja">ふね</span> | ふ + ね |
| star | <span lang="ja">ほし</span> | ほ + し |
| cat | <span lang="ja">ねこ</span> | ね + こ |
| person | <span lang="ja">ひと</span> | ひ + と |

The K and S characters here were introduced earlier. A single kana is a sound symbol, not an English meaning. For example <span lang="ja">ふね</span> means boat, while <span lang="ja">ほね</span> means bone: confusing ふ and ほ changes the word.

**Model:** For “summer,” combine <span lang="ja">な</span> (*na*) and <span lang="ja">つ</span> (*tsu*) into <span lang="ja">なつ</span>. Do not add a space. For “star,” write <span lang="ja">ほし</span> with ほ (*ho*), not は (*ha*).

## Your own short classroom note

Imagine Ren needs two picture labels: **boat** and **star**, one per line. Compose these independently in the optional draft boxes below. Reveal the models after writing and revise any confusing shapes. You can instead copy the two labels on paper and compare the result. A typed alternative is fully equivalent for this activity.

**Self-review criteria:** Did you write both intended labels? Did you use Japanese script rather than Latin letters? Are the characters in the correct order? Are ふ/ほ and ぬ/ね distinguishable? Are the written shapes legible? These are learner judgments, not automated handwriting marks.

## Independent check and privacy

Complete the twenty exact-answer writing prompts. Each is worth one point, with a stable ID, model and explanation. Only NFC Unicode normalization and surrounding whitespace are ignored. Romaji is not silently converted into a correct answer. The two open drafts are **ungraded**, because free composition and handwriting cannot be evaluated reliably by simple string matching.
