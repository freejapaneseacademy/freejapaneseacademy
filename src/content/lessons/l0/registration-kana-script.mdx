## Can-do: read and type the basic information on a registration slip

You can identify a **name**, **country** and **number** written in kana, and type a short answer for a fictional learner. This is a **written script lesson**, not a test of listening, real identity, or handwriting accuracy. Previous lessons introduced the meanings and the starter frame **です**; now focus on the actual kana shapes.

### Original registration slip

The classroom helper has made this fictional practice card. The names and details are invented; do not enter your own personal information.

| Form field | Written example | Reading support | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| なまえ | エマ | *namae / Ema* | Name: Emma |
| くに | アメリカ | *kuni / Amerika* | Country: America / United States |
| ばんごう | にじゅうさん | *bangō / nijūsan* | Number: 23 |
| ひとこと | エマです。 | *Ema desu* | I am Emma. |

**Do not confuse a country with nationality:** アメリカ is a country name. A nationality expression such as アメリカじん includes じん, meaning “person from” in this context. A country on a form does not tell us anything else about the person.

## Notice three writing patterns

**1. Small ゃ and ゅ make combined sounds.** Compare <span lang="ja">ひゃく</span> (100, *hyaku*) with <span lang="ja">ひやく</span> (*hiyaku*, a different word). The first uses **small ゃ**. Compare <span lang="ja">じゅう</span> (10, *jū*) with the incorrectly enlarged **じゆう** when writing the number. In <span lang="ja">にじゅう</span> (20) and <span lang="ja">さんじゅう</span> (30), keep the small ゅ.

**2. Dakuten changes the sound.** The two small marks in **ば** and **ご** are important in <span lang="ja">ばんごう</span> (number). <span lang="ja">はんこう</span> is not the target word. The ending **ごう** includes う, representing a long vowel in this word.

**3. Names and country names often use katakana.** <span lang="ja">エマ</span> is a fictional person's name and <span lang="ja">アメリカ</span> is a country name. These katakana spellings are useful in a form even though the field labels are hiragana. The labels <span lang="ja">なまえ</span> and <span lang="ja">くに</span> are written in hiragana here for Level 0 support; real forms may use kanji such as 名前 and 国. No kanji writing is required.

## Read a small number chart

| Value | Kana | Construction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | ゼロ / れい | Two common readings; context determines choice |
| 9 | きゅう | Small ゅ |
| 10 | じゅう | Small ゅ + う |
| 20 | にじゅう | Two tens |
| 23 | にじゅうさん | Two tens plus three |
| 30 | さんじゅう | Three tens |
| 40 | よんじゅう | Four tens |
| 100 | ひゃく | Small ゃ |

**Note:** A form may display numerals such as 23 rather than spelling every number in kana. Reading **にじゅうさん** helps you decode that number in written beginner exercises. Numbers with different conventional readings, such as 4 and 7, will receive more context in later practice.

## Controlled → guided → independent practice

1. **Notice:** point to each small ゅ in じゅう, にじゅう, and さんじゅう. Compare with full-size ゆ. Then find the two voiced kana in ばんごう.
2. **Copy or type:** use a Japanese IME to enter <span lang="ja">なまえ</span>, <span lang="ja">くに</span>, <span lang="ja">ばんごう</span>, <span lang="ja">エマ</span>, and <span lang="ja">アメリカ</span>. If your IME suggests kanji, keep kana for this task. Optional paper copying is ungraded; no stroke-order or handwriting correctness is claimed.
3. **Fill the fictional form:** write <span lang="ja">なまえ：エマ</span>, <span lang="ja">くに：アメリカ</span>, <span lang="ja">ばんごう：にじゅうさん</span>. Finish with <span lang="ja">エマです。</span>. Check each spelling against the model.
4. **Optional oral rehearsal:** silently read or say <span lang="ja">エマです。</span>. This is not scored pronunciation practice and has no audio or speech recognition.

## Common mistakes

Do not make **じゅ** into **じゆ** or **ひゃ** into **ひや**. Do not omit う in **じゅう** or **ばんごう**. Do not assume that a country's name and a nationality expression are interchangeable. In a simple polite introduction, **エマです。** is a statement; **エマか。** is not an equivalent introduction.
