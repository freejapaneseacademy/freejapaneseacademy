## Can-do: hear greetings and follow simple study instructions

Imagine a Japanese beginner class. First, a learner arrives in the morning and the teacher invites them inside. Later, a teacher gives an instruction to the whole class, and a learner asks to hear it again. Your goal is to **understand the speech before reading it**, not to recognize printed kana.

This listening lesson uses two *original* short conversations. The recordings must be produced from the approved licensed AivisSpeech voices and validated before this lesson is published. If the audio is unavailable, do not guess answers from the transcript; return after audio generation.

## 1. Prepare your ears

Listen for these three functions:

| Function | What you might hear | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Greeting | おはようございます | Good morning (polite) |
| Request | 〜てください | Please do something |
| Gratitude | ありがとうございます | Thank you |

The final **ございます** can help you recognize a polite greeting or thanks. But it does not tell you *which* one without the preceding word. Notice **おはよう** versus **ありがとう**. In **入ってください** (はいってください), the teacher asks someone to enter; in **開いてください** (ひらいてください), someone is asked to open something. They are different verbs despite their similar endings.

## 2. Conversation A — at the classroom door

A learner greets a teacher in the morning. Listen once for the situation and the tone; replay to identify the teacher's request. Then answer three questions without opening the transcript. A useful clue is the teacher's **どうぞ**, a courteous invitation.

## 3. Conversation B — after class begins

In the second conversation the teacher greets everyone and gives an instruction involving **本** (ほん, book). One learner says **すみません** to politely get attention and asks **もう一度お願いします** (もういちどおねがいします), “One more time, please.” This is a useful survival expression when you do not understand an instruction the first time.

Do not confuse **本を開いてください** (“Please open the book”) with **本を読んでください** (“Please read the book”). Both are plausible classroom directions, but only one is actually spoken in this recording.

## 4. Listen, decide, verify

Use the audio players and the six questions below. For an *unaided* attempt, keep the transcript hidden until you submit. Revealing the transcript first is useful for supported practice, but that attempt is recorded separately and should not be interpreted as unaided listening proficiency. The explanation quotes the decisive words only after you check your answer.

**Pronunciation rehearsal (optional):** after listening, repeat **おはようございます**, **入ってください**, and **もう一度お願いします** aloud at a comfortable pace. No microphone is activated and no pronunciation score is awarded.

## 5. After listening: read and type

Once you have checked the answers, type **こんにちは** and **もういちどおねがいします** with a Japanese keyboard. Compare the greeting's final **は** with its spoken *wa* sound. The word **いちど** is written with ち, not じ. This typing exercise is self-check only; it does not inflate your listening score.

**Review:** If you missed the greeting, replay the first utterance. If you missed the instruction, listen for the verb immediately before **ください**. If you missed the request for repetition, listen for **もう一度**. Return to the audio and try again before reading the evidence.
