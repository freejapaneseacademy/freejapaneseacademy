## Arriving at a station café

**Chapter Can-do:** Read everyday katakana loanwords, including the long-vowel bar and borrowed sound patterns. This first lesson asks *where are we, who is involved, and why are they reading?* Detailed spelling rules come in Lesson 03; original announcement listening comes in Lesson 06.

At a fictional station café, **Hana** is a traveler waiting for a train. **Sora** is the café clerk. Hana wants a drink and a light snack before boarding. This is an original teaching scene, not a real menu, advertisement or audio transcript.

| Person | Role | Immediate purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hana | Traveler/customer | Choose a drink and snack before a train |
| Sora | Café clerk | Help customers read the menu |

### The café's printed card

| Japanese label | Reading hint | Meaning in this scene |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">コーヒー</span> | kōhī | coffee |
| <span lang="ja">ティー</span> | tī | tea |
| <span lang="ja">ケーキ</span> | kēki | cake |
| <span lang="ja">ファイル</span> | fairu | file (a document/file), **not** food |
| <span lang="ja">チェリー</span> | cherī | cherry |
| <span lang="ja">タクシー</span> | takushī | taxi, a transport word |

**Scene clue:** Hana points to <span lang="ja">ティー</span> and <span lang="ja">ケーキ</span>. Sora shows her where to order. The nearby travel information board says <span lang="ja">タクシー</span>; that word is about transport, not a café item. <span lang="ja">ファイル</span> is shown on a separate stationery notice, so its meaning is not a menu item. Reading **location and purpose** prevents false guesses based on the script alone.

## Why these shapes matter

The horizontal mark **ー** is called <em>chōonpu</em>, a long-vowel mark. It lengthens the preceding vowel sound in common katakana spelling: <span lang="ja">コーヒー</span> has two long vowels, while <span lang="ja">ケーキ</span> has one after ケ. It is not a hyphen separating two words. Do not simply delete it: <span lang="ja">ティ</span> and <span lang="ja">ティー</span> are not the same written sequence.

Some borrowed sounds use a **small vowel kana**. <span lang="ja">ティ</span> combines テ with small ィ to represent *ti*; <span lang="ja">ファ</span> combines フ with small ァ to represent *fa*; <span lang="ja">チェ</span> combines チ with small ェ to represent *che*. The small kana are part of the spelling, not extra full-size syllables. Compare <span lang="ja">テイ</span> (*tei*, full-size イ) with <span lang="ja">ティ</span> (*ti*, small ィ). Japanese pronunciation is adapted to Japanese sound patterns; English spelling is not a reliable letter-by-letter decoder.

### Context before translation

- **Food and drink:** <span lang="ja">ティー</span> (tea), <span lang="ja">コーヒー</span> (coffee), <span lang="ja">ケーキ</span> (cake), <span lang="ja">チェリー</span> (cherry).
- **Technology/stationery:** <span lang="ja">ファイル</span> (file). It could refer to a document or a folder depending on context; this scene uses a stationery notice.
- **Transport:** <span lang="ja">タクシー</span> (taxi). Seeing it on a station board does not make it something you can order as food.

A katakana word may be borrowed from another language, but **its Japanese use and pronunciation are what matter**. For example, <span lang="ja">ティー</span> is a useful written menu word for tea; real cafés may also use <span lang="ja">お茶</span> or a more specific tea name. Do not assume every café prints this exact menu.

## Guided reading: three passes

1. **Locate the scene.** Point to the café menu and identify Hana as the customer and Sora as the clerk.
2. **Find evidence.** Underline <span lang="ja">ティー</span> and <span lang="ja">ケーキ</span>. They are Hana's selections; do not confuse the separate <span lang="ja">タクシー</span> sign with her order.
3. **Inspect spelling.** Circle ー in <span lang="ja">ティー</span> and small ィ in <span lang="ja">ティ</span>. Find small ァ in <span lang="ja">ファイル</span> and small ェ in <span lang="ja">チェリー</span>.
4. **Explain the purpose.** In one English sentence, say how a traveler uses the café card to choose a snack before boarding.

### Low-stakes rehearsal

Copy <span lang="ja">ティー</span> and <span lang="ja">ケーキ</span> on paper or type them with a Japanese IME. Compare every character with the printed models, especially ー and small ィ. You may silently mouth the words if useful, but **this lesson has no recording or scored listening/speaking assessment**. Real listening and optional shadowing are covered in their dedicated chapter lessons.
