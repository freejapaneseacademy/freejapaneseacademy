## A short story about fixing one kana mistake

**Can-do:** Read kana fluently enough for N5 and set a sustainable review routine. This is a **written reading** lesson: read first, find evidence second, and check your understanding third. There is no recording or scored pronunciation here.

Our original fictional learner, みな (Mina), reviews Japanese at school. Read the story once without stopping at every word. On a second pass, look for **when**, **who**, **what**, and **what happened next**.

## Original reading: きょうの ふくしゅう (Today's review)

<div lang="ja" style={{lineHeight:'2',fontSize:'1.12rem',whiteSpace:'pre-line'}}>

【きょうの ふくしゅう】
あさ、みなは がっこうへ いきました。
みなは せんせいに「おはようございます」と いいました。
きょうは かなの ふくしゅうを します。
つくえに ほんと ノートが あります。
みなは ノートに「きょう」「コーヒー」と かきました。
「コーヒー」の ーを ひとつ わすれました。
みなは もういちど みて、ただしく かきなおしました。
せんせいは「いいですね」と いいました。
みなは「ありがとうございます」と いいました。
いえで、みなは あしたも すこし ふくしゅうしようと おもいました。

</div>

The Japanese is deliberately kana-rich for foundation reading. The spaces separate phrases for beginners; ordinary Japanese print often omits these spaces.

## 1. Check the story's sequence

Mina goes to school **in the morning** and greets her teacher politely. She reviews kana and writes two words in her notebook. After missing one long-vowel mark in コーヒー (*coffee*), she checks and rewrites it. Her teacher responds positively; Mina thanks the teacher. At home she plans a little more review tomorrow.

This English overview is a support **after** your first reading, not a substitute for finding Japanese evidence.

## 2. Useful words and phrase clues

| Japanese | Reading / meaning | Story clue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| あさ | morning | あさ、…いきました |
| がっこう | school | がっこうへ (toward school) |
| せんせい | teacher | せんせいに (to the teacher) |
| つくえ | desk | つくえに (at/on the desk) |
| ほん | book | ほんと ノート (book and notebook) |
| ノート | notebook | katakana, with a long-vowel mark |
| きょう | today | small ょ followed by う |
| ふくしゅう | review | small ゅ followed by う |
| もういちど | once more | a second look |
| かきなおしました | rewrote | a correction action |
| あした | tomorrow | future review intention |

**Grammar noticing, not a new grammar test:** The particle **へ** in がっこうへ indicates direction; **に** in せんせいに marks the person addressed; **と** connects the two objects ほん and ノート. You only need to recognize these cues here.

## 3. Look closely at the spelling

**コーヒー** contains two long-vowel marks **ー**. The story says Mina forgot **one**; it does not specify which occurrence. Do not invent a missing-mark position when answering. **きょう** uses small **ょ**, and **ふくしゅう** uses small **ゅ**. These are not interchangeable with full-size よ and ゆ.

Notice how **かきなおしました** describes rewriting after checking, rather than merely copying a model without noticing an error. A useful personal study cycle is: **notice → compare → rewrite → return later**.

## 4. Evidence practice

For each question, locate a phrase in the story **before** choosing an answer. Example: “Who receives the greeting?” The evidence is **せんせいに「おはようございます」と いいました。** The particle に connects the greeting to the teacher.

Try two more without looking at the English overview: What was on the desk? What did Mina decide about tomorrow? Quote the exact Japanese line, even if your English explanation is short.

## 5. Independent reading check

Answer ten original questions below. All answers come directly from the Japanese passage. After submission, read the cited Japanese evidence and the explanation for each question. If an answer was wrong, find the line and read it again before retrying.

## 6. Your own two-minute review note

Type **きょう**, **ふくしゅう**, and **コーヒー** using a Japanese IME. Compare small kana and long-vowel marks with the printed models. Write one English sentence describing the exact spelling mistake you corrected, then choose a day to revisit the word. This optional self-review is not automatically graded.

**Scope:** A practice score records comprehension of this story, not verified reading fluency, official JLPT performance, or a human Japanese-language review.
