## Mio's reading mission

Sora has put up a classroom **kana chart** and five word cards. Mio's job is to **read what is actually printed**, not guess a pronunciation from a picture or infer stroke order from a font. You will locate the evidence before choosing each answer. This is written reading comprehension; syllable listening belongs to Lesson 06.

## Source A — the original classroom character grid

The left column names the **vowel sound** of each horizontal row. The top line names the vowel-only column, K column and S column. Read **across** a row to compare the same vowel; read **down** a column to review a consonant family.

| Vowel sound | Vowel-only | K column | S column |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| a | <span lang="ja">あ</span> (a) | <span lang="ja">か</span> (ka) | <span lang="ja">さ</span> (sa) |
| i | <span lang="ja">い</span> (i) | <span lang="ja">き</span> (ki) | <span lang="ja">し</span> (shi) |
| u | <span lang="ja">う</span> (u) | <span lang="ja">く</span> (ku) | <span lang="ja">す</span> (su) |
| e | <span lang="ja">え</span> (e) | <span lang="ja">け</span> (ke) | <span lang="ja">せ</span> (se) |
| o | <span lang="ja">お</span> (o) | <span lang="ja">こ</span> (ko) | <span lang="ja">そ</span> (so) |

**Evidence example:** “Which symbol is in the S column and i row?” First locate the **i** row, then follow it to the **S column**. The printed cell is **し**. The Latin reading aid is *shi*, but the actual answer in Japanese script is し. Do not confuse *shi* with the English word “she.”

**Evidence example:** “Which row contains け?” The grid prints **け** at the intersection of the **e** row and the **K column**. Cite the cell **け**, not merely your memory of the sound *ke*.

## Source B — Sora's word cards

These cards use only the fifteen kana above. The card labels identify familiar classroom items or meanings; the printed kana themselves encode sounds, not separate English words.

| Card | Printed word | Reading aid | Label |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | <span lang="ja">かさ</span> | kasa | umbrella |
| B | <span lang="ja">すし</span> | sushi | sushi |
| C | <span lang="ja">いす</span> | isu | chair |
| D | <span lang="ja">ここ</span> | koko | here, near the speaker |
| E | <span lang="ja">せかい</span> | sekai | world |

To read **Card E**, point to **せ → か → い** in the grid: S/e, K/a, vowel-only/i. The three written kana correspond to three morae in this beginner example. The meaning “world” comes from the word card, not from translating each kana individually.

To read **Card B**, find **す** in S/u and **し** in S/i. Its reading aid *sushi* contains five Latin letters but the word has **two kana**. A reading aid's letter count is not the number of Japanese sound units.

## Guided reading: locate, quote, explain

1. Find the **K column, o row**: quote <span lang="ja">こ</span> (ko). Card D repeats that symbol twice: <span lang="ja">ここ</span>.
2. Find the **S column, e row**: quote <span lang="ja">せ</span> (se). It begins Card E: <span lang="ja">せかい</span>.
3. Find the **vowel-only, i row**: quote <span lang="ja">い</span> (i). It begins Card C: <span lang="ja">いす</span>.
4. Find the **K column, a row**: quote <span lang="ja">か</span> (ka). It begins Card A: <span lang="ja">かさ</span>.

**Common reading error:** A word card and a kana grid answer different questions. The grid tells you that **さ** represents *sa*. Card A tells you that **かさ** means “umbrella.” Neither source says that **さ** alone means “umbrella.” Similarly, the printed chart does not demonstrate stroke order or provide audio.
