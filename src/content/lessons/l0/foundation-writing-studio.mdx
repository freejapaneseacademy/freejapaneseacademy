## Your final foundation writing rehearsal

**Can-do:** Read kana fluently enough to begin N5 study and set a sustainable review routine. You are helping a fictional study partner prepare labels for a review notebook. You will write familiar words in the requested script, compare each answer with a model, and create a short plan you can realistically follow.

This is a **writing and typing** lesson, not a listening or pronunciation test. You may use a Japanese IME on desktop or a Japanese keyboard on mobile. Enter the requested kana, rather than romaji. If your keyboard first produces hiragana when katakana is requested, use its katakana conversion option. Do not enter personal information in the optional plan.

## Notice what a single character changes

| Intended word | Model | Common error | Why the model matters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| postage stamp | <span lang="ja">きって</span> | <span lang="ja">きて</span> | Small っ marks a consonant closure; omitting it changes the word. |
| today | <span lang="ja">きょう</span> | <span lang="ja">きよう</span> | Small ょ combines with き to make きょ. |
| coffee | <span lang="ja">コーヒー</span> | <span lang="ja">コヒ</span> | Each ー lengthens the preceding vowel in katakana. |
| bread | <span lang="ja">パン</span> | <span lang="ja">バン</span> | パ has a circle (handakuten); バ has two marks (dakuten). |
| Japanese language | <span lang="ja">にほんご</span> | <span lang="ja">にほんこ</span> | ご needs dakuten. |

In hiragana, long vowels often appear with an additional vowel character, as in <span lang="ja">がっこう</span> (school) and <span lang="ja">きょう</span> (today). Do not assume every long vowel is written with ー: the spelling conventions differ between these examples and katakana loanwords.

## Controlled practice: observe, cover, produce

First read each model in the table. Cover the model column and try writing the word again. Then use the ten typing checks below. Each answer requires the exact script specified in its prompt. Leading/trailing whitespace and canonically equivalent Unicode composition are ignored; other spellings are not silently marked correct. Feedback appears **after** you submit, not while you type.

If you write <span lang="ja">チヨコレト</span> for chocolate, compare <span lang="ja">チョコレート</span>: replace large ヨ with small ョ and add ー after レ. If you write <span lang="ja">がこう</span> for school, insert small っ after が and う after こ: <span lang="ja">がっこう</span>. Say what changed before retrying. These are specific spelling repairs, not general handwriting judgments.

## Guided production: a notebook heading

Make a **fictional** two-line heading for a foundation review notebook. One possible model is:

> <span lang="ja">にほんご　きょう</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">コーヒー　パン</span>

These are vocabulary labels, not full sentences. You can use other words taught here. Keep hiragana words in hiragana and katakana loanwords in katakana. Your own heading is not automatically graded.

## Independent task: plan your next week

Write two or three short lines in English or simple Japanese describing a repeatable routine. For example: *Monday, Wednesday, Friday: five minutes of kana recall; Sunday: check missed small-kana words.* You may write <span lang="ja">まいにち　５ふん</span> (five minutes every day) as a supported label, but you are not required to produce new Japanese grammar. Choose a frequency you can sustain; an ambitious schedule you cannot keep is less useful than a small consistent one.

Self-review your draft using four criteria: **script choice**, **small-kana and length marks**, **readability**, and **realistic review frequency**. Compare your work to the models, correct at least one specific error if present, and save only if you wish. The free-composition draft remains on this device; no teacher or automated evaluator grades it.

## What to do after the score

Your ten constrained answers measure written recall on these examples only; they do **not** certify N5 readiness or predict a JLPT score. For any missed word, copy the correct model once, hide it, retrieve it again tomorrow, and revisit it after a few days. Return to the earlier kana and reading lessons as needed. Independent qualified Japanese-language and assessment reviews remain pending.
