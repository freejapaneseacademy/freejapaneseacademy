## Listen for a useful study plan

**Can-do:** Understand two short classroom conversations and choose a realistic next step for reviewing kana. This is listening comprehension, not a test of what written characters look like.

You have already read kana, corrected simple errors and practised short phrases. Now listen to people discussing what they can do and what still needs practice. There are **two separate original conversations**, with three questions per recording. Each scene has a different purpose.

### Before listening: meaning clues

You may recognize these spoken expressions from previous lessons. Do not memorize them as a transcript of either recording.

| Expression | Meaning and use |
| --- | --- |
| れんしゅうします | practise; a polite statement about an activity |
| むずかしいです | it is difficult |
| あります | have / there is, used for inanimate things |
| あした | tomorrow |
| もういちど | once more |
| すこしずつ | little by little |

Notice the difference between **きょう** (today) and **あした** (tomorrow). A review plan has a time as well as a target. **カタカナは だいじょうぶです** means that katakana is okay for the speaker in that context; it is not a claim of permanent mastery.

### Track A: before class

A teacher checks what a learner wants to practise. Listen first for the activity, then for the difficulty and whether the learner has a useful study tool.

**First pass:** play without revealing the transcript. Ask yourself: what is the learner planning to do?

**Second pass:** replay to check the detail that explains the teacher's suggestion. A repeated recording is still unaided listening if you have not revealed the transcript.

### Track B: after class

A learner reflects on what went well and what needs work. Listen for a contrast between something manageable and something difficult. Then identify when and how the learner plans to practise.

**First pass:** listen for the challenge.

**Second pass:** identify the next action. A useful study habit is specific: *what, when, and with what resource?*

### Controlled → guided → independent

**Controlled:** answer the six listening questions below. All answers come from spoken meaning, not from kana shapes or spellings.

**Guided:** after submitting, reveal each transcript and compare your answer with the precise spoken evidence. The transcript is an accessibility aid; revealing it before submission makes the attempt assisted, not an unaided listening score.

**Independent:** choose a five-minute review action for yourself. For example: listen to a short phrase, repeat it, compare with the transcript, and revisit one difficult sound tomorrow. This personal reflection is ungraded.

### Common mistake: confusing a sound problem with a script test

A speaker can say that long sounds are difficult. That is information you can **hear**. A question such as “Which character has the long-vowel mark?” would require looking at writing, so it does not belong in a blind listening score. This lesson checks spoken meaning only.

### After your check

A correct recognition score is useful feedback, not a certificate of N5 readiness or a verified pronunciation assessment. You can retry, use the transcript as needed, and keep completion separate from your score.
