## From sound to a written label

Mika is preparing picture labels for the classroom wall. You have read the contrast board and heard two conversations. Now you will **write** the words with the right upper-right marks. This is written kana practice, not a listening or pronunciation test.

A **dakuten** (two short strokes) turns か into **が**, き into **ぎ**, す into **ず**, た into **だ**, and は into **ば**. A **handakuten** (small circle) turns は into **ぱ** and ひ into **ぴ**. The marks change the kana; they are not decoration.

## Step 1 — Type the picture labels

| English meaning | Model label | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| key | **かぎ** | The second kana is ぎ, not き |
| water | **みず** | Use ず, not す or づ |
| pig | **ぶた** | The first kana is ぶ |
| friend | **ともだち** | The third kana is だ |
| bag | **かばん** | The middle kana is ば |
| letter | **てがみ** | The middle kana is が |
| bread | **ぱん** | The first kana has a circle |
| pencil | **えんぴつ** | The ぴ has a circle |

Bread is normally written **パン** in everyday Japanese. The hiragana **ぱん** here is deliberate script practice, not a claim that it is the standard everyday spelling. The word **みず** uses ず in standard spelling.

Cover the model column. Type the label using a Japanese IME, then uncover the spelling and check each character. If you are typing on a phone, choose the hiragana keyboard. Latin letters that have not been converted to kana are not a completed answer.

## Step 2 — Make a classroom label

The model **かぎは １ばん。** means *The key is number one.* The model **ぱんは ５ばん。** means *The bread is number five.* The spaces help beginners see the topic particle **は**, which is written は even when pronounced *wa*. The numeral and full stop are part of these models.

To ask someone to look at the picture, write **かぎをみてください。** (*Please look at the key.*) or **ぶたをみてください。** (*Please look at the pig.*). The object particle is **を**, not お. The expression **みてください** makes a polite request; it is not an assertion about what someone sees.

## Step 3 — Write, compare, revise

The ten scored prompts below check exact taught kana sequences, including two classroom sentences. Whitespace around your answer is ignored, and Unicode-equivalent composed characters are treated alike. The punctuation in the two full-sentence models is expected. This is constrained spelling practice; natural free writing has more valid possibilities and is not automatically graded here.

After the scored prompts, try a two-line message about a fictional picture board. For example:

**かぎは １ばん。**

**かぎをみてください。**

Check four things: (1) the intended voiced or p-series marks are present, (2) each word is complete, (3) the particles は and を fit their functions, and (4) the request ends in みてください。 You are responsible for the self-review; a checkbox does not prove handwriting or general Japanese writing proficiency.
