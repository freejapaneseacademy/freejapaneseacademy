## Find the right word at a station kiosk

**Chapter Can-do:** read and write the katakana vowels and K, S and T rows. Today, you move from recognizing individual symbols to finding **meaningful words** on a kiosk display. Mika is buying a drink; Ren, the clerk, also has a small shelf of study materials. These are **original teaching labels**, not a photograph or a claim about any real shop.

| Japanese label | Read in parts | Meaning in this setting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">ココア</span> | コ・コ・ア (*ko-ko-a*) | cocoa, a drink |
| <span lang="ja">カカオ</span> | カ・カ・オ (*ka-ka-o*) | cacao, an ingredient |
| <span lang="ja">アイス</span> | ア・イ・ス (*a-i-su*) | ice cream on a dessert shelf |
| <span lang="ja">テキスト</span> | テ・キ・ス・ト (*te-ki-su-to*) | text or textbook, here a study book |

The roman letters are temporary reading hints; the printed Japanese words use **katakana**. All their symbols belong to the vowel, K, S and T rows introduced in this chapter.

## Cocoa is not cacao

The two labels <span lang="ja">ココア</span> and <span lang="ja">カカオ</span> look similar because each repeats its first character. They are **different words**. ココア usually names cocoa, such as a drink or powder. カカオ names cacao, the plant or ingredient. Mika wants a cocoa drink, so she should find **ココア**. Compare コ (*ko*) with カ (*ka*), then ア (*a*) with オ (*o*). Changing the characters changes the word.

## A loanword needs context

<span lang="ja">アイス</span> often means *ice cream* in a dessert setting, but it can also mean *ice*. A single label does not always tell you which product is meant. Look at the shelf or category before translating.

<span lang="ja">テキスト</span> can mean written text or a textbook. At a study-supply shelf, a textbook is a natural interpretation. Loanwords do **not** always have one fixed English meaning.

### A supported written exchange

> Mika: <span lang="ja">ココアは ありますか。</span> — Do you have cocoa?
>
> Ren: <span lang="ja">はい、ココアは あります。</span> — Yes, we have cocoa.
>
> Mika: <span lang="ja">テキストも ありますか。</span> — Do you also have a textbook?
>
> Ren: <span lang="ja">はい、あります。</span> — Yes, we do.

The hiragana and particles are familiar from earlier chapters; your new task is to **spot the katakana words inside a sentence**.

## Read the symbols systematically

The vowels are **ア イ ウ エ オ** (*a i u e o*). The K row is **カ キ ク ケ コ** (*ka ki ku ke ko*). The S row is **サ シ ス セ ソ** (*sa shi su se so*). The T row is **タ チ ツ テ ト** (*ta chi tsu te to*). The conventional readings *shi*, *chi* and *tsu* do not introduce extra katakana characters.

Try these three guided checks before the quiz:

1. Cover the English meanings and find the word for cocoa. Point to its repeated **コ**.
2. Find cacao and identify both the different first symbol **カ** and final **オ**.
3. On a study shelf, choose **テキスト** and explain why *textbook* fits that setting.

Then reveal the table and check. **Do not assume** that katakana is always a direct translation of an English word. Use the printed word and the situation together.
