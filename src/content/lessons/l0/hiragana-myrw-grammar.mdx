## Mio labels the picture board

Mio has a **book**, a **peach** and a **mountain** picture. Yuri writes short captions under them. In this lesson you will see how the nasal kana <span lang="ja">ん</span> affects written rhythm and why the object marker <span lang="ja">を</span> is not interchangeable with <span lang="ja">お</span>.

This is a **written grammar and sentence-function lesson**, not a listening or pronunciation assessment. Spaces in the examples separate beginner learning units; ordinary Japanese normally does not put spaces between each word.

## ん: one independent mora

A **mora** is a unit used to describe Japanese rhythm. The kana <span lang="ja">ん</span> counts as **one mora of its own**, even though it does not form a normal consonant-plus-vowel syllable.

| Written word | Meaning | Count by mora |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">ほん</span> (*hon*) | book | ほ・ん = **2** |
| <span lang="ja">みかん</span> (*mikan*) | mandarin orange | み・か・ん = **3** |
| <span lang="ja">さん</span> (*san*) | polite name suffix | さ・ん = **2** |
| <span lang="ja">にほん</span> (*nihon*) | Japan | に・ほ・ん = **3** |

A beginner might count <span lang="ja">ほん</span> as one because its romanization looks like a single English syllable. For Japanese mora counting, that is incorrect: <span lang="ja">ほ</span> and <span lang="ja">ん</span> each take a count. The actual pronunciation of ん can vary with neighboring sounds; do not assume it is always identical to English *n*.

**Try it:** tap once for each written unit in <span lang="ja">みかん</span>, then in <span lang="ja">ほん</span>. The final ん gets its own tap.

## を: an object particle, not an ordinary word for “o”

Yuri writes <span lang="ja">ほんをよむ。</span> (*hon o yomu*) — **(I/someone) read(s) a book.** The verb <span lang="ja">よむ</span> means “read.” Japanese can omit the subject when context makes it clear.

- <span lang="ja">ほん</span> = book, the object being read.
- <span lang="ja">を</span> = particle marking the direct object of the verb.
- <span lang="ja">よむ</span> = read (plain, non-past form).

The particle <span lang="ja">を</span> is normally pronounced *o* in modern standard Japanese, despite its conventional romanization *wo* in kana charts. **Write を when the grammatical object particle is needed.** The separate kana <span lang="ja">お</span> represents the vowel sound in ordinary words, as in <span lang="ja">おに</span> (*oni*, ogre). Do not change every お to を just because both may sound like *o*.

Compare the following original, beginner-supported examples:

| Japanese | Reading aid | Meaning and function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">ほんをよむ。</span> | *hon o yomu* | Read a book; ほん is the object. |
| <span lang="ja">ももをたべる。</span> | *momo o taberu* | Eat a peach; もも is the object. |
| <span lang="ja">やまをみる。</span> | *yama o miru* | Look at a mountain; やま is the object. |
| <span lang="ja">おにをみる。</span> | *oni o miru* | See an ogre; the initial お belongs to おに, and を marks the object. |

These verbs are presented as reading support, not a full conjugation lesson. The particle follows the object noun, **not** the English word order.

## Two traps to avoid

**Trap 1 — counting ん incorrectly.** <span lang="ja">ほん</span> has two morae, not one. <span lang="ja">みかん</span> has three, not two. A written ん remains an independent rhythm unit.

**Trap 2 — swapping を and お.** In <span lang="ja">おにをみる。</span>, the first お is part of the noun <span lang="ja">おに</span>, while を is a grammatical particle. They have different written jobs even when their modern standard pronunciations share the vowel *o*.

## Guided practice

1. Read <span lang="ja">ほ・ん</span> and <span lang="ja">み・か・ん</span>, counting each unit.
2. Find the object in <span lang="ja">ももをたべる。</span>: it is <span lang="ja">もも</span>.
3. In <span lang="ja">おにをみる。</span>, underline the **particle** を, not the initial お.
4. Explain in English why <span lang="ja">ほんおよむ</span> is not the conventional spelling of “read a book”: the direct-object particle is written を.
