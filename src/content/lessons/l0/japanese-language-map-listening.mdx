## Listen to people, not written characters

Reading a character and understanding what someone **says** are different skills. You already explored hiragana, katakana, kanji and romaji in the reading lessons. **You will not be asked to identify written scripts from an audio recording.**

Instead, hear two brief real-life situations: one short exchange and one everyday conversation. Each has a male and female speaker. Your goal is to catch familiar words, how the other person responds, and the meaning of the interaction. You do not need to transcribe anything.

The voices are licensed Japanese synthetic voices: **阿井田 茂 (Aida Shigeru, Calm)** and **morioki (Normal)**. All dialogues were written for this academy. The recordings passed automated audio-format and integrity checks; independent qualified Japanese pronunciation and assessment review have **not** been completed.

### An effective first listening attempt

1. Play each conversation once at normal speed **without looking at the transcript**.
2. Ask yourself: who is speaking, and what does each person want or notice? Do not expect to understand every word.
3. Listen again, this time noticing the questions, answers, and short polite phrases.
4. Answer the four questions for each recording based only on what you **heard**.
5. After submitting, open the transcript and English meaning. Replay the sections you missed.

### Track A — A short exchange

You will hear a man and a woman speaking politely. Concentrate on the **request**, the woman's response, and what the man says at the end. You can replay the recording as many times as you need.

### Track B — An everyday conversation

Listen to the two speakers greet each other, comment on what is around them, and make a small plan. Your task is to understand **spoken meaning**, not identify any written symbols.

## Your listening workspace

The players below provide normal browser play, pause, volume, seek and optional speed controls. The Japanese transcripts and English translations are hidden for a genuine blind attempt. You may reveal them for accessibility or study at any time; doing so before submission marks the result as **assisted**, so it is not stored as an unseen listening score.

The eight questions check the meaning of the conversations; each answer has evidence from actual spoken words. If the audio does not load, scoring stays disabled rather than pretending listening took place.

## After the questions

Read the speaker-by-speaker transcript, then replay the corresponding line. Notice how a question leads to an answer, how politeness sounds in speech, and what people agree to do. Speaking aloud is optional.

**What this measures:** first-step understanding of short conversations. It does **not** assess the ability to read kanji, distinguish kana shapes, or pronounce words perfectly. Those belong in the separate reading and speaking lessons.
