## Optional speaking: listen, pause, repeat

In a fictional fruit shop, a customer asks for peaches. In a classroom, a teacher asks someone to look at and read a book. Both conversations were **originally recorded for Lesson 06**, using licensed synthetic Japanese voices. This lesson reuses the unchanged WAV recordings as speaking models, rather than pretending to create new speech. You can read the transcript as you rehearse; this is not a blind listening test.

The chapter goal is to read and write the remaining basic hiragana, including **わ・を・ん**. Speaking here is **optional**: it supports the sounds behind familiar kana and is never required to pass the chapter. There is no automatic accent recognition or pronunciation grade. If you cannot play audio, use the transcript for text practice, but do not claim to have heard the model.

## 1. Fruit shop: two clear morae

Listen to Track A once, then replay the woman's turns. Start with **もも** (*momo*, peach), two morae: **も・も**. Repeat both beats rather than reducing them to one. Then shadow:

- <span lang="ja">すみません。ももはありますか。</span> — “Excuse me. Do you have peaches?”
- <span lang="ja">ももをふたつください。</span> — “Two peaches, please.”

In the second request, **を** marks the object; its modern standard pronunciation is usually *o*, not *wo*. **ふたつ** means “two (items)” here. The full expression is provided as a supported phrase; you do not need to derive its grammar independently.

Listen to the shopkeeper's response **はい。ももとみかんがあります。** (“Yes, we have peaches and mandarins.”) Then imagine taking the customer's role. Say the request after the model, pause, and repeat once at a comfortable pace. Avoid forcing English stress onto one syllable.

## 2. Classroom: やま, ほん and よんで

In Track B, the teacher points to a book. The learner says **やまのほんですね。** (“It's a book about mountains, isn't it?”). Rehearse **やま** (*yama*, mountain) as や・ま and **ほん** (*hon*, book) as ほ・ん. The final **ん** is its own mora, not a silent letter.

Shadow the teacher's two requests:

- <span lang="ja">あのほんをみてください。</span> — “Please look at that book.”
- <span lang="ja">そうです。よんでください。</span> — “That's right. Please read it.”

**よんで** contains the nasal mora **ん**. The polite request **〜てください** is a supported preview. Compare **みて** (look) with **よんで** (read); do not swap the meanings. Try repeating the short word, then the full phrase. The model's rhythm is a reference, not a promise of perfect pronunciation.

## 3. Shadow a whole exchange

Play each track at normal speed. Repeat each speaker turn after it ends. Then try a role-play: ask for two peaches and answer as the shopkeeper; next, ask a classmate to read the book and respond **はい。よみます。** (“Okay, I'll read it.”). Replay any difficult turns using the controls below. The optional slower playback may alter audio quality.

A useful sequence is **listen → pause → repeat → compare → retry**. You may speak without recording. If you choose to record, the browser asks for permission only when you press Start; the recording stays in the current tab and can be discarded. It is not uploaded or stored in your learning history.

## 4. Self-review and knowledge check

Use the five self-review statements to reflect on your own attempt. They are not assessed by a speech recognizer or qualified teacher. The six optional multiple-choice questions check the meanings of the model phrases and your understanding of practice strategy. Their numerical knowledge score is saved locally, separately from optional lesson completion; **no pronunciation score is saved**.

Common errors to watch: **もも** has two morae, **ほん** ends in a nasal mora, and the object particle **を** is written を even when pronounced *o*. Read the printed examples if helpful, but listening to actual audio is necessary for an audio-based shadowing claim.
