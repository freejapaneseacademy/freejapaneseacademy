## At the community language class

You arrive at a fictional Japanese language club. A volunteer hands you a numbered registration card and asks for your name and country. **Can-do:** recognize and state basic numbers, names and nationalities. In this vocabulary lesson you will read and select written answers. Actual listening to introductions belongs to L06, and writing your own form belongs to L07.

> 受付（うけつけ）: なまえは？ — Name?
>
> 学習者（がくしゅうしゃ）: エマです。 — I'm Emma.
>
> 受付: くには？ — Country?
>
> 学習者: カナダです。 — Canada.
>
> 受付: ばんごうは、にじゅうさんです。 — Your number is twenty-three.

These shortened questions suit an instructional role-play; in a real formal interaction, a full polite question such as おなまえは なんですか would be more courteous. You do not need to produce that question yet.

## 1. Numbers you can build

| Value | Japanese | Reading cue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | ゼロ / れい | *zero / rei* |
| 1 | いち | *ichi* |
| 2 | に | *ni* |
| 3 | さん | *san* |
| 4 | よん / し | *yon / shi* |
| 5 | ご | *go* |
| 6 | ろく | *roku* |
| 7 | なな / しち | *nana / shichi* |
| 8 | はち | *hachi* |
| 9 | きゅう / く | *kyū / ku* |
| 10 | じゅう | *jū* |
| 100 | ひゃく | *hyaku* |

The alternatives are not always interchangeable in fixed expressions. For general counting, **よん**, **なな** and **きゅう** are useful default choices. For phone numbers, individual digits are read in sequence, not as one large compound number.

| Number | Written Japanese | How it is built |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 11 | じゅういち | ten + one |
| 14 | じゅうよん | ten + four |
| 20 | にじゅう | two tens |
| 23 | にじゅうさん | two tens + three |
| 40 | よんじゅう | four tens |
| 58 | ごじゅうはち | five tens + eight |
| 70 | ななじゅう | seven tens |
| 99 | きゅうじゅうきゅう | nine tens + nine |
| 100 | ひゃく | one hundred |

**Compare:** じゅうよん (14) and よんじゅう (40). The order changes the value. Say the tens before the units: にじゅうさん (23), not さんにじゅう. Tap the kana slowly as a written rhythm aid, not as a substitute for verified pronunciation audio.

## 2. Names, countries and nationality

| Japanese | Reading cue | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| なまえ / 名前 | *namae* | name field |
| くに / 国 | *kuni* | country field |
| ばんごう / 番号 | *bangō* | identifying number |
| にほん / 日本 | *Nihon* | Japan |
| カナダ | *Kanada* | Canada |
| アメリカ | *Amerika* | United States (common short form) |
| にほんじん / 日本人 | *Nihonjin* | Japanese person |
| カナダじん | *Kanadajin* | Canadian person |
| アメリカじん | *Amerikajin* | American person |

The suffix **じん（人）** identifies a person by nationality in these examples. **にほん** names a country; **にほんじん** names a person. Do not use the country word and nationality word as though they were the same.

Japanese names can be written in kanji, hiragana or katakana depending on the person's name and preferences. **エマ** is a fictional learner's name here. Never assume a person's nationality from their name.

## 3. Read a fictional registration slip

<div class="foundation-example" role="group" aria-label="Fictional registration slip">
  <p lang="ja">なまえ：エマ</p>
  <p lang="ja">くに：カナダ</p>
  <p lang="ja">こくせき：カナダ</p>
  <p lang="ja">ばんごう：23</p>
</div>

The field **こくせき（国籍）** means nationality (often recorded as a country on forms). The slip does not give Emma's age. A number beside **ばんごう** is an identifier, not automatically an age or a count of people.

**Starter pattern:** エマです。 (*I am Emma.*) and カナダじんです。 (*[I am] Canadian.*) are simple polite identification frames when the speaker is understood. **です** is not the English verb “to be” in every context; here it makes a polite statement. Later lessons explain the full sentence pattern.

## 4. Supported retrieval and study routine

Cover the English column, then read じゅうよん, よんじゅう, にじゅうさん and きゅうじゅうきゅう. Next, invent a **fictional** registration card with a name, a country and a number between 0 and 100. Write the Japanese for the number, and compare it to the patterns above. Do not enter real personal details into the practice questions.

For optional speaking preparation, read your fictional name and number aloud slowly, without treating this as a scored pronunciation test. The L06 lesson supplies authentic generated listening assets. When you miss a question below, review the explanation and retry after a short break.
