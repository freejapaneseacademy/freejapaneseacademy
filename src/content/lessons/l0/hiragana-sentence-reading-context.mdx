## At the library desk

**Chapter Can-do:** Decode short hiragana sentences without relying on romaji. In this opening context lesson, you will meet a visitor and a librarian and find the purpose of their exchange. The next lessons will teach sentence particles and punctuation more systematically.

**Setting:** A small neighborhood library. **Yui**, a visitor, wants to look at a picture book. **Sora**, the librarian, helps her locate it. The speakers use a brief, polite exchange. You do not need to speak aloud or listen to audio to complete this **written-reading** lesson.

| Person | Role | Goal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yui | Visitor | Find a picture book |
| Sora | Librarian | Help the visitor find it |

## The mini dialogue

Read each Japanese line first. Use the English support **after** making your own reading attempt.

| Speaker | Japanese | English support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yui | <span lang="ja">こんにちは。</span> | Hello. |
| Yui | <span lang="ja">えほんは ありますか。</span> | Do you have picture books? |
| Sora | <span lang="ja">はい、あります。</span> | Yes, we do. |
| Sora | <span lang="ja">えほんは ここです。</span> | The picture books are here. |
| Yui | <span lang="ja">えほんを みます。</span> | I will look at a picture book / picture books. |
| Yui | <span lang="ja">ありがとうございます。</span> | Thank you very much. |

This is an **original instructional dialogue**, not a transcript of a recording. Spaces have been added between some chunks as beginner reading support; ordinary Japanese text usually does not insert spaces between words. Japanese words and particles are not separated by spaces in the same way as English.

## What is happening?

Yui asks <span lang="ja">えほんは ありますか。</span> (“Do you have picture books?”). Sora replies <span lang="ja">はい、あります。</span> (“Yes, we do.”) and points to the location with <span lang="ja">ここ</span> (“here”). Yui then says <span lang="ja">えほんを みます。</span> (“I will look at a picture book / picture books.”). The exchange is a simple request for information, not a purchase or a borrowing transaction.

**Reading clues:** <span lang="ja">えほん</span> means “picture book”; <span lang="ja">ここ</span> means “here”; <span lang="ja">みます</span> means “look at / see” in this context. The <span lang="ja">か</span> at the end of <span lang="ja">ありますか。</span> marks a question. The <span lang="ja">。</span> ends a sentence; the <span lang="ja">、</span> marks a pause.

## Notice the little words

In <span lang="ja">えほんは ありますか。</span>, the written <span lang="ja">は</span> marks the topic and is pronounced *wa* **as a particle**. In <span lang="ja">えほんを みます。</span>, <span lang="ja">を</span> marks what is being looked at. The spaces in these teaching lines help you spot the parts; they are **not required Japanese spelling**. Lesson 03 will explore the particles more carefully.

**Common trap:** Do not assume <span lang="ja">えほんを みます。</span> means “I buy a picture book.” The verb is <span lang="ja">みます</span>, “look at / see,” not “buy.” Likewise, <span lang="ja">ありますか</span> asks whether something exists or is available in this setting; it is not asking where the book is.

## Guided reading

1. Locate the visitor's question ending in <span lang="ja">か。</span>.
2. Find Sora's answer beginning <span lang="ja">はい</span>.
3. Find the location word <span lang="ja">ここ</span>.
4. Find the line with the object marker <span lang="ja">を</span>.
5. Explain the purpose of the conversation in one English sentence.
