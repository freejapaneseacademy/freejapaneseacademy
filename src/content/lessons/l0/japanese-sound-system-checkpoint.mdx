## Your chapter Can-do checkpoint

**Can-do:** distinguish Japanese mora timing and five vowel qualities. You have already met these sounds in printed kana, short vocabulary, original reading material and two spoken conversations. This checkpoint brings the skills together, but **reading a character is not the same as understanding speech**.

This is an original practice checkpoint, not an official JLPT examination or a certified pronunciation test. Plan about **25 minutes**. You can review any earlier lesson before retrying. A practice score measures answers to these tasks, not your overall spoken proficiency.

### 1. Before you begin: five vowels, one steady sound each

The basic Japanese vowels are <span lang="ja">あ・い・う・え・お</span>. The romanized labels *a, i, u, e, o* are temporary beginner guides, **not English letter-name pronunciations**. In Japanese, vowel quality generally stays steady rather than gliding toward a second sound as in many English accents.

A **mora** is a timing unit. A short vowel ordinarily takes one mora; extending it takes another. The nasal <span lang="ja">ん</span> also counts as one mora. Morae are not identical to English syllables, and tapping is a useful learning aid rather than a command to stress each beat.

| Japanese | Contextual meaning | Mora breakdown | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">いえ</span> | house | い・え | 2 |
| <span lang="ja">いいえ</span> | no | い・い・え | 3 |
| <span lang="ja">おばさん</span> | aunt | お・ば・さ・ん | 4 |
| <span lang="ja">おばあさん</span> | grandmother | お・ば・あ・さ・ん | 5 |

**Common mistake:** <span lang="ja">おばさん</span> and <span lang="ja">おばあさん</span> are not interchangeable in the family-photo situation. The extra <span lang="ja">あ</span> changes the meaning. Do not infer that every pair of adjacent vowels always has the same meaning or pronunciation; use context.

### 2. Guided written contrasts

Imagine you are looking at two photographs with a classmate.

**Photo A:** A small house. The caption says <span lang="ja">いえ</span> (*house*). Your classmate asks whether it is a shop. You point to the caption. The two written vowel symbols are <span lang="ja">い・え</span>, **two morae**.

**Photo B:** Your grandmother. The caption says <span lang="ja">おばあさん</span> (*grandmother*). Someone says <span lang="ja">おばさん</span> (*aunt*). The correction needs the extra vowel: <span lang="ja">お・ば・あ・さ・ん</span>, **five morae**.

Try covering the mora-count column above and counting each example again. Ask yourself: *Which exact vowel would I have to add or remove to change the intended word?* This is a **written** exercise; it cannot establish that you can hear the contrast.

### 3. Read for explicit evidence

This short pronunciation-map note is an original reading stimulus for the checkpoint:

> <span lang="ja">あ・い・う・え・お</span> are the five basic vowel symbols. In the practice words <span lang="ja">いえ</span> and <span lang="ja">いいえ</span>, the second word contains one extra <span lang="ja">い</span>. In <span lang="ja">おばさん</span> and <span lang="ja">おばあさん</span>, the second word contains one extra <span lang="ja">あ</span>. The written contrast helps you prepare to listen, but it is not a recording.

**Reading check:** Which symbol is added in <span lang="ja">いいえ</span>? Answer: <span lang="ja">い</span>. Evidence: compare <span lang="ja">い・え</span> with <span lang="ja">い・い・え</span>. Which symbol is added in <span lang="ja">おばあさん</span>? Answer: <span lang="ja">あ</span>. Evidence: compare <span lang="ja">お・ば・さ・ん</span> with <span lang="ja">お・ば・あ・さ・ん</span>.

These are **guided worked examples**, not scored checkpoint questions. In the finished assessment, learners must answer fresh items before seeing the explanations.

### 4. Listen for meaning, not for printed letters

Return to the two real Chapter 02 Lesson 06 conversations. In one scene, two speakers clarify whether a photograph shows a house or a shop. In another, they clarify whether a family photograph shows an aunt or a grandmother. Their actual recorded dialogue, not a written spelling prompt, is the evidence for listening comprehension.

Check that you can explain why a long vowel adds a mora, count the nasal <span lang="ja">ん</span> as its own mora, find an answer in a printed source, and listen to a conversation for the speaker's intended meaning.
