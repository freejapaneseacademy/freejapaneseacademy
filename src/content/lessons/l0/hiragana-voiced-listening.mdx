## Listen for meaning, then notice the sounds

In this chapter, you learned that small marks change consonant sounds. Listening is different from identifying printed kana: you need to understand **what people say**, without looking at the spelling. Two short original scenes let you hear voiced and p-series sounds in familiar words. The recording is an AivisSpeech synthetic performance, not a real-world interview.

First listen without opening the transcript. Replay once for the main idea and once for details. The questions assess audible information, not whether you can name a written character.

## Track A — At a bakery

A customer asks about bread. The shopkeeper offers two kinds. Listen for **which one** the customer chooses, **how many**, and **what drink** they request. In the spoken word ぶどう (grapes), the beginning is voiced; in パン (bread), the beginning is a p-series sound. These examples help you connect the sounds to real meaning, but they are not spelling questions.

## Track B — At the zoo

Two visitors notice an animal, then another animal. Listen for **which animal comes first**, **what one visitor says about the elephant**, and **where they want to go next**. The words パンダ (panda), ぞう (elephant), and ぶた (pig) provide different sound families within a simple conversation.

## Listening practice and feedback

Each track has three multiple-choice questions. Try to answer by ear. After submitting, inspect the answer explanations and the exact spoken evidence. Revealing a transcript before scoring makes that attempt **assisted study**, not unaided listening proficiency. Assisted practice is useful, but it must not be confused with a blind listening score.

If audio playback is unavailable, use the optional transcript for reading practice and do not treat the result as proof of listening comprehension. Your progress is saved on this device; no microphone or account is required.

## Sound-and-meaning review

Compare the first sound of パン with the first sound of ぶどう, and notice the voiced ぞ in ぞう. Spoken Japanese can contain the same sounds in many different words; the question is always what the conversation communicates. Revisit the written contrast board in Lesson 05 if you want to check the corresponding kana after listening.
