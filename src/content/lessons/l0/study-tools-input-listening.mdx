# Listen, then check

**Can-do:** Enter Japanese using a keyboard and control reading aids. This listening lesson asks you to understand **spoken classroom instructions**, not to recognize written characters or spell words from audio.

You will hear two original synthetic-voice conversations. In **Scene A**, a learner asks how to begin Japanese input and follows a teacher's first two steps. In **Scene B**, a learner asks about a word's reading and how to choose a suitable kanji candidate.

## Listening routine

1. Play a recording without opening its transcript. Listen for the speaker's problem and the teacher's response.
2. Replay to catch the order of instructions. Words such as **まず** (“first”) and **次は** (“next”) signal the sequence.
3. Answer the three meaning questions for each scene. No printed spelling identification is required.
4. Submit, then reveal the Japanese transcript and English translation to check the exact spoken evidence.

Revealing a transcript before answering changes the attempt to **assisted practice**, not blind listening. Both approaches are useful, but only blind attempts represent unaided audio comprehension.

## Scene A — Beginning Japanese input

One person wants to enter Japanese on a computer. The other gives a first action and then suggests a simple practice action. Listen for **what the learner wants**, **what comes first**, and **what comes next**. The dialogue does not promise identical keyboard menus on all devices.

## Scene B — Finding a reading

A learner does not know how to read a word. A teacher recommends a reading aid, then explains what to check when selecting kanji. Listen for **the problem**, **the suggested aid**, and **the criterion for choosing a candidate**.

## After listening: connect the instructions

**入力（にゅうりょく）** means entering text; **読み方（よみかた）** is a word's reading; **ふりがな** gives a reading; **候補（こうほ）** means candidate; **意味（いみ）** means meaning. These printed cues are deliberately placed *after* the listening setup: use them to review the dialogue once you have tried answering.

Do not confuse a **reading aid** with a **conversion candidate**. Furigana helps you read a written word, while conversion lets you choose a written form. In spoken advice, the teacher asks the learner to check **meaning** when choosing a candidate, not to select the first suggestion automatically.

## Reflect and retry

Without looking at the transcript, tell yourself the teacher's two steps in Scene A. Then describe what the teacher recommends in Scene B. Replay any part you missed, check the exact supporting line after submission, and try again.
