## Listen before you read

You have practiced recognizing small ゃ, ゅ and ょ in writing. Now listen to how these blended sounds occur in everyday speech. This is a **listening comprehension** lesson: the scored questions ask what the speakers mean, not which printed characters represent a sound.

Play each recording without revealing its transcript. Listen once for the overall situation and again for details. You can replay as many times as you need. Both recordings are original synthetic Japanese conversations, not interviews with real people.

## Track A — A café order

A customer chooses a drink. Listen for **what they order**, **whether they want it cold**, and **how many**. The spoken word ぎゅうにゅう means milk and contains two contracted combinations, ぎゅ and にゅ. You do not need to see these characters to answer the questions.

## Track B — A Sunday outing

Two people plan a visit. Listen for **where they will go**, **what they will do together**, and **the time**. The word いっしょ means together; the spoken しょ is one contracted sound. You can use context to understand the plan even when you miss a word.

## Practice and review

There are three questions for each recording. Submit your answers, then compare the evidence and explanations. If you reveal a transcript before submission, your attempt is assisted practice, **not** an unaided listening score. Assisted practice remains useful, especially when audio cannot play.

After listening, compare the sound of ぎゅ in ぎゅうにゅう and しょ in いっしょ with the chart from Lesson 05. The chart helps you study spelling *after* the listening task; it is not an answer sheet for hearing.
