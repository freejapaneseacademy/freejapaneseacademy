## Small marks, different morae

Mika and Ren are checking the labels for a beginner picture-card sorting game. The marks at the upper right of a kana change **which sound it represents**. The two short strokes **゛** are called *dakuten*; the small circle **゜** is *handakuten*. This is a written sound-and-sentence-function lesson. Hearing these contrasts from genuine recordings comes in Lesson 06, not in this quiz.

A **mora** is a timing unit in Japanese. In the basic table below, each marked kana represents one mora. The marks do not add an extra mora.

| Unmarked row | Dakuten row | Reading cues |
| --- | --- | --- |
| か き く け こ | が ぎ ぐ げ ご | ka/ki/ku/ke/ko → ga/gi/gu/ge/go |
| さ し す せ そ | ざ じ ず ぜ ぞ | sa/shi/su/se/so → za/ji/zu/ze/zo |
| た ち つ て と | だ ぢ づ で ど | ta/chi/tsu/te/to → da/ji/zu/de/do |
| は ひ ふ へ ほ | ば び ぶ べ ぼ | ha/hi/fu/he/ho → ba/bi/bu/be/bo |
| は ひ ふ へ ほ | ぱ ぴ ぷ ぺ ぽ | ha/hi/fu/he/ho → pa/pi/pu/pe/po |

The **p-series** comes from the は row with a *circle*, not the two-stroke mark: **は → ば** (*ha → ba*) versus **は → ぱ** (*ha → pa*). Do not confuse **ば** and **ぱ** just because the base shape is the same. A mark is part of the written character, not a separate word.

## The two common ji/zu spellings

Both **じ** and **ぢ** are commonly pronounced *ji* in standard modern Japanese; both **ず** and **づ** are commonly pronounced *zu*. The spelling still matters. You cannot freely swap them: **ちぢむ** (*chijimu*, “to shrink”) uses **ぢ**, and **つづく** (*tsuzuku*, “to continue”) uses **づ**. For most beginner words, **じ** and **ず** are more frequent. Dialects and historical pronunciation distinctions exist, but this lesson does not test them.

The romanized reading cues are temporary support; Japanese words are written in Japanese scripts. Do not infer a different word merely from English transliteration.

## A mark changes a word, not the sentence rule

Ren places a picture of a **key** beside **かぎ** (*kagi*). The unvoiced **かき** (*kaki*) can mean a persimmon or oyster; it is **not** another spelling of “key.” Compare **は** (*ha*, a kana or a particle pronounced *wa* when marking a topic), **ば** (*ba*) and **ぱ** (*pa*): these three written forms are distinct, but they do not by themselves determine a sentence's politeness or function.

Mika writes **かぎをみてください。** (*Kagi o mite kudasai.* — “Please look at the key.”) Here **かぎ** is the object being looked at, **を** marks that object, and **みてください** makes a polite request. The **ぎ** in **かぎ** changes the word's pronunciation and identity; it does **not** change the function of **を**.

Ren writes **ぱんはどれですか。** (*Pan wa dore desu ka.* — “Which one is the bread?”). **は** is the topic particle, pronounced *wa* in this use; **どれ** means “which one,” and **か** marks the question. **ぱん** is a hiragana **practice spelling** of a loanword normally written **パン**. The p-mark on **ぱ** does not make the sentence a question; the sentence ending does.

| Printed example | Function | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **かぎ** | Object label | Names a key; no request is made |
| **かぎをみてください。** | Polite request | **を** marks the object; **みてください** requests looking |
| **ぱんはどれですか。** | Question | **は** introduces the topic; **どれですか** asks which one |

## Guided practice

First, cover the right column of the sound table and write **が ざ だ ば ぱ** from the five corresponding base kana. Next, point to the marked kana in **みず** (water), **ともだち** (friend), **ぶた** (pig) and **ぱん** (bread, practice spelling). Finally, decide whether each example above is a label, request or question.

**Common mistake:** Saying that **ば** and **ぱ** are interchangeable because both come from **は**. They are different sounds and written kana. Another mistake is saying **じ** and **ぢ** can always replace each other because their standard pronunciations often coincide. Conventional spelling must be learned with the word.
