## Read a short keyboard guide

**Can-do:** Enter Japanese using a keyboard and control reading aids. In this lesson, your job is to **read instructions** and point to the exact words that support your answer. You are not being tested on how fast you type. The original guide below is a classroom handout for a fictional beginner learner; menus vary by device.

### Before reading: useful signs

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning in this guide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 入力 | にゅうりょく | input / entering text |
| ひらがな | ひらがな | hiragana |
| 変換 | へんかん | conversion (selecting a written candidate) |
| 候補 | こうほ | candidate |
| 読み方 | よみかた | how to read a word |
| ふりがな | ふりがな | small kana showing a reading |
| 確認 | かくにん | checking |

You only need to **recognize** these kanji in the printed handout. Do not memorize their strokes. A printed label and its English gloss are provided to make this an accessible Level 0 reading task.

## Classroom handout: 日本語の入力ガイド

*Japanese input guide — original teaching text, with English support below each step.*

**① まず、日本語の入力をえらびます。**

First, select Japanese input. Here **まず** means “first,” and **えらびます** means “select.”

**② ローマ字で `neko` と入力すると、「ねこ」と出ます。**

When you enter `neko` with romaji input, “ねこ” appears. The written result is explicitly shown in the quotation marks.

**③ 「ねこ」は、ひらがなのままでもいいです。**

“ねこ” may stay in hiragana. You do not have to convert every word into kanji.

**④ 漢字にしたいときは、変換の候補を見て、意味を確認します。**

When you want kanji, look at conversion candidates and check the meaning. **候補を見て** is “look at the candidates”; **意味を確認します** is “check the meaning.” The guide does not claim that every device has the same shortcut.

**⑤ 「日本語」の読み方は「にほんご」です。ふりがなは読み方を示します。**

The reading of 日本語 is にほんご. Furigana shows a reading; it is not an English translation or a new conversion candidate.

**⑥ 小さい「ゃ」と大きい「や」を確認しましょう。「きゃ」と「きや」はちがいます。**

Check small ゃ and full-size や. きゃ and きや are different written sequences. **ちがいます** means “differ.”

**⑦ こまったら、「よみかたを おしえて ください」と聞きましょう。**

If you have trouble, ask, “Please tell me the reading.” This is a polite classroom request, not a command to the computer.

### Reading strategy: find, quote, explain

For a question such as “Can ねこ stay in hiragana?”, first scan for **ねこ**, then quote **「ねこ」は、ひらがなのままでもいいです。** (step ③). Only then explain in English: “Yes, the guide explicitly permits hiragana.” A good answer includes both the detail and the printed evidence.

Try another: “What should you check when converting to kanji?” Step ④ says **意味を確認します** — check the meaning. Do not guess “choose the first candidate”; that instruction is absent.

### Compare three actions

**Input** produces kana from keyboard entry (step ②). **Conversion** lets you select an appropriate written candidate (step ④). **Furigana** displays the reading of a written word (step ⑤). These are related but not interchangeable.

The guide uses a **common romaji-input example**, not a promise about every operating system. Reading aids can also be displayed differently in different apps. ### Independent reading and review

Cover the English support and find the Japanese evidence for these three statements: “Japanese input comes first”; “a hiragana word can remain hiragana”; “a polite question asks for a reading.” Uncover the support to check. If you miss a detail, return to its numbered step before retrying.

**Optional ungraded extension:** Write a two-line English instruction for a classmate about entering `neko` and checking a conversion candidate. Compare your wording to steps ② and ④. No handwriting, typing-speed or speech proficiency is automatically graded here.
