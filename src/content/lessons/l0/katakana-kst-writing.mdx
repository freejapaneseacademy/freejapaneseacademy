## Your writing task: prepare a kiosk label

**Can-do:** Read and write katakana vowels and K/S/T rows. Imagine you are helping label products at a station kiosk. The printed models are your reference: copy a label by hand, then type it if you have Japanese input available. This is a writing lesson; no audio or automatic handwriting judgment is claimed.

## Review the twenty characters

| Set | a | i | u | e | o |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vowels | ア | イ | ウ | エ | オ |
| K | カ | キ | ク | ケ | コ |
| S | サ | シ | ス | セ | ソ |
| T | タ | チ | ツ | テ | ト |

In beginner romanization, the S row contains **shi** (シ), while the T row contains **chi** (チ) and **tsu** (ツ). Do not mechanically write *si*, *ti* or *tu* when reading these forms.

## Model, copy, cover, write

1. Look at the model **ココア** (*ko-ko-a*, cocoa). Point to コ, コ, ア in the table.
2. Copy **ココア** carefully on paper or in a note app. Keep each character distinct and in the same left-to-right order.
3. Cover the model. Write it once from memory, then reveal and compare. A missing final ア changes the label.
4. Repeat with **カカオ** (*ka-ka-o*, cacao), **アイス** (*a-i-su*, ice cream in this context), and **テキスト** (*te-ki-su-to*, text or textbook depending on context).

These are actual common katakana words. In contrast, **スシ** is a valid sound-form practice sequence but sushi is commonly written すし or 寿司 in ordinary Japanese; do not assume every Japanese word should be written in katakana.

## Shape checkpoints

**シ (shi) versus ツ (tsu):** Compare the orientation of the short marks and the long curved stroke in the *whole* character. Printed fonts vary. **ク (ku) versus ケ (ke):** ケ has an extra stroke. **ス (su) versus ツ (tsu):** the two are completely different shapes; read all strokes before deciding. **チ (chi) versus テ (te):** compare the upper horizontal marks and the lower stroke. These are recognition cues, not a verified formal stroke-order guide.

## Type with Japanese input

If you have a Japanese IME, type `kokoa`, `kakao`, `aisu`, or `tekisuto` and convert the result to katakana. Compare it character by character with the printed models; do not trust the first conversion suggestion blindly. If you cannot enable an IME, use the optional model-copy practice instead. The scored check below is accessible without an IME.

## A writing self-review rubric

For each copied word, award yourself **0, 1 or 2 points** on each criterion: **recognizability** (0 unreadable, 1 partly clear, 2 all characters identifiable), **sequence** (0 wrong order, 1 one error, 2 exact order), and **shape distinction** (0 confusable characters merged, 1 one uncertain pair, 2 clear distinctions). Maximum **6 self-review points per word**. This is a personal reflection, not a machine-verified handwriting score. Try one improvement, then copy again.

The eight scored questions check written character choice and spelling. Submit only after choosing an answer to every question; explanations appear after submission, and attempts are saved locally when browser storage is available.
