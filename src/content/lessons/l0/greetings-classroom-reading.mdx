## A greeting is a social action

**Can-do:** Greet someone appropriately and understand basic study instructions. In this reading lesson, you will interpret four **original fictional cards** pinned to the entrance and classroom noticeboard of a small Japanese study club. You will cite the actual Japanese wording, not guess from the English description. This is written reading practice; listening and spoken accuracy are not scored.

A greeting such as <span lang="ja">おはようございます</span> (*ohayō gozaimasu*, good morning, polite) is not a command. <span lang="ja">よんでください</span> (*yonde kudasai*, please read) **is** a request. The polite ending <span lang="ja">ください</span> makes a request courteous; it does not turn it into a greeting.

## Four cards at the study club

The cards below are the complete reading stimulus. They are fictional, and all Japanese lines are original to this lesson.

### Card A — morning welcome at the entrance

> <span lang="ja">あさのクラスへようこそ。</span>  
> <span lang="ja">おはようございます。</span>  
> <span lang="ja">せんせいと いっしょに べんきょうしましょう。</span>

*Welcome to the morning class. Good morning. Let's study together with the teacher.*

### Card B — greeting for the afternoon group

> <span lang="ja">ひるのクラスのみなさんへ。</span>  
> <span lang="ja">こんにちは。</span>  
> <span lang="ja">きょうも よろしくおねがいします。</span>

*To everyone in the daytime class. Hello. I look forward to working with you again today.*

### Card C — teacher's desk instruction

> <span lang="ja">せんせいからの おねがい。</span>  
> <span lang="ja">このカードを よんでください。</span>  
> <span lang="ja">つぎに、なまえを かいてください。</span>

*A request from the teacher. Please read this card. Next, please write your name.*

### Card D — thank-you note after class

> <span lang="ja">きょうは ありがとうございました。</span>  
> <span lang="ja">また あした。</span>

*Thank you for today. See you tomorrow.*

## How to cite evidence

When a question asks **which card contains a reading instruction**, scan for the verb <span lang="ja">よんで</span> (*yonde*, read) and the request marker <span lang="ja">ください</span>. Card C says <span lang="ja">このカードを よんでください</span> (*kono kādo o yonde kudasai*, please read this card). That full phrase is evidence. By contrast, Card A's <span lang="ja">べんきょうしましょう</span> (*benkyō shimashō*) invites everyone to study together; it does not tell the reader to write a name.

When a question asks **who a message addresses**, inspect the heading: <span lang="ja">ひるのクラスのみなさんへ</span> means *to everyone in the daytime class*. The particle <span lang="ja">へ</span> here marks the addressee and is pronounced *e*. The polite greeting <span lang="ja">こんにちは</span> is written with final は, pronounced *wa* in this fixed greeting. Do not rewrite it as <span lang="ja">こんにちわ</span> in standard spelling.

## Reading clues and common traps

| Japanese | Reading | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">おはようございます</span> | ohayō gozaimasu | polite morning greeting |
| <span lang="ja">こんにちは</span> | konnichiwa | daytime greeting |
| <span lang="ja">よんでください</span> | yonde kudasai | polite request to read |
| <span lang="ja">かいてください</span> | kaite kudasai | polite request to write |
| <span lang="ja">ありがとうございました</span> | arigatō gozaimashita | thanks for something completed |
| <span lang="ja">また あした</span> | mata ashita | see you tomorrow |

Notice that Card D uses <span lang="ja">ありがとうございました</span> after class, while Card A welcomes learners **before** class. Card C has two actions in sequence: read **first**, write **next** (<span lang="ja">つぎに</span>). Do not assume every polite expression is a request.

## Guided practice

Try finding the exact wording for “write your name.” The answer is <span lang="ja">なまえを かいてください</span> on Card C. The key word <span lang="ja">なまえ</span> means *name*. Next, locate “see you tomorrow”: Card D's <span lang="ja">また あした</span>. These are examples of **quoting the text**, not translating freely.
