## Listen first, then check

In this lesson, the listening questions ask **what people say**, not what a written character looks like. Play each original Japanese recording at normal speed without opening its transcript. Replay as often as needed. Try to understand the situation and immediately stated details before checking your answers.

## Track A — At a bookstore

A customer asks for a notebook and the clerk offers one. Listen for the requested item, its color, and the quantity. The spoken expression **いっさつ** counts one book or notebook; **ちょっと** means a little or a moment. Both have a brief consonant hold represented by small っ in writing. These hints are for review **after** your first listening attempt.

## Track B — Where to meet

Two people arrange a meeting. The first suggested location is corrected, so listen carefully to the **final** location. Hear the meeting time and whether someone intends to arrive early. **がっこう** (school) has a small っ and a long vowel; **こうえん** (park) has a long vowel. You can answer by meaning even if you cannot yet transcribe every sound.

## Dictation and sound review

After submitting your comprehension answers, replay one short turn and write down what you hear on paper or in a private note. Compare your attempt with the optional transcript. In **いっさつ**, **ちょっと**, and **がっこう**, small っ marks one mora of consonant closure. In **こうえん** and **がっこう**, the written う extends the preceding *o* sound. Do not assume that all long *o* sounds are spelled おう: the spelling is word-specific. This is self-check dictation, not an automatically scored pronunciation assessment.

## Scores and accessibility

Six single-choice questions are scored from audible information (three per track). Revealing the transcript switches your work to **assisted practice**, not an unaided listening score. The native audio players offer replay and adjustable speed, and the transcripts support learners who cannot hear the recordings. No microphone is required. Progress is stored on this device.
