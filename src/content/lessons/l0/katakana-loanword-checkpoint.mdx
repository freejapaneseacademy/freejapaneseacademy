## Can-do: read everyday loanwords and understand a station visit

This checkpoint completes Chapter 04 by combining **written katakana recognition**, **reading for specific details**, and **listening for directly stated information**. Allow about 30 minutes. You may finish regardless of your score; if you score below 80%, review the missed forms and try again.

### 1. Notice the length and the small characters

| Pattern | Example | Meaning | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Long vowel ー | <span lang="ja">コーヒー</span> | coffee | Each bar lengthens the preceding vowel by one mora. |
| Small ィ | <span lang="ja">ティー</span> | tea | ティ represents a borrowed sound not written with a full-sized イ. |
| Small ァ | <span lang="ja">ファイル</span> | file/folder | ファ is one borrowed-sound combination. |
| Small ェ and ッ | <span lang="ja">チェック</span> | check | チェ uses small ェ; ッ marks the following consonant's timing. |

**Common mistakes:** <span lang="ja">テイー</span> is not the conventional spelling of ティー; <span lang="ja">フアイル</span> uses a full-sized ア rather than the small ァ of ファイル. The long-vowel bar ー is not a hyphen or a small ッ. English spellings alone do not predict Japanese mora timing.

### 2. Read the original fictional café menu

A fictional station café displays this simple menu. Prices are invented for reading practice, not actual offers.

| Menu item | Price |
| --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">コーヒー</span> — coffee | 300円 |
| <span lang="ja">ティー</span> — tea | 250円 |
| <span lang="ja">ケーキ</span> — cake | 450円 |

A nearby direction card reads **<span lang="ja">タクシー → 駅の前</span>** (taxis → in front of the station). A lost-property note says **<span lang="ja">ファイルのわすれもの</span>** (a forgotten file folder). Find the exact written phrase that supports each answer. Do not assume that the café sells anything not listed or that the sign gives a taxi timetable.

### 3. Listen to two original announcements

**Track A — station café:** The announcement states which drinks and food are available and where to order tea. **Track B — station services:** It mentions taxis, ticket assistance and a forgotten folder. These are the original licensed AivisSpeech recordings from L06; no new recordings or invented human pronunciation review are implied. Publication requires their actual WAV bytes, manifest, rights metadata and checksums to pass automated verification.

First listen without a transcript. Then answer the listening questions from the spoken words. Opening transcripts before submitting makes this an **assisted** attempt; both attempt types are recorded separately. After submitting, check the transcript against your answer and replay the exact phrase. For example, <span lang="ja">カウンターで</span> means “at the counter,” while <span lang="ja">駅の前</span> means “in front of the station.”

### 4. Review, write and optionally speak

After checking the 13 questions, type **コーヒー・ティー・ファイル・チェック** with a Japanese IME. Compare each small kana and ー with the models above. Write a fictional two-item menu with prices, then self-check: (1) recognizable katakana, (2) correct long vowels, (3) small kana where needed, (4) clear item/price pairing. This written practice is ungraded.

Optionally replay one announcement and shadow a short phrase privately. No microphone access or automatic pronunciation scoring is required for this checkpoint.
