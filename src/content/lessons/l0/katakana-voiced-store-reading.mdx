## Your reading mission

**Chapter Can-do:** Complete basic katakana and begin recognising voiced sound families. This is an **original written reading** lesson: you will read store labels and identify explicit details, then quote the exact printed word that proves each answer. No audio or pronunciation ability is assessed.

Imagine a fictional small shop. Ren is shopping for a pen and a notebook. A printed board separates stationery from food. Read the board before answering.

## The original shop board

| Printed sign | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **ブング** | Stationery (a shortened shop-category label; the more usual full term is 文房具 / ぶんぼうぐ) |
| **ペン** | Pen |
| **ノート** | Notebook |
| **カバン** | Bag |
| **パン** | Bread |
| **ソース** | Sauce |
| **コップ** | Cup |

**Important:** The board is a simplified learning display, not a claim that every Japanese store uses these exact headings. **ブング** is a pedagogical katakana rendering of *bungu*; ordinary Japanese signage commonly uses **文具**. We use it here only to practise reading voiced kana.

### Shelf note

> <span lang="ja">ペン・ノート・カバン</span> — stationery shelf  
> <span lang="ja">パン・ソース・コップ</span> — food and kitchen shelf

The middle dot **・** separates labels. It is not part of a product name. The two shelf lines are **written evidence**: if someone asks where to look for a notebook, cite **ノート** in the first line, not a guess about typical shop layouts.

## Three reading clues

**1. Dakuten:** **ブ** in ブング and **バ** in カバン carry two small marks. They represent *bu* and *ba*. Removing the marks would change the written syllables. **グ** in ブング is also voiced.

**2. Handakuten:** **パ** in パン and **プ** in コップ carry a small circle. The circle changes the H-row kana to a P-row sound. In コップ, **ッ** is a small tsu; it is not a full-size ツ.

**3. ン versus ソ:** **ペン** and **カバン** end with **ン** (*n*), while **ソース** begins with **ソ** (*so*). Look at the actual characters in the sign. Do not assume two similar shapes mean the same syllable.

The long vowel bar **ー** in ノート and ソース lengthens the preceding vowel. This chapter focuses on reading the label accurately; a later chapter studies long vowels in detail.

## Work with evidence

A customer asks, “Where is the bag?” The evidence is **カバン** in the stationery line. If asked which food label begins with ソ, the evidence is **ソース** in the food-and-kitchen line. A strong answer gives both the **meaning** and the **exact printed word**.

Read each shelf line from left to right. Find the symbol or word first, then decide its meaning. Do not rely on the English column alone when distinguishing ン/ソ or the sound marks.
