# Listen for meaning, not spelling

Japanese has a steady rhythm: each *mora* takes a beat. A long vowel takes two beats, the small っ marks a brief closure before the next consonant, and ん occupies its own beat. In this lesson, **listen for what two people actually mean**. You do not need to identify written characters by ear.

## Before you listen

You will hear two short conversations with a woman and a man. In the first, a customer buys stamps at a post office. In the second, two friends plan what to bring to a picnic. The recordings are original synthetic Japanese speech using licensed AivisSpeech voices. The written scripts and English translations are available **after** your first listening attempt.

Try this routine for each recording:

1. Listen once without reading. Identify where the speakers are and what they want.
2. Listen again for a number, destination, object or description.
3. Answer the comprehension questions using only the recording.
4. After submitting, reveal the transcript and check which exact spoken phrase supported your answer.

You can replay as often as you wish. Revealing the transcript before scoring switches the attempt to **assisted practice**; it is still useful learning, but it is not proof of blind listening comprehension.

## Scene A — At the post office

A customer and a counter worker exchange a request and a follow-up question. Listen for the requested item, the number of items and where the customer intends to send something. Do not look for written kana: these are questions about the meaning of what the speakers say.

## Scene B — Preparing for a picnic

Two friends discuss a plan for tomorrow. Listen for what one person plans to bring, the size of a container and whether an item will fit. Pay attention to the final response: a speaker may disagree with one detail but add a reassuring contrast.

## Rhythm to revisit *after* answering

Once you have checked your listening answers, replay both tracks and use the revealed transcripts to examine the rhythm. A long vowel lasts two morae; small っ marks a brief consonant closure; and ん has its own timing unit. These are useful sound-awareness cues, but your blind score is based on the conversations' meanings, **not** on identifying printed characters.

Common mistakes include guessing from familiar words instead of listening to the complete reply, assuming a yes/no answer is the whole story, and confusing a long vowel with a short one. Replay the spoken exchange before checking the evidence.

## Guided self-check

After answering, explain in your own words: What was requested at the counter? What is the destination? Why is the picnic bag answer more informative than simply “no”? Locate the supporting spoken turn when you reveal the transcript.

**No automatic pronunciation grade is given.** This is a listening lesson; optional speaking rehearsal belongs to the next speaking step. Your audio recordings, if any, are not requested or uploaded here.
