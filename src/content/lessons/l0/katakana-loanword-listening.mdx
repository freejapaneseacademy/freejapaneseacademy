## Can-do: understand a short station announcement

You are visiting a fictional Japanese station. Two **original recorded announcements** give information about the station café and travel services. Your goal is to answer practical questions from **what you hear**, without needing to see the Japanese script first. Listen to both recordings before revealing transcripts.

## Before listening: prepare the situation, not the answers

A station may have a café, a counter and a taxi area. An announcement often starts with **お知らせです** (“This is an announcement”). Listen for *what is offered*, *where to go*, and *who can help*. Do not assume that a café sells every drink or that a train departure time was mentioned.

The lesson revisits three katakana patterns from the chapter: **ー** extends the preceding vowel, as in **コーヒー** (coffee) and **タクシー** (taxi); **ティ** uses a small ィ in **ティー** (tea); **ファ** uses a small ァ in **ファイル** (file/folder). These are *written support cues*, not substitutes for listening. English loanwords do not guarantee English pronunciation.

## Listening sequence

**First pass — global meaning.** Play Track A once and decide whether it is about food service or train departures. Play Track B once and decide whether it concerns station services or a classroom. Do not reveal the transcripts yet.

**Second pass — focused details.** Replay Track A to hear the two drinks, the extra food and the place to order. Replay Track B for the taxi location, whom to ask about tickets, and the lost item. Answer the six questions below. The audio is original synthetic Japanese; you can replay it and adjust speed.

**Third pass — evidence check.** After submitting, reveal the Japanese transcript and English translation for each recording. Match each answer to its spoken phrase. If you reveal a transcript *before* submitting, the exercise becomes assisted practice and does not count as unaided listening mastery.

## Listening and language notes

The café uses **あります** (“there is/are”) for available things. **も** (“also”) introduces an additional item. **カウンターで** specifies the place where an action happens: at the counter. **ご注文ください** is a polite request to order.

The station announcement uses **駅の前** (“in front of the station”), **については** (“as for / regarding”) and **おたずねください** (“please ask”). **わすれもの** means a forgotten or lost item. These expressions help with comprehension, but the scored task tests **spoken facts**, not advanced grammar production.

## After the listening check

Try to type the heard loanwords with a Japanese IME: **コーヒー**, **ティー**, **ケーキ**, **タクシー**, **チケット**, **ファイル**. Check ー and the small ィ/ァ against the revealed transcript. This typing activity is ungraded; writing is developed in L07. Optionally repeat one short announcement aloud for personal practice, without claiming pronunciation certification; dedicated speaking practice is L08.

**Self-check:** Can you name the café items, explain where taxis are, and identify the lost object without a transcript? If not, replay and use assisted practice. No train times, ticket prices or actual station services are asserted by these fictional announcements.
