## Can-do: talk about what to practise next

This optional speaking lesson is a rehearsal, **not a pronunciation examination**. The original L06 recordings are the model. Listen first without looking at the printed turns, then repeat with the speaker and finally switch roles. If the recordings are unavailable, you may practise the written dialogue but must not count it as audio shadowing. You can complete this lesson without recording yourself.

### 1. Before class: name one difficult point

A teacher asks: <span lang="ja">きょうは、なにを れんしゅうしますか。</span> — “What will you practise today?” The learner answers <span lang="ja">ひらがなを れんしゅうします。</span> — “I will practise hiragana.”

The teacher checks for a notebook: <span lang="ja">ノートは ありますか。</span> — “Do you have a notebook?” The learner replies <span lang="ja">はい、あります。でも、ちいさい「つ」が むずかしいです。</span> — “Yes, I do. But the small tsu is difficult.” The teacher proposes <span lang="ja">ゆっくり れんしゅうしましょう。</span> — “Let's practise slowly.”

**Notice:** **でも** introduces a contrast. **むずかしいです** reports difficulty, not failure. The small **っ** marks a consonant closure in many words; do not pronounce it as a full extra “tsu” syllable in words such as <span lang="ja">きって</span> (stamp).

### 2. After class: say when and how

The second speaker says <span lang="ja">カタカナは だいじょうぶです。でも、ながい おとが むずかしいです。</span> — “Katakana is okay, but long sounds are difficult.” Here **だいじょうぶ** means manageable in this context; it is not a formal test result.

The teacher asks <span lang="ja">あしたも れんしゅうしますか。</span> — “Will you practise tomorrow too?” The reply is <span lang="ja">はい。あした、ノートを みて、もういちど れんしゅうします。</span> — “Yes. Tomorrow I'll look at my notebook and practise once more.”

**Useful model:** <span lang="ja">あした、もういちど れんしゅうします。</span> — “Tomorrow I will practise once more.” It is fine to rehearse this exact model before attempting a personal variation.

### 3. A three-pass shadowing routine

**Pass one — listen:** play each original recording without looking at the text and identify the topic. **Pass two — pause and echo:** replay, pause after each turn, and repeat at a comfortable pace. **Pass three — change roles:** say the teacher's lines first, then the learner's. Aim for understandable phrase boundaries rather than an invented numeric fluency score.

For the small-っ contrast, compare <span lang="ja">きて</span> (“come,” て-form) and <span lang="ja">きって</span> (“stamp”). For length, compare <span lang="ja">おばさん</span> (“aunt”) and <span lang="ja">おばあさん</span> (“grandmother”). These are written practice contrasts, **not** extra audio tracks; do not claim you heard them in the recordings.

### 4. Optional private rehearsal

You may opt into microphone recording below. Permission is requested only when you press Start. Playback stays in the current tab, and you can discard the recording. No upload, speech recognition, automatic accent score, or teacher evaluation is performed. If the browser denies permission, practise without a microphone.

Use the self-check for four things: complete phrases, time words, careful small-っ/long-vowel timing, and turn-taking. The eight multiple-choice questions below check **written meaning and context only**; their score is not a measure of spoken ability.

### 5. Make tomorrow manageable

Write a private one-line plan such as “Tomorrow: five minutes of small っ, then revisit one long-vowel word.” You can use your own notebook. After practice, mark one thing you want to revisit; you do not need to share personal information. Return to L06 for listening comprehension or L07 for supported typing.
