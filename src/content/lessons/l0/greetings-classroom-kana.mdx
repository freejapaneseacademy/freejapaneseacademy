## Can-do: recognize and write useful classroom Japanese

In this script lesson you will read and type the **hiragana** of greetings and simple classroom instructions. You are not expected to know kanji or write a full conversation. These are original classroom examples. Reading printed words is **not** a substitute for listening practice; the chapter's L06 provides real audio.

Imagine that a teacher has written a small welcome board. You arrive in the morning and read:

> <span lang="ja">おはようございます。</span>  
> <span lang="ja">みてください。</span>  
> <span lang="ja">ありがとうございます。</span>

The first line greets you politely, the second asks you to look, and the third expresses thanks. Each function is different even though all are polite.

## 1. Notice the kana in three greetings

| Japanese | Reading support | Meaning and writing detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">おはよう</span> | ohayō | Good morning (casual); よ + う represents a long /o/ sound |
| <span lang="ja">おはようございます</span> | ohayō gozaimasu | Polite good morning; keep all of ございます |
| <span lang="ja">こんにちは</span> | konnichiwa | Hello / good afternoon; final **は** is pronounced *wa* |
| <span lang="ja">ありがとう</span> | arigatō | Thanks; と + う represents a long /o/ sound |
| <span lang="ja">ありがとうございます</span> | arigatō gozaimasu | Polite thanks; do not omit う or い |

The long vowel in **よう** and **とう** is written with **う** in these hiragana words, not the katakana long-vowel mark ー. The spelling **こんにちわ** may appear informally, but **こんにちは** is the standard spelling taught here. The final は has the pronunciation *wa* because it is historically a particle in this fixed greeting. Do not generalize that every は sounds *wa*.

**Spot the parts:** お・は・よ・う / ご・ざ・い・ま・す. The dakuten marks in **ざ** turn さ into ざ. Copying a mark on the wrong kana changes the word.

## 2. Read four requests on a teacher's board

| Written request | Reading support | What you should do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">みてください。</span> | mite kudasai | Please look |
| <span lang="ja">きいてください。</span> | kiite kudasai | Please listen |
| <span lang="ja">よんでください。</span> | yonde kudasai | Please read |
| <span lang="ja">かいてください。</span> | kaite kudasai | Please write |

The ending **ください** is shared. Notice the verb forms before it: **みて**, **きいて**, **よんで**, **かいて**. The voiced **で** in よんで is not interchangeable with **て**. These are whole classroom phrases for now; full verb conjugation comes later.

A particularly important contrast is:

- <span lang="ja">きてください。</span> (*kite kudasai*) — Please come.
- <span lang="ja">きいてください。</span> (*kiite kudasai*) — Please listen.

The extra **い** is meaningful. If a teacher points to a sound clip and says きいてください, listen; do not walk over to the teacher.

## 3. Small っ and a longer instruction

<span lang="ja">もういちど いってください。</span> (*mō ichido itte kudasai*) means “Please say it one more time.” Here **もういちど** means once more. **いって** has a small **っ**, signalling a held consonant before て. Compare **いって** with **いて**: the missing small っ changes the written form and can change meaning. This example is about orthographic recognition, not scored pronunciation.

## 4. Guided typing and optional tracing

Enable a Japanese input method (IME) or copy kana for practice. Try these steps in order:

1. Read **おはようございます** and find the two-character sequence **よう**. Type it without omitting う.
2. Read **こんにちは** and circle the final **は**. Say to yourself “written は, pronounced wa here.”
3. Copy **きいてください** and **きてください** side by side. Underline the extra い in the listening request.
4. Copy **よんでください** and underline the voiced で. Optionally trace the kana on paper; this is ungraded handwriting, not an automatic stroke assessment.
5. Read **もういちど いってください** and notice the small っ. You can rehearse aloud privately, but no pronunciation score is claimed.

### Tiny original board exercise

A fictional teacher writes:

> <span lang="ja">① みてください。 ② よんでください。 ③ かいてください。</span>

You have a greeting card in front of you. At ① look at it; at ② read it; at ③ write the greeting. The **order** and the written verbs tell you what to do. These examples are invented for this lesson, not quotations from a textbook.

## 5. Common errors and review

| Mistake | Better form | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">こんにちわ</span> | <span lang="ja">こんにちは</span> | Standard greeting ends in は |
| <span lang="ja">おはよございます</span> | <span lang="ja">おはようございます</span> | Keep う in よう |
| <span lang="ja">きてください</span> for “please listen” | <span lang="ja">きいてください</span> | The extra い matters |
| <span lang="ja">よんてください</span> | <span lang="ja">よんでください</span> | Voiced で is required |
| <span lang="ja">いてください</span> for “please say” | <span lang="ja">いってください</span> | Small っ is required |
