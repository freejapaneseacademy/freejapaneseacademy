## A picture-card game at the reading club

**Chapter Can-do:** Recognize and produce combinations such as <span lang="ja">きゃ・しゅ・ちょ</span> and related contracted kana. Today is a **written context introduction**. Later lessons teach more sound families and recorded listening. No spoken accuracy is assessed here.

At a neighborhood reading club, **Yui** prepares three word cards and **Sora** matches each card to a drawing. Their tutor **Aki** checks whether the small kana have been printed correctly. The purpose is to connect a familiar written word to its picture **without overlooking the size of ゃ・ゅ・ょ**.

| Person | Role | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yui | Learner | Prepares three written word cards |
| Sora | Learner | Matches words to picture clues |
| Aki | Tutor | Checks the size of the kana |

## What changes when the kana is small?

Compare <span lang="ja">きや</span> (*ki-ya*, two separate morae) with <span lang="ja">きゃ</span> (*kya*, one blended mora). The first spelling contains a full-sized <span lang="ja">や</span>; the second contains a small <span lang="ja">ゃ</span>. The difference is meaningful, not decoration.

In these combinations, an *i*-row kana such as <span lang="ja">き・し・ち</span> is followed by small <span lang="ja">ゃ・ゅ・ょ</span>. The two written signs together represent **one mora**, a rhythmic unit of Japanese. Thus <span lang="ja">しゅ</span> (*shu*) is one mora, not *shi-yu*. This does **not** mean the signs become one character: they remain two written kana.

| Blended pair | Reading cue | Compare with separate kana |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">きゃ</span> | kya | <span lang="ja">きや</span> = ki-ya |
| <span lang="ja">しゅ</span> | shu | <span lang="ja">しゆ</span> = shi-yu |
| <span lang="ja">ちょ</span> | cho | <span lang="ja">ちよ</span> = chi-yo |

The full-sized comparison strings are **decoding examples**, not claims that they are common standalone words. Japanese timing is taught here through written contrasts; real listening practice comes in Lesson 06.

## Three cards on the table

Yui writes three ordinary Japanese words in hiragana. Aki checks the small kana and Sora chooses the matching picture.

| Word card | Reading cue | Picture clue | Notice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">きゃく</span> | kyaku | a guest visiting the club | <span lang="ja">きゃ</span> uses small ゃ |
| <span lang="ja">しゅくだい</span> | shukudai | homework on a desk | <span lang="ja">しゅ</span> uses small ゅ |
| <span lang="ja">ちょきん</span> | chokin | savings in a piggy bank | <span lang="ja">ちょ</span> uses small ょ |

These are real words: <span lang="ja">きゃく</span> means a guest or customer (depending on context); <span lang="ja">しゅくだい</span> means homework; <span lang="ja">ちょきん</span> means savings. They may also appear with kanji, such as 客, 宿題 and 貯金. **You do not need to memorize these kanji now.**

Aki points to the homework picture and says <span lang="ja">しゅくだいです。</span> — “It's homework.” This simple sentence identifies the card. The polite ending <span lang="ja">です</span> does not change the small <span lang="ja">ゅ</span> in the noun.

## Guided rehearsal

**First**, find the smaller second kana in <span lang="ja">きゃ・しゅ・ちょ</span>. **Next**, read the picture clues and find the guest, homework and savings cards. **Then**, compare <span lang="ja">しゅ</span> and <span lang="ja">しゆ</span>: the first is one blended mora, the second represents two separate morae. **Finally**, name who prepared the cards, who matched the pictures and who checked the kana sizes.

A common mistake is to treat small <span lang="ja">ゃ</span> as a full-sized <span lang="ja">や</span>. That changes the reading. Another is to assume every two written kana represent two morae; contracted combinations are an important exception. The precise natural pronunciation will be supported by licensed recordings in the later listening lesson, not simulated by a browser voice.

## Check your understanding

Answer eight written questions about the club, the small-kana contrasts and the three word cards. Each question has one keyed answer and an explanation. This is a **written recognition** score, not a pronunciation grade or official JLPT score.
