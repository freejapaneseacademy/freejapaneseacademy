## A tiny classroom exchange

You are writing labels for **fictional** people and objects at a classroom desk. Your goal is one short statement and one short question. No real name or personal information is needed.

> ミナ：これは ペンです。 — This is a pen.  
> リオ：これは ノートですか。 — Is this a notebook?  
> ミナ：はい、ノートです。 — Yes, it's a notebook.

The second line asks for confirmation; the third answers it. The topic **これ** means “this (one)” in this context. In Japanese, a topic can be omitted when already clear, so **ノートです** is a natural short answer.

## 1. Build a statement

| Slot | Choose | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | これ / わたし / あのひと | Topic: this / I / that person |
| は | は | Topic marker, pronounced *wa* |
| B | ペン / がくせい / せんせい | Pen / student / teacher |
| です | です | Polite noun-predicate ending |

Match a sensible A and B: **これは ペンです。** (“This is a pen.”), **わたしは がくせいです。** (“I am a student.”), or **あのひとは せんせいです。** (“That person is a teacher.”). These are example fictional contexts, not facts about you.

**Spelling check:** the topic particle is **は**, not わ. It is pronounced *wa* in this role. The direct-object particle **を** belongs to a different construction with many verbs; **これは ペンです** does not use を.

## 2. Ask instead of telling

Compare **これは ノートです。** (“This is a notebook.”) with **これは ノートですか。** (“Is this a notebook?”). The final **か** changes the speech act from statement to polite question. Do not put か before です.

Short answers: **はい、ノートです。** (“Yes, it's a notebook.”) and **いいえ、ノートではありません。** (“No, it isn't a notebook.”). The negative expression is provided for recognition only; you do not need to produce it in this task.

## 3. Kana and typing checkpoints

**ノート** uses the katakana long-vowel mark **ー**; **ペン** has a small circle on ペ. **がくせい** begins with voiced が, and **せんせい** includes ん. Use a Japanese IME to type the whole sentence. Some keyboards produce わ when you type *wa*; type *ha* to enter the written particle は, or correct the conversion before saving. Spaces in the models are teaching aids; Japanese normally does not separate every word with spaces.

Try a controlled copy: **これは ペンです。** Then change **ペン** to **ノート**, preserving は and です. Finally add **か** to make the second line a question. If you prefer handwriting, copy the lines on paper; no automatic handwriting accuracy claim is made.

## 4. Your two-line writing task

Imagine a pen and a notebook on a fictional classroom table. On line one, identify the pen. On line two, ask whether the other object is a notebook. Write only two short Japanese sentences; you may include or omit beginner-friendly spaces.

A suitable model is:

> これは ペンです。  
> これは ノートですか。

Other contextually appropriate objects and questions are possible, but check that the question actually ends in **ですか**. Your text stays in your browser unless you explicitly save it to local device storage; it is not sent for teacher grading.

## 5. Compare, revise, and explain

Use the interactive draft and four-criterion self-review below. Look for **(1)** a clear topic and sensible noun, **(2)** written は rather than わ or を, **(3)** a correct final か only on the question, and **(4)** accurate kana, katakana long marks and polite です. This is learner self-assessment, **not** a graded writing proficiency judgment.

Common repairs:

- **これわ ペンです。** → **これは ペンです。** The topic particle is spelled は.
- **これは ノトですか。** → **これは ノートですか。** Keep the long mark.
- **これは ペンかです。** → **これは ペンですか。** Put か after です.

After composing, try reading the two sentences aloud slowly if comfortable; speaking is optional and unscored. The eight scored items below check written recognition, not the quality of your original composition.
