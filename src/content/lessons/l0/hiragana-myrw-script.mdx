## Mio and Yuri complete the classroom kana board

Mio is filling the final sixteen spaces on a basic-hiragana chart. Yuri has six picture cards waiting for labels. First **recognize** the characters, then **type** them with a Japanese IME or kana keyboard. You may copy them on paper, but handwriting is self-reviewed, not automatically graded.

## Observe the sixteen models

| Row | Written hiragana and reading |
| --- | --- |
| M | **ま** ma · **み** mi · **む** mu · **め** me · **も** mo |
| Y | **や** ya · **ゆ** yu · **よ** yo |
| R | **ら** ra · **り** ri · **る** ru · **れ** re · **ろ** ro |
| W | **わ** wa · **を** wo (object particle, normally pronounced *o*) |
| Nasal | **ん** n (independent mora) |

The Y row has only three ordinary modern basic kana: や, ゆ and よ. The W row here has わ and を; ん is listed separately. These sixteen characters finish the standard forty-six basic hiragana.

## Shape contrasts: notice the endings

Compare **め / ぬ**: <span lang="ja">め</span> (*me*) lacks the final loop of <span lang="ja">ぬ</span> (*nu*). Compare **る / ろ**: <span lang="ja">る</span> (*ru*) ends in a small loop; <span lang="ja">ろ</span> (*ro*) does not. Compare **わ / れ / ね**: <span lang="ja">わ</span> (*wa*), <span lang="ja">れ</span> (*re*) and <span lang="ja">ね</span> (*ne*) have different right-hand endings. Compare **ま / も**: <span lang="ja">ま</span> (*ma*) and <span lang="ja">も</span> (*mo*) have different lower shapes. Fonts and handwriting styles vary; these are visual comparisons, not stroke-order diagrams.

The kana <span lang="ja">を</span> (*wo* in charts) is used as the object particle, normally pronounced *o* in modern Japanese: <span lang="ja">ほんをよむ。</span> means “read a book.” The vowel kana <span lang="ja">お</span> is different, as in <span lang="ja">おに</span> (“ogre”). The kana <span lang="ja">ん</span> is one independent mora: <span lang="ja">ほん</span> is ほ・ん, **two** morae. Its sound varies with neighboring sounds; this is a written-script lesson, not a pronunciation test.

## Guided typing ladder

1. **Look and copy:** <span lang="ja">まみむめも</span>, <span lang="ja">やゆよ</span>, <span lang="ja">らりるれろ</span>, <span lang="ja">わをん</span>.
2. **Compare:** type <span lang="ja">めぬ</span>, <span lang="ja">るろ</span>, and <span lang="ja">われね</span> while inspecting their endings.
3. **Cover the chart:** retrieve *ma*, *yu*, *ro*, *wa*, and the nasal *n*. Some romanization IMEs require typing **nn** to commit <span lang="ja">ん</span>; the final Japanese character, not your keystrokes, is graded.
4. **Apply:** label Yuri's six picture cards without spaces inside a word.

## Six everyday picture labels

| Picture | Japanese model | Character combination |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mountain | <span lang="ja">やま</span> (*yama*) | や + ま |
| Dream | <span lang="ja">ゆめ</span> (*yume*) | ゆ + め |
| Peach | <span lang="ja">もも</span> (*momo*) | も + も |
| Crocodile | <span lang="ja">わに</span> (*wani*) | わ + に |
| Book | <span lang="ja">ほん</span> (*hon*) | ほ + ん |
| Mandarin orange | <span lang="ja">みかん</span> (*mikan*) | み + か + ん |

Previously learned kana such as に, ほ and か appear in the labels. A single kana represents a sound or mora, not the English meaning of the whole word. Compare <span lang="ja">ゆめ</span> (“dream”) and <span lang="ja">やま</span> (“mountain”): changing characters changes the label.
