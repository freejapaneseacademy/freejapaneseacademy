## Can-do: rehearse a short introduction

You can **state and recognize basic names, numbers and countries** in a polite conversation. This optional speaking lesson reuses the two original dialogues planned for L06. Speaking and microphone permission are never required to mark lesson completion. A written knowledge check measures meaning, **not** spoken accuracy.

### 1. At the community-class registration desk

The staff member opens with <span lang="ja">こんにちは。おなまえは？</span> — “Hello. Your name?” The visitor replies <span lang="ja">たなかです。</span> — “I'm Tanaka.” Here the short question omits words understood from the situation; it is normal in conversation, but a complete polite question is <span lang="ja">おなまえはなんですか。</span> The staff member confirms: <span lang="ja">たなかさんですね。ばんごうは？</span> — “Tanaka, right? Your number?”

The visitor says <span lang="ja">じゅうにです。</span> — “Twelve.” The staff member repeats <span lang="ja">はい、じゅうにですね。ありがとうございます。</span> — “Yes, twelve. Thank you.” **じゅうに** means twelve (10 + 2), not twenty-one (**にじゅういち**). In this scene it is a registration number, **not** the visitor's age.

**Role practice:** listen to Track A once without speaking. Replay and pause after each staff question; answer as Tanaka. Next switch roles: ask the name and number, and confirm politely. The complete WAV is not split into timed phrases; use the player pause control.

### 2. Two classmates meet

The first learner says <span lang="ja">はじめまして。わたしはケンです。カナダからきました。</span> — “Nice to meet you. I'm Ken. I came from Canada.” The second says <span lang="ja">はじめまして。わたしはミカです。にほんからきました。</span> — “Nice to meet you. I'm Mika. I came from Japan.”

**からきました** describes where someone came from; it does **not** establish their nationality. The exchange continues:

> <span lang="ja">ミカさん、クラスはなんばんですか。</span> — “Mika, what number is your class?”

> <span lang="ja">さんばんです。ケンさんは？</span> — “Number three. And you, Ken?”

> <span lang="ja">わたしもさんばんです。よろしくおねがいします。</span> — “I'm in class three too. I look forward to getting along with you.”

The particle **は** is written *ha* but pronounced *wa* when it marks the topic. **も** means “also” here. **さんばん** is “number three” in a sequence; **さん** alone is simply “three,” and **さん** after a person's name is an honorific.

**Role practice:** play Track B once, then take Ken's role while the recording supplies Mika's turns. Switch roles. Try a fictional variation: <span lang="ja">わたしはユミです。にほんからきました。</span> — “I'm Yumi. I came from Japan.” Do not use real personal details.

### 3. Listen, then speak, then check meaning

Use the players and written-response workshop below. If the authentic recordings are unavailable, you can read the text, but **do not call it completed audio shadowing**. The written multiple-choice questions check which phrases suit the scenario. Their score is not a pronunciation grade. The optional microphone tool records only after your action, keeps the recording within this tab and allows you to discard it.

### 4. Self-review without a fabricated grade

After rehearsing, check whether you (1) gave a name with **です**, (2) distinguished **じゅうに** from **にじゅういち**, (3) left pauses for the other speaker, and (4) distinguished **からきました** (origin) from a claim about nationality. Repeat one difficult line slowly and compare its rhythm with the actual recording, if available. This is learner reflection, not qualified human pronunciation review.
