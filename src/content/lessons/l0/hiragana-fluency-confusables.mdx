## A second look at the word-card board

Hana and Sora are playing the written classroom card game again. Hana reads a word, then matches it to a picture. Today the challenge is to check **the complete kana shape** and find **where one word ends and a particle or instruction begins**. This is written reading practice, not a listening or pronunciation test.

### Look-alike kana are not interchangeable

| Contrast | Readings | Helpful check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">ね / れ</span> | ne / re | Check the lower-right finish: ね curls into a loop; れ extends to the right. |
| <span lang="ja">ぬ / め</span> | nu / me | ぬ ends with a loop; め does not. |
| <span lang="ja">る / ろ</span> | ru / ro | る closes into a loop at the bottom; ろ does not. |
| <span lang="ja">わ / ね</span> | wa / ne | ね has a loop at the lower right; わ has a different open finish. |

These are **shape cues**, not rules about how Japanese words are pronounced. Compare whole printed words instead of guessing from a single stroke.

- <span lang="ja">ねこ</span> (*neko*) — cat. Changing ね to れ does not preserve the word.
- <span lang="ja">いぬ</span> (*inu*) — dog. The last kana is ぬ, not め.
- <span lang="ja">くるま</span> (*kuruma*) — car. The middle kana is る, not ろ.
- <span lang="ja">やま</span> (*yama*) — mountain. Both kana must be read in order.

### Where do the words stop?

Japanese normally **does not put spaces between every word**. For study, we can temporarily add spaces to show the parts of a sentence:

| Teaching spaces (not ordinary spelling) | Ordinary Japanese | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">ねこ を みてください。</span> | <span lang="ja">ねこをみてください。</span> | Please look at the cat. |
| <span lang="ja">ほん を みてください。</span> | <span lang="ja">ほんをみてください。</span> | Please look at the book. |
| <span lang="ja">やま は どれですか。</span> | <span lang="ja">やまはどれですか。</span> | Which one is the mountain? |

Here <span lang="ja">を</span> marks the object of <span lang="ja">みてください</span> (“please look”). The particle <span lang="ja">は</span> marks the topic and is pronounced *wa* in this grammatical use, although the kana is written は. In <span lang="ja">はな</span> (“flower” or “nose”, depending on context), the first kana is pronounced *ha*. The same written kana can therefore have different readings **depending on its function**.

### A common mistake

A learner may read <span lang="ja">ほんをみてください。</span> as one long, indivisible word or mistake <span lang="ja">を</span> for part of <span lang="ja">ほん</span>. Instead, identify the known word <span lang="ja">ほん</span>, then the object marker <span lang="ja">を</span>, then the request <span lang="ja">みてください</span>. The teaching spaces help explain the grammar, but do **not** belong in ordinary Japanese spelling.

Another trap is to assume that <span lang="ja">やまは</span> ends in the word <span lang="ja">はな</span>. Check the whole sentence: <span lang="ja">やま</span> is the topic; <span lang="ja">は</span> is a particle; <span lang="ja">どれですか</span> asks which one.

### Guided practice

First cover the romanized reading column and compare <span lang="ja">ね / れ</span>, <span lang="ja">ぬ / め</span> and <span lang="ja">る / ろ</span>. Next, read <span lang="ja">ねこをみてください。</span> and point to the word for *cat*, the object marker and the request. Finally, compare the spaced teaching form with ordinary spelling. You may return to the tables before taking the check.
