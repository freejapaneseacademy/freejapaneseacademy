## Can-do: recognize names, numbers and country details

You have arrived at a beginner Japanese class. At the registration desk, a staff member collects a learner's name, country and identifying number. This first lesson focuses on **context and meaning**; later lessons develop numbers 0–100, the grammar of **です**, independent reading, authentic listening and writing.

## 1. Identify the roles and purpose

The **staff member** checks a registration card. The **learner** supplies details. The purpose is to register for a class, not buy a ticket.

| Japanese | Reading aid | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| なまえ | namae | name |
| くに | kuni | country |
| ばんごう | bangō | number or identifier |
| わたし | watashi | I / me |
| です | desu | polite ending used in starter introductions |

These readings are temporary support; look at the Japanese characters first.

## 2. Read an original registration card

> **にほんごクラス — とうろくカード**  
> なまえ：エマ  
> くに：カナダ  
> ばんごう：12

The card gives the fictional learner's **name: Emma**, **country: Canada**, and **number: 12**. The label **ばんごう** does not mean age or telephone number by itself. A different form would need to specify that.

### Compare what each field tells you

- **なまえ** identifies a person by name, which may be written in kanji, kana or another permitted form.
- **くに** requests country information. It does not automatically prove citizenship or nationality.
- **ばんごう** requests a number. The purpose of the number comes from the form's context.

## 3. Read a short original exchange

**Staff:** おなまえは？ — Your name?  
**Learner:** エマです。 — I'm Emma.  
**Staff:** くには？ — Your country?  
**Learner:** カナダです。 — Canada.

The short answers make sense because of the preceding questions. A fuller beginner introduction is **わたしは エマです。** (“I am Emma.”). The particle **は** is pronounced *wa* here; the structure is taught carefully in L03. The staff's questions are shortened conversational forms, not models for every formal registration situation.

## 4. Notice numbers without guessing

Digits are commonly written **0–100** in everyday forms. In the next lesson you will meet **いち** (1), **に** (2), **じゅう** (10), **じゅうに** (12), and **にじゅう** (20). Here the goal is to notice **12** and **20** accurately in context.

**Common mistake:** Interpreting ばんごう：12 as “twelve years old.” The form only says “number 12.” Likewise **アメリカから きました。** (“I came from America”) is an origin statement, not proof of nationality. Residence, origin and citizenship are distinct concepts.

## 5. Guided transfer: another fictional card

> なまえ：リナ  
> くに：アメリカ  
> ばんごう：20

Cover the English labels and identify the name, country and number. **Model response:** Rina, America, 20. No age or citizenship is specified.

For optional writing practice, copy **なまえ：リナ** and substitute a fictional name. Check the kana visually. You can rehearse **エマです** aloud without scoring yourself; this written L01 does not provide a pronunciation assessment. Authentic listening belongs to L06.
