## Read an instruction, then do it

**Can-do:** Read short Japanese instructions about everyday actions and recognize the kanji that remain visible when verbs change into the **て-form**. In this lesson you will read a fictional library craft-club notice. You do not need to handwrite every kanji from memory; reading and supported typing come first.

### 1. Six action words worth recognizing

| Dictionary form | て-form | English | Notice the written part |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 書く（かく） | 書いて（かいて） | write | 書 stays; く changes to いて |
| 読む（よむ） | 読んで（よんで） | read | 読 stays; む changes to んで |
| 見る（みる） | 見て（みて） | look / see | 見 stays; Group 2 る becomes て |
| 行く（いく） | 行って（いって） | go | 行 stays; 行く is an exception |
| 買う（かう） | 買って（かって） | buy | 買 stays; う becomes って |
| 食べる（たべる） | 食べて（たべて） | eat | 食 stays; べ is part of the reading |

The kanji carries the lexical identity while the **kana ending** carries the form change. The same character can have different readings in other words: **行く（いく）** versus **銀行（ぎんこう）**. Here, read the **whole word in context**, not an isolated character.

### 2. Follow the kana ending

**本（ほん）を読（よ）んで、名前（なまえ）を書（か）きます。** — “Read a book and write your name.” The first verb is 読んで, but the final verb is 書きます, not 書いて. A て-form links actions; the final polite verb closes the sentence.

**駅（えき）に行（い）ってから、切符（きっぷ）を買（か）います。** — “After going to the station, I buy a ticket.” The exception **行って（いって）** has a small っ; **買います（かいます）** is a polite nonpast verb. Do not mistake **行いて** for the correct form.

**絵（え）を見（み）てから、紙（かみ）に書（か）きます。** — “After looking at the picture, I write on paper.” The particles **を** (object) and **に** (surface/destination for writing) matter alongside the kanji.

### 3. Original craft-club notice

> **土曜日（どようび）・図書館（としょかん）の工作（こうさく）クラブ**
>
> ① 本（ほん）を読（よ）んで、作（つく）りたいものを決（き）めます。
>
> ② 絵（え）を見（み）てから、紙（かみ）に名前（なまえ）を書（か）きます。
>
> ③ 店（みせ）に行（い）って、必要（ひつよう）なものを買（か）います。
>
> ④ クラブのあとで、おやつを食（た）べます。

**English:** “Saturday library craft club. (1) Read a book and decide what you want to make. (2) After looking at the picture, write your name on the paper. (3) Go to the store and buy what you need. (4) After the club, eat a snack.”

**Evidence hunt:** Which instruction explicitly requires looking at a picture **before** writing? Number ②: **絵を見てから、紙に名前を書きます**. Which instruction mentions buying? Number ③: **買います**. Note that the notice says **クラブのあとで** (“after the club”), not **食べてから**.

### 4. Confusions to catch

**読んで（よんで）** is “read and…”; **呼んで（よんで）** can mean “call and…”. Their readings match but their kanji and meanings differ. This is a recognition contrast, not a new memorization requirement. **見て（みて）** means “look and…” while **来て（きて）** means “come and…”; do not read 来て as くて. **書いて（かいて）** and **買いて** are not interchangeable: **買う → 買って（かって）**, not 買いて.

### 5. Guided reading and typing

Cover the English meanings and read the six て-forms aloud or silently. Then rewrite **本を読んで、名前を書きます** with kana for the two verbs: **ほんをよんで、なまえをかきます**. Compare the kanji version with the kana version. The kana is valid support, but the kanji makes the action words easier to distinguish.

Now write two steps for a fictional activity. Include one **〜てから** and one final **〜ます** verb. Model (compare only after drafting):

> 本（ほん）を読（よ）んでから、絵（え）を見（み）ます。絵を見（み）て、名前（なまえ）を書（か）きます。

“After reading the book, I look at the picture. I look at the picture and write my name.”

**Self-check:** Are the kanji attached to the correct kana endings? Is **行く → 行って** correct? Does **〜てから** signal the intended order? Is the final verb polite? Your writing is ungraded; the check below assesses written recognition, not handwriting quality or pronunciation.

### 6. Review

Recognize **書・読・見・行・買・食** inside actual verbs; match the kana endings to the action. Revisit the original notice and underline evidence for each answer. The following L05 reading lesson will use a longer instruction card. No audio is required for this script-focused lesson.
