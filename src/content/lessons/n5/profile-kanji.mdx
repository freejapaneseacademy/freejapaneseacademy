## Reading at the orientation desk

**Can-do:** Write and understand a short self-introduction. At the university orientation desk, two fictional visitors have filled out profile cards. Kanji are not puzzles with one fixed sound per character: learn the **whole word** and check its meaning against the situation.

> A：こちらは田中（たなか）ゆいさんです。二十歳（はたち）です。大阪（おおさか）出身（しゅっしん）です。大学生（だいがくせい）です。  
> B：こちらはリーさんです。二十三歳（にじゅうさんさい）です。東京（とうきょう）出身です。会社員（かいしゃいん）です。

**Meaning:** “This is Yui Tanaka. She is twenty, from Osaka, and a university student.” “This is Lee. They are twenty-three, from Tokyo, and a company employee.” The names and profiles are fictional. **出身** tells where someone is from, not necessarily where they live now or their nationality.

## 1. Read useful kanji in whole words

| Word | Reading | Meaning | Reading note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 歳 | さい | years old (age suffix) | 二十三歳 = にじゅうさんさい; **二十歳** is exceptionally **はたち** |
| 出身 | しゅっしん | place of origin | small ゅ and small っ; not しゅしん |
| 大学 | だいがく | university | long vowel sequence だい |
| 大学生 | だいがくせい | university student | 学生 (がくせい) = student |
| 会社 | かいしゃ | company | small ゃ in しゃ |
| 会社員 | かいしゃいん | company employee | 員 (いん) adds a member/employee sense |

**Important:** 二十歳 is normally read **はたち** for “twenty years old,” not にじゅっさい. 二十一歳 is にじゅういっさい, and 二十三歳 is にじゅうさんさい. In everyday writing, ages may also be written with numerals: 23歳.

The word **大学生** describes someone studying at university. **会社員** describes a company employee; it does not specify a particular job title. Do not infer an occupation from a person's hometown.

## 2. Compare the two original profile cards

| Card | 名前 (name) | 年齢 (age) | 出身 (from) | 仕事・学校 (work/school) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 田中ゆい | 二十歳（はたち） | 大阪（おおさか） | 大学生（だいがくせい） |
| B | リー | 二十三歳（にじゅうさんさい） | 東京（とうきょう） | 会社員（かいしゃいん） |

**Find evidence, not guesses.** “Who is a university student?” Card A explicitly says 大学生. “Who is from Tokyo?” Card B explicitly says 東京出身. Neither card states a current home address, a nationality, or a native language.

Read the next exchange:

> A：リーさんは大学生ですか。  
> B：いいえ、大学生じゃありません。会社員です。  
> A：出身はどこですか。  
> B：東京です。

**Meaning:** “Is Lee a university student?” “No, they aren't. They're a company employee.” “Where are they from?” “Tokyo.”

## 3. How the parts work together

**リーさんは会社員です。** The particle **は** marks Lee as the topic; **です** ends the polite nominal sentence. **リーさんの出身は東京です。** The particle **の** links Lee to their place of origin; it does not claim Tokyo is Lee's property. **大阪出身です** is a natural shortened way of stating a hometown/origin when the subject is understood.

**Common mistake:** Do not read **出身** as the person's present address. “東京出身” means “from Tokyo,” and that person may now live somewhere else. Similarly, **二十歳** is a special whole-word reading; don't mechanically apply the usual さい to the number 20.

## 4. Controlled, guided and independent practice

**Controlled reading:** Cover the kana column and read 出身・大学・大学生・会社・会社員. Reveal the readings and check the small ゅ/ゃ and っ. Then read 二十歳 and 二十三歳, noticing the exception.

**Guided evidence:** Without looking at the English, find the kanji on Card A that proves Yui is a university student. Find the exact word on Card B that establishes Lee's occupation. Underline 出身 on both cards; point to the two different place names.

**Independent writing:** Invent a profile for a fictional visitor and write three short sentences, for example **私はさくらです。京都（きょうと）出身です。大学生です。** You can choose a different city, age and work/school role. Check that each fact follows from the fictional profile you created. Optionally read your sentences aloud for self-rehearsal; there is no recording or pronunciation grade in this written lesson.
