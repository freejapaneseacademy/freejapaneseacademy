## A busy afternoon at the community center

You arrive at a fictional community center. A volunteer wants to know what people are doing **right now**. The goal is to describe activities as they are happening—not to list everyone's usual habits.

**Observe the conversation.** Read once for the setting, then again for the person and action in each line.

> 田中（たなか）：今（いま）、みなさんは何（なに）をしていますか。  
> 佐藤（さとう）：山田（やまだ）さんは図書室（としょしつ）で本（ほん）を読（よ）んでいます。  
> 田中：鈴木（すずき）さんは？  
> 佐藤：鈴木さんは教室（きょうしつ）で日本語（にほんご）を書（か）いています。  
> 田中：佐藤さんは何をしていますか。  
> 佐藤：私（わたし）はお茶（ちゃ）を飲（の）んでいます。

**English:** Tanaka: What is everyone doing now? Sato: Yamada is reading a book in the library room. Tanaka: And Suzuki? Sato: Suzuki is writing Japanese in the classroom. Tanaka: What are you doing, Sato? Sato: I'm drinking tea.

The conversation is invented for practice; it does not describe real people. The question **何をしていますか** asks what someone is doing. The answers name an activity in progress.

## Notice the clues

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning | What to notice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 今 | いま | now | Establishes the time of the scene |
| 何をしていますか | なにをしていますか | What are you doing? | Asks about an activity |
| 読んでいます | よんでいます | is reading | 読む → 読んで + います |
| 書いています | かいています | is writing | 書く → 書いて + います |
| 飲んでいます | のんでいます | is drinking | 飲む → 飲んで + います |
| 図書室で | としょしつで | in the library room | で marks the place of an action |
| 本を | ほんを | a book (object) | を marks what is being read |

**Pattern:** Person は + place で + object を + verb て-form + います。 The place and object are optional when context already makes them clear.

**Example:** 山田さんは図書室で本を読んでいます。 = Yamada is reading a book in the library room.

**Question:** 山田さんは何をしていますか。 = What is Yamada doing? Answer: 本を読んでいます。 = (Yamada) is reading a book.

## Meaning before rules

Compare **毎日（まいにち）本を読みます** (“I read books every day”) with **今、本を読んでいます** (“I am reading a book now”). The first describes a habit. The second highlights an ongoing activity. This chapter begins with the **progressive** use of 〜ています; later lessons will explore other uses of the same form, so it does not always mean English “-ing.”

**Do not confuse the て-form with the whole expression.** 読んで alone is not the polite complete answer here. For an ongoing action, say **読んでいます**. Likewise, 書く → 書いています and 飲む → 飲んでいます.

**Common particle error:** 図書室**で**本を読んでいます, not 図書室**を**本で読んでいます. The place of the activity takes で; the object takes を.

## A second snapshot: compare two scenes

**Scene A — now:** 今、ミカさんは公園（こうえん）で走（はし）っています。ケンさんはベンチで休（やす）んでいます。  
**English:** Mika is running in the park now. Ken is resting on a bench.

**Scene B — usual schedule:** ミカさんは毎朝（まいあさ）公園で走ります。  
**English:** Mika runs in the park every morning.

Ask yourself: Which scene shows an action happening at this moment? **Scene A**. What marks the habitual sentence? **毎朝** and **走ります**.

## Guided practice: say it in three steps

1. **Find the person.** In the first conversation, who is writing Japanese? **鈴木さん**.
2. **Find the activity.** What is Yamada doing? **本を読んでいます**.
3. **Find the place.** Where is Suzuki writing? **教室で**.

Now recombine: **ケンさんは図書室で本を読んでいます。** Change the person and place, but keep the action expression intact.

## Independent mini-dialogue (ungraded)

Imagine two fictional classmates in a shared activity room. Write **two to four lines**: ask **今、何をしていますか**, answer with **〜ています**, and optionally ask about a second person. You may use **絵（え）を描（か）いています** (“is drawing a picture”), **本を読んでいます**, or **日本語を勉強（べんきょう）しています** (“is studying Japanese”).

**Model:**

> A：今、何をしていますか。  
> B：図書室で本を読んでいます。  
> A：ユキさんは？  
> B：ユキさんは教室で日本語を勉強しています。

**English:** A: What are you doing now? B: I'm reading in the library room. A: What about Yuki? B: Yuki is studying Japanese in the classroom.

Compare your own lines with the model. Check whether your question uses **何をしていますか**, your answer uses the **て-form + います**, and your place uses **で**. Natural alternatives are welcome. The writing is self-reviewed, not automatically graded. No recording, microphone, or actual audio is required for this written context lesson.
