## A note for a guest

**Can-do:** Give a basic description of a room. In this reading lesson you will help a fictional visitor understand a note about a room. The note is original teaching material, not a real address or a floor plan.

### 1. Words that locate things

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning in this note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 部屋 | へや | room |
| 机 | つくえ | desk |
| いす | いす | chair |
| 本棚 | ほんだな | bookshelf |
| 窓 | まど | window |
| 上 | うえ | on top of / above |
| 下 | した | under / below |
| 前 | まえ | in front of |
| 後ろ | うしろ | behind |
| 中 | なか | inside |
| 隣 | となり | next to |

Read **机の上に** as “on the desk”: **の** joins the reference object to its relative position, and **に** marks the location. **あります** describes the existence or location of an inanimate object. **います** is used for a person or animal. In this note, all the located things are inanimate.

### 2. Original reading: ゆきさんの部屋

> **ゆきさんの 部屋（へや）の メモ**
>
> **A.** <span lang="ja">部屋に 机（つくえ）と いすが あります。</span>
>
> **B.** <span lang="ja">机の 上（うえ）に ノートが あります。</span>
>
> **C.** <span lang="ja">机の 下（した）に かばんが あります。</span>
>
> **D.** <span lang="ja">机の 隣（となり）に 本棚（ほんだな）が あります。</span>
>
> **E.** <span lang="ja">本棚の 中（なか）に 日本語（にほんご）の 本（ほん）が あります。</span>
>
> **F.** <span lang="ja">窓（まど）の 前（まえ）に 小（ちい）さい テーブルが あります。</span>
>
> **G.** <span lang="ja">いすの 後ろ（うしろ）に 時計（とけい）が あります。</span>

**English support (check after reading):** A: There is a desk and a chair in the room. B: A notebook is on the desk. C: A bag is under the desk. D: A bookshelf is next to the desk. E: A Japanese-language book is inside the bookshelf. F: A small table is in front of the window. G: A clock is behind the chair.

The note does not say which side of the desk the bookshelf is on. **隣** means “next to,” not necessarily “to the right.” It also does not tell us the color of the bag or whether anyone is in the room.

### 3. Read relationships, not just nouns

**Worked example:** Where is the notebook? Line **B: 机の 上に ノートが あります。** The evidence is **机の 上に**. A notebook *under* the desk would contradict B.

**Second example:** Where is the Japanese book? Line **E: 本棚の 中に 日本語の 本が あります。** Here the first **の** gives the bookshelf as the reference location, while **日本語の** describes the kind of book. Both are useful, but they do different jobs.

**Third example:** Is the bookshelf to the right of the desk? **D** only says **机の 隣**. Right or left is **not stated**. Do not turn missing evidence into “no bookshelf.”

### 4. Guided evidence hunt

Cover the English support. Find the line for each request: (1) a bag, (2) a small table, (3) a clock. Say the letter and copy the Japanese location phrase. Check yourself: **C — 机の 下に**; **F — 窓の 前に**; **G — いすの 後ろに**.

Now compare **机の 上** and **机の 下**. They share the same reference object, but swapping **上** and **下** changes the fact. **本棚の 中** identifies a different relationship: inside the shelf, not next to it.

Answer the ten evidence-based questions below. Each answer has one best choice supported by A–G. You may reread the note. After submitting, compare the Japanese evidence and explanations. This formative score is not a JLPT score.

### 6. Optional writing

Write two connected sentences describing two different objects in this fictional room. Use **[place] に [thing] が あります**. For example: <span lang="ja">机の 上に ノートが あります。本棚の 中に 日本語の 本が あります。</span>

Self-check: Does each sentence name a reference place? Is **に** attached to that place? Is **が** attached to the object? Does the sentence match the original note? This is learner self-review, not automated grading.
