## At the lunch counter

**Can-do:** Name common foods and express simple preferences. You are not yet learning to place a complete restaurant order. The fictional exchange below takes place at a community-center lunch counter. Read it first for meaning, then find each particle.

**あや：** 何（なに）が好きですか。  
**けん：** わたしはカレーが好きです。魚（さかな）はあまり好きではありません。  
**あや：** わたしはパンとサラダが好きです。今日（きょう）は何を食べますか。  
**けん：** カレーを食べます。水（みず）を飲（の）みます。  
**あや：** だれと食べますか。  
**けん：** 友（とも）だちと食べます。

**English:** Aya asks what Ken likes. Ken likes curry, but does not like fish very much. Aya likes bread and salad, then asks what he will eat today. Ken will eat curry and drink water. Asked whom he will eat with, Ken answers that he will eat with a friend.

**Reading support:** 好き（すき） liked/favorite; 魚（さかな） fish; 食べます（たべます） eat; 飲みます（のみます） drink; 今日（きょう） today; 友だち（ともだち） friend; 何（なに） what; だれ who.

## Three patterns that do different jobs

| Japanese pattern | Meaning | Why that particle? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| カレー**が**好きです。 | I like curry. | **が** marks the thing liked with the adjectival noun 好き. |
| 魚**が**嫌いです。 | I dislike fish. | **が** marks the thing disliked with 嫌い（きらい）. |
| カレー**を**食べます。 | I eat curry. | **を** marks the direct object of the action 食べます. |
| 水**を**飲みます。 | I drink water. | **を** marks the thing being drunk. |
| パン**と**サラダが好きです。 | I like bread and salad. | **と** joins two explicitly named foods. |
| 友だち**と**食べます。 | I eat with a friend. | **と** marks a companion. |

**好き and 嫌い are not verbs.** In beginner polite sentences, say **りんごが好きです** (I like apples), not **りんごを好きます**. The form **嫌いです** means “dislike”; it need not imply an extreme personal aversion. **好きではありません** is a polite negative (“do not like”), which may sound softer than 嫌いです.

**は and が can both appear in one sentence.** In **わたしはカレーが好きです**, **は** establishes the person as topic; **が** identifies what that person likes. In **魚はあまり好きではありません**, **は** contrasts fish with other foods. This is not a universal rule that が can never be replaced by は; it is the clear first pattern for this chapter.

**と has two related uses here.** **パンとサラダ** is a closed list of two named foods; **友だちと食べます** means eating *with* a friend. Do not interpret **友だちと食べます** as “eat a friend”: a food object would be marked with **を**. **パンやサラダ** would suggest examples from a non-exhaustive list; や is not the target in this lesson.

## Controlled contrasts

1. Choose the particle for the **liked item**: **お茶（ちゃ）___好きです。** → **が**. “I like tea.”
2. Choose the particle for the **thing eaten**: **パン___食べます。** → **を**. “I eat bread.”
3. Name both foods: **パン___サラダが好きです。** → **と**. “I like bread and salad.”
4. Name a companion: **母（はは）___食べます。** → **と**. “I eat with my mother.”
5. Change the preference politely: **魚が好きです。 → 魚は好きではありません。** “I like fish” → “I do not like fish.” Notice the contrasting topic は.

## Guided exchange

Use the model below to substitute one food and one drink. The questions are polite and appropriate for classmates who are getting to know one another.

**A：** 何が好きですか。 (“What do you like?”)  
**B：** りんごが好きです。 (“I like apples.”)  
**A：** 何を飲みますか。 (“What will you drink?”)  
**B：** お茶を飲みます。 (“I'll drink tea.”)

Try **バナナ**, **パン**, **水**, or **コーヒー**. For a negative preference, use **コーヒーは好きではありません** (“I don't like coffee”). Do not use **コーヒーを好きです**.

## Independent transfer and self-check

Write three original short sentences about a **fictional** lunch visitor: one food they like, one food they will eat, and a companion they eat with. Use **が**, **を**, and **と** once each. Compare with this model:

**りなさんはサラダが好きです。パンを食べます。友だちと食べます。**  
“Rina likes salad. She eats bread. She eats with a friend.”

Self-review: (1) Is the liked food marked by が? (2) Is the food eaten marked by を? (3) Does と mean “and” or “with” in your sentence? (4) Are the verbs and です polite? Your writing is not automatically graded.

**Reading:** Re-read Aya and Ken's dialogue, identifying who likes what and what Ken eats. **Listening preparation:** A partner may read the dialogue aloud while you hide the text; reveal it afterward to check your interpretation. No recorded audio or listening score is provided in this written L03. **Optional speaking:** Rehearse the guided exchange aloud; no recording or pronunciation assessment is required.
