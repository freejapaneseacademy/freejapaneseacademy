## Mission: help prepare a community cooking class

At a fictional neighborhood cooking class, you and a partner prepare vegetables and set the table. **This lesson is optional and ungraded for speaking.** The lines below are original written models, **not recorded audio**. You may read them silently; no microphone is requested and no pronunciation score is generated. For chapter listening practice, return to L06 once its genuine audio has been verified.

### Notice the actions

| Dictionary form | Reading | Te-form | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 洗う | あらう | 洗って（あらって） | wash |
| 切る | きる | 切って（きって） | cut |
| 入れる | いれる | 入れて（いれて） | put in |
| 混ぜる | まぜる | 混ぜて（まぜて） | mix |
| 食べる | たべる | 食べて（たべて） | eat |
| する | する | して | do |
| 待つ | まつ | 待って（まって） | wait |

**〜てください** is a polite request: 野菜を洗ってください。 (*Please wash the vegetables.*) **〜てから** means *after doing*: 野菜を洗ってから、切ります。 (*After washing the vegetables, I cut them.*) By contrast, **洗って、切ります** simply links washing and cutting in sequence; it does not emphasize the completion of washing before cutting as strongly as 〜てから.

**Common trap:** 切る（きる） becomes **切って**, not 切て. The small っ represents a consonant closure. **混ぜる** becomes **混ぜて**, not 混ぜって. The spelling of the dictionary form alone does not mean every verb ending in る belongs to the same group.

### Scene A — getting the ingredients ready

**Situation:** A class leader (A) gives a partner (B) the first steps.

| Role | Japanese model | Faithful English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | まず、手を洗ってください。 | First, please wash your hands. |
| B | はい。次に、何をしますか。 | Yes. What shall we do next? |
| A | 野菜を洗ってから、切ってください。 | After washing the vegetables, please cut them. |
| B | わかりました。 | Understood. |

**Reading support:** 手（て） = hands; 野菜（やさい） = vegetables; 次（つぎ）に = next; 何（なに） = what. Here **何をしますか** asks what action to do next, rather than asking permission.

### Scene B — finishing the preparation

**Situation:** The partners have cut the vegetables and are ready to prepare the dish.

| Role | Japanese model | Faithful English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | 切った野菜をボウルに入れてください。 | Please put the cut vegetables into the bowl. |
| B | はい。それから、混ぜますか。 | Yes. Shall I mix them after that? |
| A | はい、混ぜてください。最後に、皿を並べましょう。 | Yes, please mix them. Finally, let's arrange the plates. |
| B | はい、そうしましょう。 | Yes, let's do that. |

**Reading support:** 切った（きった） = cut (past form modifying 野菜); 皿（さら） = plate; 並べる（ならべる） = arrange. **〜ましょう** proposes doing something together; it is not the same as a command. The past form **切った** is included as a recognition cue; this lesson focuses on the te-form.

### Three-pass rehearsal

1. **Notice:** Read Scene A slowly, pointing to each action. Pause after まず and 次に. Compare **洗ってください** (request) with **洗ってから** (after washing).
2. **Switch:** Choose A, then B in the practice panel. Say or silently read each line. Repeat with Scene B. Keep the order of the actions consistent.
3. **Adapt:** Replace 野菜 with じゃがいも (*potatoes*) in Scene A. Try: **じゃがいもを洗ってから、切ってください。** (*After washing the potatoes, please cut them.*) Do not change the te-form of 洗う.

### Independent mini-dialogue

Imagine a partner has to prepare a snack. Create **two to five connected Japanese sentences** with まず, 次に, and 最後に. Include one polite request and one **〜てから** clause. Example:

> まず、手を洗ってください。次に、りんごを洗ってから、切ってください。最後に、皿に入れてください。

*First, please wash your hands. Next, after washing the apple, please cut it. Finally, please put it on a plate.*

This model is a fictional classroom instruction, not a food-safety protocol.

### Self-review without automatic speech grading

Check whether you (1) used at least two correct te-forms, (2) made an appropriate polite request, (3) used 〜てから for the intended order, and (4) switched roles while keeping the meaning. The written questions below check form recognition **only**. They do not measure speech, listening or pronunciation. No recording is made or stored.
