## Welcome to Haru's study room

**Chapter can-do:** Give a basic description of a room or neighborhood. In this first lesson, focus on a single room. Haru is showing visiting classmate Mei around a small study room before they begin homework. Your goal is to understand **who is speaking, why they are talking and where things are**. This is an original *written* conversation, not an audio recording.

| Speaker | Japanese | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Haru (host) | <span lang="ja">ここは わたしの へやです。</span> | This is my room. |
| Mei (visitor) | <span lang="ja">つくえは どこですか。</span> | Where is the desk? |
| Haru | <span lang="ja">つくえは まどの まえに あります。</span> | The desk is in front of the window. |
| Mei | <span lang="ja">かばんは どこですか。</span> | Where is the bag? |
| Haru | <span lang="ja">かばんは いすの したに あります。</span> | The bag is under the chair. |
| Mei | <span lang="ja">ほんは どこですか。</span> | Where is the book? |
| Haru | <span lang="ja">ほんは つくえの うえに あります。</span> | The book is on the desk. |
| Mei | <span lang="ja">ねこも いますか。</span> | Is there a cat too? |
| Haru | <span lang="ja">はい。ねこは いすの となりに います。</span> | Yes. The cat is beside the chair. |

**Room facts in accessible text:** One desk stands in front of a window. A book rests on the desk. A bag is under a chair. A cat sits beside that chair. No diagram or color recognition is needed.

## 1. Notice the conversation's purpose

Haru is the **host** describing the room; Mei is the **visitor** asking questions. <span lang="ja">どこですか</span> asks *where?* The replies give positions relative to objects already known to the listener: the window, desk and chair. This is a room tour, not a request to buy furniture or find a missing person.

Read the exchange twice. On the first pass, follow the speakers. On the second, underline the location phrases. Ask yourself: which objects are used as landmarks, and which things are being located?

## 2. Spatial words in context

| Japanese phrase | Reading | Meaning in this room |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">まどの 前</span> | まどの まえ | in front of the window |
| <span lang="ja">いすの 下</span> | いすの した | under the chair |
| <span lang="ja">つくえの 上</span> | つくえの うえ | on / above the desk |
| <span lang="ja">いすの 隣</span> | いすの となり | beside the chair |
| <span lang="ja">かばんの 中</span> | かばんの なか | inside the bag (a useful comparison, **not** where the book is in this scene) |
| <span lang="ja">いすの 後ろ</span> | いすの うしろ | behind the chair (not used as a location fact in this tour) |

These kanji are reading aids, not a new kanji memorization test in L01. The spoken-style kana versions in the dialogue are enough for now.

The pattern is **landmark + の + position + に + thing + が + あります／います**. For example, <span lang="ja">つくえの うえに ほんが あります。</span> means “There is a book on the desk.” **の** connects the landmark to the position; **に** marks where something exists. In the dialogue, Haru often uses **は** to continue talking about an already-mentioned item: <span lang="ja">ほんは つくえの うえに あります。</span>

## 3. Things and living beings

Use **あります** for furniture and objects: <span lang="ja">いすが あります。</span> (“There is a chair.”) Use **います** for people and animals: <span lang="ja">ねこが います。</span> (“There is a cat.”) Both are polite forms. A cat is not furniture: <span lang="ja">ねこが あります</span> is not the intended ordinary existence expression.

**Common mistake:** <span lang="ja">かばんは いすの したが あります</span> does not correctly mark the location. The natural sentence is <span lang="ja">かばんは いすの したに あります。</span> **に** follows the location. Do not confuse **前（まえ）**, “in front of,” with **後ろ（うしろ）**, “behind.” Position is relative to the named landmark.

## 4. Controlled noticing

Cover the English translations and answer from the Japanese text. Is the book on the desk or under the chair? Is the cat beside the chair or inside the bag? Which sentence tells you that the desk is in front of the window?

**Check your evidence:** <span lang="ja">つくえの うえ</span>; <span lang="ja">いすの となり</span>; <span lang="ja">つくえは まどの まえに あります。</span>

## 5. Guided room reconstruction

Complete these lines before checking the dialogue:

1. <span lang="ja">かばんは いすの ______ に あります。</span>
2. <span lang="ja">ねこは いすの ______ に います。</span>
3. <span lang="ja">ほんは つくえの ______ に あります。</span>

**Answers:** <span lang="ja">した</span> (under), <span lang="ja">となり</span> (beside), <span lang="ja">うえ</span> (on). Notice that **います** is used for the cat.

## 6. Independent transfer

Imagine a *different* room: a book is under a chair, a bag is on a desk, and a dog is beside the window. Write three Japanese sentences using **の**, **に**, and the appropriate existence verb. One possible model:

> <span lang="ja">いすの したに ほんが あります。つくえの うえに かばんが あります。まどの となりに いぬが います。</span>

Check your own sentences for (1) the correct landmark, (2) a spatial word, (3) **に** after the location and (4) **あります／います** appropriate to the entity. This is self-review, **not** automatically graded writing.
