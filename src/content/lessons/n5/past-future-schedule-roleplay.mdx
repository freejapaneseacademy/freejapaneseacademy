## Make a simple plan with a classmate

Imagine two students discussing yesterday's activities and tomorrow's plans after class. Your goal is to **ask, answer, and switch roles** using short polite sentences. The dialogue is original and fictional. You may practise aloud, silently or with a partner; no recording is required and no speech is graded.

### 1. Words to retrieve

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 昨日 | きのう | yesterday |
| 今日 | きょう | today |
| 明日 | あした | tomorrow |
| 来週 | らいしゅう | next week |
| 今朝 | けさ | this morning |
| 図書館 | としょかん | library |
| 映画 | えいが | film |
| 友だち | ともだち | friend |
| 勉強しました | べんきょうしました | studied |
| 見ませんでした | みませんでした | did not watch |
| 行きます | いきます | go / will go |
| 読みます | よみます | read / will read |

**Time and tense:** **昨日** normally accompanies a past form when reporting what happened: **勉強しました** (studied) or **勉強しませんでした** (did not study). **明日** and **来週** commonly accompany the polite nonpast **ます** when expressing plans. The nonpast ending itself does not exclusively mean future; the time expression provides context.

### 2. First model: yesterday and tomorrow

**A:** 昨日、何をしましたか。  
*What did you do yesterday?*

**B:** 図書館で日本語を勉強しました。  
*I studied Japanese at the library.*

**A:** 昨日、映画を見ましたか。  
*Did you watch a film yesterday?*

**B:** いいえ、見ませんでした。  
*No, I didn't.*

**A:** 明日、何をしますか。  
*What will you do tomorrow?*

**B:** 家で本を読みます。  
*I'll read a book at home.*

**A:** 何時に読みますか。  
*At what time will you read?*

**B:** 午後三時に読みます。  
*I'll read at 3 p.m.*

The answers consistently describe a fictional student's schedule. **で** marks the location of an action (**図書館で** / **家で**), **を** marks its object (**日本語を** / **本を**), and **に** marks a specific time (**午後三時に**). **何をしましたか** asks about a past action; **何をしますか** asks about a present or future action depending on context.

### 3. Second model: change the plans

**A:** 今朝、朝ごはんを食べましたか。  
*Did you eat breakfast this morning?*

**B:** はい、食べました。  
*Yes, I did.*

**A:** 来週、どこに行きますか。  
*Where will you go next week?*

**B:** 土曜日に公園に行きます。  
*I'll go to the park on Saturday.*

**A:** 友だちと行きますか。  
*Will you go with a friend?*

**B:** はい、友だちと行きます。  
*Yes, I'll go with a friend.*

**と** here means *with* a companion. **公園に** marks a destination; **土曜日に** marks the scheduled day. Two **に** phrases can occur in the same sentence because they perform different roles.

### 4. Avoid these common errors

- **昨日、映画を見ません。** is not the expected form for a completed negative event yesterday. Say **昨日、映画を見ませんでした。**
- **明日、図書館で勉強しました。** reports a past action despite tomorrow's time anchor. For a planned action, say **明日、図書館で勉強します。**
- **図書館に勉強しました** confuses destination with location of action. Say **図書館で勉強しました。** For movement, **図書館に行きました** is natural.
- **昨日** and **明日** ordinarily do not need **に** as time markers: **昨日、勉強しました。** For clock times, use **三時に**.

### 5. Guided substitutions

Read A's question aloud and supply B's answer before revealing the suggested response in the interactive role cards below. Switch roles after each card.

| Prompt | Change | Model answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 昨日、何をしましたか。 | Studied at home | 家で勉強しました。 |
| 昨日、映画を見ましたか。 | No | いいえ、見ませんでした。 |
| 明日、何をしますか。 | Read at the library | 図書館で本を読みます。 |
| 来週、どこに行きますか。 | Go to the park | 公園に行きます。 |

Try one additional substitution: change **図書館** to **学校** while keeping **で** for studying, then change the verb to **行きます** and use **に** for the destination.

### 6. Independent two-day exchange

Create a fictional conversation of **four to six turns**. Ask what happened yesterday, answer using a past affirmative or negative form, then ask about tomorrow and respond using **ます**. Include one location and one specific time. You can use these starters:

**A:** 昨日、何をしましたか。  
**B:** 昨日、＿＿＿＿ました。  
**A:** 明日、何をしますか。  
**B:** 明日、＿＿＿＿ます。

One possible response: **昨日、学校で日本語を勉強しました。明日、午後四時に図書館で本を読みます。** (*Yesterday I studied Japanese at school. Tomorrow I'll read a book at the library at 4 p.m.*) Other natural responses are possible.

### 7. Self-review

After switching roles, check four things: (1) yesterday's completed events use **ました／ませんでした**; (2) future plans use a suitable **ます** form; (3) **で／に／を／と** express the intended roles; (4) your partner can tell which event is past and which is planned. This is self-reflection, **not** verified pronunciation feedback. The written check below tests recognition and interpretation, not spoken proficiency.
