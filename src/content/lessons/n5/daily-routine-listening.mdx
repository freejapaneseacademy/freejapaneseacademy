## Hear a daily routine, not just isolated verbs

Two fictional people talk about different parts of a usual day. **Track A** is a morning conversation before school; **Track B** is an evening conversation after work. Listen for **when** an action happens, **what** the speaker does, and **what they explicitly do not do**. These conversations are original teaching scenarios, not recordings of real people.

### Listening targets

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 毎朝 | まいあさ | every morning |
| 起きます | おきます | get up |
| 朝ご飯を食べます | あさごはんをたべます | eat breakfast |
| 学校へ行きます | がっこうへいきます | go to school |
| 家に帰ります | いえにかえります | return home |
| 晩ご飯を作ります | ばんごはんをつくります | make dinner |
| 音楽を聞きます | おんがくをききます | listen to music |
| 飲みません | のみません | do not drink |

**に** marks a specific clock time: **七時に** (at seven). **を** marks the object of an action: **朝ご飯を食べます** (eat breakfast). **へ** in **学校へ行きます** marks direction toward school, not the object of eating or watching.

### Hear the positive and negative

**見ます** means “watch” in polite nonpast; **見ません** means “do not watch.” Similarly, **飲みます** means “drink” and **飲みません** means “do not drink.” Nonpast forms can describe habits. They do not by themselves mean that an action is happening at this instant.

When a speaker asks **テレビを見ますか**, the reply **いいえ、見ません** is negative. Do not treat the word テレビ in the question as proof the person watches it. In the evening conversation, the speaker *does* listen to music but *does not* drink coffee at night.

### Track A — before school

First listen for the main topic: a usual morning. On the second play, separate **六時半** (6:30), **七時** (7:00), and **八時** (8:00). On the third, pay attention to the short negative answer about television. Remember that **半** means half past the hour.

### Track B — after work

First listen for the speaker's evening sequence. On the second play, distinguish returning home from making dinner. On the third, listen for **はい** and **でも** (but), which introduce a contrast between music and coffee. The question about music and the final statement about coffee concern different objects.

### Three listening passes

1. Listen to each track once without reading a transcript. Summarize the speaker's day in English.
2. Listen again and write down two times and two actions. Listen for **ます** versus **ません**.
3. Answer the six audio questions. Only then reveal the transcript to check your evidence. Revealing either transcript labels your attempt **transcript-assisted**, even if you hide it again.

The practice below locks scoring until both authentic audio recordings are playable. The candidate synthesis job alone is not a recording.

### Guided writing

Write three sentences about an imaginary day. Use **[time] に [action] ます**, then one **を** object, and one polite negative. Example (different from either recording):

**午前九時に本を読みます。午後五時に家に帰ります。夜はジュースを飲みません。**

“I read a book at 9 a.m. I return home at 5 p.m. I do not drink juice at night.”

Self-check: Is **に** attached to a specific time? Does **を** mark the thing read or drunk? Is **ません** attached to the action you are negating? This is ungraded writing practice, not a language proficiency score.

### Optional speaking

After the recordings are available, shadow **何時に起きますか。** and **六時半に起きます。** Then answer with your own fictional time. Try the negative response **いいえ、見ません。** Recording yourself is optional; no microphone is required and no pronunciation grade is generated.

The six questions require facts explicitly present in the two conversations. Re-listen before opening a transcript if you want an unaided attempt.
