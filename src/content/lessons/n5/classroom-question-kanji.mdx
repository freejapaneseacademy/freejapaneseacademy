## At the classroom welcome table

**Can-do:** Ask and answer who, what, which and where questions. Today you will recognize kanji on fictional classroom role cards, then use them to understand short questions. You already know だれ (who), どこ (where), どれ (which one) and も (also). Kanji are introduced as **whole words with readings**, not as isolated characters whose every reading you must memorize.

> 名前（なまえ）：山田（やまだ）  
> 役割（やくわり）：先生（せんせい）  
> 場所（ばしょ）：教室（きょうしつ）

**Meaning:** Name: Yamada. Role: teacher. Place: classroom. These are invented classroom cards, not real people. The labels 役割 (role) and 場所 (place) are reading-supported context words, not extra tested kanji targets.

## 1. Read words rather than guessing single characters

| Word | Reading | Meaning | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 人 | ひと | person | Helps you identify people in a card |
| 先生 | せんせい | teacher | A role, and also a respectful form of address |
| 学生 | がくせい | student | Different role from 先生 |
| 名前 | なまえ | name | A card label asking for a person's name |
| 何 | なに | what | In これは何ですか, asks about an object |
| 教室 | きょうしつ | classroom | A place, not a person |

**Notice:** The common question **これは何（なん）ですか** is pronounced **これはなんですか**. In **何を見ますか**, 何 is read **なに**. For this lesson, remember both contextual readings: **何（なん）ですか** and **何（なに）を**. Do not assume a character has just one reading. The written form 先生 is **せんせい**, with final い; 教室 is **きょうしつ**, with small ょ and long う.

## 2. Four question patterns in context

> A: あの人（ひと）はだれですか。  
> B: 山田（やまだ）先生（せんせい）です。

**Meaning:** “Who is that person?” “That's Teacher Yamada.” The word 人 refers to a person, and だれ asks for an identity. 山田 is a fictional name whose reading is supplied; do not guess unfamiliar name readings.

> A: これは何（なん）ですか。  
> B: 名札（なふだ）です。

**Meaning:** “What is this?” “It's a name badge.” 名札 is a reading-supported example; you do not need to memorize its kanji yet. **何ですか** is pronounced **なんですか** in this pattern.

> A: 教室（きょうしつ）はどこですか。  
> B: あそこです。

**Meaning:** “Where is the classroom?” “Over there.” どこ asks for a place, so 教室 is an appropriate subject. **あそこ** points to a location away from both speakers in this imagined setting.

> A: 山田先生のカードはどれですか。  
> B: このカードです。

**Meaning:** “Which is Teacher Yamada's card?” “This card.” **どれ** selects one from a visible set; **どこ** would ask for its location, not which one it is.

## 3. Compare similar-looking words

**人（ひと） versus 何（なに／なん）:** 人 is “person”; 何 asks “what.” The question **あの人はだれですか** asks who someone is; **これは何ですか** asks what an item is.

**学生 versus 先生:** 学生（がくせい） is a student; 先生（せんせい） is a teacher here. Both contain 生, but the complete words are read differently. Reading 生 as one fixed syllable everywhere is a common beginner mistake.

**教室 versus 先生:** 教室（きょうしつ） is a room; 先生 is a person or title. In **先生も教室にいます**, も means “also” and に marks the location in this example. The lesson checks only the recognition of も; it does not require mastery of the full existence pattern.

## 4. Read a second card and infer only what it says

> 名前：ミナ  
> 役割：学生  
> 場所：教室

This fictional card identifies Mina as a student in the classroom. It does **not** say that Mina is a teacher, where Mina was born or which card is hers among multiple cards. Answer **ミナさんはだれですか** with **学生です** only if the intended question is asking about role; to ask for a name, use **名前は何ですか** or **この人はだれですか** with a suitable identifying response.

**Controlled practice:** Cover the readings in the table. Read 先生, 学生 and 教室; then uncover the readings and check every kana, especially せんせい and きょうしつ.

**Guided practice:** Choose whether だれ, 何（なん）, どこ or どれ belongs in each blank: **教室は＿＿ですか** (where); **これは＿＿ですか** (what); **あの人は＿＿ですか** (who); **山田先生のカードは＿＿ですか** (which).

**Independent practice:** Write two short Q&A pairs: one about a person's role and one about a classroom location. Use the fictional cards above. Read your pairs aloud if useful; this is optional, ungraded oral rehearsal, not recorded or pronunciation-scored.

## 5. Check, correct, revisit

Complete the ten written recognition questions below. On a missed question, point to the **exact word or card line** that proves the answer. Retry after reviewing the table. Lesson completion and written quiz attempts are tracked separately on your device. Authentic listening comprehension belongs to L06, not this written kanji lesson.

**Takeaway:** Read kanji as meaningful words in their sentence. A person (人), role (先生／学生), name (名前), question (何) and place (教室) give different clues about what an answer must contain.
