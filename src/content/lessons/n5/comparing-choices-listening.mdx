## Your listening mission

Two fictional pairs make everyday decisions. In **Track A**, two students choose a café for studying. In **Track B**, friends decide which fruit to buy for a picnic. Listen for **which option wins on which criterion**. A place can be cheaper without being quieter; a person's favorite fruit is not necessarily everybody's favorite.

## Before listening: hear the comparison

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| どちら | どちら | which of two |
| 静か | しずか | quiet (な-adjective) |
| 安い | やすい | inexpensive |
| より | より | compared with; than |
| 〜のほうが | 〜のほうが | the ... option (is more ...) |
| 一番 | いちばん | most; number one |
| みかん | みかん | mandarin orange |
| 買いません | かいません | do not / will not buy (polite) |

For two options, **AよりBのほうが静かです** means “B is quieter than A.” **どちらが静かですか** asks “Which is quieter?” **一番好きです** expresses a favorite in a group. The **が** in 好きです marks the liked thing. These are general language clues, not the answers to the questions.

## Track A — Choose a study café

Listen first without the transcript. The speakers mention **two cafés**, and each has a different advantage. On the second play, distinguish **静か** (*shizuka*, quiet) from **安い** (*yasui*, cheap). Then notice where the speakers propose going. A suggestion with **〜ましょう** does not mean they have already arrived.

## Track B — Choose picnic fruit

Listen for three fruit names: **りんご** (*ringo*, apple), **バナナ** (*banana*, banana), and **みかん** (*mikan*, mandarin). One speaker uses **一番好き** to identify a favorite; the other compares **two** fruits. The final negative **買いません** identifies the item they will leave out today.

## The listening cycle

1. Play Track A without opening its transcript. Identify the main topic, then replay for details.
2. Answer Track A's three questions and submit. Repeat for Track B.
3. Reveal the Japanese transcript and English accessibility translation to check each cited phrase.
4. Replay a difficult turn and optionally shadow it aloud. Self-review is ungraded.

Transcripts are always available for accessibility. Opening one before submitting means that attempt is **transcript-assisted**, not an unaided listening result. If audio is unavailable, do not claim a blind listening score.

## Meaning checks and common traps

- **公園のカフェのほうが静かです。** (*Kōen no kafe no hō ga shizuka desu.*) “The park café is quieter.” The comparison does **not** say it is cheaper.
- **駅のカフェのほうが安いです。** (*Eki no kafe no hō ga yasui desu.*) “The station café is cheaper.” This does **not** establish that it is quieter.
- **私はみかんが一番好きです。** (*Watashi wa mikan ga ichiban suki desu.*) “I like mandarins best.” This is one person's preference, not a universal ranking.
- **りんごは今日は買いません。** (*Ringo wa kyō wa kaimasen.*) “We won't buy apples today.” The statement is about today's purchase, not a permanent dislike.

## After listening: guided use

Imagine choosing between a quiet library and a busy café. Write two sentences: one comparing the places, one saying where you would go. For example: **カフェより図書館のほうが静かです。今日は図書館に行きます。** (*Kafe yori toshokan no hō ga shizuka desu. Kyō wa toshokan ni ikimasu.* “The library is quieter than the café. I'm going to the library today.”)

Check the order of **より** and **ほうが**, whether your adjective fits the comparison, and whether your second sentence makes a clear choice. This is self-assessed writing, not a scored composition. Lesson 07 extends this to connected writing.
