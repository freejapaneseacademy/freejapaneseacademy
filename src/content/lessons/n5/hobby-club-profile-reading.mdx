## Your reading mission

A fictional neighborhood club is planning a weekend activity day. Four members have written short profiles. **Can you match each member to an activity without confusing a preference with a skill?** By the end, you will find the precise Japanese words that support an answer, not just guess from a familiar noun.

This is original academy reading practice, not a real person's profile or an official JLPT passage. Read once for the main idea, again for details, and only then check the English support.

## Before you read: three useful distinctions

- **〜が好きです** means “likes …”; it does **not** mean “is good at …”.
- **〜が上手です** means “is good at …”; **〜が下手です** means “is not good at …”. Be tactful when using these about other people.
- **〜が少しできます** says someone can do an activity *a little*. It does not claim expertise. **あまり〜じゃありません** usually means “not particularly / not very …” with the negative form.

Look for **でも** (“but”), **も** (“also”) and **は** when the writer contrasts two hobbies. In these profiles, **できます** is a first preview of ability; you will meet more precise verb-based forms later.

## The club's four profiles

### Card A — さき

> 私（わたし）はさきです。音楽（おんがく）が好きです。特（とく）に歌（うた）が好きです。でも、歌（うた）はあまり上手（じょうず）じゃありません。ピアノが少（すこ）しできます。

**English:** I'm Saki. I like music. I especially like singing. But I'm not very good at singing. I can play a little piano.

**Evidence to notice:** **歌が好きです** expresses preference, whereas **歌はあまり上手じゃありません** evaluates skill. The same hobby can have different answers to those two questions.

### Card B — たくみ

> 私（わたし）はたくみです。水泳（すいえい）が好きです。水泳（すいえい）は得意（とくい）です。映画（えいが）も好きですが、料理（りょうり）はあまり好きじゃありません。

**English:** I'm Takumi. I like swimming. Swimming is one of my strengths. I also like movies, but I don't particularly like cooking.

**Evidence to notice:** **得意です** (とくいです) describes something someone is good at or considers a strength. It is a useful related expression, not identical to “likes.” **好きですが** politely joins a contrast.

### Card C — みな

> 私（わたし）はみなです。読書（どくしょ）が好きです。写真（しゃしん）も好きで、休（やす）みの日（ひ）に写真（しゃしん）を撮（と）ります。でも、スポーツはあまり得意（とくい）じゃありません。

**English:** I'm Mina. I like reading. I also like photography, and I take photos on my days off. But sports are not really my strength.

**Evidence to notice:** **写真が好き** states an interest; **写真を撮ります** names an action. **休みの日に** means “on a day off.” Do not infer that Mina dislikes sports: the card only says they are not a strength.

### Card D — りく

> 私（わたし）はりくです。料理（りょうり）が好きです。毎週（まいしゅう）、家（いえ）で料理（りょうり）をします。絵（え）を描（か）くことも好きです。でも、ピアノはできません。

**English:** I'm Riku. I like cooking. I cook at home every week. I also like drawing pictures. But I cannot play the piano.

**Evidence to notice:** **毎週** means “every week.” **絵を描くこと** treats “drawing pictures” as an activity that can be liked. **ピアノはできません** is inability, not dislike.

## Reading method: claim → evidence → interpretation

For each question, find a **named person**, then underline the **whole phrase** containing the relevant hobby. A bare noun such as ピアノ is not enough: **ピアノが少しできます** and **ピアノはできません** say opposite things about ability.

**Guided example:** Who can play a little piano? The answer is Saki, because Card A says **ピアノが少しできます**. Card D says **ピアノはできません**, so Riku is not an acceptable answer.

**Second example:** Does Mina dislike sports? The text says **スポーツはあまり得意じゃありません** (“sports are not really my strength”). It does **not** say **スポーツが好きじゃありません**. Therefore “Mina dislikes sports” is **not established** by the passage.

## Glossary and reading support

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning in these profiles |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 特に | とくに | especially |
| 得意 | とくい | strength; good at |
| 休みの日 | やすみのひ | day off |
| 写真を撮ります | しゃしんをとります | take photographs |
| 毎週 | まいしゅう | every week |
| 絵を描くこと | えをかくこと | drawing pictures (as an activity) |
| できません | できません | cannot do (the activity) |

**Kanji noticing:** 音楽（おんがく）, 映画（えいが）, 読書（どくしょ） and 料理（りょうり） are whole words. Use the supplied readings; you do not need to infer every character's other readings. Read the original Japanese again without the English translation and locate each contrast word.

## Independent reading and writing

Without looking at the English, answer these questions in Japanese or English and quote a phrase: (1) Who likes both swimming and films? (2) Who takes photographs on days off? (3) Who enjoys cooking and drawing? (4) Who likes singing but is not very good at it?

**Evidence key (reveal after attempting):** Takumi — **水泳が好きです / 映画も好き**; Mina — **休みの日に写真を撮ります**; Riku — **料理が好きです / 絵を描くことも好きです**; Saki — **歌が好きです / 歌はあまり上手じゃありません**.

Now write two or three polite sentences for a **fictional fifth member**. Include a preference, one contrast or additional interest, and an ability statement. You may use the nouns above.

**Model:** 私は読書が好きです。映画も好きです。でも、ピアノはできません。 — “I like reading. I also like films. But I can't play piano.”

**Ungraded self-check:** Are the hobbies connected to the right particles? Does 好き express preference rather than skill? If you use あまり, is there a negative form? Is each statement supported by the fictional profile you created? Read your sentences aloud if you wish; no microphone or pronunciation grading is involved.
