## Two classes, one Saturday

At a fictional neighborhood community center, friends **あき** and **ゆい** look at a noticeboard. They want to choose **one** class for Saturday. Read their conversation to understand the **decision**, not to memorize every grammar form at once.

**Can-do:** Make a basic comparison and select an option. In this L01, your goal is to **recognize the meaning in context**; L03 will teach how to build the comparison yourself.

### Original noticeboard

| Class | Start | Fee | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 料理（りょうり）クラス — cooking class | 10:00 | 800円（えん） | おもしろい — interesting |
| 絵（え）のクラス — art class | 11:00 | 500円 | たのしい — fun |

The schedule and prices are invented for this learning scenario. **円（えん）** means yen; the amounts are examples, not real course prices.

### Original conversation

> **あき:** 土曜日（どようび）、料理（りょうり）クラスと絵（え）のクラス、どちらがいいですか。  
> *On Saturday, which would be better: the cooking class or the art class?*
>
> **ゆい:** 絵のクラスのほうが安（やす）いです。料理クラスより三百円（さんびゃくえん）安いです。  
> *The art class is cheaper. It is 300 yen cheaper than the cooking class.*
>
> **あき:** そうですね。でも、料理クラスのほうが早（はや）いです。十時（じゅうじ）からです。  
> *That's true. But the cooking class is earlier. It starts at ten.*
>
> **ゆい:** 私（わたし）は絵が好（す）きです。二（ふた）つの中（なか）では、絵のクラスが一番（いちばん）いいです。  
> *I like art. Of the two, the art class is the best for me.*
>
> **あき:** じゃあ、絵のクラスにしましょう。  
> *Then let's choose the art class.*

**Who?** Aki and Yui are friends. **Where?** They are looking at a community-center noticeboard. **Why?** They need to pick one Saturday class. Their final choice is the **art class**.

## How to notice a comparison

| Clue | Example from the conversation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **どちら** | どちらがいいですか。 | Which of the two is better? |
| **A のほうが** | 絵のクラスのほうが安いです。 | The art class is the cheaper option. |
| **B より** | 料理クラスより三百円安いです。 | 300 yen cheaper **than** cooking class. |
| **一番** | 絵のクラスが一番いいです。 | The best within the stated set. |

**A のほうが B より adjective** expresses a comparison: **絵のクラスのほうが料理クラスより安いです** (*The art class is cheaper than the cooking class*). The conversation splits this information across two sentences. **より** marks the baseline; **のほうが** marks the side with more of the quality. A simple **A は安いです** only says A is cheap; it does not compare A with B.

**どちら** is useful for choosing between **two** alternatives. **一番** means “most” or “number one” in a set; here **二つの中では** limits the set to the two classes. It is not a claim that art is the world's best class. When you compare three or more, **どれ** (“which one?”) is often useful instead of どちら.

### Reading and meaning aids

- **りょうり** — cooking; **え** — art/picture; **クラス** — class.
- **やすい** — inexpensive; **はやい** — early (or fast in other contexts).
- **どちらがいいですか** — which of the two is better?
- **さんびゃくえん** — 300 yen; **じゅうじ** — ten o'clock.
- **すきです** — like; **いちばんいいです** — best in the relevant group.
- **にしましょう** — let's choose/settle on (that option).

**Check the numbers:** 800 − 500 = **300 yen**. The art class starts at 11:00; the cooking class starts at 10:00. Thus **cheaper** and **earlier** refer to *different* classes. Neither is automatically “better” for everyone.

## Guided reading: find evidence

1. Underline **どちらがいいですか**. Is Aki asking about two classes or asking what time it is?
2. Find **絵のクラスのほうが安いです**. Which class costs less? Confirm with the noticeboard.
3. Find **料理クラスのほうが早いです**. Which begins earlier? Verify 10:00 versus 11:00.
4. Find **私は絵が好きです**. Which personal preference influences Yui's choice?
5. Find **絵のクラスにしましょう**. Which option do they finally select?

### A second original mini-scene

> **先生（せんせい）:** 赤（あか）いノートと青（あお）いノート、どちらがいいですか。  
> *Which do you prefer, the red notebook or the blue notebook?*
>
> **学生（がくせい）:** 青いノートのほうが好（す）きです。  
> *I like the blue notebook better.*

Here the comparison is about **preference**, not a measurable price. **好き** is a な-adjective-like expression: **青いノートのほうが好きです** is natural. Do not add **安い** when you mean “I like it better.”

## Common misunderstandings

**“より” does not mean “the winner.”** In **料理クラスより安い**, the cooking class is the reference point, *not* the cheaper class. The subject of the preceding sentence, **絵のクラス**, supplies the cheaper option.

**“早い” is not “安い.”** Both are い-adjectives but 早い means early/fast, while 安い means inexpensive. Read the time and price data to decide which applies.

**“一番” needs a comparison set.** Yui is choosing among these two classes; the sentence does not assert a universal ranking.

## Independent transfer

Imagine a new fictional choice: a music class at 9:00 costing 700 yen, and a dance class at 10:00 costing 400 yen. Without looking back, answer in English or Japanese: Which is cheaper? Which is earlier? Which would **you** choose, and why? Try writing **ダンスのクラスのほうが安いです** (*The dance class is cheaper*). Your personal choice is not automatically graded.

**Self-review:** Did you identify both alternatives? Did you use the numbers correctly? Did you distinguish an objective comparison from a personal preference? Did you locate a Japanese phrase as evidence?
