## Your reading mission

At a fictional language-exchange event, three people submit brief introductions. Read the Japanese **before** the English support. These people, biographies and places of residence are invented for practice. A bio is not a complete life history.

### Profile A — 田中みお（たなか みお）

> はじめまして。わたしは たなか みおです。二十歳（はたち）です。京都（きょうと）出身（しゅっしん）です。大学生（だいがくせい）です。今（いま）は 大阪（おおさか）に 住（す）んでいます。よろしく おねがいします。

**English support:** Nice to meet you. I'm Mio Tanaka. I'm 20. I'm from Kyoto. I'm a university student. I currently live in Osaka. Pleased to meet you.

### Profile B — 山本けん（やまもと けん）

> はじめまして。ぼくは やまもと けんです。二十五歳（にじゅうごさい）です。福岡（ふくおか）出身です。会社員（かいしゃいん）です。今は 東京（とうきょう）に 住んでいます。よろしく おねがいします。

**English support:** Nice to meet you. I'm Ken Yamamoto. I'm 25. I'm from Fukuoka. I'm a company employee. I currently live in Tokyo. Pleased to meet you. **ぼく** is a casual-masculine first-person pronoun, used here within an otherwise polite introduction; it is not appropriate for everyone.

### Profile C — 佐藤あや（さとう あや）

> はじめまして。わたしは さとう あやです。十八歳（じゅうはっさい）です。札幌（さっぽろ）出身です。高校生（こうこうせい）です。今も 札幌に 住んでいます。よろしく おねがいします。

**English support:** Nice to meet you. I'm Aya Sato. I'm 18. I'm from Sapporo. I'm a high-school student. I still live in Sapporo.

## Step 1 — Read one fact at a time

Look for **二十歳**, **二十五歳**, **十八歳** to identify ages. **二十歳** is commonly read **はたち**, an irregular age expression; do not mechanically read it as にじゅうさい here. **大学生**, **会社員** and **高校生** identify roles, not workplaces or the names of schools. The three bios give no university, employer or school names.

The pattern **京都出身です** means “(I am) from Kyoto.” It indicates a place of origin, not necessarily the place where the speaker lives today. **今は大阪に住んでいます** explicitly describes current residence. **今も札幌に住んでいます** uses **も** (“also/still”) to say that Aya continues living in Sapporo. It is not evidence that she has never traveled elsewhere.

## Step 2 — Recover an omitted subject carefully

After **わたしは たなか みおです**, the following sentences **二十歳です** and **大学生です** omit the subject because the same speaker is understood. Do not assume this applies to another profile. The subject resets when a new named profile begins. **ぼく** in Ken's bio refers to Ken, not Mio.

In Japanese, **は** marks the topic; **に** marks the place of residence with **住んでいます**. **出身** does not require に in the expression **京都出身です**. These are useful patterns to recognize, not a demand to memorize every verb conjugation today.

## Step 3 — Cite evidence rather than infer

**Worked question:** Where does Mio live now? **Answer:** Osaka. **Evidence:** 「今は 大阪に 住んでいます。」 The separate phrase 「京都出身です」 supports her hometown, *not* her current residence.

**Another question:** What company employs Ken? **Answer:** Not stated. **Evidence check:** 「会社員です」 says only that he is a company employee. There is no company name in his bio.

## Guided reading

Cover the English support and draw four columns on paper: name, age, hometown, current city. Fill them using exact Japanese phrases. Then add a fifth column for school/work role. For Aya, compare **札幌出身** with **今も札幌に住んでいます**: they name the same city, but answer different questions.

## Independent practice

Complete the ten reading questions below. For each choice, locate the Japanese words that prove it. If information is absent, choose **not stated** rather than making a plausible guess. Feedback reveals the evidence after submission.

Afterward, optionally write two short sentences about one fictional profile. Compare with the model in the practice area. Your written response is self-reviewed, not automatically scored; this lesson does not assess listening or spoken pronunciation.

**Takeaway:** A good reader distinguishes a person's hometown, present city, age and role—and quotes the phrase that actually supports the answer.
