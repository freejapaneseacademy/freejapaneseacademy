## Reading mission: What happens at the community center?

**Can-do:** read a short, original Japanese **verb list** and find stated facts. You will identify who does an action, what the action is, and when it happens. You will also connect dictionary forms such as **読む（よむ）** to polite forms such as **読みます（よみます）**.

Imagine a fictional neighborhood center preparing a Saturday program. A volunteer posts two pieces of information: a compact verb list for helpers, and a polite visitor notice. These are invented practice materials, not a real schedule.

## 1. Preview the useful words

| Japanese | Kana | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 土曜日 | どようび | Saturday |
| 午前 | ごぜん | morning / a.m. |
| 午後 | ごご | afternoon / p.m. |
| 本 | ほん | book |
| 絵 | え | picture |
| お茶 | おちゃ | tea |
| 教室 | きょうしつ | classroom |
| 読む | よむ | read |
| 書く | かく | write |
| 見る | みる | see / look at |
| 飲む | のむ | drink |
| 来る | くる | come |

A dictionary form labels an action in a list. A polite announcement typically uses **〜ます**. For example, **読む → 読みます** names the same reading action. The difference is the grammatical form and register, not a different activity.

## 2. Original reading A: the helper's verb list

Read the list once without translating every word. The heading **すること** means “things to do.”

> **土曜日の すること（どようびの すること） — Saturday tasks**  
> **午前九時（ごぜん くじ）**：本を 読む（ほんを よむ） — read a book  
> **午前十時（ごぜん じゅうじ）**：絵を 見る（えを みる） — look at pictures  
> **午後一時（ごご いちじ）**：名前を 書く（なまえを かく） — write a name  
> **午後二時（ごご にじ）**：お茶を 飲む（おちゃを のむ） — drink tea

**Notice:** the list uses dictionary forms as concise labels. It does not say that all activities are happening right now. The times give the schedule.

Find and underline the evidence for these facts before reading the answers: What happens at ten in the morning? What happens at two in the afternoon? Which action uses **書く**?

**Evidence and answers:** **午前十時：絵を 見る** says to look at pictures at 10 a.m.; **午後二時：お茶を 飲む** says to drink tea at 2 p.m.; **午後一時：名前を 書く** identifies the writing action at 1 p.m.

## 3. Original reading B: the visitor notice

> **土曜日の お知らせ（どようびの おしらせ） — Saturday notice**  
> 午前九時に 本を 読みます。  
> （ごぜん くじに ほんを よみます。）  
> *At 9 a.m., we read books.*  
> 午前十時に 絵を 見ます。  
> （ごぜん じゅうじに えを みます。）  
> *At 10 a.m., we look at pictures.*  
> 午後一時に 名前を 書きます。  
> （ごご いちじに なまえを かきます。）  
> *At 1 p.m., we write names.*  
> 午後二時に お茶を 飲みます。  
> （ごご にじに おちゃを のみます。）  
> *At 2 p.m., we drink tea.*

Here **に** marks the specified time. The polite verbs **読みます・見ます・書きます・飲みます** correspond to the list's **読む・見る・書く・飲む**. Japanese can omit the subject when context makes it clear; the English “we” is a natural contextual rendering, not a word literally printed in the notice.

## 4. Read for evidence, not guesses

Compare **午前九時に 本を 読みます** with **午前十時に 絵を 見ます**. The first refers to reading a book; the second refers to looking at pictures. **見る** is not a synonym for **読む**. Compare **午後一時に 名前を 書きます** with **午後二時に お茶を 飲みます**. The particles are the same, but the objects and actions are different.

**Common trap:** **来る（くる）→ 来ます（きます）** is an irregular pair. It does not appear as a scheduled activity in these two texts. A familiar verb from your vocabulary list is not automatically evidence for a reading answer.

## 5. Guided evidence search

Cover the English translations. Answer in Japanese or English and copy the exact Japanese phrase that supports your answer.

1. What is the first activity? **午前九時に 本を 読みます。**
2. Which task comes after looking at pictures? **午後一時：名前を 書く。**
3. Which form in the notice matches the dictionary form 飲む? **飲みます。**
4. Is the picture activity in the morning or afternoon? **午前十時** shows morning.

## 6. Independent reading and writing

Imagine you are helping a new visitor. Write two or three short Japanese sentences about the notice using **〜ます** and a time with **に**. Then annotate each sentence with the corresponding dictionary form.

**Model:** 午前十時に 絵を 見ます。（見る）午後二時に お茶を 飲みます。（飲む）

Self-check your writing: (1) Did you copy the correct time? (2) Did you match the object to the action? (3) Did you use the polite form correctly? (4) Can you point to the supporting phrase in the reading? Different natural sentence choices are welcome; this writing is **self-reviewed**, not automatically graded.

## 7. Check your understanding

Complete the twelve-question interactive reading check. Every correct answer can be verified directly from the two original texts or the form comparisons above. Read feedback even when you are correct. No audio or pronunciation grading is provided in this written L05 reading lesson.
