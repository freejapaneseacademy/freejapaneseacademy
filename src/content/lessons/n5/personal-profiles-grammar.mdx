## At the community language exchange

You are preparing a name card and introducing yourself to a partner. The goal is to write a **short, accurate self-introduction**, not to memorize a long biography. These profiles and people are fictional; you need not share personal information.

### 1. State a fact politely: AはBです

**は** (pronounced *wa* when used as a particle) sets the topic; **です** finishes a polite noun sentence.

- <span lang="ja">わたしは森（もり）です。</span> — I'm Mori.
- <span lang="ja">わたしは大学生（だいがくせい）です。</span> — I'm a university student.
- <span lang="ja">出身（しゅっしん）は大阪（おおさか）です。</span> — I'm from Osaka. Literally, the place of origin is Osaka.
- <span lang="ja">仕事（しごと）は教師（きょうし）です。</span> — As for my work, I'm a teacher. In conversation, <span lang="ja">教師です。</span> alone may suffice.

In a self-introduction, Japanese often omits repeated **わたしは** once the subject is clear. The English translation may still supply “I” for clarity.

### 2. Ask and correct politely

Add **か** after **です** for a polite yes/no question:

<span lang="ja">森さんは学生（がくせい）ですか。</span> — Is Mori a student?

<span lang="ja">いいえ、学生じゃありません。会社員（かいしゃいん）です。</span> — No, I'm not a student. I'm a company employee.

**じゃありません** is a common polite negative in conversation. **ではありません** is a more formal alternative. Do not attach **か** to a plain noun and assume it automatically becomes the same polite pattern.

### 3. Review the particles: は, も and の

| Particle | Job | Japanese | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **は** | Sets the topic | <span lang="ja">森さんは学生です。</span> | Mori is a student. |
| **も** | Adds “also” | <span lang="ja">わたしも学生です。</span> | I'm also a student. |
| **の** | Links nouns | <span lang="ja">森さんの学校（がっこう）</span> | Mori's school |

**も** normally takes the place of topic **は** in this simple pattern, not <span lang="ja">はも</span>. **の** connects nouns and cannot substitute for **は** in <span lang="ja">わたしは学生です</span>. In <span lang="ja">日本（にほん）の会社</span>, **の** can express association (“a Japanese company”), not necessarily legal ownership.

### 4. Age and origin without overgeneralizing

- <span lang="ja">森さんは二十歳（はたち）です。</span> — Mori is twenty years old. **二十歳** has the special reading *はたち*.
- <span lang="ja">出身は京都（きょうと）です。</span> — I'm from Kyoto.
- <span lang="ja">学校は東京（とうきょう）です。</span> — My school is in Tokyo (when the context makes “school location” clear).

For age questions, <span lang="ja">何歳（なんさい）ですか。</span> is grammatically possible but can feel intrusive depending on context; use it only when relevant and welcome. We are not asking you to disclose your age.

### Original exchange: two new classmates

| Speaker | Japanese | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 森 | <span lang="ja">はじめまして。森です。大阪出身です。</span> | Nice to meet you. I'm Mori. I'm from Osaka. |
| アキ | <span lang="ja">はじめまして。アキです。森さんは学生ですか。</span> | Nice to meet you. I'm Aki. Mori, are you a student? |
| 森 | <span lang="ja">いいえ、学生じゃありません。会社員です。</span> | No, I'm not a student. I'm an office/company employee. |
| アキ | <span lang="ja">そうですか。わたしは大学生です。</span> | I see. I'm a university student. |
| 森 | <span lang="ja">アキさんの学校は東京ですか。</span> | Is your school in Tokyo, Aki? |
| アキ | <span lang="ja">はい、東京です。</span> | Yes, it's in Tokyo. |

**Read for evidence:** Mori is from Osaka (<span lang="ja">大阪出身です</span>) and is a company employee (<span lang="ja">会社員です</span>). Aki is a university student (<span lang="ja">大学生です</span>). The dialogue is original written material, not recorded audio.

### Repair a common error

| Intended meaning | Avoid | Better |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I'm a student. | <span lang="ja">わたしの学生です。</span> | <span lang="ja">わたしは学生です。</span> |
| I'm also a student. | <span lang="ja">わたしはも学生です。</span> | <span lang="ja">わたしも学生です。</span> |
| Mori's school | <span lang="ja">森さんは学校</span> | <span lang="ja">森さんの学校</span> |

### Guided practice → independent use

**Observe:** <span lang="ja">わたしは学生です。出身は京都です。</span> — I'm a student. I'm from Kyoto.

**Recombine:** Replace **学生** with **会社員** and **京都** with **大阪**. Model: <span lang="ja">わたしは会社員です。出身は大阪です。</span>

**Write:** Invent a fictional person and compose two to four short connected sentences: name, hometown, work/school, and optionally a correction to a mistaken assumption. One model is <span lang="ja">はじめまして。ユキです。出身は京都です。大学生です。</span> — Nice to meet you. I'm Yuki. I'm from Kyoto. I'm a university student.

**Self-review rubric:** Check that (1) the profile communicates at least two clear facts, (2) noun predicates end in **です** or **じゃありません**, (3) **は / も / の** express the intended relationship, and (4) a reader can identify the person without guessing. Alternative natural wording is welcome; this open response is not automatically graded.

**Optional oral rehearsal:** Read the fictional model aloud, then ask <span lang="ja">学生ですか。</span> and reply <span lang="ja">いいえ、会社員です。</span> at a comfortable pace. This written lesson has no recorded audio or pronunciation scoring; the chapter L06 provides authentic listening practice.
