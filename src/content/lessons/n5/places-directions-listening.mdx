## A visitor needs directions

Imagine a public community center. You have two short conversations to understand: one about the library and cafeteria, the other about the restroom and reception. **Your goal is to understand what people say**, not to identify written characters by sound. The recordings are generated only through the approved licensed audio workflow; the scoring interface remains locked until genuine audio can play.

### Before listening: prepare your ears

You already know <span lang="ja">どこですか</span> (where is it?), <span lang="ja">ここ</span> (here), and <span lang="ja">あそこ</span> (over there). Listen for these additional phrases:

| Spoken Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">一階</span> | いっかい | first floor |
| <span lang="ja">二階</span> | にかい | second floor |
| <span lang="ja">入口</span> | いりぐち | entrance |
| <span lang="ja">近く</span> | ちかく | nearby |
| <span lang="ja">階段</span> | かいだん | stairs |
| <span lang="ja">右</span> | みぎ | right-hand side |
| <span lang="ja">受付</span> | うけつけ | reception desk |

**Sound focus:** <span lang="ja">いっかい</span> contains a small pause before <span lang="ja">か</span>, while <span lang="ja">にかい</span> does not. This is a useful auditory contrast, not a spelling quiz.

### Track A: finding two facilities

First, listen without opening the transcript. What two facilities does the visitor ask about? On a second play, note the floor number of each and the landmark mentioned at the end. The staff member says <span lang="ja">いいえ</span> to correct the visitor's guess: that correction changes the answer.

### Track B: restroom and reception

Listen for the order of the two requests. Notice that <span lang="ja">あそこ</span> points away from the speaker, while <span lang="ja">ここ</span> identifies the speaker's own location. Also listen for <span lang="ja">階段の右</span>: the restroom is to the **right** of the stairs.

### Listening strategy

1. **First pass:** identify the place names without taking notes.
2. **Second pass:** write a tiny English map: library → floor; cafeteria → floor + landmark; restroom → direction; reception → here/there.
3. **Third pass:** answer the six English-language comprehension questions. Do not open the Japanese transcripts until you have attempted to understand the speech.
4. **Review:** after checking answers, compare the original speaker-by-speaker transcript with the words you actually heard. The interface clearly labels transcript-assisted attempts.

### Guided example

If someone asks <span lang="ja">図書館はどこですか。</span> and receives <span lang="ja">図書館は二階にあります。</span>, the evidence for *second floor* is <span lang="ja">二階にあります</span>. The particle <span lang="ja">に</span> marks the location in this existence sentence. This example is a **pre-listening grammar reminder**; do not assume it answers every question in the two conversations.

### After listening: transfer the skill

Draw a two-floor fictional community center. In three short sentences, place the library, cafeteria and restroom. Example: <span lang="ja">図書館は二階にあります。食堂は一階です。トイレは入口の近くです。</span> Compare your directions with the recorded conversations: is each floor or landmark correctly identified? This writing task is self-reviewed, not automatically graded.

**Optional speaking:** Practise asking <span lang="ja">すみません。トイレはどこですか。</span> and answering <span lang="ja">あそこです。</span> Use the authentic recordings as models once available. No microphone capture, native-speaker evaluation or pronunciation score is implied.

### Self-check

Can you distinguish the two floor numbers by sound? Can you hear a negative correction and update your first guess? Can you identify the right-hand landmark and the location of reception? Replay and review the transcript if necessary. Your six-question score records listening practice, not JLPT certification.
