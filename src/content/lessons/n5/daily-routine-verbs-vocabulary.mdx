## Why these verbs matter

**Can-do:** Describe a usual day using polite present verbs. In a fictional school exchange, Haru tells a new classmate what a normal weekday looks like. The verbs below express *usual actions*, not necessarily actions happening right now. This is written word study: no audio, native-speaker pronunciation review or speaking grade is claimed.

## 1. Twelve actions to recognize and retrieve

Read each verb aloud if you wish, but check the kana reading visually. All forms below end in polite **ます**.

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">起きます</span> | おきます | get up |
| <span lang="ja">寝ます</span> | ねます | go to bed / sleep |
| <span lang="ja">食べます</span> | たべます | eat |
| <span lang="ja">飲みます</span> | のみます | drink |
| <span lang="ja">行きます</span> | いきます | go |
| <span lang="ja">帰ります</span> | かえります | return / go home |
| <span lang="ja">勉強します</span> | べんきょうします | study |
| <span lang="ja">読みます</span> | よみます | read |
| <span lang="ja">書きます</span> | かきます | write |
| <span lang="ja">見ます</span> | みます | watch / look at |
| <span lang="ja">聞きます</span> | ききます | listen / ask |
| <span lang="ja">働きます</span> | はたらきます | work |

**Study strategy:** Cover the meaning column. Retrieve the English meaning from the Japanese, then cover the Japanese column and retrieve the verb from the meaning. Say the kana slowly if helpful; this is not assessed pronunciation.

## 2. Everyday objects and destinations

Japanese often marks the object of an action with **を** (pronounced *お*): <span lang="ja">朝ごはんを食べます。</span> — “I eat breakfast.” A destination commonly takes **に**: <span lang="ja">学校に行きます。</span> — “I go to school.” A clock time can also take **に**: <span lang="ja">七時に起きます。</span> — “I get up at seven.” A location where an activity happens commonly takes **で**: <span lang="ja">図書館で勉強します。</span> — “I study at the library.”

These particles are *context clues* for the vocabulary; the next grammar lesson explains them systematically. Do not put **を** after a destination just because an English sentence contains “to.”

## 3. See each action in a complete sentence

1. <span lang="ja">朝七時に起きます。</span> (おきます) — I get up at seven in the morning. The main action is **起きます**.

2. <span lang="ja">夜十一時に寝ます。</span> (ねます) — I go to bed at eleven at night. The main action is **寝ます**.

3. <span lang="ja">朝ごはんを食べます。</span> (たべます) — I eat breakfast. The main action is **食べます**.

4. <span lang="ja">水を飲みます。</span> (のみます) — I drink water. The main action is **飲みます**.

5. <span lang="ja">学校に行きます。</span> (いきます) — I go to school. The main action is **行きます**.

6. <span lang="ja">六時に家に帰ります。</span> (かえります) — I return home at six. The main action is **帰ります**.

7. <span lang="ja">日本語を勉強します。</span> (べんきょうします) — I study Japanese. The main action is **勉強します**.

8. <span lang="ja">本を読みます。</span> (よみます) — I read a book. The main action is **読みます**.

9. <span lang="ja">日記を書きます。</span> (かきます) — I write a diary. The main action is **書きます**.

10. <span lang="ja">テレビを見ます。</span> (みます) — I watch television. The main action is **見ます**.

11. <span lang="ja">音楽を聞きます。</span> (ききます) — I listen to music. The main action is **聞きます**.

12. <span lang="ja">店で働きます。</span> (はたらきます) — I work at a shop. The main action is **働きます**.

Notice that **寝ます** means “go to bed / sleep,” while **起きます** means “wake up / get up.” **行きます** is movement *to* somewhere; **帰ります** is returning to a place such as home. **読みます** is reading written material, whereas **見ます** is looking at or watching something. **聞きます** can mean listening or asking, depending on context; here **音楽を聞きます** means listening to music.

## 4. A short fictional weekday

> <span lang="ja">はるの一日（いちにち）</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">朝七時に起きます。</span> — I get up at seven in the morning.
>
> <span lang="ja">朝ごはんを食べます。水を飲みます。</span> — I eat breakfast and drink water.
>
> <span lang="ja">八時に学校に行きます。</span> — I go to school at eight.
>
> <span lang="ja">学校で日本語を勉強します。</span> — I study Japanese at school.
>
> <span lang="ja">夕方六時に家に帰ります。</span> — I return home at six in the evening.
>
> <span lang="ja">夜、本を読みます。十一時に寝ます。</span> — At night I read a book. I go to bed at eleven.

**Reading check:** What does Haru drink? Find <span lang="ja">水を飲みます</span>. Where does Haru study? Find <span lang="ja">学校で日本語を勉強します</span>. What happens after returning home? The note mentions reading a book at night. The order in the text is the evidence; do not invent missing activities.

## 5. Common mix-ups

- **勉強します** is a します verb: the whole polite expression means “study.” **勉強をします** is also possible, but you do not need to insert **を** into **勉強します**.
- **朝ごはんを飲みます** would be unusual for an ordinary breakfast; use **食べます** for eating and **飲みます** for beverages.
- **学校に行きます** means “go to school”; **学校で勉強します** means “study at school.” **に** marks the destination, **で** the action location.
- The nonpast **〜ます** can describe habits and future actions. It does not by itself mean “am doing right now.” **〜ません** is the polite negative form; you will practise it in L03.

## 6. Guided retrieval and independent transfer

Without looking at the table, supply the verb: “drink water” → <span lang="ja">水を＿＿。</span>; “read a book” → <span lang="ja">本を＿＿。</span>; “return home” → <span lang="ja">家に＿＿。</span>. Answers: **飲みます**, **読みます**, **帰ります**.

Now write three connected sentences describing a fictional weekday, including a wake-up time, one daytime activity and a night activity. Model: <span lang="ja">七時に起きます。学校で日本語を勉強します。夜、本を読みます。</span> — I get up at seven. I study Japanese at school. At night, I read a book. Check the action verb, its object/destination particle and whether the sequence is plausible. Your writing is self-reviewed, not automatically graded.
