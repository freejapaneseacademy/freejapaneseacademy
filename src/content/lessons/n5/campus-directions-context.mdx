## A new student needs the library

**Chapter can-do:** say where common places and objects are. At a fictional language school's entrance, student **Mika** asks **Sato**, a receptionist, where to find the library and restroom. Their conversation is a written model, **not audio**. The building's places are invented for practice.

| Speaker | Japanese | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mika | <span lang="ja">すみません。としょしつは どこですか。</span> | Excuse me. Where is the library room? |
| Sato | <span lang="ja">としょしつは あそこです。</span> | The library room is over there. |
| Mika | <span lang="ja">あの へやですか。</span> | Is it that room over there? |
| Sato | <span lang="ja">はい。あの へやです。</span> | Yes, that room. |
| Mika | <span lang="ja">トイレは どこですか。</span> | Where is the restroom? |
| Sato | <span lang="ja">トイレは そこです。ミカさんの うしろです。</span> | The restroom is there, behind you. |
| Mika | <span lang="ja">ありがとうございます。</span> | Thank you. |

The desk is at the entrance. Mika and Sato stand together. The **library is across the hall**, away from both. The **restroom is behind Mika**, near her but not Sato. These positions matter more than memorizing an English translation of “there.”

## 1. Read the scene, not just individual words

Mika is asking for directions, not reporting a lost item. Sato works at the entrance and answers with locations. The key question is <span lang="ja">どこですか</span> — “Where is it?” In <span lang="ja">としょしつは どこですか</span>, **は** marks the place being asked about; **どこ** asks for its location.

The answer <span lang="ja">あそこです</span> works because the library is far from both speakers. If they moved to the library doorway, the same place could be called **ここ** instead. Location words depend on viewpoint.

## 2. A three-way location contrast

| Form | Usual perspective | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">ここ</span> | here, near the speaker | <span lang="ja">ここは きょうしつです。</span> — This place is a classroom. |
| <span lang="ja">そこ</span> | there, near the listener or a previously indicated location | <span lang="ja">そこは トイレです。</span> — That place is the restroom. |
| <span lang="ja">あそこ</span> | over there, away from both | <span lang="ja">あそこは としょしつです。</span> — Over there is the library room. |
| <span lang="ja">どこ</span> | where? | <span lang="ja">じむしつは どこですか。</span> — Where is the office? |

These are **place words**. Contrast the object words from the previous chapter: **これ** = this object, but **ここ** = here. Similarly, **あれ** refers to a distant thing, while **あそこ** refers to a distant place. A place can also be introduced with **あの + noun**, as in <span lang="ja">あの へや</span> (“that room over there”).

## 3. Vocabulary in a school building

| Japanese | Reading / meaning | Scene clue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">としょしつ（図書室）</span> | としょしつ — library room | across the hall |
| <span lang="ja">きょうしつ（教室）</span> | きょうしつ — classroom | where lessons happen |
| <span lang="ja">じむしつ（事務室）</span> | じむしつ — office | staff work here |
| <span lang="ja">トイレ</span> | トイレ — restroom | behind Mika |
| <span lang="ja">へや（部屋）</span> | へや — room | a general room word |
| <span lang="ja">いりぐち（入口）</span> | いりぐち — entrance | where the desk is |

Kanji in parentheses are **recognition support**, not new mastery requirements in this context lesson. Pronunciation is not assessed without real recordings.

## 4. Location with に: a preview

Compare <span lang="ja">としょしつは あそこです。</span> (“The library room is over there”) with <span lang="ja">としょしつは あそこに あります。</span> (“The library room is located over there”). **に** marks the location with the existence verb **あります**. You cannot simply insert に before です: <span lang="ja">あそこにです</span> is not the intended complete location answer. The **あります/います** contrast is taught systematically later in this module; here it is a supported preview.

For a person, <span lang="ja">せんせいは ここに います。</span> (“The teacher is here”) uses **います**, not あります. You only need to notice that the location takes に in these patterns; this lesson's scored check focuses on the scene and the introductory forms.

## 5. Find evidence before answering

**Question:** Where is the library? The text says <span lang="ja">としょしつは あそこです</span>; the setup specifies that the library is across the hall. Answer: away from both speakers.

**Question:** Where is the restroom? The text says <span lang="ja">トイレは そこです</span> and <span lang="ja">ミカさんの うしろです</span>. Answer: behind Mika, near the listener. Do not mistake **そこ** for a fixed compass direction.

**Question:** Does Mika already know the location? Her first <span lang="ja">どこですか</span> shows that she is asking. Sato provides the information.

## 6. Guided practice and independent transfer

**Step A:** Imagine you are standing in the classroom. Say <span lang="ja">ここは きょうしつです。</span> (“This place is the classroom.”)

**Step B:** Imagine a partner stands by the office. Ask <span lang="ja">じむしつは どこですか。</span> Your partner can answer <span lang="ja">ここです。</span> (“Here”) while you might say <span lang="ja">そこです。</span> (“There, near you”). Both can be appropriate from different perspectives.

**Step C:** A library is far from both of you. Answer <span lang="ja">あそこです。</span> (“Over there”). Pointing words change when the speaker moves.

**Independent task:** Write a three-line exchange asking about two facilities in an imaginary building. Include one **どこですか** question and one **ここ／そこ／あそこです** answer. Compare your work to this model:

> <span lang="ja">すみません。じむしつは どこですか。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">じむしつは あそこです。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">ありがとうございます。</span>

**Self-check:** (1) Is the place named? (2) Does the question use **どこ**? (3) Does the answer match the speaker's position? (4) Is the reply polite and understandable? Read aloud optionally; no audio or pronunciation grade is claimed.

## Review

Use **ここ** for the speaker's area, **そこ** for the listener's area or an indicated place, and **あそこ** for somewhere away from both. **どこ** asks where. In a location sentence, **に** commonly accompanies **あります/います**, but not the bare **です** answer.
