## Your task: help new classmates use a shared room

A fictional neighborhood language club is preparing a small study room. You are helping make a **friendly Japanese notice** for new members. Your notice should tell people what to do, what is allowed, and what is not allowed. Write **two to five short connected sentences**. You can also write a short question to ask a teacher for permission.

This is a writing and typing lesson. No microphone, handwriting recognition or audio recording is required. Use a Japanese keyboard or IME; you may type kana first and convert to kanji if comfortable.

### Observe: the model notice

> ここは日本語（にほんご）の教室（きょうしつ）です。  
> 入（い）り口（ぐち）で手（て）を洗（あら）ってください。  
> 水（みず）を飲（の）んでもいいです。  
> この部屋（へや）で食（た）べ物（もの）を食（た）べてはいけません。  
> 帰（かえ）るときは、ドアを閉（し）めてください。

**English:** This is a Japanese classroom. Please wash your hands at the entrance. Drinking water is allowed. You must not eat food in this room. Please close the door when leaving.

The example is a fictional room policy, not a statement about actual schools. Notice that **〜てもいいです** is a statement granting permission; **〜てもいいですか** asks for permission.

### Observe: asking before acting

> 学生（がくせい）：先生（せんせい）、ここで写真（しゃしん）を撮（と）ってもいいですか。  
> 先生：すみません。ここでは写真を撮ってはいけません。  
> 学生：わかりました。

**English:** Student: Teacher, may I take a photo here? Teacher: Sorry, taking photos here is not allowed. Student: Understood.

A request for help is different: **窓（まど）を開（あ）けてください。** means “Please open the window.” It is not a question about permission.

## The three useful endings

| Purpose | Pattern | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ask someone to act | verb て-form + ください | ドアを閉めてください。 | Please close the door. |
| Ask permission | verb て-form + もいいですか | 水を飲んでもいいですか。 | May I drink water? |
| State a prohibition | verb て-form + はいけません | ここで走ってはいけません。 | You must not run here. |
| Grant permission | verb て-form + もいいです | 水を飲んでもいいです。 | Drinking water is allowed. |

**Particle reminder:** **で** marks the location of an action (教室で, ここで); **を** marks the object (水を飲む, ドアを閉める). **は** in **〜てはいけません** is part of the prohibition pattern; **も** in **〜てもいい** is essential for permission. Do not swap them.

**Form reminder:** 飲む → 飲んで, 洗う → 洗って, 閉める → 閉めて, 撮る → 撮って, 走る → 走って. The form **飲みて** is not correct; the te-form of 飲む is **飲んで**.

## Guided writing ladder

**Step 1 — Recombine.** Choose one place and one action: **入り口で + 手を洗う**. Change 洗う to 洗って and add ください: **入り口で手を洗ってください。** Keep the location and object particles.

**Step 2 — Change the function.** Start with **水を飲む**. To ask, write **水を飲んでもいいですか。** To grant permission, remove the question ending **か**: **水を飲んでもいいです。** To forbid it in a fictional setting, write **水を飲んではいけません。** These sentences have very different meanings.

**Step 3 — Repair a draft.** A learner writes **ここで写真を撮ってもいいです。** but intends to ask permission. Add **か**: **ここで写真を撮ってもいいですか。** Another writes **ドアを閉めてもいいですか** to tell a classmate to close the door; change it to **ドアを閉めてください。**

**Step 4 — Plan your own notice.** Pick a fictional room, such as a reading room, art club or language classroom. Plan at least one request, one permission statement or question, and one prohibition. Write two to five connected sentences, with a short opening such as **ここは勉強（べんきょう）の部屋です。** if helpful.

## Independent writing task

Write **2–5 Japanese sentences** for the shared classroom. Include (1) at least one polite request with **〜てください**, (2) at least one permission expression with **〜てもいいです** or **〜てもいいですか**, and (3) at least one prohibition with **〜てはいけません**. You may use the model vocabulary or invent a different sensible fictional policy. Do not enter real names, addresses or contact details.

**Another valid model:**

> ここは読書（どくしょ）の部屋です。  
> 本（ほん）を静（しず）かに読（よ）んでください。  
> 水を飲んでもいいです。  
> ここで電話（でんわ）を使（つか）ってはいけません。

**English:** This is a reading room. Please read books quietly. Drinking water is allowed. You must not use a phone here.

These are *examples*, not the only acceptable answers. Natural alternative wording and word order are possible. Your composition is not automatically graded.

## Self-review: five observable checks

1. **Purpose:** Does each sentence clearly ask, permit or prohibit what you intended?
2. **Te-form:** Are forms such as 洗って, 飲んで, 閉めて and 使って correct?
3. **Particles:** Are を for objects and で for action locations appropriate?
4. **Politeness and clarity:** Is the tone suitable for a classroom notice? Does a permission *question* end in か?
5. **Connected output:** Do you have two to five related sentences about one room, with readable punctuation?
