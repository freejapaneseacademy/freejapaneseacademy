## A Saturday plan

Your classmate wants to eat together after class. You need to understand **when**, **where**, and whether the message is an invitation or a decision. In this vocabulary lesson, we learn the words first; Lesson 03 explores the grammar of <span lang="ja">ませんか・ましょう・〜にします</span> in depth.

### 1. Words for arranging a meeting

| Japanese | Reading | English | In a natural phrase |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">予定</span> | よてい | plan; schedule | <span lang="ja">土曜日の予定</span> — Saturday's plans |
| <span lang="ja">約束</span> | やくそく | promise; appointment | <span lang="ja">友だちとの約束</span> — an appointment with a friend |
| <span lang="ja">明日</span> | あした | tomorrow | <span lang="ja">明日、会います。</span> — We'll meet tomorrow. |
| <span lang="ja">今日</span> | きょう | today | <span lang="ja">今日は忙しいです。</span> — I'm busy today. |
| <span lang="ja">土曜日</span> | どようび | Saturday | <span lang="ja">土曜日に行きます。</span> — I'll go on Saturday. |
| <span lang="ja">何時</span> | なんじ | what time | <span lang="ja">何時ですか。</span> — What time is it? |
| <span lang="ja">いっしょに</span> | いっしょに | together | <span lang="ja">いっしょに行きます。</span> — We go together. |
| <span lang="ja">昼ご飯</span> | ひるごはん | lunch | <span lang="ja">昼ご飯を食べます。</span> — I eat lunch. |
| <span lang="ja">喫茶店</span> | きっさてん | café / coffee shop | <span lang="ja">喫茶店で会います。</span> — We meet at a café. |
| <span lang="ja">食堂</span> | しょくどう | cafeteria; dining hall | <span lang="ja">食堂で食べます。</span> — We eat at the cafeteria. |
| <span lang="ja">映画館</span> | えいがかん | cinema | <span lang="ja">映画館に行きます。</span> — We go to the cinema. |
| <span lang="ja">駅</span> | えき | station | <span lang="ja">駅で会います。</span> — We meet at the station. |

**Meaning matters:** <span lang="ja">予定</span> is something on a schedule; <span lang="ja">約束</span> is an arrangement or promise with someone. A <span lang="ja">喫茶店</span> is a café, while a <span lang="ja">食堂</span> is usually a cafeteria or dining hall. A café can also be called <span lang="ja">カフェ</span> in everyday Japanese.

### 2. Invitations and responses

These are useful **chunks** to recognize before you study the full verb pattern:

- <span lang="ja">いっしょに昼ご飯を食べませんか。</span> — Would you like to have lunch together? A polite invitation, not a statement that someone will not eat.
- <span lang="ja">いいですね。</span> — That sounds good. A positive response.
- <span lang="ja">はい、ぜひ。</span> — Yes, I'd love to. An enthusiastic acceptance.
- <span lang="ja">すみません、今日はちょっと。</span> — Sorry, today is a bit difficult. A softened refusal; the rest is left unsaid.
- <span lang="ja">じゃあ、土曜日にしましょう。</span> — Well then, let's make it Saturday. A joint decision.

The last expression uses <span lang="ja">〜にしましょう</span> for agreeing on a choice. It is not the same as <span lang="ja">〜にします</span>, which can state **my** decision (“I'll choose ...”). Lesson 03 develops both forms.

### 3. Read an original planning message

**Message from Haru to Mina:**

> <span lang="ja">みなさん、こんにちは。明日、いっしょに昼ご飯を食べませんか。駅の近くの喫茶店はどうですか。十二時に会いましょう。</span>

“Hi, Mina. Would you like to have lunch together tomorrow? How about the café near the station? Let's meet at twelve.”

**Mina replies:**

> <span lang="ja">いいですね。でも、明日は予定があります。土曜日はどうですか。</span>

“That sounds good. But I have plans tomorrow. How about Saturday?”

**Haru replies:**

> <span lang="ja">わかりました。じゃあ、土曜日の十二時にしましょう。</span>

“Got it. Then let's make it twelve on Saturday.”

**Find the evidence:** <span lang="ja">明日</span> is the **original** proposal; <span lang="ja">明日は予定があります</span> explains why it changes; <span lang="ja">土曜日の十二時</span> gives the **final** plan. The café is proposed, but the exchange does not explicitly reconfirm its location after the date changes. Do not invent a final meeting place.

### 4. Watch for three common mistakes

**Invitation versus negative statement:** <span lang="ja">食べませんか</span> literally has negative-question morphology, but in this context it invites: “Would you like to eat?” It is not equivalent to <span lang="ja">食べません</span> (“I do not eat / will not eat”).

**Time versus place:** <span lang="ja">何時</span> (なんじ) asks **what time**; <span lang="ja">どこ</span> asks **where**. The phrase <span lang="ja">駅で会います</span> uses <span lang="ja">で</span> to mark the place where meeting happens, whereas <span lang="ja">映画館に行きます</span> uses <span lang="ja">に</span> for a destination.

**A polite decline:** <span lang="ja">今日はちょっと</span> may signal reluctance or refusal in an invitation exchange. It is context-dependent; it does not literally specify the reason.

### 5. Practice: controlled, guided and independent

**Controlled retrieval:** Cover the English column. Say the meanings of <span lang="ja">予定・約束・何時・喫茶店・食堂</span>. Then uncover and correct your answers. Distinguish <span lang="ja">明日</span> from <span lang="ja">今日</span>.

**Guided substitution:** Change just the destination in <span lang="ja">いっしょに喫茶店に行きませんか。</span> (“Would you like to go to a café together?”). Try <span lang="ja">映画館</span> and <span lang="ja">食堂</span>. Then answer <span lang="ja">いいですね。</span> or <span lang="ja">すみません、今日はちょっと。</span>

**Independent writing:** Draft a three-line invitation to a classmate. Include a day, a place and a proposed time. Example: <span lang="ja">土曜日、いっしょに映画館に行きませんか。三時に駅で会いましょう。どうですか。</span> (“Would you like to go to the cinema together on Saturday? Let's meet at the station at three. How does that sound?”) Check that the date, location and action are consistent. This composition is self-reviewed, not machine-graded.

**Optional speaking:** Read the three messages aloud, alternating speakers, then substitute your own location and day. No microphone or pronunciation scoring is required here.
