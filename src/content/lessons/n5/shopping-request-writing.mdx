## Your writing mission

You are visiting a **fictional stationery shop**. Write **2–5 short, connected Japanese sentences** that ask the price of an item and politely request the color or size you want. Your work is a customer-and-clerk mini-dialogue, not a real purchase or a message sent to anyone. You will self-review it; no automated system will claim to grade your free writing.

**Can-do:** Ask prices, amounts and sizes politely. You will move from noticing a model, to building short sentences, to writing and revising your own exchange.

## 1. Read the shop's original signs

| Item | Japanese sign | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Red pen | <span lang="ja">赤いペン　200円</span> | Red pen, ¥200 |
| Blue pen | <span lang="ja">青いペン　250円</span> | Blue pen, ¥250 |
| Small white notebook | <span lang="ja">小さい白いノート　300円</span> | Small white notebook, ¥300 |
| Large white notebook | <span lang="ja">大きい白いノート　500円</span> | Large white notebook, ¥500 |

All prices are invented for practice. <span lang="ja">赤い（あかい）</span> = red; <span lang="ja">青い（あおい）</span> = blue; <span lang="ja">小さい（ちいさい）</span> = small; <span lang="ja">大きい（おおきい）</span> = large; <span lang="ja">円（えん）</span> = yen. The price belongs to the specific item on its line.

## 2. Three useful sentence frames

**Ask:** <span lang="ja">この青いペンはいくらですか。</span> (この あおい ペンは いくらですか。) — How much is this blue pen? The topic particle **は** is written は but pronounced わ.

**Answer:** <span lang="ja">250円です。</span> (にひゃくごじゅうえん です。) — It is 250 yen. The clerk can omit the repeated item.

**Request:** <span lang="ja">では、その青いペンをお願いします。</span> (では、その あおい ペンを おねがいします。) — In that case, the blue pen, please. The particle **を** marks what you are requesting. A simpler alternative is <span lang="ja">その青いペンをください。</span> — Please give me that blue pen.

**Notice the distance:** **この** means “this [noun]” near the speaker, while **その** means “that [noun]” near the listener or already mentioned. In a shop, either can work depending on what you point to; do not automatically swap them in every context.

## 3. Observe a complete model

> 客：<span lang="ja">この小さい白いノートはいくらですか。</span> — How much is this small white notebook?
>
> 店員：<span lang="ja">300円です。</span> — It is ¥300.
>
> 客：<span lang="ja">では、そのノートを一冊お願いします。</span> — Then one copy of that notebook, please.

**Reading support:** <span lang="ja">一冊（いっさつ）</span> is a counter for bound books and notebooks. You may omit the counter at this stage: <span lang="ja">そのノートをお願いします。</span> is also polite and natural. The model has three short turns, a clear price question, an answer supported by the sign, and a request.

## 4. Build before you write

Choose one item from the signs. Fill the slots orally or on paper before typing:

1. <span lang="ja">この＿＿はいくらですか。</span> — How much is this ___?
2. <span lang="ja">＿＿円です。</span> — It is ___ yen.
3. <span lang="ja">では、その＿＿をお願いします。</span> — Then ___, please.

For a blue pen, use <span lang="ja">青いペン / 250 / 青いペン</span>. For a large white notebook, use <span lang="ja">大きい白いノート / 500 / 大きい白いノート</span>. Japanese adjectives go **before** the noun: <span lang="ja">青いペン</span>, not <span lang="ja">ペン青い</span>.

**Common repairs:** <span lang="ja">このペンはいくらですか。</span> is a question, so keep **か**. <span lang="ja">青いペンをお願いします。</span> needs **を**, not **が**, for this request pattern. <span lang="ja">小さいノート</span> uses **小さい**, not *小さな* as a required substitution; both are possible in other contexts, but the lesson's safe frame is 小さい + noun. Do not write a price that contradicts your chosen sign.

## 5. Independent writing and self-review

In the practice area, write a **2–5 sentence mini-dialogue** for a fictional customer and clerk. Include an item with a color or size, a price question, a matching price answer, and a polite request. A three-turn exchange is ideal; two sentences may combine the price answer and request if the roles remain clear.

Compare your draft with the model only **after** you have tried it. The rubric checks: **(1)** understandable item and color/size, **(2)** correct いくらですか question, **(3)** price consistent with the sign, **(4)** polite request with を, and **(5)** readable Japanese punctuation and connected turns. These are self-review prompts, **not** a teacher grade. Revise at least one detail.

**Optional speaking:** Read your revised dialogue aloud, alternating customer and clerk. No microphone or pronunciation score is required. Listening comprehension is assessed separately in L06, using actual recordings when verified.

## 6. Review

You can now ask <span lang="ja">いくらですか</span>, respond with a yen amount, and request an item using <span lang="ja">〜をお願いします</span> or <span lang="ja">〜をください</span>. Practise writing one different item and check the sign again. The interactive mini-check below focuses on **controlled sentence choices**; your independent draft remains your own ungraded writing.
