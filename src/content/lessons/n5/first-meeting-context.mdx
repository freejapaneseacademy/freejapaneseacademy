## First meetings in Japanese

**Can-do:** Understand *who is speaking, where they are and why* when two people meet for the first time. You do not need to disclose your real identity to practise.

**はじめまして** (*hajimemashite*) is a conventional first-meeting greeting, often translated “Nice to meet you.” It is not used as a generic greeting every time you see someone. **よろしくおねがいします** (*yoroshiku onegai shimasu*) expresses goodwill and an expectation of a positive relationship; no single English phrase matches every use.

### Scene A — A class welcome desk

**受付の人（うけつけのひと）:** はじめまして。おなまえは？

*Reception helper:* Nice to meet you. Your name?

**ミナ:** はじめまして。ミナです。

*Mina:* Nice to meet you. I'm Mina.

**受付の人:** ミナさんですね。よろしくおねがいします。

*Helper:* Mina, right? I look forward to working with you.

**ミナ:** よろしくおねがいします。

*Mina:* Likewise.

**Notice:** The helper asks for a name, not a nationality or age. **さん** is a polite suffix attached to the name, not part of the name itself. The setting is a welcome desk, so the helper is registering a new participant.

### Scene B — Two classmates

**ユイ:** はじめまして。ユイです。がくせいです。

*Yui:* Nice to meet you. I'm Yui. I'm a student.

**レオ:** はじめまして。レオです。わたしも がくせいです。

*Leo:* Nice to meet you. I'm Leo. I'm a student too.

**ユイ:** よろしくおねがいします。

*Yui:* I look forward to studying together.

Here, both speakers are classmates. **も** means “also” in this context. It does not tell us whether they attend the same year group or share a nationality.

### Meaning clues to notice

| Japanese | Reading | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| はじめまして | hajimemashite | Opens a first meeting |
| おなまえは？ | onamae wa? | Asks for a name in a supported polite context |
| ミナです。 | Mina desu. | States a name politely |
| がくせいです。 | gakusei desu. | States that someone is a student |
| がくせいですか。 | gakusei desu ka. | Asks whether someone is a student |
| せんせい | sensei | Teacher, a role word |
| くに | kuni | Country |
| よろしくおねがいします | yoroshiku onegai shimasu | Conventional goodwill |

**は** as a topic particle is pronounced *wa*, although it is written *ha*. **です** gives a polite nominal statement; sentence-final **か** marks a question. This lesson introduces the function in context; the grammar lesson will explain the patterns in detail.

### A fictional profile card

**なまえ：アキ / くに：カナダ / やくわり：がくせい**

**アキ:** はじめまして。アキです。カナダから きました。

*Aki:* Nice to meet you. I'm Aki. I came from Canada.

**ハル:** はじめまして。ハルです。よろしくおねがいします。

*Haru:* Nice to meet you. I'm Haru. I look forward to getting along.

We know Aki's given name in this scene and that Aki says they came from Canada. We cannot conclude citizenship, gender, native language, permanent residence or age. A question like **がくせいですか** does not establish that someone is a student until an answer is given.

### Controlled, guided and independent practice

**Controlled:** Answer the eight scene questions below. Identify the setting, roles and purpose before selecting an answer. Each question has one explicitly supported interpretation.

**Guided:** Return to Scene A. Locate the name question and the response. Then compare Scene B: both people volunteer their names without being asked.

**Independent:** Invent two fictional classmates and write a three-line meeting using **はじめまして**, a fictional name followed by **です**, and **よろしくおねがいします**. Optionally rehearse it aloud without recording. Check whether your characters have a reason to meet and whether you have assumed any facts not actually spoken.

### Common misunderstanding and self-check

**おなまえは？** is a shortened prompt appropriate in this supportive context, not a universal full formal question. A speaker who says **カナダから きました** states where they came from, not necessarily citizenship.

After practising, ask yourself: Can I identify *where* the speakers meet, *who* they are in the scene, and *what* they exchange? A practice score is not an official JLPT score or a verified pronunciation evaluation.
