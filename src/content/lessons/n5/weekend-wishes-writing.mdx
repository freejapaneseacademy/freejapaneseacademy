## Write a wish list that someone can understand

**Can-do:** Write two to five connected sentences in Japanese explaining what you want, what you want to do, and where you intend to go. Imagine you are filling in a fictional community club's weekend-planning card. This is original instructional material, not a real event or a report of anyone's private wishes.

### Model A: a bookstore afternoon

> 土曜日（どようび）に本屋（ほんや）に行（い）きます。新（あたら）しい本（ほん）がほしいです。本屋（ほんや）で日本語（にほんご）の本（ほん）を見（み）たいです。  
> *On Saturday, I am going to a bookstore. I want a new book. I want to look at Japanese-language books at the bookstore.*

Notice the difference: **本がほしいです** names a thing wanted, **本を見たいです** names an action wanted, and **本屋に行きます** states a plan to go to a destination. **で** in 本屋で marks the location of looking.

### Model B: a day outdoors

> 日曜日（にちようび）に山（やま）に行（い）きたいです。山（やま）で写真（しゃしん）を撮（と）りたいです。でも、新（あたら）しいカメラはほしくないです。  
> *On Sunday, I want to go to the mountains. I want to take photos there. But I do not want a new camera.*

**行きたいです** expresses a desire, not a confirmed arrangement. **ほしくないです** is the negative of ほしいです, not a prohibition. Here the topic カメラ takes は in the contrastive negative statement.

## Build sentences from meaningful parts

| Communicative purpose | Pattern | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Want an item | noun + がほしいです | かばんがほしいです。— I want a bag. |
| Do not want an item | noun + はほしくないです | 新しいかばんはほしくないです。— I do not want a new bag. |
| Want an action | verb ます-stem + たいです | 本を読みたいです。— I want to read a book. |
| Do not want an action | stem + たくないです | 今は買いたくないです。— I do not want to buy it now. |
| State a destination plan | place + に行きます | 店に行きます。— I am going to a shop. |
| State an activity location | place + で + action | 店で本を見たいです。— I want to look at books in a shop. |

**Verb-building workshop:** 読む（よむ）→ 読みます → 読みたいです; 買う（かう）→ 買います → 買いたいです; 見る（みる）→ 見ます → 見たいです; 行く（いく）→ 行きます → 行きたいです. Remove **ます** from the polite form before adding **たい**. The beginner object-desire pattern is **本がほしいです**, not 本をほしいです. For an action object, **本を読みたいです** is natural; が can also occur with some たい objects, but を is a useful starting pattern.

### Controlled practice: repair the sentence

1. × 本をほしいです。 → **本がほしいです。** (A desired thing takes が.)
2. × 山で行きます。 → **山に行きます。** (に marks the destination.)
3. × 本を読むたいです。 → **本を読みたいです。** (Use the ます-stem.)
4. × 店に本を見たいです。 → **店で本を見たいです。** (で marks where the looking happens.)
5. × 買いたいないです。 → **買いたくないです。** (Negative たい becomes たくない.)

### Guided composition

Fill the slots with your own choices, then connect them with a time word or **それから** (*after that*).

> ［土曜日／日曜日］に［場所］に行きます。［もの］がほしいです。それから、［場所］で［もの］を［動詞のます語幹］たいです。

Example: **日曜日に店に行きます。新しいかばんがほしいです。それから、店でかばんを見たいです。**  
*On Sunday I am going to a shop. I want a new bag. After that I want to look at bags in the shop.*

For a *wish* rather than a decided plan, change **行きます** to **行きたいです**. The word それから signals sequence; it does not prove that an action will happen.

## Independent writing task

Write **two to five connected Japanese sentences** as a fictional club participant. Include (1) an item you want or do not want, (2) an action you want to do, and (3) a place you want to go or plan to go. Give a time or a connecting expression if you can. You may use kana instead of unfamiliar kanji. Use the writing area below; your draft is not sent to a server or automatically graded.

**Five-point self-review rubric (ungraded):** Does your text (1) express an item with がほしい/はほしくない, (2) form たい from a verb stem, (3) use に for a destination, (4) use で for an activity location if needed, and (5) form a connected, understandable two-to-five-sentence message? A model is a comparison aid, not the only valid answer.

**Reader check:** Circle the desired *thing*, underline the desired *action*, and mark the *destination*. If a sentence could be mistaken for a confirmed plan when you only mean a wish, replace 行きます with 行きたいです. Avoid stating another person's private desires as certain without evidence.
