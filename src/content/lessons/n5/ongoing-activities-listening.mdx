## A phone call, then a classroom check-in

When someone asks **今、何をしていますか** (*ima, nani o shite imasu ka*), they want to know **what you are doing now**. Listen for the person, activity and location. The answer often ends in **〜ています**, as in **本を読んでいます** (*hon o yonde imasu*, “is reading a book”). You do not need to identify any printed characters by ear.

These are two **original fictional dialogues**, prepared for synthesis with the licensed AivisSpeech voices 阿井田 茂 (Calm) and morioki (Normal). **Recordings must be generated and independently checked before this listening assessment can be published.** No human pronunciation review is claimed.

### Before you listen

Recall the question-and-answer pattern from Lesson 03. **何をしていますか** asks about an action. The particle **で** often marks where an action takes place, while **を** marks an object and **に** may mark the recipient of an action such as watering flowers (**花に水をやる**). Do not confuse **公園にいます** (“is at the park”) with **公園で読んでいます** (“is reading at the park”).

Try to listen for these meanings, rather than memorizing the written forms. The Japanese examples above are preparation, **not** the transcript or the answers to the two recordings.

### Track A: A call from the community garden

Two people speak on the telephone. One is already outdoors; the other is on the way. Find out **where the people are and what they are doing**. Play once for the general situation and again for details.

### Track B: An activity-room update

Two people check what others are doing inside a community center. Listen for the location and two different activities. The names are clues to who is doing each action.

## Listening procedure

1. Play **both** recordings at normal speed without opening the transcripts.
2. Listen again and answer the three questions for each track from **spoken evidence**.
3. Submit to see explanations, then reveal the Japanese transcript and English translation.
4. Replay a missed sentence, notice its **〜ています** ending, and optionally repeat it aloud.

Transcripts are always available as an accessibility alternative. Revealing one before submitting changes the attempt to **assisted practice** and does not save a blind listening score. If sound cannot load, use the transcript for learning, but do not claim an unaided listening result.

## Common listening traps

**読んでいます** (*yonde imasu*, “is reading”) and **聞いています** (*kiite imasu*, “is listening”) are different actions. **絵を描いています** (*e o kaite imasu*) means “is drawing a picture,” not “is writing a letter.” **今** (*ima*) means “now,” so the conversations describe current activity, not a habitual schedule. A listener can recognize these words without writing them down.

## Independent follow-up

After checking your answers, write two brief Japanese sentences describing an imaginary scene: one person doing something in a location, and another person doing a different activity. For example, **母は台所で料理をしています。弟は部屋で本を読んでいます。** (“My mother is cooking in the kitchen. My younger brother is reading in his room.”) Compare your work: did you choose **で** for the activity location, **を** for the object, and a suitable **〜ています** form? This writing reflection is **not automatically graded**.
