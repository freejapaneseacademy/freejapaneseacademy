## Your can-do: ask and answer simple questions

Imagine joining a new Japanese-language class. You need to find the teacher, locate the classroom, identify an unfamiliar object and choose one notebook from a group. Japanese uses different question words for these different information gaps. Choose the word **from the kind of answer you need**, not merely from the English sentence order.

### Four useful question words

| Word | Reading | Asks for | Model |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">だれ</span> | dare | a person | <span lang="ja">せんせいは だれですか。</span> — Who is the teacher? |
| <span lang="ja">何</span> | なん in 何ですか | an identity or kind of thing | <span lang="ja">これは 何ですか。</span> — What is this? |
| <span lang="ja">どこ</span> | doko | a place or location | <span lang="ja">きょうしつは どこですか。</span> — Where is the classroom? |
| <span lang="ja">どれ</span> | dore | one item from alternatives | <span lang="ja">どれですか。</span> — Which one? |

**Reading note:** 何 has more than one reading. It is normally **なん** in <span lang="ja">何ですか</span> but **なに** in expressions such as <span lang="ja">何を</span>. This lesson practices **何ですか** only. In Japanese writing, a question may end with **。** or **？**; the polite question particle **か** is the key grammatical signal here.

### Form the question without moving the verb

Start with <span lang="ja">たなかさんは せんせいです。</span> — Tanaka is a teacher.

Replace the unknown identity with **だれ**: <span lang="ja">せんせいは だれですか。</span> — Who is the teacher?

Replace the unknown location with **どこ**: <span lang="ja">きょうしつは どこですか。</span> — Where is the classroom?

The topic particle **は** is written *ha* but pronounced *wa*. Japanese keeps the predicate at the end, so do not copy English question inversion. **だれ** is used for people; **どこ** asks where, not who.

### Ask which one, not which + noun

Three notebooks are on a table. You can ask <span lang="ja">どれですか。</span> — Which one? **どれ** is a stand-alone pronoun, so <span lang="ja">どれ ノート</span> is not the pattern. The separate determiner **どの** can modify a noun (<span lang="ja">どのノートですか。</span>), but today focus on **どれ**.

### Add someone with も

<span lang="ja">たなかさんは せんせいです。</span> — Tanaka is a teacher.

<span lang="ja">さとうさんも せんせいです。</span> — Sato is a teacher **too**.

Here **も** takes the place of **は** after the added person. Do not stack them as <span lang="ja">さとうさんはも</span>. **も** does not mean that every person is a teacher; it only adds the named person to the same description.

### Original classroom dialogue

| Speaker | Japanese | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New student | <span lang="ja">すみません。せんせいは だれですか。</span> | Excuse me. Who is the teacher? |
| Receptionist | <span lang="ja">たなかさんです。さとうさんも せんせいです。</span> | Tanaka is. Sato is a teacher too. |
| New student | <span lang="ja">きょうしつは どこですか。</span> | Where is the classroom? |
| Receptionist | <span lang="ja">あそこです。</span> | Over there. |
| New student | <span lang="ja">これは 何ですか。</span> | What is this? |
| Receptionist | <span lang="ja">ノートです。</span> | It's a notebook. |

**Evidence-reading:** The phrase <span lang="ja">さとうさんも せんせいです</span> tells you Sato shares Tanaka's role. The reply <span lang="ja">あそこです</span> answers a location question; it does not identify a person.

### Guided practice and independent transfer

First, label the missing information in each prompt as *person*, *thing*, *place*, or *selection*. Next, cover the question-word table and produce four questions aloud or in writing. Finally, imagine two classmates are students: write <span lang="ja">アキさんは がくせいです。ユイさんも がくせいです。</span> and explain the role of **も**.

For an optional two-line writing task, ask where your classroom is and who the teacher is. Compare with <span lang="ja">きょうしつは どこですか。せんせいは だれですか。</span>. Check that each question ends in **ですか**, and that the question word matches the information sought. Open writing is self-checked, not automatically graded.
