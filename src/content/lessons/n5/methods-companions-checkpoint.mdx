## Mission: check the weekend travel plan

Three classmates are preparing short weekend outings. Your task is to explain **how** each person travels and **with whom**, using the exact words in their plans. This original checkpoint is a written review, not an official JLPT test.

### 1. Three expressions with different jobs

| Expression | Reading | Function | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">電車で</span> | でんしゃで | by train; **で** marks means | <span lang="ja">電車で行きます。</span> I go by train. |
| <span lang="ja">友だちと</span> | ともだちと | with a friend; **と** marks a companion | <span lang="ja">友だちと行きます。</span> I go with a friend. |
| <span lang="ja">一人で</span> | ひとりで | alone, by oneself | <span lang="ja">一人で行きます。</span> I go alone. |

The particle **で** has more than one use. In <span lang="ja">バスで行きます</span>, it marks transportation; in <span lang="ja">一人で行きます</span>, the entire phrase means “alone.” Do not treat **一人** as a vehicle. <span lang="ja">友だちで行きます</span> does **not** mean “go with a friend”; use <span lang="ja">友だちと行きます</span>. To add a destination, use <span lang="ja">図書館（としょかん）に行きます</span> (“go to the library”).

### 2. Useful words in actual sentences

| Word | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">電車</span> | でんしゃ | train |
| <span lang="ja">自転車</span> | じてんしゃ | bicycle |
| <span lang="ja">友だち</span> | ともだち | friend |
| <span lang="ja">兄</span> | あに | my older brother |
| <span lang="ja">母</span> | はは | my mother |
| <span lang="ja">一人</span> | ひとり | one person; alone in 一人で |
| <span lang="ja">博物館</span> | はくぶつかん | museum |
| <span lang="ja">図書館</span> | としょかん | library |

**Reading tip:** The small <span lang="ja">ゃ</span> in <span lang="ja">電車（でんしゃ）</span> forms the sound *sha*. <span lang="ja">自転車（じてんしゃ）</span> ends in the same <span lang="ja">しゃ</span>. Kanji readings here are taught through complete words, not as isolated one-to-one character rules.

### 3. Original itinerary — read before checking English

This is a fictional class notice. All times and travel arrangements are invented for practice.

| Student | Japanese itinerary |
| --- | --- |
| あき | <span lang="ja">土曜日（どようび）に兄（あに）とバスで博物館（はくぶつかん）に行きます。午前（ごぜん）十時（じゅうじ）に会（あ）います。</span> |
| りな | <span lang="ja">日曜日（にちようび）に一人（ひとり）で自転車（じてんしゃ）で図書館（としょかん）に行きます。午後（ごご）二時（にじ）に帰（かえ）ります。</span> |
| けん | <span lang="ja">土曜日に母（はは）と電車（でんしゃ）で公園（こうえん）に行きます。午後三時（さんじ）に帰ります。</span> |

**Meaning:** Aki goes to the museum by bus with her older brother on Saturday; they meet at 10 a.m. Rina goes alone to the library by bicycle on Sunday and returns at 2 p.m. Ken goes to the park by train with his mother on Saturday and returns at 3 p.m.

Find **both** the companion and the vehicle before answering a question about an outing. For Aki, <span lang="ja">兄と</span> identifies the companion and <span lang="ja">バスで</span> identifies the vehicle. For Rina, <span lang="ja">一人で</span> says she goes alone; <span lang="ja">自転車で</span> separately tells how.

### 5. Independent writing and optional speaking

Write **two to five connected Japanese sentences** about a weekend trip. Include a day, destination, means of transport, and either a companion or <span lang="ja">一人で</span>. Add a return time if you can.

Model: <span lang="ja">日曜日に友だちと電車で博物館に行きます。午前十時に会います。午後四時に家に帰ります。</span>

Meaning: “On Sunday I go to the museum by train with a friend. We meet at 10 a.m. I return home at 4 p.m.”

Review your draft against five criteria: (1) a clear day and destination, (2) a transport phrase ending in で, (3) a companion with と **or** 一人で, (4) correct polite verb endings, and (5) two to five connected sentences. Writing is **self-reviewed**, not automatically graded.

Optional speaking: rehearse your sentences aloud and replace <span lang="ja">友だちと</span> with <span lang="ja">一人で</span>, adjusting the meaning. No microphone is started and no pronunciation grade is assigned.
