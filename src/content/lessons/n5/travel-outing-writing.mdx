## A plan your friend can follow

**Can-do:** Write a clear plan for a simple outing, using two to five connected Japanese sentences. Imagine that two classmates are visiting a **fictional** science museum on Sunday. Your message must tell the reader when and where to meet, how to travel, where to go and when to arrive. No real personal or travel details are needed.

> 日曜日（にちようび）の午前（ごぜん）九時（くじ）に駅（えき）の前（まえ）で集合（しゅうごう）します。  
> We meet in front of the station at 9 a.m. on Sunday.
>
> 九時半（くじはん）にバスで科学館（かがくかん）に行（い）きます。  
> At 9:30, we go to the science museum by bus.
>
> 午前十時（じゅうじ）に科学館に着（つ）きます。  
> We arrive at the science museum at 10 a.m.

**科学館** (かがくかん) means science museum. **着きます** (つきます) means arrive; **到着します** (とうちゃくします) is a more formal alternative. These are original instructional examples, not an actual itinerary.

## 1. Give each particle a job

| Detail | Pattern | Example | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Specific time | Time **に** | 九時に | At nine |
| Meeting location | Place **で** + 集合します | 駅の前で集合します | The action of meeting happens there |
| Transport | Vehicle **で** | バスで | By bus |
| Destination | Place **に／へ** + 行きます | 科学館に行きます | Toward the museum |
| Arrival point | Place **に** + 着きます | 科学館に着きます | Arrive at the museum |

The same particle **で** has different jobs in **駅の前で集合します** (place where the meeting happens) and **バスで行きます** (means of travel). Read the noun and verb together to identify its job. A specific clock time commonly takes **に**, but relative time words such as **明日** (あした, tomorrow) usually do not require it: **明日、行きます。**

**Contrast:** **駅で集合します** means “meet at the station”; **駅に行きます** means “go to the station.” Do not use **駅に集合します** as the default pattern in this beginner exercise; meeting at a place is naturally expressed with **で**.

## 2. Make the timeline coherent

Our fictional trip has three events:

| Event | Japanese | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meet | 駅の前で集合します | 午前九時 (9:00 a.m.) |
| Depart | バスで出発（しゅっぱつ）します | 午前九時半 (9:30 a.m.) |
| Arrive | 科学館に着きます | 午前十時 (10:00 a.m.) |

**出発します** is “depart,” not “arrive.” **午前** is before noon and **午後** is after noon. A meeting at 9:00 and a departure at 9:30 leave thirty minutes between them; avoid writing an arrival time before departure.

You may also add a fare: **バスの片道（かたみち）の運賃（うんちん）は二百円（にひゃくえん）です。** — “The one-way bus fare is ¥200.” This is a fictional price, not a current fare. **片道** means one way; **往復** (おうふく) means round trip.

## 3. Observe, change, then compose

**Controlled substitution.** Start with **九時半にバスで科学館に行きます。** Change the transport to **電車（でんしゃ）** (train). The resulting sentence is **九時半に電車で科学館に行きます。** Then change the destination to **公園（こうえん）** (park): **九時半に電車で公園に行きます。**

**Guided reconstruction.** Given the facts “meet at the library at 10:00 a.m.; go to the park by bicycle at 10:30 a.m.,” write two sentences before revealing this model:

> 午前十時に図書館（としょかん）で集合します。  
> 午前十時半に自転車（じてんしゃ）で公園に行きます。

You can omit repeated subjects when the context makes them clear. These sentences are polite **ます** forms appropriate for a straightforward written plan. **で** with 図書館 marks the meeting location, while **で** with 自転車 marks transport.

## 4. Independent writing task

Write **two to five connected Japanese sentences** for a fictional Saturday outing. Include (a) a meeting time and location, (b) a destination and transport method, and (c) an arrival time. Optionally include a one-way fare. Keep the times in logical order. Use a Japanese keyboard or IME; kana support is acceptable where kanji is not yet familiar.

**Model answer (one of many):**

> 土曜日（どようび）の午前八時に駅の前で集合します。  
> 午前八時半に電車で動物園（どうぶつえん）に行きます。  
> 午前九時に動物園に着きます。  
> 片道の運賃は三百円（さんびゃくえん）です。

English: “We meet in front of the station at 8 a.m. on Saturday. At 8:30 we go to the zoo by train. We arrive at the zoo at 9 a.m. The one-way fare is ¥300.” All details are invented.

### Five-point self-review (not a grade)

1. The message contains two to five connected Japanese sentences.
2. A specific meeting time and meeting place are clear; the meeting action uses **で**.
3. The destination uses **に／へ** with 行きます and transport uses **で**.
4. Meeting, departure and arrival times are chronological, with 午前／午後 when needed.
5. Spelling, readable kana/kanji and polite verb endings are consistent.

The writing workshop saves your draft **only on this device** if browser storage is available. Its self-checks are not teacher grading. The scored questions check written recognition only; they cannot judge every valid free-composition alternative.

## 5. Read, review and optionally speak

Before checking the model, reread your draft as if you were the friend receiving it. Could you identify where to meet and when to leave? For optional speaking, say the two or three key sentences aloud and practise changing the destination. This lesson neither records speech nor evaluates pronunciation; authentic listening practice is separate in L06.
