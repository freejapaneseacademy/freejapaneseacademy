## A snack after shopping

**Chapter can-do:** Describe food, places and objects with adjectives. In this first lesson you will **understand a short conversation**, not memorize every adjective form. Two classmates, **Yui** and **Kenta**, stop at a fictional café after shopping. They want to choose a snack and describe their experience to a friend.

This is an original **written** scene, not recorded speech. Read each line and its English meaning before taking the written comprehension check.

| Speaker | Japanese | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yui | <span lang="ja">このカフェは しずかですね。</span> | This café is quiet, isn't it? |
| Kenta | <span lang="ja">はい。きれいな カフェですね。</span> | Yes. It's a lovely/clean café, isn't it? |
| Yui | <span lang="ja">このケーキは おいしいですか。</span> | Is this cake tasty? |
| Kenta | <span lang="ja">はい。とても おいしいです。</span> | Yes. It's very tasty. |
| Yui | <span lang="ja">このコーヒーは あついです。</span> | This coffee is hot. |
| Kenta | <span lang="ja">そうですね。でも、このおちゃは あつくないです。</span> | That's right. But this tea isn't hot. |

The written scene provides context. **しずか** means quiet, **きれい** means clean, beautiful or neat depending on context, **おいしい** means tasty, and **あつい** describes temperature. The meaning of **きれい** here is a favorable impression of the café, not a guarantee of hygiene.

## 1. Who is speaking, and why?

Yui and Kenta are **customers**, not café employees. Yui first notices the atmosphere, then asks about a cake. Kenta gives a positive evaluation. Later they contrast the temperatures of two drinks. These are descriptions of **different things**; do not assume a comment about coffee also applies to tea.

**Find the evidence:** <span lang="ja">このコーヒーは あついです</span> explicitly says the coffee is hot. <span lang="ja">このおちゃは あつくないです</span> explicitly says the tea is *not* hot. Neither sentence tells you whether the drinks are sweet.

## 2. Two useful adjective families

Japanese adjectives do not all attach to nouns in the same way.

| Family | Example before a noun | Example as a sentence | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| い-adjective | <span lang="ja">おいしい ケーキ</span> | <span lang="ja">ケーキは おいしいです。</span> | tasty cake / The cake is tasty |
| な-adjective | <span lang="ja">しずかな カフェ</span> | <span lang="ja">カフェは しずかです。</span> | quiet café / The café is quiet |
| な-adjective | <span lang="ja">きれいな みせ</span> | <span lang="ja">みせは きれいです。</span> | nice/clean shop / The shop is nice/clean |

**Important trap:** <span lang="ja">きれい</span> ends in the sound *i* but is a **な-adjective**. Say <span lang="ja">きれいな カフェ</span>, not <span lang="ja">きれいい カフェ</span>. Conversely, <span lang="ja">おいしいな ケーキ</span> is not the standard noun-modifying form: use <span lang="ja">おいしい ケーキ</span>.

The polite sentence ending **です** is common for both types, but the way adjectives change in negative sentences differs. L03 will explain the patterns systematically.

## 3. Notice a negative description

Compare:

- <span lang="ja">このコーヒーは あついです。</span> — This coffee is hot.
- <span lang="ja">このおちゃは あつくないです。</span> — This tea is not hot.
- <span lang="ja">このへやは しずかです。</span> — This room is quiet.
- <span lang="ja">このへやは しずかじゃありません。</span> — This room is not quiet.

For many い-adjectives, replace the final **い** with **くない** for a plain negative adjective form, optionally followed by **です** in polite speech. For な-adjectives, a polite negative can use **じゃありません**. These are first-exposure examples; you are not expected to master all exceptions here.

**Common misunderstanding:** <span lang="ja">あつくないです</span> means *not hot*, not necessarily *cold*. A drink can be lukewarm.

## 4. Reading cues

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">静か</span> | しずか | quiet |
| <span lang="ja">綺麗</span> | きれい | clean, pretty (usually written きれい at this stage) |
| <span lang="ja">美味しい</span> | おいしい | tasty (usually written おいしい at this stage) |
| <span lang="ja">熱い</span> | あつい | hot to the touch, including hot drinks |
| <span lang="ja">とても</span> | とても | very |
| <span lang="ja">でも</span> | でも | but, however |

Read the kana first. The kanji above are **recognition support**, not a handwriting requirement. <span lang="ja">暑い（あつい）</span> is used for hot *weather*, whereas <span lang="ja">熱い（あつい）</span> is used for hot coffee.

## 5. Guided rehearsal

**A. Identify the topic.** In <span lang="ja">このカフェは しずかですね</span>, the café is the thing being described. In <span lang="ja">このケーキは おいしいですか</span>, Yui is asking about the cake, not the whole café.

**B. Match description to evidence.** Find the words <span lang="ja">とても おいしい</span> in Kenta's answer. They support a positive evaluation of the cake. Find <span lang="ja">あつくない</span> in his final line. They support the statement that the tea is not hot.

**C. Change the noun.** Try <span lang="ja">しずかな へや</span> (“a quiet room”) and <span lang="ja">おいしい パン</span> (“tasty bread”). Why is **な** used only in the first phrase?

## 6. Independent use and self-check

Imagine you visit a fictional bakery. Write **two or three short Japanese sentences** about a pastry, the shop or a drink. You may use the models <span lang="ja">このパンは おいしいです。</span> (“This bread is tasty”) and <span lang="ja">このみせは しずかです。</span> (“This shop is quiet”). Check that each adjective describes the intended noun, that **な** is present before a noun when required, and that a negative sentence does not imply more than it states.

Optional speaking: read the model exchange aloud slowly and notice the contrast between **あつい** and **あつくない**. This activity is ungraded and does not claim pronunciation verification.

### Remember

Adjectives help you say **what** is good, quiet or hot, not merely that you like something. The written quiz below checks the setting, roles and meaning of these descriptions. Later lessons add vocabulary, full grammar, reviews, listening with real audio, and writing.
