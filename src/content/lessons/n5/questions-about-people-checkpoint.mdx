## A classroom help-desk challenge

You are helping new students locate a teacher and identify their belongings. First interpret four short fictional cards; then, when the genuine chapter recordings are available, listen to two interviews. This checkpoint has **six language questions, four reading questions and four listening questions**. The ten written questions remain usable without audio; the listening questions are not scored unless both recordings are playable.

### Question-word toolkit

| Form | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">だれ</span> | who | <span lang="ja">あの人（ひと）は、だれですか。</span> — Who is that person? |
| <span lang="ja">何（なん）</span> | what | <span lang="ja">これは、何ですか。</span> — What is this? |
| <span lang="ja">どこ</span> | where | <span lang="ja">やまださんは、どこですか。</span> — Where is Yamada? |
| <span lang="ja">どれ</span> | which one (stands alone) | <span lang="ja">ノートは、どれですか。</span> — Which one is the notebook? |
| <span lang="ja">どの + noun</span> | which + noun | <span lang="ja">どのノートですか。</span> — Which notebook is it? |
| <span lang="ja">も</span> | also / too | <span lang="ja">たなかさんも、きょうしつです。</span> — Tanaka is in the classroom too. |

The topic marker <span lang="ja">は</span> is pronounced *wa*. The particle <span lang="ja">も</span> replaces <span lang="ja">は</span> in a simple additional-topic statement such as <span lang="ja">たなかさんも、がくせいです</span>. The question <span lang="ja">どれノート</span> is not grammatical: say <span lang="ja">どのノート</span> before a noun, or <span lang="ja">どれ</span> alone. <span lang="ja">何ですか</span> is pronounced *nan desu ka* in this context.

### Read four original classroom cards

These cards are fictional and independent of the listening interviews.

| Card | Japanese evidence | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | <span lang="ja">やまださん／にほんごのせんせい／きょうしつ</span> | Yamada / Japanese-language teacher / classroom |
| B | <span lang="ja">さとうさんのもの／あおいノート</span> | Sato's item / blue notebook |
| C | <span lang="ja">やまださんは、きょうしつです。たなかさんも、きょうしつです。</span> | Yamada is in the classroom; Tanaka is there too. |
| D | <span lang="ja">Q：これは、何ですか。 A：じしょです。</span> | What is this? It is a dictionary. |

In card C, <span lang="ja">きょうしつです</span> is a short contextual answer about location; the fuller expression is <span lang="ja">きょうしつにいます</span>. Do not assume a question establishes a fact: look for the **answer**. For card B, <span lang="ja">さとうさんのもの</span> means “Sato's thing,” and <span lang="ja">あおい</span> describes the notebook's color.

### Listening from the chapter

Use the two original N5-M01-C02-L06 interview recordings. Interview A identifies Yamada as a Japanese-language teacher in the classroom and adds Tanaka with <span lang="ja">も</span>. Interview B identifies a dictionary, selects the blue notebook, and rejects the red one with <span lang="ja">わたしのじゃありません</span>. First listen without the transcript. Replay for evidence, then reveal the transcript if needed; doing so marks your attempt assisted. The interface will not replace missing audio with a simulated recording or grade transcript-only answers as listening.

### Guided writing and optional speaking

Write **three connected Japanese sentences** about two fictional classmates and one object. A model:

<span lang="ja">やまださんは、きょうしつです。たなかさんも、きょうしつです。あおいノートは、さとうさんのです。</span>

“Yamada is in the classroom. Tanaka is there too. The blue notebook is Sato's.”

Then write a matching question: <span lang="ja">あおいノートは、だれのですか。</span> (“Whose is the blue notebook?”). Check the topic, question word, and whether <span lang="ja">も</span> really adds a second fact. Read the model aloud only if you wish; this is not a pronunciation test.

### Review plan

After checking your answers, find the exact Japanese phrase supporting each correction. Make a private flashcard for a weak question word or particle; revisit tomorrow and again a few days later.
