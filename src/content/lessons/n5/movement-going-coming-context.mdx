## A journey after class

You are at a university in Japan. Two classmates are planning what to do after their afternoon lesson. **Where is each person going, and where will they return?** This chapter's Can-do is to talk about destinations and movement. Today you will understand a realistic situation before studying the full grammar in Lesson 03.

### 1. Meet the speakers

**あき (Aki)** is standing at the university. **けん (Ken)** is also at the university, but he lives near the station. Read their original conversation:

> **あき:** <span lang="ja">けんさん、今日、どこへ行きますか。</span>
>
> **けん:** <span lang="ja">駅へ行きます。電車で家に帰ります。あきさんは？</span>
>
> **あき:** <span lang="ja">図書館に行きます。友だちが図書館に来ます。</span>
>
> **けん:** <span lang="ja">そうですか。また明日。</span>

**English:**

- Aki: “Ken, where are you going today?”
- Ken: “I'm going to the station. I'll go home by train. How about you, Aki?”
- Aki: “I'm going to the library. A friend is coming to the library.”
- Ken: “I see. See you tomorrow.”

The scene is fictional, but the expressions are useful for everyday travel.

### 2. Locate the key information

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning in this scene |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">今日</span> | きょう | today |
| <span lang="ja">どこ</span> | どこ | where |
| <span lang="ja">駅</span> | えき | train station |
| <span lang="ja">電車</span> | でんしゃ | train |
| <span lang="ja">家</span> | いえ | home; house |
| <span lang="ja">図書館</span> | としょかん | library |
| <span lang="ja">友だち</span> | ともだち | friend |
| <span lang="ja">行きます</span> | いきます | go / will go |
| <span lang="ja">来ます</span> | きます | come / will come |
| <span lang="ja">帰ります</span> | かえります | return / go home |

The polite nonpast form can describe a habit or a future plan. Here **今日** and the conversation context make these plans for today.

### 3. Three movement verbs, three viewpoints

**行きます (いきます)** means “go.” Ken says <span lang="ja">駅へ行きます。</span> — “I'm going to the station.” The station is his destination.

**来ます (きます)** means “come.” Aki says <span lang="ja">友だちが図書館に来ます。</span> — “A friend is coming to the library.” The library is the arrival point relevant to Aki's plan. Japanese “come” depends on viewpoint and context; it is not interchangeable with “go.”

**帰ります (かえります)** means “return,” often “go home.” Ken says <span lang="ja">家に帰ります。</span> — “I'm going home.” He is not saying that his home is a train station.

**A note about particles:** <span lang="ja">駅へ行きます</span> and <span lang="ja">図書館に行きます</span> both identify a destination. <span lang="ja">へ</span> is pronounced **え** when it is a particle. <span lang="ja">電車で帰ります</span> uses <span lang="ja">で</span> to indicate **by train**, not a destination. Lesson 03 will compare these uses in detail.

### 4. Check what the dialogue actually says

Ken will **go to the station** and **return home by train**. The dialogue does **not** say whether he walks to the station or how long the trip takes.

Aki will **go to the library**, and a friend **will come to the library**. The dialogue does **not** specify whether the friend is coming by train, bus or on foot. Distinguish stated information from a plausible guess.

### 5. Guided practice

**Find the destination.** Underline the destination in <span lang="ja">駅へ行きます。</span> (駅) and <span lang="ja">図書館に行きます。</span> (図書館). Circle the transport in <span lang="ja">電車で家に帰ります。</span> (電車).

**Change the destination.** Substitute <span lang="ja">学校 (がっこう, school)</span> for 駅 in <span lang="ja">駅へ行きます。</span>. Your new sentence is <span lang="ja">学校へ行きます。</span> — “I go to school.” Then substitute <span lang="ja">公園 (こうえん, park)</span>.

**Perspective check.** Imagine your friend is waiting at the library. From that friend's viewpoint, <span lang="ja">あきさんが図書館に来ます。</span> can mean “Aki is coming to the library.” From Aki's viewpoint, <span lang="ja">図書館に行きます。</span> is “I'm going to the library.” The viewpoint matters.

### 6. Independent mini-task

Write three short sentences about a fictional afternoon using **行きます**, **帰ります** and one place. Example:

> <span lang="ja">今日、学校へ行きます。午後、図書館に行きます。五時に家に帰ります。</span>

“Today I go to school. In the afternoon I go to the library. At five I return home.”

Check your own work: (1) Is the destination clear? (2) Is 帰ります used for returning? (3) Are time expressions consistent? This open writing is self-reviewed, not machine-scored.

**Optional oral rehearsal:** Read Aki's and Ken's lines aloud slowly, taking both roles. This is text-guided rehearsal only; no audio recording or pronunciation grade is claimed.

### 7. Review

Answer the questions below without rereading the dialogue. Then return to the original text and locate evidence for each answer. Keep the three verbs distinct: **go**, **come**, **return**.
