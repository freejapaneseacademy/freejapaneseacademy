## A photograph at the welcome table

**Can-do for this chapter:** Describe family members and simple relationships. Today you will understand who is related to whom in a short conversation. Later lessons will help you make your own descriptions.

At a community Japanese class, **ユイ** shows a family photograph to **マリ**, a classmate. Both people and the family are fictional. A photograph is a useful reason to ask about a person's relationship without assuming everyone has the same kind of family.

### Original conversation

**マリ:** それはユイさんの写真（しゃしん）ですか。  
**ユイ:** はい、私（わたし）の家族（かぞく）の写真です。  
**マリ:** この人（ひと）はだれですか。  
**ユイ:** 私の母（はは）です。名前（なまえ）はあきこです。  
**マリ:** そうですか。こちらの人は？  
**ユイ:** 私の兄（あに）です。名前はけんです。  
**マリ:** あきこさんとけんさんは日本（にほん）にいますか。  
**ユイ:** いいえ。母は大阪（おおさか）にいます。兄は京都（きょうと）にいます。  
**マリ:** ありがとうございます。

**Meaning:** Mari asks whether the picture is Yui's. Yui says it is a photograph of her family. Mari asks about two people. Yui identifies her mother Akiko and older brother Ken. Her mother is in Osaka, and her brother is in Kyoto. The final location sentences are contextual reading support, not a new grammar target.

### Read the relationships, not just the names

| Expression | Reading | Meaning here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 私の家族 | わたしのかぞく | my family |
| 私の母 | わたしのはは | my mother (when talking about one's own mother to someone outside the family) |
| 私の兄 | わたしのあに | my older brother (when talking about one's own older brother) |
| ユイさんの写真 | ユイさんのしゃしん | Yui's photograph / a photograph associated with Yui |
| こちらの人 | こちらのひと | this person here (polite pointing expression) |
| 名前 | なまえ | name |

**Family-word caution:** **母（はは）** and **兄（あに）** refer to your own family when talking to outsiders. To address or refer respectfully to someone else's mother and older brother, **お母さん（おかあさん）** and **お兄さん（おにいさん）** are common. A later vocabulary lesson develops these choices. A family may have many different structures; these words describe the people in *this* fictional scene.

## What does の connect?

In **私の母**, の connects **私** (“I/me”) to **母** (“mother”): *my mother*. In **家族の写真**, it connects *family* and *photograph*: *a family photograph*. It does not always mean legal ownership. **ユイさんの写真** could mean a photo belonging to Yui or a photo depicting Yui; the next line establishes that this one depicts her family.

**Compare:**

- **これは私の写真です。** — This is my photograph. The context decides whether I own it or appear in it.
- **この人は私の母です。** — This person is my mother. The relationship is explicit.
- **こちらは私の兄です。** — This is my older brother. **こちら** can politely introduce a person; it is not limited to directions.

**Notice:** **その人** means “that person,” referring to someone already identified or near the listener. It can sound impersonal when introducing someone face-to-face. **こちらは母です** is a more courteous introduction; use **その人** primarily when the context makes its referent clear. Do not treat **こちら** and **その人** as universally interchangeable.

## Controlled practice: point to the evidence

Read **私の母です。名前はあきこです。** Who is Akiko? Yui's mother. The relationship comes from **母**, not from guessing a name's gender.

Read **私の兄です。名前はけんです。** Is Ken a younger brother? No: **兄（あに）** explicitly means *older brother*. A younger brother is **弟（おとうと）**, a different word.

Read **母は大阪にいます。兄は京都にいます。** Are the two relatives in the same city? No. The text states **大阪** for Yui's mother and **京都** for her brother.

## Guided practice: reconstruct the photo

Without looking back at the English summary, answer aloud or on paper:

1. Whose family photo is being discussed? Cite **私の家族の写真**.
2. Which person is named あきこ? Cite **私の母です**.
3. Which person is named けん? Cite **私の兄です**.
4. Where is each person? Point to the two location sentences.

Now cover the conversation and try again. Reopen it only to verify evidence. The interactive check below assesses recognition; it does not claim to grade spoken performance.

## Independent transfer

Imagine a *different fictional* photo with a person named さき and her older sister. Write two short labels: **さきさんの写真** and **さきさんの姉（あね）**. Compare the relationship: **姉** means older sister, whereas **兄** means older brother. You do not need to disclose or describe your own family.
