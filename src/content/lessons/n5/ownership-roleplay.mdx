## Your mission: help at the lost-and-found counter

This **optional** lesson helps you practise short memorized conversations about objects and owners. The two role-play dialogues are original fictional scenarios, not recordings. You do not need a microphone or any recording to finish. The related L06 listening lesson has separate candidate audio whose quality and delivery require verification; do not treat these written lines as an authentic audio recording.

### First, choose the right demonstrative

| Japanese | Meaning | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| これ | this one, near speaker | これは だれのですか。 — Whose is this? |
| それ | that one, near listener | それは ミカさんのです。 — That's Mika's. |
| あれ | that one over there | あれは だれのですか。 — Whose is that over there? |
| この | this + noun | この ノート — this notebook |
| その | that + noun | その かさ — that umbrella |
| あの | that ... over there + noun | あの かばん — that bag over there |

**の** shows ownership: <span lang="ja">リナさんの ノート</span> means “Rina's notebook”; <span lang="ja">リナさんのです</span> means “it's Rina's” when the object is understood. <span lang="ja">だれのですか</span> asks “whose?” Avoid **このは ノートです**: **この** must be followed by a noun. Use <span lang="ja">これは ノートです</span> or <span lang="ja">この ノートは…</span>.

### Scene A: two umbrellas

**Setting:** The visitor holds a green umbrella at the counter. A blue umbrella is on a distant shelf. The attendant is across the counter.

| Speaker | Japanese | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visitor | すみません。この みどりの かさは だれのですか。 | Excuse me, whose green umbrella is this? |
| Attendant | それは ナオさんのです。 | That is Nao's. |
| Visitor | あの あおい かさも ナオさんのですか。 | Is that blue umbrella over there Nao's too? |
| Attendant | いいえ。あれは ミカさんのです。 | No. That one is Mika's. |

The attendant says **それ** because the green umbrella is near the *visitor*, not the attendant. **あれ** refers to the distant blue umbrella. **も** means “also”; **いいえ** politely corrects the proposed owner.

### Scene B: a notebook and bag

**Setting:** The visitor points to a notebook on the counter; a bag is on a distant shelf.

| Speaker | Japanese | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visitor | この ノートは だれのですか。 | Whose notebook is this? |
| Attendant | その ノートは リナさんのです。 | That notebook is Rina's. |
| Visitor | あの かばんは リナさんのですか。 | Is that bag over there Rina's? |
| Attendant | いいえ。あれは わたしのです。 | No. That one is mine. |

Here **わたしのです** means “it's mine,” referring to the attendant. The notebook belongs to Rina; the bag does not. Do not infer ownership merely from an object's location.

## Three-pass guided shadowing

1. Read the visitor's lines aloud slowly, pointing to the correct object. Pause at **だれのですか**.
2. Read the attendant's replies, especially **それは…のです** and **いいえ。あれは…のです**. Then swap roles.
3. Cover the English meanings and act out both scenes using Japanese only. Change <span lang="ja">ノート</span> to <span lang="ja">ほん</span> (book) and <span lang="ja">リナさん</span> to <span lang="ja">タクさん</span>. Ask <span lang="ja">この ほんは だれのですか。</span> and reply <span lang="ja">タクさんのです。</span>.

Use the optional role switch below for a short cue. No speech recognition or pronunciation score is provided. You may repeat as often as you like.

## Self-review checklist

Check whether you **asked whose item it was**, **distinguished near and distant objects**, **used この/その/あの before nouns**, and **corrected an incorrect owner with いいえ**. These are personal observations, not automatically assessed oral performance. The scored check below is written recognition practice only; lesson completion is separate from that score.
