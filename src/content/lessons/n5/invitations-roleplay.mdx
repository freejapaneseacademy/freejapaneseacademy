## Your optional speaking mission

You and a friend are planning a simple outing. **Invite, respond, and decide on a place** in two to five short lines. This L08 is **optional**: no microphone, pronunciation score or speaking assessment is required for course completion. If you cannot speak aloud, read silently, type your lines, or practise with a partner.

This is a *rehearsal*, not a claim of verified pronunciation. The earlier L06 invitation dialogue is the chapter's listening resource; use its actual player only if that recording is available and verified. No synthetic audio or pretend playback is embedded here.

## 1. The conversational moves

| What you want to do | Japanese | Reading | English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Invite | いっしょにカフェに行きませんか。 | いっしょに カフェに いきませんか | Would you like to go to a café together? |
| Accept | いいですね。行きましょう。 | いいですね。いきましょう | Sounds good. Let's go. |
| Decline gently | すみません。今日はちょっと……。 | すみません。きょうは ちょっと | Sorry, today is a little difficult… |
| Ask preference | カフェと公園、どちらがいいですか。 | カフェと こうえん、どちらが いいですか | Which do you prefer, the café or the park? |
| Choose | 公園にします。 | こうえんに します | I'll choose the park. |

**Notice the different functions.** 行きませんか is an invitation, 行きましょう proposes a shared action, and 公園にします announces a choice. **今日はちょっと……** is a soft refusal, not an acceptance. A response like **いいですね** is positive, while **そうですか** acknowledges information and does not itself accept.

## 2. Model exchange A: accept and choose

> はる：土曜日（どようび）にいっしょにお茶（ちゃ）を飲（の）みませんか。  
> Haru: Would you like to have tea together on Saturday?
>
> なお：いいですね。飲みましょう。  
> Nao: Sounds good. Let's have tea.
>
> はる：駅（えき）のカフェと公園（こうえん）のカフェ、どちらがいいですか。  
> Haru: Which do you prefer, the café at the station or the café at the park?
>
> なお：駅のカフェにします。  
> Nao: I'll choose the café at the station.

**Evidence check:** The invitation ends with **飲みませんか**; the acceptance uses **飲みましょう**; the choice ends with **にします**. The place choice is *the station café*, not the park café.

## 3. Model exchange B: decline and offer another day

> りく：今日（きょう）、いっしょに昼（ひる）ご飯（はん）を食（た）べませんか。  
> Riku: Would you like to have lunch together today?
>
> さき：すみません。今日はちょっと……。  
> Saki: Sorry, today is a little difficult…
>
> りく：じゃあ、日曜日（にちようび）はどうですか。  
> Riku: Then how about Sunday?
>
> さき：日曜日なら大丈夫（だいじょうぶ）です。行きましょう。  
> Saki: Sunday works for me. Let's go.

**Culture and meaning:** A trailing **ちょっと……** often implies difficulty without a detailed explanation. It does not guarantee the person is available another day. **日曜日なら大丈夫です** explicitly accepts Sunday. You can omit this extension if it is beyond your comfort level.

## 4. Build your own mini-dialogue

Choose **one** of these situations. Say the lines aloud at a comfortable pace, or type them if you prefer.

**Situation A — A café after class:** Invite a classmate to drink tea. The classmate accepts. Ask them to choose between two cafés and give the choice.

**Situation B — Lunch on Saturday:** Invite a friend to eat lunch. They politely decline Saturday. Suggest Sunday; they accept.

**Situation C — A park outing:** Invite a friend to go to a park. They accept. Choose between the park and a café. Keep the setting coherent: choosing a café means the destination changes.

A reusable structure:

1. **A:** いっしょに＿＿に行きませんか。 (Would you like to go to ___ together?)
2. **B:** いいですね。行きましょう。 (Sounds good. Let's go.)
3. **A:** ＿＿と＿＿、どちらがいいですか。 (Which do you prefer, ___ or ___?)
4. **B:** ＿＿にします。 (I'll choose ___.)

Use **カフェ**, **公園（こうえん）**, **レストラン** or **図書館（としょかん）** for destinations. For the park, **公園に行きませんか** is natural. For lunch, **いっしょに昼ご飯を食べませんか** is natural. Do not replace the object particle **を** in **お茶を飲みませんか** with the destination particle **に**.

## 5. Three passes: supported → independent → role switch

**Pass 1 — Supported.** Read model A slowly. Pause after each line and identify the move: invitation, acceptance, question, decision. Do not worry about speed.

**Pass 2 — Independent.** Hide the model with your eyes or scroll away. Produce two to five lines for situation A, B or C. Use your own choices, not the same location twice.

**Pass 3 — Role switch.** Play the other person. If you first invited, now respond. Practise **both** a friendly acceptance and a considerate refusal. A refusal is not rude simply because it is short; tone and context matter.

### Self-review rubric (not automatically graded)

| Criterion | Ready for another try | Meets the practice target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Invitation | No invitation or incorrect verb form | Clear **〜ませんか** invitation |
| Reply | Reply doesn't fit the invitation | Appropriate **いいですね・ましょう** or gentle refusal |
| Choice | Location not identified | Clear noun + **にします** choice where relevant |
| Interaction | One disconnected sentence | Two to five coherent turns, with role switch |

Mark these criteria for yourself; they are not an official JLPT score, speech recognition result or human evaluation.

## Recap

**〜ませんか** invites. **〜ましょう** suggests a shared action. **〜にします** selects a choice. **すみません。今日はちょっと……** can decline tactfully. You have completed the optional role-switch practice when you can make and respond to a simple invitation in your own words, without a claimed automated speaking grade.
