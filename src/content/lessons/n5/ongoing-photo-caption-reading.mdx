## Read a moment in progress

**Can-do:** Understand short captions describing what someone is doing **now**, identify the place and action, and show which Japanese words support your answer. These are **fictional, text-only photo descriptions**; no actual photograph or audio is required.

### First, spot the action

**何（なに）をしていますか。** means “What are you doing?” or “What is [someone] doing?” depending on context. An answer can be **本（ほん）を読（よ）んでいます** — “(Someone) is reading a book.”

The **〜ています** form describes an action in progress for the verbs in today's captions. Build it with the **て-form + います**: 読む → 読んでいます, 書（か）く → 書いています, 食（た）べる → 食べています, 話（はな）す → 話しています. The small っ and voiced sounds in other verbs still follow their normal て-form rules.

**Place + で** locates the activity: **図書館（としょかん）で** = “at the library.” **Object + を** marks what is read, eaten or written: **本を読んでいます**. Do not substitute に for で when naming where an activity happens. Compare **図書館にいます** (“is at the library”) with **図書館で読んでいます** (“is reading at the library”).

### Caption A — A library table

> **写真（しゃしん）A：図書館（としょかん）**  
> 田中（たなか）さんは図書館で日本語（にほんご）の本を読んでいます。  
> となりで、森（もり）さんはノートに新（あたら）しい言葉（ことば）を書いています。

**Translation:** “Mr./Ms. Tanaka is reading a Japanese-language book at the library. Beside them, Mr./Ms. Mori is writing new words in a notebook.”

**Evidence:** **田中さんは図書館で日本語の本を読んでいます** tells us who reads, where, and what. **森さんはノートに新しい言葉を書いています** tells us what Mori writes and where the words are written. Here **ノートに** marks the surface/destination of writing, not the location of the whole activity. **となりで** means “next to [them].”

### Caption B — The community kitchen

> **写真B：町（まち）のキッチン**  
> 佐藤（さとう）さんはキッチンで野菜（やさい）を切（き）っています。  
> 木村（きむら）さんはテーブルでサンドイッチを食べています。  
> 二人（ふたり）は今（いま）、昼（ひる）ごはんの準備（じゅんび）をしています。

**Translation:** “Sato is cutting vegetables in the kitchen. Kimura is eating a sandwich at the table. The two are now preparing lunch.”

**Evidence:** **野菜を切っています** names Sato's action; **テーブルでサンドイッチを食べています** names Kimura's action. The final sentence describes their shared situation. Do not assume Sato is eating or that Kimura is cutting vegetables.

### Caption C — A park on Sunday

> **写真C：日曜日（にちようび）の公園（こうえん）**  
> 山本（やまもと）さんは公園で犬（いぬ）と歩（ある）いています。  
> 小林（こばやし）さんはベンチで友達（ともだち）と話しています。  
> 空（そら）は青（あお）いです。

**Translation:** “Yamamoto is walking with a dog in the park. Kobayashi is talking with a friend on a bench. The sky is blue.”

**Evidence:** **犬と歩いています** shows companionship (と = “with”), not a direct object. **友達と話しています** shows whom Kobayashi is speaking with. **空は青いです** is a description of a state, not another 〜ています action.

### Reading carefully: four moves

1. Find **who**: 田中さん, 森さん, 佐藤さん, 木村さん, 山本さん or 小林さん.
2. Find **where**: 図書館で, キッチンで, テーブルで, 公園で or ベンチで.
3. Find **what is happening**: 読んでいます, 書いています, 切っています, 食べています, 歩いています or 話しています.
4. **Cite the shortest phrase** that proves your answer. A caption gives only a snapshot. It does not prove what happened earlier or what someone will do next.

### Guided reading with evidence

**Question:** Who is writing new words? **Answer:** Mori. **Evidence:** **森さんはノートに新しい言葉を書いています**.

**Question:** Is Kimura preparing vegetables? **Answer:** The caption does not say that. **Evidence:** It says **木村さんはテーブルでサンドイッチを食べています**. The vegetables belong to Sato's action.

**Question:** Is Yamamoto walking with a friend? **Answer:** No: the caption says **犬と歩いています**. A dog is the stated companion.

### Independent reading

Cover the English translations. For each caption, make a three-column note: **person / place / ongoing action**. Then answer the interactive questions without looking at the translations; return to the Japanese to verify your evidence.

**Optional writing:** Write two fictional captions about classmates or family members, using **[person] は [place] で [object] を [て-form] います** where natural. Model: **兄（あに）は家（いえ）で音楽（おんがく）を聞（き）いています。** — “My older brother is listening to music at home.” For walking or talking with someone, use **と**, not を: **友達と話しています**. Check your particles and verb forms; writing is ungraded.

**Optional speaking:** Read one caption aloud and say its English meaning. No microphone, speech grade or verified recording is provided in this reading lesson.

### Remember

**〜ています** can describe an action in progress here; **で** marks its setting, **を** marks an object, and **と** can mark a companion. A strong reading answer is supported by the Japanese caption—not by an imagined photograph.
