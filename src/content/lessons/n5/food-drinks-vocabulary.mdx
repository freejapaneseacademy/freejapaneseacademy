## A café vocabulary mission

**Can-do:** Name common foods and express simple preferences. Imagine reading a small café menu before choosing lunch. In this lesson you will learn the words on the menu, distinguish a meal from a particular dish, and say what someone likes or eats. This is a **written vocabulary lesson**; the chapter's listening lesson uses real generated audio only after its separate verification.

## 1. Twelve useful words

Read the kana aloud; the kanji forms are for recognition. These are original teaching examples, not a real café's menu.

| Word | Reading | Meaning | In a sentence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">食べ物</span> | たべもの | food | <span lang="ja">日本の食べ物が好きです。</span> — I like Japanese food. |
| <span lang="ja">飲み物</span> | のみもの | drink / beverage | <span lang="ja">飲み物は何ですか。</span> — What is the drink? |
| <span lang="ja">朝ごはん</span> | あさごはん | breakfast | <span lang="ja">朝ごはんを食べます。</span> — I eat breakfast. |
| <span lang="ja">昼ごはん</span> | ひるごはん | lunch | <span lang="ja">昼ごはんはパンです。</span> — Lunch is bread. |
| <span lang="ja">晩ごはん</span> | ばんごはん | dinner | <span lang="ja">晩ごはんにごはんを食べます。</span> — I eat rice for dinner. |
| <span lang="ja">ごはん</span> | ごはん | cooked rice; also meal | <span lang="ja">ごはんを食べます。</span> — I eat rice. |
| <span lang="ja">パン</span> | パン | bread | <span lang="ja">パンが好きです。</span> — I like bread. |
| <span lang="ja">卵</span> | たまご | egg | <span lang="ja">卵を食べます。</span> — I eat an egg. |
| <span lang="ja">魚</span> | さかな | fish | <span lang="ja">魚が好きです。</span> — I like fish. |
| <span lang="ja">水</span> | みず | water | <span lang="ja">水を飲みます。</span> — I drink water. |
| <span lang="ja">お茶</span> | おちゃ | tea | <span lang="ja">お茶が好きです。</span> — I like tea. |
| <span lang="ja">牛乳</span> | ぎゅうにゅう | milk | <span lang="ja">牛乳を飲みます。</span> — I drink milk. |

**Reading detail:** The first kanji in **食べ物** appears in **食べます** (たべます); **飲み物** connects with **飲みます** (のみます). The suffix **物／もの** makes a noun for a thing. **ごはん** can mean cooked rice or a meal, so context matters. **朝ごはん・昼ごはん・晩ごはん** refer to meals, not necessarily a plate of rice. **パン** is written in katakana even though it is an everyday Japanese word.

## 2. A preference is not an action

Compare three patterns:

- <span lang="ja">パンが好きです。</span> (パンがすきです。) — I like bread. **が** marks the thing liked.
- <span lang="ja">パンを食べます。</span> (パンをたべます。) — I eat bread. **を** marks the thing eaten.
- <span lang="ja">お茶を飲みます。</span> (おちゃをのみます。) — I drink tea.

Do not swap the particles just because the English sentence uses “bread” in each case. **パンを好きです** is not the standard beginner pattern. **お茶を食べます** means eating tea, not drinking it. A noun can appear with **が好きです** regardless of whether it is something one eats or drinks.

For a simple dislike, <span lang="ja">魚が嫌いです。</span> (さかながきらいです。) means “I dislike fish.” **嫌い** is a な-adjective, not a verb. The nuance can sound strong; use it thoughtfully about people's food choices.

## 3. The joining particle と

<span lang="ja">パンと卵を食べます。</span> — I eat bread **and** eggs. Here **と** joins the two nouns in a complete list. Compare <span lang="ja">パンとお茶</span> (bread and tea). When the foods are both objects of eating, one **を** can follow the joined noun phrase. For a drink, write a separate clause: <span lang="ja">パンを食べます。お茶を飲みます。</span> — I eat bread. I drink tea.

## 4. Original fictional café note

> <span lang="ja">みおさんのカフェメモ</span> — Mio's café note
>
> <span lang="ja">昼ごはんにパンと卵を食べます。</span> — For lunch, I eat bread and an egg.
>
> <span lang="ja">飲み物はお茶です。</span> — My drink is tea.
>
> <span lang="ja">水も飲みます。</span> — I drink water too.
>
> <span lang="ja">魚が好きです。でも、今日は魚を食べません。</span> — I like fish. But today I am not eating fish.
>
> <span lang="ja">牛乳はあまり飲みません。</span> — I do not drink much milk.

**Find evidence:** What does Mio eat for lunch? Quote **パンと卵を食べます**. Does liking fish mean Mio eats fish today? **No**. The note explicitly says **今日は魚を食べません**. Which two drinks appear? **お茶** and **水**. The note also mentions milk, but says she does not drink much of it.

## 5. Three common reading traps

**ごはん** can mean rice or a meal. In **昼ごはん**, it refers to lunch. **飲み物** means a beverage, not the verb “drink”; **飲みます** is the polite verb. **好きです** describes preference, while **食べます** and **飲みます** describe actions. **あまり〜ません** means “not much,” not “never.”

## 6. Guided practice

First cover the English column. Read **食べ物・飲み物・卵・魚・水・牛乳** using the kana, then identify the matching item from the meaning. Next replace the noun in <span lang="ja">パンが好きです。</span> with **魚**, then replace it with **お茶**. Both still take **が**.

Now correct these deliberate mistakes:

- <span lang="ja">水を食べます。</span> → <span lang="ja">水を飲みます。</span>
- <span lang="ja">魚を好きです。</span> → <span lang="ja">魚が好きです。</span>
- <span lang="ja">パンと卵が食べます。</span> → <span lang="ja">パンと卵を食べます。</span>

## 7. Independent transfer

Write **two connected sentences** about a fictional café customer: one preference and one action. Model: <span lang="ja">ゆきさんはパンが好きです。昼ごはんにパンを食べます。</span> — Yuki likes bread. She eats bread for lunch. Self-check: is the liked item marked **が**? Is the eaten or drunk item marked **を**? Does the verb fit the object? This free writing is self-reviewed, not automatically graded.

**Optional speaking:** Say your two sentences slowly and compare them with the kana readings above. No recording or pronunciation score is required. The written quiz below checks vocabulary and context; it does not assess spoken Japanese.
