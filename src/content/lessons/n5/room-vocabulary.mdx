## A second look around Haru's room

**Can-do:** Give a basic description of a room. Haru is preparing a study space for a visitor. You will learn to name the furniture and tell where an object is **relative to another object**. This is written vocabulary study with kana readings; no recording or pronunciation evaluation is provided.

## 1. Furniture and room features

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning | When to use it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">机</span> | つくえ | desk | A surface for studying or writing. |
| <span lang="ja">いす</span> | いす | chair | A seat; distinguish it from 机. |
| <span lang="ja">本棚</span> | ほんだな | bookshelf | Furniture for keeping books, not a single book. |
| <span lang="ja">ベッド</span> | ベッド | bed | Katakana loanword for bed. |
| <span lang="ja">テーブル</span> | テーブル | table | A table; 机 often suggests a study/work desk. |
| <span lang="ja">窓</span> | まど | window | A landmark, not a piece of furniture. |
| <span lang="ja">ドア</span> | ドア | door | An entrance/exit panel, not a window. |

**机（つくえ）** is typically a desk for work or study, while **テーブル** can refer to a dining or other table. **本棚（ほんだな）** is the *bookshelf*, whereas **本（ほん）** is one *book*. **ベッド**, **テーブル**, and **ドア** are katakana loanwords. Say them with their Japanese mora patterns; spelling alone does not verify spoken accuracy.

## 2. Position words need a landmark

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning | Example phrase |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">上</span> | うえ | above / on | <span lang="ja">机の上</span> |
| <span lang="ja">下</span> | した | under / below | <span lang="ja">机の下</span> |
| <span lang="ja">前</span> | まえ | in front of | <span lang="ja">机の前</span> |
| <span lang="ja">後ろ</span> | うしろ | behind | <span lang="ja">机の後ろ</span> |
| <span lang="ja">中</span> | なか | inside | <span lang="ja">机の中</span> |
| <span lang="ja">隣</span> | となり | next to | <span lang="ja">机の隣</span> |

The pattern **landmark の position に** describes *where* something exists. For example, <span lang="ja">机の上に本があります。</span> (つくえの うえに ほんが あります。) means “There is a book on the desk.” The landmark is **机**; the position is **上**. The particle **の** links them, and **に** marks the location of existence.

**上** can mean “above” as well as “on”; it does not always guarantee contact. **隣** often suggests adjacency at roughly the same level. For objects positioned immediately next to something, **横（よこ）** is another useful word, but this lesson focuses on 隣.

## 3. Six short room descriptions

1. <span lang="ja">本は机の上にあります。</span> (ほんは つくえの うえに あります。) — The book is on the desk.
2. <span lang="ja">かばんはいすの下にあります。</span> (かばんは いすの したに あります。) — The bag is under the chair.
3. <span lang="ja">机は窓の前にあります。</span> (つくえは まどの まえに あります。) — The desk is in front of the window.
4. <span lang="ja">いすはドアの後ろにあります。</span> (いすは ドアの うしろに あります。) — The chair is behind the door.
5. <span lang="ja">本はかばんの中にあります。</span> (ほんは かばんの なかに あります。) — The book is inside the bag.
6. <span lang="ja">猫はいすの隣にいます。</span> (ねこは いすの となりに います。) — The cat is beside the chair.

The room examples are **separate invented snapshots**, not a single consistent floor plan. The subject marked by **は** is already the topic. In an existence sentence introducing an item, you can also say <span lang="ja">机の上に本があります。</span> The object **本** takes **あります**; the living **猫** takes **います**.

## 4. Read and notice

Haru says: <span lang="ja">わたしの部屋に机と本棚があります。机は窓の前にあります。ベッドは本棚の隣にあります。</span>

(わたしの へやに つくえと ほんだなが あります。つくえは まどの まえに あります。ベッドは ほんだなの となりに あります。)

“In my room there is a desk and a bookshelf. The desk is in front of the window. The bed is next to the bookshelf.”

First find the furniture words. Then find the two **landmark の position** phrases. **と** links the desk and bookshelf as an “and” list; it does not mean “with” in every situation.

## 5. A common mistake

Do not swap the *thing located* and the *landmark*: <span lang="ja">机は窓の前にあります。</span> means “The desk is in front of the window.” <span lang="ja">窓は机の前にあります。</span> describes a different relationship. Likewise, do not write <span lang="ja">机の上が本があります</span> for “There is a book on the desk”: the location takes **に**, not **が**.

## 6. Guided retrieval

Without looking at the tables, name in Japanese: **chair, bookshelf, window, door, behind, inside**. Then cover the Japanese column and retrieve the words again. Check: <span lang="ja">いす、本棚、窓、ドア、後ろ、中</span>.

Complete these phrases: <span lang="ja">かばんの ___</span> (“inside the bag”); <span lang="ja">窓の ___</span> (“in front of the window”); <span lang="ja">本棚の ___</span> (“next to the bookshelf”). Answers: <span lang="ja">中、前、隣</span>.

## 7. Independent written transfer

Invent a different study room. Write **three** sentences: locate a book relative to a desk, locate a bed relative to a bookshelf, and locate a cat relative to a chair. Use **あります** for objects and **います** for the cat. One possible model:

> <span lang="ja">本は机の下にあります。ベッドは本棚の隣にあります。猫はいすの前にいます。</span>

Self-check: (1) a clear object, (2) a landmark followed by の, (3) a position followed by に, and (4) an appropriate existence verb. Your writing is ungraded. You may optionally read your own sentences aloud, but this page does not score speech or provide verified audio.
