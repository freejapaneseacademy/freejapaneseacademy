## Listening in everyday action sequences

Two fictional conversations tell you what people do **first**, **next** and **after** another action. Track A follows students preparing a poster for a presentation. Track B follows two people preparing lunch. Both are original Japanese scripts intended for approved AivisSpeech synthetic voices; they are not recordings of real speakers.

**Can-do:** Understand the main topic and four clearly spoken details in each short conversation, without needing to see its written transcript.

### Prepare your ears

These expressions are useful for recognizing action order. They are *examples*, not the answers to the scored questions.

| Expression | Reading | Listening meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| まず | まず | first |
| そのあと | そのあと | after that |
| 〜て | 〜て | links actions or clauses; context gives the relationship |
| 〜てから | 〜てから | after doing something |
| 取って | とって | take (て-form of 取る) |
| 書いて | かいて | write (て-form of 書く) |
| 洗って | あらって | wash (て-form of 洗う) |
| 切って | きって | cut (て-form of 切る) |
| 入れて | いれて | put in (て-form of 入れる) |

**Listen for the verb endings.** 書く becomes 書いて, not 書くて; 洗う becomes 洗って, not 洗いて. The verb 切る belongs to Group 1 and becomes 切って, even though it ends in る. 入れる is Group 2 and becomes 入れて.

### First pass: the topic

Play each track once with the transcript hidden. For A, ask yourself whether the conversation is about preparing a school project or buying a ticket. For B, decide whether the speakers are cooking or asking for directions. These are orientation checks, not graded questions.

### Second pass: the sequence

Replay track A. Listen for **まず** and **〜てから**; ask what happens before the final instruction. Replay B. Listen for two connected kitchen actions and a final step before eating. The words **そのあと** and **〜てから** signal an explicit relationship, while **〜て** alone may simply connect actions.

### Third pass: check comprehension

Answer four questions for each track in the listening activity below. Replaying is allowed. For an unaided listening score, keep transcripts hidden until you submit. If you reveal text, your attempt is **assisted practice**, not a blind listening score. You may still use the transcript to learn, including if audio playback is unavailable.

## Why て and てから differ

**窓を開けて、座ります。**（まどをあけて、すわります。）— “I open the window and sit down.” The actions are linked; their exact timing depends on context.

**窓を開けてから、座ります。**（まどをあけてから、すわります。）— “After opening the window, I sit down.” This explicitly says opening happens first.

These two new examples are for grammar transfer; they are not from the listening tracks. In the recordings, pay attention to the actual sequence rather than guessing from world knowledge. If a speaker says **〜てから**, the first action precedes the second.

## After listening: verify and transfer

Reveal the Japanese transcript and its speaker-by-speaker English translation after submitting. Find the exact sentence supporting each answer, and replay that part. A correct answer must be grounded in the spoken words, not in assumptions about classrooms or cooking.

**Writing (ungraded):** Write two to four short Japanese sentences about preparing for a picnic. Use one て-form chain and one 〜てから sentence. A separate model: **水を買って、公園へ行きます。公園に着いてから、お昼ご飯を食べます。** (I buy water and go to the park. After arriving, I eat lunch.) Self-check your verb transformations and whether the action order is clear.

**Optional speaking:** After a replay, pause and repeat a short sentence at a comfortable pace. This is private, self-directed shadowing. No microphone recording or pronunciation score is requested.
