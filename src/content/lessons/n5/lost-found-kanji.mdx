## The lost-and-found desk

A student has left belongings at school. A receptionist writes labels to help return them. Learn to read the **object** and the **person** on each label. The reading is original; there is no recorded audio or pronunciation grade.

## Eight kanji words in context

| Kanji word | Reading | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">本</span> | ほん | book | <span lang="ja">これは 本です。</span> — This is a book. |
| <span lang="ja">学校</span> | がっこう | school | <span lang="ja">学校の 本です。</span> — This is a school book. |
| <span lang="ja">名前</span> | なまえ | name | <span lang="ja">この 本に 名前が あります。</span> — There is a name on this book. |
| <span lang="ja">学生</span> | がくせい | student | <span lang="ja">学生の ノートです。</span> — It is a student's notebook. |
| <span lang="ja">先生</span> | せんせい | teacher | <span lang="ja">先生の かばんです。</span> — It is the teacher's bag. |
| <span lang="ja">何</span> | なん | what | <span lang="ja">これは 何ですか。</span> — What is this? |
| <span lang="ja">人</span> | ひと | person | <span lang="ja">あの 人の かさです。</span> — It is that person's umbrella. |
| <span lang="ja">物</span> | もの | thing | <span lang="ja">この 物は だれのですか。</span> — Whose item is this? |

Read **whole words**: 学校 is がっこう, with a small っ; 学生 is がくせい; 先生 is せんせい. Although 学生 and 先生 share 生, they are not pronounced or used identically. 名前 is なまえ, not a combination of independent everyday readings of 名 and 前. 本 means “book” and is read ほん here. 物 is read もの as the standalone word for “thing,” though its readings vary in compounds.

## Original school reception log

| Label | Reading | What the receptionist knows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">学生の 本</span> | がくせいの ほん | A student's book, but no named student |
| <span lang="ja">先生の かばん</span> | せんせいの かばん | A teacher's bag, but no named teacher |
| <span lang="ja">名前の ない ノート</span> | なまえの ない ノート | A notebook without a name written on it |
| <span lang="ja">あの 人の かさ</span> | あの ひとの かさ | That person's umbrella, with the person indicated in context |

**Evidence matters.** The notebook without a name has no confirmed owner. 学生の 本 identifies the owner's role, not the owner's identity. Do not invent a name.

## Ask about a found item

**Staff:** <span lang="ja">これは 何ですか。</span> (*これは なんですか。*) — What is this?

**Student:** <span lang="ja">本です。</span> (*ほんです。*) — It's a book.

**Staff:** <span lang="ja">この 本は だれのですか。</span> (*この ほんは だれのですか。*) — Whose book is this?

**Student:** <span lang="ja">先生のです。</span> (*せんせいのです。*) — It's the teacher's.

**これ** means “this one” and stands alone. **この** must precede a noun, as in この 本. **の** can link owner and object (先生の 本), or stand for an understood object (先生のです). The written particle **は** is pronounced *wa*. In 何ですか, 何 is normally read **なん**; other constructions may use なに.

## Form recognition and common mistakes

Do not mistake 学校 (school) for 学生 (student). Do not assume 先生の かばん reveals a particular teacher's name. Learn the actual word **名前** rather than guessing its reading from character shapes. The objects かさ, かばん and ノート intentionally stay in common kana spellings; uncommon object kanji are not tested.

## Controlled → guided → independent practice

**Controlled:** Cover the readings above. Read 本, 学校, 名前 and 先生, then check: ほん, がっこう, なまえ, せんせい.

**Guided:** In 先生の かばん, underline the owner **先生** and circle the object **かばん**. In 学生の 本, identify 学生 as an unnamed student, not a specific person.

**Independent:** Write two original labels following 学生の 本 and 先生の ノート. Then write one question using **この + object + は だれのですか**. Check that この is followed by a noun and that the possessor precedes の.
