## Your writing mission

**Chapter can-do:** Explain transport choices and who accompanies you. You are helping a fictional classmate plan a Saturday visit to a museum. By the end, write **two to five short connected Japanese sentences** explaining where someone goes, who goes along (or that they go alone), and how they travel. You can use kana where you have not learned the kanji.

**Situation:** Your classmate asks, <span lang="ja">土曜日、どうやって博物館に行きますか。だれと行きますか。</span>（どようび、どうやって はくぶつかんに いきますか。だれと いきますか。） — “How will you get to the museum on Saturday? Who will you go with?” You may invent the traveller and plan; no personal information is required.

## 1. Observe a complete model

> <span lang="ja">土曜日、博物館に行きます。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">友だちと行きます。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">電車で行きます。</span>

**Reading:** どようび、はくぶつかんに いきます。ともだちと いきます。でんしゃで いきます。

**Meaning:** “On Saturday, I will go to the museum. I will go with a friend. I will go by train.”

Japanese present polite **行きます** can describe a future plan when the time context makes that clear. The three sentences share an implied subject; repeating 私は (“as for me”) is unnecessary. The model says nothing about the departure time or fare—do not add details that are not given.

## 2. Build the three information pieces

| Job | Japanese pattern | Example and meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Destination | Place **に** 行きます | <span lang="ja">公園に行きます。</span>（こうえんに いきます） — I go to the park. |
| Companion | Person **と** 行きます | <span lang="ja">母と行きます。</span>（ははと いきます） — I go with my mother. |
| Means | Vehicle **で** 行きます | <span lang="ja">バスで行きます。</span>（ばすで いきます） — I go by bus. |
| Travelling alone | **一人で** 行きます | <span lang="ja">一人で行きます。</span>（ひとりで いきます） — I go alone. |

**Useful words:** 博物館（はくぶつかん） museum; 公園（こうえん） park; 図書館（としょかん） library; 駅（えき） station; 電車（でんしゃ） train; 自転車（じてんしゃ） bicycle; 車（くるま） car; 友だち（ともだち） friend; 母（はは） my mother; 父（ちち） my father; 妹（いもうと） my younger sister.

For transport, **歩いて行きます**（あるいて いきます） means “go on foot.” Do not treat 歩いて as a vehicle noun followed by で: **歩いてで行きます** is not the pattern.

## 3. Compare meanings before you write

- <span lang="ja">友だちと電車で行きます。</span>（ともだちと でんしゃで いきます） — I go **with a friend, by train**. The person takes **と**; the train takes **で**.
- <span lang="ja">一人で電車で行きます。</span>（ひとりで でんしゃで いきます） — I go **alone, by train**. The first で describes the group of travellers; the second describes transport. Splitting this into <span lang="ja">一人で行きます。電車で行きます。</span> is also clear.
- <span lang="ja">友だちと二人で行きます。</span>（ともだちと ふたりで いきます） — I go **with a friend; there are two of us**. 二人で gives a group size, not a vehicle.

**Common mistakes:** <span lang="ja">電車と行きます</span> does not express travelling by train; choose **電車で**. <span lang="ja">友だちで行きます</span> does not express “with a friend”; choose **友だちと**. **一人と** is not the ordinary way to say “alone”; use **一人で**. Do not add **と** after 一人で.

## 4. Controlled writing: replace one part

Start with <span lang="ja">日曜日、父と車で公園に行きます。</span>（にちようび、ちちと くるまで こうえんに いきます。） — “On Sunday, I go to the park with my father by car.”

Change **only the transport** to train. Model answer: <span lang="ja">日曜日、父と電車で公園に行きます。</span> Keep **父と** because father is still the companion.

Now change **only the companion** to friend. Model answer: <span lang="ja">日曜日、友だちと電車で公園に行きます。</span> Keep **電車で** because the means has not changed.

Finally change the trip to travelling **alone**. One natural answer: <span lang="ja">日曜日、一人で電車で公園に行きます。</span> Or use two short sentences: <span lang="ja">日曜日、一人で公園に行きます。電車で行きます。</span>

## 5. Guided composition: put the clues together

**Clues:** Saturday / library / younger sister / bicycle. Write two or three sentences. Try without looking at the answer, then compare:

> <span lang="ja">土曜日、図書館に行きます。妹と行きます。自転車で行きます。</span>

**Reading:** どようび、としょかんに いきます。いもうとと いきます。じてんしゃで いきます。

**Meaning:** “On Saturday, I go to the library. I go with my younger sister. I go by bicycle.” This describes each person travelling by bicycle in the simple scenario; it does not specify a tandem bicycle. You may choose a different plausible transport method in your independent task.

**Alternative:** For “Sunday / park / alone / on foot,” write <span lang="ja">日曜日、公園に行きます。一人で歩いて行きます。</span>（にちようび、こうえんに いきます。ひとりで あるいて いきます。） — “On Sunday, I go to the park. I walk there alone.”

## 6. Independent writing: your fictional outing

Use the writing area below. Choose **one** of these fictional prompts:

**A.** Saturday / museum / friend / bus.

**B.** Sunday / park / alone / train.

**C.** Invent a destination, companion (or alone), and means of travel.

Write **two to five short connected Japanese sentences**. You may combine information in one sentence or split it across sentences. Use polite **行きます** consistently. ## 7. Compare, revise and self-review

For prompt A, one valid model is:

> <span lang="ja">土曜日、博物館に行きます。友だちと行きます。バスで行きます。</span>

For prompt B:

> <span lang="ja">日曜日、公園に行きます。一人で行きます。電車で行きます。</span>

These are **examples, not the only correct answers**. If you chose C, compare the *function* of each particle rather than copying the nouns. Check your draft against four criteria: (1) a clear destination with **に** or **へ**; (2) a companion with **と**, or **一人で** for alone; (3) a transport method with **で**, or **歩いて** for walking; and (4) two to five connected, understandable sentences with a consistent polite style.

Use the self-review controls to reveal the models and revise. No automatic system can judge the naturalness or correctness of an open composition here. The ten fixed-answer questions separately test recognition of the patterns; they do not grade your writing.

## 8. Review and optional speaking

Read your revised plan aloud slowly, pausing at each sentence. You can also read the model silently. This optional rehearsal uses **text only**: no recording, verified model audio or pronunciation grade is provided in this writing lesson.

Remember the three contrasts: **公園に** (destination), **友だちと** (companion), **電車で** (means). For “alone,” use **一人で**. When you are ready, complete the ten written-pattern checks below and mark the lesson complete separately from your quiz score.
