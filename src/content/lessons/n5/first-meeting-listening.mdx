## Listen before you read

You are helping at a language-exchange welcome desk. Two short conversations introduce people, ask about their occupations and correct an assumption. Your goal is to **understand what the speakers actually say**, not to recognize a written character from its sound.

**First pass:** Play each recording without opening the transcript. Who speaks first? What question is asked? Does the other speaker agree or correct the assumption? Replay as often as needed.

**Second pass:** Answer the three questions for each conversation from the recording. The question itself is not proof of the answer: a speaker asking whether someone is a teacher does not establish that they are one.

### Listening signals

| What you hear | Meaning | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">わたしは エマです。</span> | I'm Emma. | The name follows the topic marker は (pronounced *wa*). |
| <span lang="ja">せんせいですか。</span> | Are you a teacher? | Sentence-final か signals a polite question. |
| <span lang="ja">はい。</span> | Yes. | Listen for the confirming information that follows. |
| <span lang="ja">いいえ。</span> | No. | Listen for the corrected information that follows. |
| <span lang="ja">せんせいじゃありません。</span> | I'm not a teacher. | じゃありません negates a noun predicate politely. |
| <span lang="ja">がくせいです。</span> | I'm a student. | This gives the positive replacement after a correction. |

The country word <span lang="ja">ちゅうごく</span> means China, whereas <span lang="ja">ちゅうごくじん</span> means a Chinese person. A nationality is not an occupation. Likewise, <span lang="ja">にほんごのせんせい</span> means a Japanese-language teacher; の links the subject taught to the teacher.

### Scene A — language exchange

A new participant and a teacher meet. On your first listen, identify the name the first speaker gives, then listen for the occupation in the last reply. The name **Tanaka** in a question is not by itself the answer; listen for <span lang="ja">はい</span> and the following phrase.

### Scene B — registration desk

A staff member asks for a name, then guesses an occupation. The visitor corrects the guess. Pay particular attention to <span lang="ja">いいえ</span> and the contrast between <span lang="ja">せんせい</span> and <span lang="ja">がくせい</span>.

### A three-pass study routine

1. **Gist:** Listen once without reading; decide whether the scene is an introduction or a correction.
2. **Evidence:** Listen again and answer each question. If uncertain, replay the specific turn.
3. **Verification:** Reveal the transcript only after trying to listen. Read the Japanese and English meanings, identify the exact supporting phrase and replay the audio once more.

The transcript button marks your attempt **assisted** even if you later hide it. Unaided and assisted results are saved under different progress IDs. There is no automatic pronunciation grade. If a recording is unavailable, do not treat the transcript as proof of listening achievement.

### Optional speaking bridge

After listening, pause and say one original sentence about yourself, such as <span lang="ja">わたしは がくせいです。</span> (“I'm a student”), only if that is true. If it is not true, replace the role with one you know. This is private, ungraded rehearsal, not an audio recording.

**Review:** Can you hear the difference between the question <span lang="ja">せんせいですか</span> and the negative reply <span lang="ja">せんせいじゃありません</span>? If not, replay the correction scene, then check the transcript. Independent Japanese and pronunciation reviews remain pending.
