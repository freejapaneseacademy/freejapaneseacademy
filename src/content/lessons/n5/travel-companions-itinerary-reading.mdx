## A plan tells you more than a destination

**Can-do:** Explain transport choices and who accompanies you. In this written reading lesson, you will follow a fictional Saturday trip itinerary, identify **how** each person travels and **with whom**, and show the exact Japanese words that support your answer. No audio or pronunciation score is claimed.

Read the Japanese itinerary once without translating every word. On a second pass, look for the three clues **で** (means of transport), **と** (with someone) and **一人で** (alone).

## 1. Before you read

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning in a trip plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">電車で</span> | でんしゃで | by train |
| <span lang="ja">バスで</span> | バスで | by bus |
| <span lang="ja">自転車で</span> | じてんしゃで | by bicycle |
| <span lang="ja">友達と</span> | ともだちと | with a friend |
| <span lang="ja">母と</span> | ははと | with my mother (talking about one's own mother) |
| <span lang="ja">一人で</span> | ひとりで | alone / by oneself |
| <span lang="ja">いっしょに</span> | いっしょに | together |
| <span lang="ja">帰ります</span> | かえります | return home |

A vehicle plus **で** identifies the means: <span lang="ja">電車で行きます</span> = “I go by train.” A person plus **と** identifies a companion: <span lang="ja">友達と行きます</span> = “I go with a friend.” <span lang="ja">一人で</span> means “alone,” **not** “by one vehicle.” A destination can be marked by **へ** or **に**.

## 2. Original itinerary: Saturday at the museum

This itinerary is fictional. The speaker is Yui, who is planning a Saturday outing.

> ### 土曜日の予定（どようびの よてい）— Saturday's itinerary
>
> <span lang="ja">午前九時に、ゆいは一人で家を出ます。</span>  
> ごぜん くじに、ゆいは ひとりで いえを でます。
>
> <span lang="ja">駅までは自転車で行きます。駅で友達のあきと会います。</span>  
> えきまでは じてんしゃで いきます。えきで ともだちの あきと あいます。

> <span lang="ja">九時半に、ゆいはあきと電車で町の博物館へ行きます。</span>

> しょうごに、ふたりは ちかくの カフェで ひるごはんを たべます。
>
> <span lang="ja">午後一時に、あきはバスで家に帰ります。</span>  
> ごご いちじに、あきは バスで いえに かえります。
>
> <span lang="ja">ゆいは一人で本屋へ行きます。本屋から駅までは歩いて行きます。</span>

>
> <span lang="ja">午後三時に、ゆいは自転車で家に帰ります。</span>

### English meaning — check after your first read

At 9 a.m., Yui leaves home alone. She cycles to the station and meets her friend Aki there. At 9:30, Yui goes with Aki by train to the town museum. They visit the museum from 10 to 11:30. At noon they eat lunch at a nearby café. At 1 p.m., Aki returns home by bus. Yui goes alone to a bookstore, walks from the bookstore to the station, and cycles home at 3 p.m.

## 3. Read the itinerary as evidence

| Time / stage | Evidence from Japanese | What we can say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 9:00 a.m. | <span lang="ja">一人で家を出ます</span> | Yui leaves alone |
| Before 9:30 | <span lang="ja">駅までは自転車で行きます</span> | Yui cycles to the station |
| At the station | <span lang="ja">友達のあきと会います</span> | She meets Aki |
| 9:30 a.m. | <span lang="ja">あきと電車で…博物館へ行きます</span> | Yui and Aki take the train |
| 10:00–11:30 | <span lang="ja">二人で博物館を見ます</span> | Both visit the museum |
| Noon | <span lang="ja">カフェで昼ご飯を食べます</span> | Lunch happens at a café |
| 1:00 p.m. | <span lang="ja">あきはバスで家に帰ります</span> | Aki returns by bus |
| Later | <span lang="ja">ゆいは一人で本屋へ行きます</span> | Yui goes to the bookstore alone |
| 3:00 p.m. | <span lang="ja">自転車で家に帰ります</span> | Yui cycles home |

Notice that the text **does not say** what Aki does after returning home. It also does not specify a train fare or the name of the café. A reading answer must not add those details.

## 4. Three small words, different jobs

**Transport で:** <span lang="ja">あきと電車で町の博物館へ行きます。</span> — “(Yui) goes with Aki to the town museum by train.” Here **電車で** names the vehicle and **あきと** names the companion. Both can appear in one sentence.

**Companion と:** <span lang="ja">友達のあきと会います。</span> — “(She) meets her friend Aki.” In this phrase, **と** also marks the person she meets; do not assume every **と** in a text means they travel together. The later phrase <span lang="ja">あきと電車で…行きます</span> confirms their shared train journey.

**Alone 一人で:** <span lang="ja">ゆいは一人で本屋へ行きます。</span> — “Yui goes to the bookstore alone.” The itinerary explicitly separates Aki's return from Yui's later solo visit.

**Location で:** <span lang="ja">カフェで昼ご飯を食べます。</span> — “They eat lunch at a café.” This **で** marks where the action happens, not a means of transport. Ask whether the word before **で** is a vehicle, a location, or a phrase such as **一人で**.

## 5. Guided rereading

Cover the English translation. Find and copy the Japanese evidence for each answer.

1. Who leaves home alone at 9 a.m.? Find <span lang="ja">ゆいは一人で家を出ます</span>.
2. How does Yui reach the station? Find <span lang="ja">自転車で行きます</span>.
3. Who goes with Yui to the museum? Find <span lang="ja">あきと電車で…行きます</span>.
4. How does Aki go home? Find <span lang="ja">あきはバスで家に帰ります</span>.
5. What does Yui do without Aki? Find <span lang="ja">一人で本屋へ行きます</span>.

A useful reading strategy is to draw three columns: **person / transport / companion**. Do not confuse a person met at a location with a person accompanying the whole journey.

## 6. Transfer to your own itinerary

Write three short Japanese sentences for a fictional outing: where you go, what vehicle you use and whether you go with someone or alone. A model:

<span lang="ja">日曜日に友達と公園へ行きます。バスで行きます。午後四時に一人で家に帰ります。</span>  
にちようびに ともだちと こうえんへ いきます。バスで いきます。ごご よじに ひとりで いえに かえります。  
“On Sunday I go to the park with a friend. I go by bus. At 4 p.m. I return home alone.”

Check the destination particle **へ/に**, vehicle **で**, companion **と**, and whether **一人で** fits your intended meaning. Different natural answers are possible. This open writing is **not automatically graded**.
