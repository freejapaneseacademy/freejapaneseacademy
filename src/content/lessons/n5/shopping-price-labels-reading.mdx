## Reading mission: a small stationery shop

You are helping a fictional student, ゆい, choose supplies for school. The signs below are original practice material, not real store offers. Read the Japanese signs first; then use the English support to check. Prices are in Japanese yen (円・えん).

### Sign A — notebooks / ノート

> 青（あお）い ノート　**一冊（いっさつ）150円（ひゃくごじゅうえん）**  
> 赤（あか）い ノート　**一冊 180円（ひゃくはちじゅうえん）**

**English support:** Blue notebook: ¥150 per notebook. Red notebook: ¥180 per notebook. **一冊** counts one bound book or notebook; it is not a generic counter for every object.

### Sign B — bags / かばん

> 小（ちい）さい かばん　**900円（きゅうひゃくえん）**  
> 大（おお）きい かばん　**1,400円（せんよんひゃくえん）**

**English support:** Small bag: ¥900. Large bag: ¥1,400. The price tags describe the size of each bag; they do not state its color.

### Sign C — paper / かみ

> 白（しろ）い かみ　**一枚（いちまい）20円（にじゅうえん）**  
> 五枚（ごまい）で **100円（ひゃくえん）**

**English support:** White paper: ¥20 per sheet. Five sheets cost ¥100. **枚（まい）** counts thin, flat objects such as sheets of paper.

### Sign D — pencils / えんぴつ

> えんぴつ　**一本（いっぽん）80円（はちじゅうえん）**  
> 三本（さんぼん）で **240円（にひゃくよんじゅうえん）**

**English support:** Pencil: ¥80 each. Three pencils cost ¥240. **本（ほん）** counts long, thin objects; its pronunciation changes in 一本 and 三本.

## Step 1 — Read a label in three passes

First locate the **object** (ノート, かばん, かみ, えんぴつ). Next identify the **descriptor** (青い, 赤い, 小さい, 大きい, 白い). Finally find the **quantity and price**. For example, 「青い ノート　一冊150円」 supports “one blue notebook costs ¥150.” It does **not** say that two notebooks cost ¥150.

## Step 2 — A unit price is not always the total

「一枚20円」 means one sheet is ¥20; 「五枚で100円」 gives the total for five sheets. The particle **で** here marks the stated quantity for that price. Likewise 「三本で240円」 is the price for three pencils, not one. When a question asks “How many?”, look for 枚・冊・本 rather than guessing from the price.

## Step 3 — Distinguish stated facts from assumptions

**Worked example:** How much is the large bag? **Answer:** ¥1,400. **Evidence:** 「大きい かばん　1,400円」.

**Another example:** What color is the small bag? **Answer:** Not stated. **Evidence check:** 「小さい かばん　900円」 gives size and price but no color. Do not invent a color from the other signs.

## Useful shopping language

- **これは いくらですか。** — How much is this? A polite question.
- **青い ノートを 一冊 ください。** — One blue notebook, please.
- **白い かみを 五枚 お願（ねが）いします。** — Five sheets of white paper, please.

**ください** and **お願いします** are useful polite requests in this setting. **を** marks the requested object; it does not mark the price. Compare 「青いノートを一冊ください」 with the incorrect 「青いノートが一冊ください」 for this request pattern.

## Guided practice

Cover the English support. Write a two-column list for the four signs: **Japanese evidence** and **English fact**. For example, write 「赤い ノート　一冊180円」 → “one red notebook costs ¥180.” Then find the phrases for five sheets and three pencils. If a detail is absent, write **not stated** rather than infer it.

## Independent reading

Complete the ten questions below. For each answer, point to the **exact Japanese phrase** in a sign. Some options intentionally confuse a unit price with a multi-item total; feedback explains the difference.

Afterward, optionally write a two-sentence shopping request about items on the signs. Compare with the model in the practice panel. Your composition is self-reviewed, not automatically graded. This is a reading lesson; there is no recorded listening test or pronunciation assessment.

**Takeaway:** Read object + description + counter + price together, and cite the sign instead of guessing.
