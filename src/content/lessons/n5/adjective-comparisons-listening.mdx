## Mission: understand a comparison without reading it first

**Can-do:** Describe food, places and objects with adjectives. This listening lesson uses two fictional conversations: people comparing cafés and a shopper deciding between bags. Play both recordings before revealing the transcripts. Reading the transcript is valuable assisted practice, but does **not** count as unaided listening.

### Prepare your ears

Listen for the contrasting word **でも** (but), which often signals a different evaluation. An adjective before **です** can describe a noun as in **静かです** (it is quiet). The negative **おいしくないです** means “it is not tasty”; **あまりおいしくないです** means “it is not very tasty.” Be careful not to hear the first positive statement as applying to every object.

| Listening cue | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 静か | しずか | quiet (な-adjective) |
| にぎやか | にぎやか | lively (な-adjective) |
| おいしい | おいしい | tasty (い-adjective) |
| 大きい | おおきい | big (い-adjective) |
| 大きくない | おおきくない | not big |
| 安くて便利 | やすくてべんり | inexpensive and convenient |
| 〜にします | 〜にします | I'll choose / take … |

## Track A — Which café?

Two people are sitting in a café. They compare its atmosphere with another café near a station and discuss coffee and cake.

**First pass:** Without looking at a transcript, decide which café is quiet. **Second pass:** Write down two separate evaluations: coffee / cake. Do not assume both are praised. The word **あまり** together with a negative matters.

**Guided retrieval:** On paper, complete: “The café here is ___; the café near the station is ___.” Then listen again to check. Use the transcript toggle only after an unaided attempt.

## Track B — Which bag?

A shopper considers a red bag and a blue bag. Listen for the contrast between size, price and usefulness. The adjective **便利（べんり）** means convenient or useful. The connecting form **安くて（やすくて）** links “inexpensive” to the next description; it is not a negative.

**First pass:** Identify the color of the big bag. **Second pass:** Identify the qualities of the blue bag and the final decision. A bag mentioned first is not necessarily the one purchased.

## How to verify evidence

After answering the six listening questions, reveal each Japanese transcript and its English translation. Compare your answer with the exact Japanese phrase in the feedback. Replay that segment. You may change playback speed, but speed adjustments do not replace understanding the spoken words.

A useful contrast from the preparatory vocabulary is **大きいです** (is big) versus **大きくないです** (is not big). For な-adjectives, the negative pattern is different: **静かじゃありません** (is not quiet). Do not attach **くない** directly to **静か**.

## Transfer to your own descriptions

Write or type two short sentences about imaginary places or objects. For example: **この店は静かです。あの店はにぎやかです。** (“This shop is quiet. That shop is lively.”) Or: **このかばんは大きくないです。でも、便利です。** (“This bag isn't big. But it is convenient.”) Read them aloud if you wish. This is **ungraded** writing and optional speaking, not a pronunciation test.

**Self-check:** Can you match the right adjective to the right object, recognize a negative, and follow a final choice? If not, replay the relevant track and review its transcript before moving on. The six-question check is formative, not an official JLPT score.
