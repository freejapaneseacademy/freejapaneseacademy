## Two cards at a community-class welcome desk

**Chapter Can-do:** Write and understand a short self-introduction. In this first lesson, your job is to *understand* two short profiles before writing your own in L07.

A volunteer at a Japanese community class asks two fictional newcomers to write a short introduction for their classmates. The cards are read aloud only by learners themselves here; **this is a written context lesson, not a recorded listening test**.

### Card A — さき

> はじめまして。さきです。二十歳（はたち）です。出身（しゅっしん）は大阪（おおさか）です。大学生（だいがくせい）です。よろしくお願いします。

**Meaning:** Nice to meet you. I'm Saki. I'm 20 years old. I'm from Osaka. I'm a university student. I look forward to getting to know you.

### Card B — レオ

> はじめまして。レオです。二十三歳（にじゅうさんさい）です。出身（しゅっしん）はカナダのトロントです。会社員（かいしゃいん）です。よろしくお願いします。

**Meaning:** Nice to meet you. I'm Leo. I'm 23 years old. I'm from Toronto, Canada. I work for a company. I look forward to getting to know you.

Both introductions use polite nominal sentences. They are *fictional learning examples*, not real people's biographical data.

## What can we learn from a profile?

| Detail | さき | レオ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Name | さき | レオ |
| Age | 二十歳 (20) | 二十三歳 (23) |
| Hometown / place of origin | 大阪 | カナダのトロント |
| Study / work | 大学生 (university student) | 会社員 (company employee) |

**出身（しゅっしん）** means one's place of origin; it is not necessarily where someone currently lives. **大学生** describes a student at a university. **会社員** describes an employee of a company; it does not identify a specific occupation.

**二十歳** has the special reading **はたち**. For 23 years old, **二十三歳** is **にじゅうさんさい**. Do not assume all ages use the same pronunciation pattern.

## How do the sentences work?

**さきです。** — “I'm Saki.” The topic “I” is understood from the introduction context. A full version is **私はさきです。** (*わたしはさきです*). Here **は** is written *ha* but pronounced *wa* as a particle.

**出身は大阪です。** — “My place of origin is Osaka.” The topic is **出身**; **です** gives the polite nominal ending. It does not say Saki lives in Osaka now.

**カナダのトロント** — “Toronto in Canada.” **の** connects the country and city in this place expression. It does not mean Canada *owns* Toronto.

**大学生です。** and **会社員です。** — These are descriptions of a role, not actions. The learner should not translate them as “studying now” or “working right now.”

**はじめまして** begins a first meeting. **よろしくお願いします** is a conventional polite closing; its exact English translation depends on context. The English meaning above is natural in a class introduction, not a word-for-word equation.

## Controlled practice: find evidence

Read **出身は大阪です**. Is Osaka Saki's current address? **No**: only her place of origin is stated.

Read **会社員です**. Does Leo's card say what company employs him? **No**: it only states his general work status.

Read **二十三歳です**. Who is 23? **Leo**: the age appears on his card, not Saki's.

## Guided practice: compare without guessing

Cover the English translations. Find the Japanese phrase supporting each statement:

1. Saki is a university student — **大学生です**.
2. Leo comes from Toronto — **出身はカナダのトロントです**.
3. Both are introducing themselves for the first time — **はじめまして**.
4. The profiles do not say where either person lives now — no current-residence statement is present.

Notice that an *unstated* fact cannot be inferred from a name, language, nationality or hometown. For example, the card does not say Leo's nationality.

## Independent transfer

Write a fictional two-line profile for **ミナ**, a university student from **京都（きょうと）**:

**はじめまして。ミナです。出身は京都です。大学生です。よろしくお願いします。**

Now change the fictional name and city while preserving the polite pattern. You can write about an invented person; no personal information is required. Check that each sentence communicates one clear fact and that you did not accidentally claim a current residence.
