## Your listening mission

**Can-do:** Name familiar foods and express simple preferences. In this listening lesson, hear how those expressions work in real situations. You will listen to **two different fictional café conversations**. The first involves a customer and a server ordering lunch. The second involves two friends deciding on a snack.

**First listen without a transcript.** Your task is to understand what people request, decline or choose, not to identify how words are written. Each scene has three questions that can be answered from the spoken words alone.

## Listening strategy: follow the speaker's decision

1. **Find the role.** In scene A, one voice welcomes a customer and asks for an order. In scene B, friends ask each other about preferences.
2. **Listen for food and drink words.** Familiar words include カレー (curry), お茶（おちゃ, tea）, サラダ (salad), ケーキ (cake), アイスクリーム (ice cream), コーヒー (coffee) and 水（みず, water). These are study aids; they are **not** the transcripts.
3. **Catch the decision word.** お願いします (please / I'd like), ください (please give me), 食べます (eat), 飲みます (drink), and 食べません (do not eat) carry the actual choice. A mentioned food is not always an ordered food.
4. **Replay at a comfortable speed.** Pause after a speaker turn and say in English what that person decided. Don't infer a purchase from a question alone.

## Scene A: Ordering lunch

A café worker asks for an order. Listen for **one main dish, one drink and one item the customer declines**. The server's offer of another food is not itself a customer order.

After the first play, write three brief notes in English, then replay and complete the three questions below. For example, distinguish **サラダも食べますか** (“Will you eat salad too?”) from **サラダは食べません** (“I won't eat salad”). A question about an item and a refusal are different acts.

## Scene B: Choosing a snack

Two friends discuss cake and decide what they will actually have. The question **好きですか** (“Do you like it?”) does not mean the person will necessarily order that item. Listen for the contrast signaled by **でも** (“but”) and the present decision **今日は…食べます** (“today I'll eat…”).

Make separate notes for the man and woman. Do not transfer one speaker's drink choice to the other.

## After you answer: reveal, check and learn

The player below offers **transcript reveal** for accessibility and learning. Revealing a transcript before submitting switches the attempt to **assisted practice**; that attempt is not recorded as unaided listening mastery. The transcript includes speaker-by-speaker Japanese and English translations.

Compare the recorded lines with your notes and locate the exact spoken evidence for each answer. If you missed a negative verb, listen again for the ending **ません**. If you mixed up two speakers, follow the alternating voices.

### Useful contrast

| Heard phrase | Meaning | Listening implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ケーキが好きですか。 | Do you like cake? | A question about preference, not an order. |
| アイスクリームを食べます。 | I will eat ice cream. | An actual food choice. |
| コーヒーにします。 | I'll choose coffee. | The speaker settles on coffee. |
| 水を飲みます。 | I drink / will drink water. | A drink decision in context. |

## Independent transfer

Without looking at the transcript, replay both recordings and summarize the decisions in **four short English notes**: one dish and one drink from scene A, plus each friend's drink from scene B. Then reveal the transcripts to verify the notes. For optional speaking, shadow one short turn after replaying it; do not record unless you choose to use your own recording tool. No automated pronunciation grade is given.

**Self-check:** Can you distinguish an offer from an order, a preference from today's choice, and the two speakers' different drinks? Try again if any of those remain uncertain. The six-question assessment below is formative and **not an official JLPT score**.
