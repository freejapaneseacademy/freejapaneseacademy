## An afternoon workshop: what is happening?

At a fictional community workshop, two classmates look across the room. Their conversation answers the question **「今、何をしていますか。」** (“What are you doing now?”). Read the exchange first for meaning; then notice how each verb changes.

> あき：今（いま）、何（なに）をしていますか。  
> ゆい：絵（え）を描（か）いています。  
> あき：たけしさんは何をしていますか。  
> ゆい：机（つくえ）で本（ほん）を読（よ）んでいます。  
> あき：先生（せんせい）は？  
> ゆい：先生は教室（きょうしつ）で日本語（にほんご）を教（おし）えています。

**English:** Aki: What are you doing now? Yui: I'm drawing a picture. Aki: What is Takeshi doing? Yui: He's reading a book at a desk. Aki: And the teacher? Yui: The teacher is teaching Japanese in the classroom.

This is an invented learning scene, not a report about real people. The question **何をしていますか** is neutral and polite. A topic can be omitted when the speaker is clear.

## 1. Form the progressive: verb て-form + います

The polite **〜ています** pattern describes an action in progress in these examples. Start with a familiar dictionary-form verb, make its **て-form**, and attach **います** without adding another particle.

| Dictionary form | て-form | Progressive | Meaning in this scene |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 読む（よむ） | 読んで | 読んでいます | is reading |
| 書く（かく） | 書いて | 書いています | is writing |
| 描く（かく） | 描いて | 描いています | is drawing |
| 飲む（のむ） | 飲んで | 飲んでいます | is drinking |
| 食べる（たべる） | 食べて | 食べています | is eating |
| 見る（みる） | 見て | 見ています | is watching / looking |
| する | して | しています | is doing |
| 来る（くる） | 来て（きて） | 来ています（きています） | has come / is here (context-dependent) |

**Form reminders:** 読む and 飲む change **む → んで**; 書く and 描く change **く → いて**. 食べる and 見る are Group 2 verbs here: remove **る** and add **て**. The irregular verb **する → して**. The verb **行く → 行って** is a common exception to the ordinary く pattern. Do not write 読みています or 書きています.

### て-form versus complete sentence

**本を読んで** is a て-form fragment; it may connect to something else or serve another conversational function. For a polite statement of an ongoing action, use **本を読んでいます。** (“[Someone] is reading a book.”)

**今、何をしていますか。** (“What are you doing now?”)  
**今、宿題（しゅくだい）をしています。** (“I'm doing homework now.”)

The question uses **しています** because it asks about the activity itself; you can also specify a person: **田中（たなか）さんは何をしていますか。**

## 2. Put person, place and object in the right roles

A useful template is **person は + place で + object を + て-form + います**. The location and object are included only when they are relevant.

> ゆいさんは教室で日本語を書いています。  
> Yui is writing Japanese in the classroom.

**教室で** names where the action happens; **日本語を** names what is being written. The action-place particle is **で**, not **に**. For example, **公園（こうえん）で走（はし）っています** (“is running in the park”). An action with no direct object, such as 走る, does not require **を**.

**Guided substitution:** Change **本を読んでいます** to **新聞（しんぶん）を読んでいます** (“is reading a newspaper”). Then change the place: **図書室（としょしつ）で新聞を読んでいます。** The て-form stays the same.

## 3. Does 〜ています always mean “is -ing”?

No. **今、絵を描いています** clearly describes an action happening now. Compare it with **毎朝（まいあさ）、絵を描きます** (“I draw every morning”), which uses a simple polite verb and a habitual time expression. A habitual action can sometimes also be expressed with 〜ています in Japanese; the time words and context decide the interpretation.

Another important ambiguity: **田中さんは結婚（けっこん）しています** ordinarily means “Tanaka is married,” a **continuing state**, not “Tanaka is getting married at this moment.” Likewise, **知（し）っています** usually means “know.” These are recognition notes; you do not need to master every use yet. Avoid the rule “〜ています always equals English -ing.”

## 4. Compare two mini-scenes

**Scene A — happening now**

> 今、まいさんは台所（だいどころ）でお茶（ちゃ）を飲んでいます。  
> けんさんは部屋（へや）で音楽（おんがく）を聞（き）いています。

**English:** Mai is drinking tea in the kitchen now. Ken is listening to music in the room.

**Scene B — a regular routine**

> まいさんは毎日（まいにち）、朝（あさ）にお茶を飲みます。  
> けんさんは毎晩（まいばん）、音楽を聞きます。

**English:** Mai drinks tea every morning. Ken listens to music every evening.

The adverb **今** supports a current-action reading in A. **毎日・毎晩** support repeated habits in B. Do not decide solely from the English translation.

## 5. Practise, then produce your own exchange

**Controlled practice:** Complete the progressive form before revealing the answers.

1. 読む → 読んで → **読んでいます**
2. 食べる → 食べて → **食べています**
3. 書く → 書いて → **書いています**
4. する → して → **しています**

**Guided exchange:** Imagine a fictional library. Ask **「今、何をしていますか。」** and answer **「図書室で本を読んでいます。」** Switch the verb to **勉強（べんきょう）しています** (“am studying”) and the location to **教室で**.

**Independent writing (ungraded):** Write two to four short lines about fictional classmates. Include a question, a progressive answer, and one location marked by **で**. If useful, contrast a daily routine using **毎日**. Compare your work with the model:

> A：今、何をしていますか。  
> B：教室で日本語を勉強しています。  
> A：みきさんは？  
> B：みきさんは図書室で本を読んでいます。

**English:** A: What are you doing now? B: I'm studying Japanese in the classroom. A: And Miki? B: Miki is reading a book in the library room.

**Self-check:** (1) Is the て-form correct? (2) Does **います** follow it? (3) Does **で** mark the action location? (4) Is **を** used for an object when needed? (5) Is the time meaning clear? Natural alternatives are welcome. This writing is not automatically graded. No recording or audio is required for this grammar lesson.
