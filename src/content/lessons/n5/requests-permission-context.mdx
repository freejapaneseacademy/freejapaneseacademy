## A first visit to the community center

**Can-do preview:** Ask someone to do something, ask whether you may do something, and understand a simple rule. Today you will **recognize the purpose** of these expressions; L03 will develop the grammar patterns in detail.

Imagine visiting a fictional community center for a Japanese class. You have a question for the receptionist, the teacher needs the class to settle, and a sign explains where food is not allowed. The same verb can express three very different social actions depending on its ending.

### Step 1 — Who wants whom to act?

**Receptionist:** <ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いてください。  
*Please write your name.*

**Visitor:** ここに<ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>ってもいいですか。  
*May I sit here?*

**Receptionist:** はい、いいですよ。  
*Yes, that's fine.*

**Teacher:** ここで<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてはいけません。  
*You must not eat here.*

The receptionist's first line **requests** an action from the visitor. The visitor's question **asks permission** for their own action. The teacher's statement **prohibits** an action in this location. Do not translate all three as “please.”

### Step 2 — Notice the three endings

| Expression | Reading | Communicative job | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 〜てください | てください | Ask another person to do something politely | <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ってください。 *Please wait.* |
| 〜てもいいですか | てもいいですか | Ask whether an action is permitted | <ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ってもいいですか。 *May I enter?* |
| 〜てはいけません | てはいけません | State that an action is not allowed | <ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>ってはいけません。 *You must not run.* |

All three attach to a **て-form**, which you met in the previous chapter. In **読んでください** (*please read*), the connector ends in **で**; **て-form** is the name of the pattern, not a promise that every form literally ends in て.

**Important nuance:** 〜てください is a polite request, but it can still sound directive. With strangers, context and tone matter. **〜てはいけません** is a firm rule or prohibition, not a soft personal preference. Asking permission with **〜てもいいですか** does not itself mean permission has been granted.

### Step 3 — Read the situation before choosing the ending

The fictional center has three locations.

**At the entrance:** ドアを<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けてください。  
*Please open the door.* The speaker wants **someone else** to open it.

**In the study room:** <ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>撮<rt>と</rt></ruby>ってもいいですか。  
*May I take a photo?* The speaker is asking about **their own** action.

**In the reading room:** ここで<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>ってはいけません。  
*You must not use a phone here.* This states a **rule**, not a request to use a phone.

Compare the responses:

- **はい、いいですよ。** *Yes, that's fine.* (informal-friendly permission)
- **すみません、ここではだめです。** *Sorry, not here.* (a simple refusal)
- **わかりました。** *Understood.* (acknowledging a request or rule)

The response depends on what was asked. **はい、いいですよ** naturally answers a permission question; it is not the ordinary answer to every 〜てください request.

### Step 4 — Build meaning from known verbs

| Dictionary form | て-form | Sample use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 書く（かく） write | 書いて（かいて） | 名前を書いてください。 *Please write your name.* |
| 読む（よむ） read | 読んで（よんで） | これを読んでください。 *Please read this.* |
| 待つ（まつ） wait | 待って（まって） | ここで待ってください。 *Please wait here.* |
| 座る（すわる） sit | 座って（すわって） | 座ってもいいですか。 *May I sit?* |
| 食べる（たべる） eat | 食べて（たべて） | 食べてはいけません。 *You must not eat.* |
| 入る（はいる） enter | 入って（はいって） | 入ってもいいですか。 *May I enter?* |

**Reading tip:** ここで means *here / at this place* for an action; it is different from the **で** ending of **読んで**. Watch the whole phrase rather than interpreting each character alone.

### Step 5 — Controlled noticing

For each line, identify **request**, **permission question**, or **prohibition** before revealing the explanation.

1. **本を読んでください。** *Please read the book.* **Request:** another person should read.
2. **水を飲んでもいいですか。** *May I drink water?* **Permission question:** the speaker wants to drink.
3. **ここで走ってはいけません。** *You must not run here.* **Prohibition:** running is disallowed.
4. **ちょっと待ってください。** *Please wait a moment.* **Request:** someone else should wait.

**Common mistake:** **座ってください** (*please sit*) is **not** the same as **座ってもいいですか** (*may I sit?*). One asks another person to act; the other asks whether the speaker may act.

### Step 6 — Guided role choice

Imagine you are the visitor. You want to take a picture of the class notice. Choose **写真を撮ってもいいですか** rather than **写真を撮ってください**: the first asks whether **you** may take the picture; the second asks someone else to take it.

Now imagine you are a volunteer telling a guest to wait. Choose **ちょっと待ってください** rather than **ちょっと待ってはいけません**. The latter would prohibit waiting, the opposite of your intention.

### Step 7 — Independent mini-dialogue

Write or say three lines for a fictional classroom: (1) ask permission to sit, (2) answer with permission, and (3) ask someone to write their name. Kana is acceptable.

**Model for self-comparison:**

A: ここに座ってもいいですか。 — *May I sit here?*  
B: はい、いいですよ。 — *Yes, that's fine.*  
B: 名前を書いてください。 — *Please write your name.*

Check that the first line ends in **もいいですか**, that the answer actually grants permission, and that the final line ends in **ください**. This open-ended production is **ungraded**.
