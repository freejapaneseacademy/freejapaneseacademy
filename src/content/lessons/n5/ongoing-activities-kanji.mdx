## A snapshot of a Saturday neighborhood fair

A volunteer is preparing captions for a fictional community photo display. The photographs are not supplied: the following **original captions** describe the scenes. Your job is to read the **kanji inside whole words**, locate the people and places, and explain what is happening *now*. This is written recognition practice, not a test of pronunciation or listening.

**Can-do:** You can read short captions such as **学生が本を読んでいます** and explain who is doing what.

### First look: four caption cards

> **A — 学校（がっこう）の図書室（としょしつ）**  
> 学生（がくせい）が本（ほん）を読（よ）んでいます。  
> *A student is reading a book in the school library.*

> **B — 小（ちい）さな店（みせ）**  
> 店（みせ）の人（ひと）がパンを売（う）っています。  
> *A person working at the shop is selling bread.*

> **C — 公園（こうえん）**  
> 子（こ）どもがパンを食（た）べています。  
> *A child is eating bread in the park.*

> **D — 学校（がっこう）の前（まえ）**  
> 先生（せんせい）が地図（ちず）を見（み）ています。  
> *A teacher is looking at a map in front of the school.*

The location headings supply the setting. The sentence itself tells you the participant and action. Do not assume that the location is always repeated with **で** inside the caption sentence.

## Six target kanji in words

| Character | Word and reading | Meaning | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 人 | 人（ひと） | person | 店の人 |
| 本 | 本（ほん） | book | 本を読んでいます |
| 校 | 学校（がっこう） | school | 学校の前 |
| 店 | 店（みせ） | shop | 小さな店 |
| 食 | 食べる（たべる） | to eat | 食べています |
| 見 | 見る（みる） | to look / see | 見ています |

**Reading is word-specific.** In **学校（がっこう）**, 校 is read **こう**; the small **っ** is part of the whole word's pronunciation. In **店（みせ）**, 店 is **みせ**, but **店員（てんいん）** “shop employee” uses **てん**. **人（ひと）** means a person, while **三人（さんにん）** means three people; **にん** is another common reading. Learn the actual word rather than guessing a single reading for each character.

**Shape and usage note:** **本** has a short stroke near the base of **木** (“tree”); that is a useful visual distinction, not a guarantee about meaning in unfamiliar compounds. **見** is not **貝** (“shellfish”), despite a similar top shape. When unsure, compare the complete word and the sentence.

## From the word to the action

The ongoing-action pattern is **verb て-form + います**. The captions refer to an activity in progress at the moment shown.

- **本を読んでいます** (*ほんを よんでいます*) — is reading a book. **本** is the object marked by **を**.
- **パンを食べています** (*パンを たべています*) — is eating bread. **食べる → 食べて → 食べています**.
- **地図を見ています** (*ちずを みています*) — is looking at a map. **見る → 見て → 見ています**.
- **パンを売っています** (*パンを うっています*) — is selling bread. **売る → 売って → 売っています**.

**A useful distinction:** **本を読みます** may describe a habit or a future action in context. **本を読んでいます** in these snapshot captions means the reading is underway. The broader **〜ています** construction can also describe resulting states (for example, **結婚しています**), but this chapter focuses on visible ongoing activities. Do not translate every instance mechanically as “is -ing” without context.

### Location: の versus で

**学校の前** (*がっこうの まえ*) means “in front of the school”; **の** links the school to the position. **学校で本を読んでいます** (*がっこうで ほんを よんでいます*) means “is reading a book at school”; **で** marks the place where the action happens. Compare **店の人** (“a person at / from the shop”) with **店で働いています** (“is working at the shop”). A location phrase does not itself identify the actor.

### Short dialogue at the photo display

> A：この人（ひと）は何（なに）をしていますか。  
> B：本（ほん）を読（よ）んでいます。  
> A：どこですか。  
> B：学校（がっこう）の図書室（としょしつ）です。

**English:** A: “What is this person doing?” B: “They are reading a book.” A: “Where is it?” B: “In the school library.”

**この人** is a neutral way to point out the person in a photograph. The answer can omit a repeated subject when the context makes it clear.

## Guided reading

1. In card A, underline **本**. Read **ほん** and find the action **読んでいます**.
2. In card B, circle **店** twice. Explain why **店の人** names a person rather than the building performing the action.
3. In card C, locate **食**. Say the whole verb **食べています**, not just the character's reading.
4. In card D, distinguish **見ています** (“is looking”) from **見ます** (“looks / will look,” depending on context).
5. Cover the English captions. For each card, state *who*, *what action*, and *where the heading places the scene*.

## Independent caption writing

Imagine two more fictional snapshots: (1) a student reading at school and (2) a child eating at a shop. Write two Japanese captions, using the models below only **after** trying yourself.

- **学生が学校で本を読んでいます。** (*がくせいが がっこうで ほんを よんでいます*) — A student is reading a book at school.
- **子どもが店でパンを食べています。** (*こどもが みせで パンを たべています*) — A child is eating bread at a shop.

**Self-review, not graded:** Did you choose the intended kanji word? Is **で** attached to the action location? Does **が** identify the actor? Does the verb end in **〜ています**? Can another reader tell who is doing what? Rewrite one caption without looking at the model.
