## Read the room, not just the characters

**Can-do:** Give a basic description of a room. Imagine a new student reading a short handwritten guide to a study room. The guide tells them where books, furniture, and a teacher are. This lesson practices **reading words in context**, not memorizing every possible kanji reading. No recording is supplied or assessed.

## 1. Six location words

| Word | Kana reading | Meaning in this lesson | A useful phrase |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">上</span> | うえ | on / above | <span lang="ja">机の上（つくえのうえ）</span> — on the desk |
| <span lang="ja">下</span> | した | below / under | <span lang="ja">机の下（つくえのした）</span> — under the desk |
| <span lang="ja">前</span> | まえ | in front of | <span lang="ja">入口の前（いりぐちのまえ）</span> — in front of the entrance |
| <span lang="ja">後ろ</span> | うしろ | behind | <span lang="ja">いすの後ろ（いすのうしろ）</span> — behind the chair |
| <span lang="ja">中</span> | なか | inside | <span lang="ja">かばんの中（かばんのなか）</span> — inside the bag |
| <span lang="ja">隣</span> | となり | beside / next to | <span lang="ja">机の隣（つくえのとなり）</span> — next to the desk |

**Reading trap:** <span lang="ja">後ろ</span> is read **うしろ**, not *あとろ*. The single character <span lang="ja">後</span> can have other readings in other words; do not transfer one reading mechanically. <span lang="ja">上</span> may mean *above* without touching; use the room context to decide whether English *on* is appropriate.

## 2. Read three common room labels

- <span lang="ja">机（つくえ）</span> — desk. <span lang="ja">机の下</span> is *under the desk*, not *inside the desk*.
- <span lang="ja">本（ほん）</span> — book. <span lang="ja">本が あります。</span> says a book is present.
- <span lang="ja">入口（いりぐち）</span> — entrance. <span lang="ja">入口の前</span> is the area in front of the entrance.

**Word-level recognition matters:** <span lang="ja">入口</span> is one word, not an instruction to read the two characters separately with arbitrary readings. This lesson does not require writing these kanji from memory; copying and recognizing them in familiar words is enough.

## 3. Read an original room guide

The following is a **fictional** note on the door of a study room. Read once with the kana hints, then cover the English.

> <span lang="ja">ここは べんきょうの へやです。入口の前（いりぐちのまえ）に 先生（せんせい）が います。机の上（つくえのうえ）に 本（ほん）が あります。机の下（つくえのした）に かばんが あります。かばんの中（なか）に ノートが あります。机の隣（となり）に いすが あります。いすの後ろ（うしろ）に ねこが います。</span>

*This is a study room. A teacher is in front of the entrance. A book is on the desk. A bag is under the desk. A notebook is inside the bag. A chair is beside the desk. A cat is behind the chair.*

**Evidence practice:** Which phrase locates the notebook? <span lang="ja">かばんの中に ノートが あります。</span> Which phrase locates the cat? <span lang="ja">いすの後ろに ねこが います。</span> Do not infer that the cat is beside the desk merely because the chair is.

## 4. How the kanji phrases work in sentences

The reusable pattern is **reference noun + の + position + に + located item + が + existence verb**.

- <span lang="ja">机の上に 本が あります。</span> — There is a book on the desk.
- <span lang="ja">入口の前に 先生が います。</span> — There is a teacher in front of the entrance.

**の** connects the landmark and the relative position. **に** marks the place where something exists. **が** identifies the item or person present. Use **あります** for a book, desk, or bag; **います** for a teacher or cat. When a known item is the topic, <span lang="ja">本は 机の上に あります。</span> (“The book is on the desk”) is also natural.

**Common errors to repair:**

- <span lang="ja">机上に 本が あります。</span> is a different compact expression; for this beginner pattern, write <span lang="ja">机の上に 本が あります。</span>
- <span lang="ja">いすの後ろに ねこが あります。</span> uses the wrong existence verb. Say <span lang="ja">いすの後ろに ねこが います。</span>
- <span lang="ja">かばんの隣</span> means *beside the bag*, not *inside the bag*. For *inside*, use <span lang="ja">かばんの中</span>.

## 5. Practice in three steps

**Controlled recognition:** Read <span lang="ja">机の下</span>, <span lang="ja">入口の前</span>, <span lang="ja">かばんの中</span>. Match them to *under the desk*, *in front of the entrance*, *inside the bag*. Then read the words aloud using the kana column; this is optional rehearsal, not pronunciation evaluation.

**Guided substitution:** Start with <span lang="ja">机の上に 本が あります。</span> Change **上** to **下** and translate. Then replace **本** with **ねこ**, changing the existence verb as needed. Model: <span lang="ja">机の下に ねこが います。</span> — There is a cat under the desk.

**Independent writing:** Describe a different imaginary room in **three connected sentences**. Include two different spatial kanji and one person or animal. Example: <span lang="ja">へやに 机が あります。机の隣に いすが あります。いすの後ろに ねこが います。</span> Check (1) correct reading and position, (2) の after the landmark, (3) に after the location, and (4) あります versus います. This writing is **self-reviewed**, not automatically scored.
