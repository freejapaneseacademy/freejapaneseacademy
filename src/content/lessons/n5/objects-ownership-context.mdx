## A missing umbrella after class

**Can-do for this chapter:** identify an object and ask who it belongs to. Imagine a school reception desk after an afternoon class. **Aki** is the receptionist, **Mina** is a student looking for her umbrella, and **Ren** is another student. The dialogue is original and fictional; it is a **written** model, not a recording.

| Speaker | Japanese | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mina | <span lang="ja">すみません。かさは ありますか。</span> | Excuse me. Is there an umbrella? |
| Aki | <span lang="ja">はい。これは ミナさんの かさですか。</span> | Yes. Is this Mina's umbrella? |
| Mina | <span lang="ja">いいえ、それは わたしの かさじゃありません。</span> | No, that isn't my umbrella. |
| Ren | <span lang="ja">その あおい かさは だれのですか。</span> | Whose is that blue umbrella? |
| Aki | <span lang="ja">これは タナカさんのです。</span> | This one is Tanaka's. |
| Mina | <span lang="ja">あそこに ある あかい かさは わたしのです。</span> | The red umbrella over there is mine. |

The color words **あおい** (blue) and **あかい** (red) help distinguish objects. The expression **あそこに ある** means “located over there”; its grammar is previewed for context, not assessed in this introductory lesson.

## 1. What is the purpose?

Mina is not shopping for a new umbrella. She wants to **find her own umbrella** among items left behind. Aki helps by checking ownership, and Ren asks about another umbrella. The goal is to avoid taking somebody else's property. Notice that a polite question can be short and still useful.

**Check the evidence:** Mina says <span lang="ja">わたしの かさじゃありません</span>, “it isn't my umbrella.” This is evidence that she rejects the umbrella Aki is showing. It does **not** mean she has no umbrella at all.

## 2. Pointing to things: これ, それ, あれ

| Form | Basic use | Model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">これ</span> | this one, near the speaker | <span lang="ja">これは かさです。</span> — This is an umbrella. |
| <span lang="ja">それ</span> | that one, often near the listener | <span lang="ja">それは ほんですか。</span> — Is that a book? |
| <span lang="ja">あれ</span> | that one over there, away from both | <span lang="ja">あれは かばんです。</span> — That over there is a bag. |

These are **stand-alone** pointing words: <span lang="ja">これは</span> can be the topic of a sentence. Actual distance and shared attention affect what sounds natural. In the dialogue, the receptionist holds an umbrella and calls it **これ**; Mina calls the one near Aki **それ**.

## 3. Pointing before a noun: この, その, あの

| Form | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">この</span> | <span lang="ja">この かさ</span> | this umbrella |
| <span lang="ja">その</span> | <span lang="ja">その かさ</span> | that umbrella |
| <span lang="ja">あの</span> | <span lang="ja">あの かさ</span> | that umbrella over there |

**Common error:** <span lang="ja">このは かさです</span> is not a standard way to say “This is an umbrella.” **この** needs a noun: <span lang="ja">この かさは あおいです</span> (“This umbrella is blue”). Use **これ** when the noun is omitted: <span lang="ja">これは かさです</span>.

## 4. Ask about ownership with の

In <span lang="ja">タナカさんの かさ</span>, **の** links the person to the object: “Tanaka's umbrella.” In <span lang="ja">タナカさんのです</span>, the noun is understood from context: “It's Tanaka's.” To ask who owns something, use <span lang="ja">だれのですか</span> (“Whose is it?”).

Compare:
- <span lang="ja">これは だれの かさですか。</span> — Whose umbrella is this?
- <span lang="ja">それは わたしのです。</span> — That one is mine.
- <span lang="ja">この ほんは レンさんのです。</span> — This book is Ren's.

Do not assume that **の** always means legal ownership: it also links nouns in many other relationships. In this lost-and-found scene, ownership is the relevant interpretation.

## 5. Guided rehearsal

**Step A — identify the roles.** Who is asking for help? Mina. Who is helping at the desk? Aki. Ren is another student, not the receptionist.

**Step B — point and name.** Imagine you hold a pencil: <span lang="ja">これは えんぴつです。</span> (“This is a pencil.”) Now refer to a pencil in your partner's hand: <span lang="ja">その えんぴつ</span> (“that pencil”).

**Step C — check ownership.** A friend shows you a notebook. Ask <span lang="ja">それは だれのですか。</span> (“Whose is that?”) A model answer is <span lang="ja">わたしのです。</span> (“It's mine.”)

**Step D — reject politely.** If the umbrella shown is not yours, say <span lang="ja">いいえ、わたしのじゃありません。</span> (“No, it isn't mine.”) Here **じゃありません** is a polite negative of the nominal predicate.

## 6. Independent use: your own lost-item exchange

Choose an ordinary item, such as <span lang="ja">ほん</span> (book), <span lang="ja">かばん</span> (bag) or <span lang="ja">かさ</span> (umbrella). Write **two to four short lines**: ask about the item, ask who owns it, and give an answer. You may use names of fictional people rather than personal information.

**Example model:**

> <span lang="ja">これは だれの ほんですか。</span> — Whose book is this?
>
> <span lang="ja">それは レンさんのです。</span> — That's Ren's.
>
> <span lang="ja">ありがとうございます。</span> — Thank you.

Self-check without a grade: Is **これ** used by itself and **この** with a noun? Does **の** link the owner to the item? Is the answer consistent with the question? Try reading your lines aloud if you wish, but this lesson does not assess listening or pronunciation.
