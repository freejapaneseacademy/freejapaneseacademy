## A school has more than one kind of room

**Can-do:** say where common places and objects are. When you visit a new school, you need to name the place you want before asking where it is. This written lesson teaches the **meaning and use** of facility words; no audio or pronunciation accuracy is assessed here.

Imagine a fictional school. Yui needs a book, Ren wants lunch, and Hana needs help from school staff. They will ask for different places even though all three are inside the same building.

## 1. Ten places to recognize

| Japanese | Reading | English and practical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">教室</span> | きょうしつ | **classroom** — A room where a class meets. |
| <span lang="ja">図書室</span> | としょしつ | **library room** — A room with books for reading or borrowing. |
| <span lang="ja">事務室</span> | じむしつ | **administrative office** — A room where school staff handle administrative work. |
| <span lang="ja">保健室</span> | ほけんしつ | **school health room** — A room for first aid or resting when unwell. |
| <span lang="ja">食堂</span> | しょくどう | **cafeteria** — A place for meals. |
| <span lang="ja">入口</span> | いりぐち | **entrance** — Where people enter a building. |
| <span lang="ja">出口</span> | でぐち | **exit** — Where people leave a building. |
| <span lang="ja">階段</span> | かいだん | **stairs** — Steps between floors. |
| <span lang="ja">トイレ</span> | トイレ | **restroom** — A restroom; the borrowed word is written in katakana. |
| <span lang="ja">廊下</span> | ろうか | **corridor** — A passage connecting rooms. |

The readings are supplied in kana for recognition. Some kanji will be studied more closely in L04. Say each word aloud only if you wish; these are written reading cues, not verified recordings.

## 2. Choosing a destination by purpose

**Scene A — a book.** Yui says <span lang="ja">図書室は どこですか。</span> (としょしつは どこですか。 — “Where is the library room?”). A staff member answers <span lang="ja">図書室は あそこです。</span> (“The library room is over there.”) The key noun is **図書室**, not 食堂.

**Scene B — lunch.** Ren asks <span lang="ja">食堂は どこですか。</span> (しょくどうは どこですか。 — “Where is the cafeteria?”). The answer is <span lang="ja">そこです。</span> (“It's there, near you / at the place indicated.”). The choice of そこ depends on where the people are standing.

**Scene C — school staff.** Hana asks <span lang="ja">事務室は どこですか。</span> (じむしつは どこですか。 — “Where is the office?”). The receptionist says <span lang="ja">ここです。</span> (“It's here.”). The receptionist is at the office; Hana's own perspective might differ.

**Scene D — feeling unwell.** A learner asks <span lang="ja">保健室は どこですか。</span> (ほけんしつは どこですか。 — “Where is the school health room?”). This is different from the administrative office. A school health room is not a hospital; it is a facility within a school.

## 3. Related words that are easy to confuse

**入口 (いりぐち)** is an entrance; **出口 (でぐち)** is an exit. Both contain 口 (“opening” in these compounds), but the first characters distinguish going in and going out. **廊下 (ろうか)** is a hallway or corridor; **階段 (かいだん)** are stairs, not a room. **教室 (きょうしつ)** is a classroom, whereas **図書室 (としょしつ)** is a library room. The ending 室 (しつ) appears in many room names; it does not tell you the room's purpose by itself.

**トイレ** is a common restroom word written in katakana. It is not normally written as a kanji compound in this introductory vocabulary.

## 4. Make a usable location question

The beginner pattern is **place + は + どこですか**. The topic particle は is written *ha* but pronounced *wa* here.

- <span lang="ja">教室は どこですか。</span> (きょうしつは どこですか。 — “Where is the classroom?”)
- <span lang="ja">出口は あそこです。</span> (でぐちは あそこです。 — “The exit is over there.”)
- <span lang="ja">トイレは ここです。</span> (“The restroom is here.”)

Remember: **ここ** is near the speaker, **そこ** is near the listener or an indicated place, and **あそこ** is away from both. These are place words, unlike **これ／それ／あれ**, which refer to things. The pattern <span lang="ja">教室は ここに あります。</span> (“The classroom is located here”) uses **に** with あります. Do **not** say <span lang="ja">ここにです</span>; with the simpler answer use <span lang="ja">ここです</span>. You will study the existence pattern in the next chapter.

## 5. Guided retrieval

Cover the English column in the table. For each situation, recall the Japanese word before uncovering it.

1. You need a book: **図書室** (としょしつ).
2. You need a meal: **食堂** (しょくどう).
3. You are leaving the building: **出口** (でぐち).
4. You need to change floors: **階段** (かいだん).
5. You want to speak with office staff: **事務室** (じむしつ).

Now reverse the direction: look at the Japanese word first and explain its purpose in English. The two directions of retrieval are different skills.

## 6. Independent mini-dialogue

Imagine a school with a cafeteria far from both speakers and a restroom near the listener. Write two questions and answers, using **食堂**, **トイレ**, **どこ**, **あそこ**, and **そこ**. One possible model:

> <span lang="ja">食堂は どこですか。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">食堂は あそこです。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">トイレは どこですか。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">トイレは そこです。</span>

Check whether each noun matches the intended facility, each question includes どこですか, and each answer agrees with the described viewpoint. This open-ended writing is **self-reviewed, not automatically graded**.

## Review

A useful place word identifies **what** you are looking for; どこですか asks **where** it is. Keep **入口／出口** and **廊下／階段** distinct. The written quiz below tests vocabulary recognition and practical choice, not audio comprehension or speaking fluency.
