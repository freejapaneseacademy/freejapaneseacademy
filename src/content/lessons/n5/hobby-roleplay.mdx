## Your mission: join the weekend club

You are visiting a fictional neighborhood club. You want to find a partner for a hobby. Practice asking **what someone likes**, **what they are good at**, and **what they can do**. This L08 speaking task is **optional and ungraded**. The models below are written rehearsal prompts; no recording, synthesized audio, or verified pronunciation is supplied.

## Three meanings you must keep separate

| Japanese model | Reading support | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 何（なに）が好（す）きですか。 | なにがすきですか | What do you like? |
| 音楽（おんがく）が好（す）きです。 | おんがくがすきです | I like music. |
| 料理（りょうり）が上手（じょうず）ですか。 | りょうりがじょうずですか | Are you good at cooking? |
| 料理（りょうり）はあまり上手（じょうず）じゃありません。 | りょうりはあまりじょうずじゃありません | I'm not very good at cooking. |
| ピアノが少（すこ）しできます。 | ピアノがすこしできます | I can play a little piano. |
| 水泳（すいえい）はできません。 | すいえいはできません | I cannot swim. |

**好き** expresses preference, **上手 / 下手** evaluates skill, and **できます** expresses ability. Liking music does not imply being good at singing. Use **あまり** with a negative form: **あまり上手じゃありません**, not **あまり上手です**. When speaking about your own strengths, **得意（とくい）です** (“it's one of my strengths”) is often more natural than praising yourself with **上手です**. Asking another person directly whether they are 下手 (“bad at”) can sound blunt; use a gentler question about what they can do.

## Scene A: A club welcome table

Two new members are choosing a weekend activity. Read each line aloud slowly, then exchange roles.

**A:** はじめまして。何（なに）が好（す）きですか。  
*Nice to meet you. What do you like?*

**B:** 読書（どくしょ）が好（す）きです。映画（えいが）も好（す）きです。  
*I like reading. I also like films.*

**A:** いいですね。ピアノはできますか。  
*That sounds nice. Can you play the piano?*

**B:** はい、少（すこ）しできます。でも、歌（うた）はあまり得意（とくい）じゃありません。  
*Yes, a little. But singing isn't really my strength.*

**A:** そうですか。私（わたし）は音楽（おんがく）が好（す）きです。  
*I see. I like music.*

Notice that **ピアノはできますか** asks about ability, not enjoyment. **いいですね** responds positively without claiming expertise. **そうですか** acknowledges new information; its tone depends on delivery.

## Scene B: Planning a weekend workshop

**A:** 土曜日（どようび）は何（なに）をしますか。  
*What will you do on Saturday?*

**B:** 料理（りょうり）をします。料理（りょうり）が好（す）きです。  
*I'll cook. I like cooking.*

**A:** 料理（りょうり）は得意（とくい）ですか。  
*Is cooking one of your strengths?*

**B:** はい、少（すこ）し得意（とくい）です。でも、水泳（すいえい）はできません。  
*Yes, I'm somewhat good at it. But I can't swim.*

**A:** 私（わたし）は水泳（すいえい）が好（す）きです。でも、料理（りょうり）はあまり得意（とくい）じゃありません。  
*I like swimming. But cooking isn't really my strength.*

**A:** じゃあ、いっしょに料理（りょうり）をしましょう。  
*Then let's cook together.*

The last line is an invitation. **〜ましょう** suggests doing something together; it does not mean the person is already cooking. **土曜日は** sets Saturday as the topic. You can replace the hobby nouns while preserving the particle and sentence pattern.

## Rehearsal ladder

**Round 1 — Listen to yourself, not to a model recording.** Read Scene A once while pointing to each line. Pause at punctuation. If a word is unfamiliar, use the kana above. There is no audio playback in this lesson.

**Round 2 — Swap roles.** Speak B's lines first, then A's. Keep the distinction between **好き**, **得意**, and **できます**. You may use a partner, or read both parts by yourself.

**Round 3 — Change two facts.** Replace 読書 with 写真（しゃしん） and ピアノ with 水泳. For example: **写真が好きです。水泳はできません。** — “I like photography. I cannot swim.” Do not change the ability meaning into dislike.

**Round 4 — Create a new exchange.** Ask one preference question, answer with two interests, ask one ability question, and respond with an ability or limitation. Write your script first if you prefer.

## A worked learner script

**A:** 何（なに）が好（す）きですか。  
**B:** 映画（えいが）が好（す）きです。読書（どくしょ）も好（す）きです。  
**A:** ピアノはできますか。  
**B:** いいえ、できません。でも、歌（うた）は少（すこ）し得意（とくい）です。

*What do you like? — I like films. I also like reading. — Can you play piano? — No, I can't. But singing is somewhat a strength of mine.*

## Optional speaking self-review

After your own practice, check: (1) Did you ask a clear question ending in **か**? (2) Did you use **が好き** for a preference? (3) Did you keep **できます** for ability rather than liking? (4) If you used **あまり**, did you include a negative? (5) Could a listener distinguish your two hobbies? You may repeat the exchange as often as useful. **Do not submit a voice recording**; this page does not capture, grade, or store speech.
