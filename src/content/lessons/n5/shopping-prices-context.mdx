## A first visit to a stationery shop

**Chapter Can-do:** Ask prices, amounts and sizes politely. This context lesson begins with understanding an original, fictional interaction rather than memorizing a complete shopping grammar system. **ゆい (Yui)** is a customer buying notebooks; **店員（てんいん）** is the shop clerk. All prices below are invented for this learning example, not real-world offers.

### Read the conversation

**店員（てんいん）:** いらっしゃいませ。  
**ゆい:** すみません。このノートは いくらですか。  
**店員:** 300円（さんびゃくえん）です。  
**ゆい:** あの青（あお）いノートも 300円ですか。  
**店員:** いいえ。青いノートは 400円（よんひゃくえん）です。  
**ゆい:** 小（ちい）さいノートは ありますか。  
**店員:** はい。こちらです。小さいノートは 200円（にひゃくえん）です。  
**ゆい:** じゃあ、小さいノートを 二冊（にさつ）ください。  
**店員:** はい。400円です。  
**ゆい:** お願（ねが）いします。  
**店員:** ありがとうございます。

### Meaning in English

The clerk welcomes Yui. Yui asks how much this notebook costs: ¥300. She asks whether the blue notebook also costs ¥300; the clerk says no, it costs ¥400. She then asks whether a small notebook is available. The clerk shows one costing ¥200. Yui requests **two small notebooks**, so the total is ¥400. She confirms the request, and the clerk thanks her.

**Reading guide:** この = this (near the speaker); あの = that (away from both); 青い（あおい） = blue; 小さい（ちいさい） = small; 円（えん） = yen; 二冊（にさつ） = two bound books/notebooks; こちら = this way / this one (polite in context). ありますか asks whether an item exists or is available. You do not need to produce the book counter 冊 independently yet.

## What does each person know or do?

| Evidence in Japanese | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| このノートは いくらですか。 | Yui is asking the price of a notebook. |
| 300円です。 | The first notebook costs ¥300. |
| 青いノートは 400円です。 | The blue notebook costs ¥400, not ¥300. |
| 小さいノートは 200円です。 | Each small notebook costs ¥200. |
| 小さいノートを 二冊ください。 | Yui asks for two small notebooks. |
| はい。400円です。 | The clerk states the total for the two small notebooks. |

**Important evidence distinction:** The blue notebook is discussed, but **not purchased**. The final purchase is two small notebooks. Do not confuse the ¥400 price of one blue notebook with the ¥400 total for two small notebooks.

## Notice the useful language

### Asking a price: いくらですか

**このノートは いくらですか。** means “How much is this notebook?” The topic marker **は** introduces the item being priced. **いくら** asks the amount of money, not the size or color.

### Making a polite request: ください

**小さいノートを 二冊ください。** means “Two small notebooks, please.” **を** marks the item requested. **二冊** counts two notebooks. In a store, this is a normal polite request; the clerk may confirm the total.

**〜をお願いします** is another polite request form, as in **このノートをお願いします。** (“This notebook, please.”) **お願いします** can also be said alone to confirm a request in context. Neither expression automatically tells you the price.

### Size and color are different properties

**青いノート** describes color (“blue notebook”). **小さいノート** describes size (“small notebook”). The scene does not tell us the color of the small notebook. Avoid inventing details the dialogue never states.

## Practise in three stages

**1. Controlled reading:** Without the English translation, find the two lines containing **いくら** or **円** that establish the first and blue notebook prices. Say who speaks each line.

**2. Guided substitutions:** Replace the item in **このノートは いくらですか。** with **このペン** (“this pen”). Then practise **このペンをお願いします。** (“This pen, please.”) These are model sentences, not additional purchases in Yui's story.

**3. Independent use:** Imagine buying one item in a shop. Write a two-line customer–clerk exchange: first ask its price, then request it politely. You may invent a price and clearly label your exchange as fictional.
