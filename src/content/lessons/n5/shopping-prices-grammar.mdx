## Your mission: buy supplies without guessing

You are visiting a fictional stationery shop. You want to know a notebook's price, choose a color and size, and request the correct number of items. In this grammar lesson, you will move from **recognizing** shop language to **making** polite requests. All prices and dialogues are invented for practice.

## 1. Ask a price with いくらですか

The basic pattern is **[item/topic] は いくらですか。** (How much is [the item]?). The particle **は** (pronounced わ) marks the item you are asking about; **いくら** asks the price, and **ですか** makes the question polite.

| Japanese | Reading | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">これはいくらですか。</span> | これは いくらですか。 | How much is this? |
| <span lang="ja">このノートはいくらですか。</span> | この ノートは いくらですか。 | How much is this notebook? |
| <span lang="ja">その青いノートはいくらですか。</span> | その あおい ノートは いくらですか。 | How much is that blue notebook (near you)? |
| <span lang="ja">この小さいノートは百円です。</span> | この ちいさい ノートは ひゃくえんです。 | This small notebook is 100 yen. |

**この + noun** means *this [noun]*, while **これ** means *this one* and stands alone. Say **このノート**, not **これノート**. **その** points to something near the listener. In **百円です**, 円 (えん) follows the number. The question **何冊ですか** (なんさつですか) asks about the number of bound books, **not** the price.

## 2. Ask for an item with ください

To request goods at a shop, use **[item] を [quantity] ください。** Here **を** (pronounced お) marks what you want; the counter expresses how many.

| Request | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">ノートを一冊ください。</span> | ノートを いっさつ ください。 | One notebook, please. |
| <span lang="ja">紙を二枚ください。</span> | かみを にまい ください。 | Two sheets of paper, please. |
| <span lang="ja">消しゴムを一つください。</span> | けしゴムを ひとつ ください。 | One eraser, please. |
| <span lang="ja">青いノートを一冊ください。</span> | あおい ノートを いっさつ ください。 | One blue notebook, please. |

**ください** is a polite request, not a price question. For a notebook, **一冊** (いっさつ) is a natural specific counter; for flat sheets, **一枚** (いちまい) and **二枚** (にまい) are appropriate. A general item such as an eraser can take **一つ** (ひとつ). If you only say **ノートください**, you may be understood, but including **を** and the quantity makes a clear, useful beginner model.

## 3. Use 〜をお願いします for a shop order

**[item] を [quantity] お願いします。** is another polite way to request an item or place an order. **お願いします** (おねがいします) is a fixed expression in this context, not a word-for-word replacement for English *please* in every sentence.

- <span lang="ja">赤いノートを一冊お願いします。</span> (あかい ノートを いっさつ おねがいします。) — One red notebook, please.
- <span lang="ja">このペンを二本お願いします。</span> (この ペンを にほん おねがいします。) — Two of these pens, please.
- <span lang="ja">小さいノートを一冊お願いします。</span> (ちいさい ノートを いっさつ おねがいします。) — One small notebook, please.

Both **ください** and **お願いします** work naturally for a straightforward purchase. **お願いします** is also used for many kinds of requests outside shopping, but we focus on shop orders here. **本** (ほん) is a counter used for long cylindrical objects such as pens; **二本** is read **にほん**.

## 4. Follow an original shop conversation

> **Customer:** <span lang="ja">すみません。この青いノートはいくらですか。</span> (Excuse me. How much is this blue notebook?)
>
> **Clerk:** <span lang="ja">三百円です。</span> (It is 300 yen.)
>
> **Customer:** <span lang="ja">小さいノートもありますか。</span> (Do you also have small notebooks?)
>
> **Clerk:** <span lang="ja">はい。小さいノートは百円です。</span> (Yes. Small notebooks are 100 yen.)
>
> **Customer:** <span lang="ja">では、小さいノートを一冊お願いします。</span> (Then, one small notebook, please.)
>
> **Clerk:** <span lang="ja">はい、ありがとうございます。</span> (Certainly, thank you.)

The customer **asks** about a blue notebook but ultimately **orders** a small notebook. Do not confuse a price inquiry with a completed purchase. **も** in **小さいノートもありますか** means *also*; **では** signals a decision (*then/in that case*).

## 5. Common mistakes to repair

| Not the intended beginner pattern | Better form | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">これノートはいくらですか。</span> | <span lang="ja">このノートはいくらですか。</span> | この modifies a noun; これ stands alone. |
| <span lang="ja">ノートを一枚ください。</span> | <span lang="ja">ノートを一冊ください。</span> | A bound notebook takes 冊; a loose sheet takes 枚. |
| <span lang="ja">これは何冊ですか。</span> (when asking price) | <span lang="ja">これはいくらですか。</span> | 何冊 asks how many bound books, not how much money. |
| <span lang="ja">青いノートはいくらください。</span> | <span lang="ja">青いノートはいくらですか。</span> | Price questions use ですか; ください requests an item. |

**Important nuance:** The counter is often placed after the object and を, as in **ノートを一冊ください**. Other natural orders exist in Japanese, so this is a reliable beginner model, not the only possible word order.

## 6. Controlled → guided → independent practice

**Controlled:** Fill the gaps aloud: <span lang="ja">このペンは＿＿ですか。</span> (**いくら**); <span lang="ja">紙を二＿＿ください。</span> (**枚**); <span lang="ja">ノートを一＿＿お願いします。</span> (**冊**).

**Guided:** Using the fictional conversation, answer: What is priced at 300 yen? **この青いノート**. What does the customer order? **小さいノートを一冊**. Identify the exact phrase supporting each answer.

**Independent:** Imagine a different fictional shop. Write three lines: (1) ask the price of a red notebook, (2) request one notebook, (3) request two sheets of paper. One possible model is: <span lang="ja">この赤いノートはいくらですか。赤いノートを一冊ください。紙を二枚お願いします。</span> Other natural wording is possible; this is learner self-review, not automatic grading.

**Optional speaking:** Read the two roles in section 4, switch roles, then substitute **赤い** for **青い**. No recorded pronunciation or speaking score is claimed.
