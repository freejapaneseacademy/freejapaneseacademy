## A notebook has gone missing

**Chapter can-do:** say that an object or person exists in a place. In this original, fictional classroom scene, Yui is getting ready for class. Her classmate Taku helps her locate a notebook and book. They also check where their teacher is. Read the conversation first for its **purpose**, then for the **location clues**. This is a written scene; no recorded speech is provided.

| Speaker | Japanese | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yui | <span lang="ja">すみません。わたしの ノートは どこですか。</span> | Excuse me. Where is my notebook? |
| Taku | <span lang="ja">ノートは かばんの なかに あります。</span> | The notebook is in the bag. |
| Yui | <span lang="ja">ありがとうございます。ほんは どこですか。</span> | Thank you. Where is the book? |
| Taku | <span lang="ja">ほんは つくえの うえに あります。</span> | The book is on the desk. |
| Yui | <span lang="ja">せんせいは どこですか。</span> | Where is the teacher? |
| Taku | <span lang="ja">せんせいは きょうしつに います。</span> | The teacher is in the classroom. |

### The room, in words

Imagine a classroom with a **desk**, a **bag** and a **teacher**. A book lies on the desk. Yui's notebook is inside the bag. The teacher is standing in the classroom. The description is an original text alternative to a room diagram; you need no image to answer the questions.

| --- | --- | --- |

## 1. Notice who is asking and why

Yui asks three **どこですか** (“where?”) questions. Taku gives three location answers. This is a **search for belongings and a person**, not a conversation about ownership alone. The earlier chapter used <span lang="ja">ここ・そこ・あそこ</span> (“here / there / over there”) to identify places; here we say what **exists** at a particular location.

Read the first exchange again. The question <span lang="ja">わたしの ノートは どこですか。</span> identifies Yui's notebook with <span lang="ja">わたしの</span> (“my”). The answer tells us **where** it is, not who bought it or why it was misplaced.

## 2. Two existence verbs: あります and います

In these polite present-tense sentences, <span lang="ja">あります</span> reports the existence or presence of an **inanimate thing**, while <span lang="ja">います</span> reports the presence of a **person or animal**.

| Japanese | Reading aid | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">ほんが あります。</span> | ほん が あります | There is a book. |
| <span lang="ja">せんせいが います。</span> | せんせい が います | There is a teacher. |
| <span lang="ja">ねこが います。</span> | ねこ が います | There is a cat. |

The difference is **not** singular versus plural. The key contrast here is the kind of entity. Do not say <span lang="ja">せんせいが あります</span> for a teacher, or <span lang="ja">ノートが います</span> for a notebook. These are basic classroom rules; later lessons will explore broader usage.

## 3. Where something exists: に and が

A useful new pattern is **place + に + thing/person + が + あります／います**.

- <span lang="ja">つくえの うえに ほんが あります。</span> — There is a book on the desk.
- <span lang="ja">きょうしつに せんせいが います。</span> — There is a teacher in the classroom.

**に** marks the location of existence. **が** identifies the thing or person whose presence is being stated. The dialogue also uses a topic-first form: <span lang="ja">ほんは つくえの うえに あります。</span> (“As for the book, it is on the desk”). **は** introduces the known topic; **が** is common when introducing what is present. Both forms can be natural, depending on the conversation.

**の** links nouns in <span lang="ja">かばんの なか</span> (“inside the bag”) and <span lang="ja">つくえの うえ</span> (“on top of the desk”). Here, <span lang="ja">なか（中）</span> means inside, and <span lang="ja">うえ（上）</span> means above/on top. You do not need to memorize the kanji spellings in this first context lesson.

## 5. Guided practice: reconstruct the room

Without looking at the evidence table, finish these statements aloud or on paper, then check the conversation.

1. <span lang="ja">ノートは ______ に あります。</span> (Where is the notebook?)
2. <span lang="ja">ほんは ______ に あります。</span> (Where is the book?)
3. <span lang="ja">せんせいは ______ に います。</span> (Where is the teacher?)

**Check:** <span lang="ja">かばんの なか</span>; <span lang="ja">つくえの うえ</span>; <span lang="ja">きょうしつ</span>. Notice that the person takes <span lang="ja">います</span>.

## 6. Independent transfer

Imagine a **different** room: a pencil is on a desk, a cat is in the room, and a bag is in the classroom. Write three short sentences using <span lang="ja">に</span> and the correct existence verb. A possible model is:

> <span lang="ja">つくえの うえに えんぴつが あります。へやに ねこが います。きょうしつに かばんが あります。</span>

Self-check each sentence: (1) is the location clear, (2) is <span lang="ja">に</span> attached to the place, (3) does the thing/person fit <span lang="ja">あります／います</span>, and (4) is the Japanese readable? This writing is **not automatically graded**.
