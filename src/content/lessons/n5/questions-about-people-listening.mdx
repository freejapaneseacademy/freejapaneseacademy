## The listening mission

You are visiting a language school's welcome area. In **Interview A**, a visitor asks who a person is and where she can be found. In **Interview B**, a student asks about an object and which notebook belongs to someone. Listen to each original recording before opening the transcript. The interviews are fictional and their voices are synthetic.

**First pass — gist:** Is the speaker asking about a person, a place, an object or a choice? Listen for the question word, not a single memorized sentence.

**Second pass — evidence:** Listen for the answer after each question. A question such as <span lang="ja">どこですか</span> does not itself establish a location.

| Spoken cue | Meaning | Listening strategy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">だれですか</span> | Who is it? | Wait for the name in the reply. |
| <span lang="ja">何（なん）ですか</span> | What is it? | Listen for the object noun. |
| <span lang="ja">どこですか</span> | Where is it / are they? | Listen for the place. |
| <span lang="ja">どれですか</span> | Which one? | Listen for the identifying color or position. |
| <span lang="ja">たなかさんも</span> | Tanaka too | も adds Tanaka to a previously mentioned fact. |
| <span lang="ja">じゃありません</span> | Is not | A polite negative correction. |

### Interview A — a person at school

The visitor asks <span lang="ja">あの人（ひと）は、だれですか。</span> (“Who is that person?”). Listen for the name and occupation, then for the location. The reply <span lang="ja">たなかさんも、きょうしつです。</span> means Tanaka is in the classroom **too**. Here <span lang="ja">きょうしつです</span> is a short contextual answer (“[She/they are] in the classroom”); a fuller location expression is <span lang="ja">きょうしつにいます</span>.

### Interview B — choosing a notebook

The first question asks **what** an object is: <span lang="ja">これは、何ですか。</span> The later question asks **which one** is the speaker's notebook: <span lang="ja">あなたのノートは、どれですか。</span> Notice the difference between <span lang="ja">あのあおいノート</span> (“that blue notebook”) and <span lang="ja">あかいノート</span> (“red notebook”). A negative reply rules out one candidate. The natural noun-modifying form is <span lang="ja">あの + あおい + ノート</span>; do not place <span lang="ja">どれ</span> directly before a noun. Use <span lang="ja">どのノート</span> for “which notebook.”

### A three-pass listening routine

1. Listen to each recording without the transcript and select your answers.
2. Replay the turn that contains the evidence. Check whether you heard the question or the reply.
3. Reveal the Japanese transcript and translation to verify. Revealing it makes your attempt **assisted**, even if you close it again. Then replay without looking.

Afterwards, write two short question-and-answer pairs of your own using <span lang="ja">だれ</span> and <span lang="ja">どれ</span>. Example: <span lang="ja">あの人は、だれですか。— さとうさんです。</span> (“Who is that person? — It's Sato.”) and <span lang="ja">ノートは、どれですか。— このノートです。</span> (“Which is the notebook? — This notebook.”) These are **written practice**, not listening evidence.

### Optional speaking

Repeat one question and its reply aloud after replaying the recording. You may use your own voice privately; this page does not upload a microphone recording or issue a pronunciation grade. If the genuine WAV recordings are unavailable, the listening assessment must remain locked rather than substituting written transcripts.

**Review checkpoint:** Can you explain why <span lang="ja">も</span> adds a second person and why <span lang="ja">じゃありません</span> rules out the red notebook? Independent Japanese and pronunciation reviews are pending.
