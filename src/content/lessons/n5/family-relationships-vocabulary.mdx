## A family photo at a language exchange

**Can-do:** Describe family members and simple relationships. You are at a fictional language-exchange meeting. A classmate shows a family photo. You will learn how to refer to your own family and how to speak politely about another person's family.

This lesson teaches **word choice in context**, not a claim that every family uses the same terms. Families have many forms; use only relationships that fit the person you are describing.

## 1. Listen with your eyes: an original introduction

> A：こちらは、わたしの母（はは）です。  
> B：お母（かあ）さんですか。  
> A：はい。こちらは、わたしの兄（あに）です。  
> B：そうですか。

**Meaning:** A: This is my mother. B: Your mother? A: Yes. This is my older brother. B: I see.

Here **こちら** politely presents a person. **わたしの** means “my”; **の** links the possessor and the family word. **お母さん** is a respectful way to refer to or address someone else's mother. **母（はは）** is the usual neutral/humble reference to one's own mother when speaking to an outsider. **そうですか** is a conversational acknowledgement; its nuance depends on intonation.

## 2. A practical family word bank

| Relationship | Speaking about your own family to outsiders | Referring to someone else's family |
| --- | --- | --- |
| mother | 母（はは） | お母さん（おかあさん） |
| father | 父（ちち） | お父さん（おとうさん） |
| older sister | 姉（あね） | お姉さん（おねえさん） |
| older brother | 兄（あに） | お兄さん（おにいさん） |
| younger sister | 妹（いもうと） | 妹さん（いもうとさん） |
| younger brother | 弟（おとうと） | 弟さん（おとうとさん） |

**Important nuance:** Within a family, speakers often call their *own* parents **お母さん／お父さん** and older siblings **お姉ちゃん／お兄ちゃん**. The left column describes the conventional choice **when telling an outsider about your own family**. **妹さん／弟さん** are polite references to another person's younger siblings, not universal forms of address. You may also hear **ご家族（ごかぞく）** for “(your) family” and **家族（かぞく）** for “family.”

**Kanji note:** The readings of 母, 父, 兄 and 姉 change in the polite expressions above. Read each **whole word**, not each character in isolation. The long vowels in **おかあさん**, **おとうさん**, **おねえさん**, and **おにいさん** matter in both spelling and speech.

## 3. Age and relationship are different facts

**兄（あに）** is an *older* brother; **弟（おとうと）** is a *younger* brother. **姉（あね）** is an older sister; **妹（いもうと）** is a younger sister. These words do not simply mean “brother” or “sister” without age information.

> わたしの妹です。 — This is my younger sister.  
> 田中さんのお兄さんです。 — This is Tanaka's older brother.  
> こちらは父です。 — This is my father. (Context supplies “my.”)

**の** connects a person to a relationship: **田中さんの母** means “Tanaka's mother” as a literal relation, but in polite conversation with or about Tanaka, **田中さんのお母さん** is usually more appropriate. Avoid applying the respectful **お** to **母** in an ordinary introduction of your own mother to an outsider.

## 4. Two fictional families

**Mika's introduction:** 「こちらは、わたしの父です。こちらは、わたしの妹です。」  
“This is my father. This is my younger sister.”

**Ken's question:** 「ミカさんのお父さんですか。」  
“Is that Mika's father?”

**Mika's reply:** 「はい、そうです。」  
“Yes, that's right.”

The same person is **父** in Mika's own introduction and **お父さん** in Ken's respectful question. Neither choice is a translation error; the speaker's relationship to the person changes the wording.

## 5. Controlled retrieval and guided practice

Before checking the interactive questions, cover the table and retrieve these five pairs: **母／お母さん**, **父／お父さん**, **兄／お兄さん**, **姉／お姉さん**, **妹／妹さん**. Say what relationship each word indicates and whose relative is being discussed.

Then complete these prompts on paper:

1. Introduce your fictional father to a classmate: **こちらは、わたしの父です。**
2. Ask politely about Mika's mother: **ミカさんのお母さんですか。**
3. Say “my younger brother”: **わたしの弟**.
4. Say “Tanaka's older sister”: **田中さんのお姉さん**.

Each prompt has a clear contextual reason for the family term. The scored quiz below checks recognition, not pronunciation.

## 6. Independent use and review

Invent a family of two or three characters; do not use personal names or photos. Write two short introductions with **こちらは** and **の**, then write one respectful question about someone else's relative. Compare each term against the word bank and check whether **older/younger** and **my/your** are both correct.

**Common mistakes:** **兄** and **弟** are not interchangeable; **おかさん** is not the standard spelling of **お母さん（おかあさん）**; and **わたしのお母さんです** is possible in family-internal or childlike speech, but **わたしの母です** is the conventional adult introduction to an outsider. The quiz assesses these conventional contexts rather than declaring all other registers universally wrong.

Review tomorrow: cover the right-hand column, reconstruct four polite forms, and make a sentence with **の**. Reading the forms aloud is optional; this written vocabulary lesson does not provide recorded audio or assess pronunciation.
