## The scene: a busy Saturday workshop

You are helping with a fictional community-center workshop. Someone asks **今（いま）、何（なに）をしていますか。** — “What are you doing now?” To answer, you need a word for the activity and often a word for the place. This lesson builds those words before the next grammar lesson explains the pattern in detail.

**First read.** Look for *who*, *where*, and *what* in this original scene.

> 午後（ごご）二時（にじ）です。教室（きょうしつ）で、ユキさんは絵（え）を描（か）いています。ケンさんは日本語（にほんご）を書（か）いています。図書室（としょしつ）で、ミナさんは本（ほん）を読（よ）んでいます。台所（だいどころ）で、先生（せんせい）はお茶（ちゃ）を飲（の）んでいます。庭（にわ）で、タロウさんは写真（しゃしん）を撮（と）っています。

**English:** It is 2 p.m. In the classroom, Yuki is drawing a picture and Ken is writing Japanese. In the reading room, Mina is reading a book. In the kitchen, the teacher is drinking tea. In the garden, Taro is taking photos.

All names and places in this workshop are fictional.

## Ten actions worth recognizing

Read the **dictionary form** as a vocabulary label; use the **〜ています form** to describe the action happening in the scene.

| Dictionary form (reading) | Action happening now | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 読む（よむ） | 読んでいます（よんでいます） | is reading |
| 書く（かく） | 書いています（かいています） | is writing |
| 描く（かく） | 描いています（かいています） | is drawing |
| 飲む（のむ） | 飲んでいます（のんでいます） | is drinking |
| 食べる（たべる） | 食べています（たべています） | is eating |
| 話す（はなす） | 話しています（はなしています） | is speaking / talking |
| 聞く（きく） | 聞いています（きいています） | is listening / asking, depending on context |
| 勉強する（べんきょうする） | 勉強しています（べんきょうしています） | is studying |
| 写真を撮る（しゃしんをとる） | 写真を撮っています（しゃしんをとっています） | is taking a photograph |
| 休む（やすむ） | 休んでいます（やすんでいます） | is resting / taking a break |

**Notice the two かく words.** 書く is for writing characters or text; 描く is for drawing pictures. They sound the same but use different kanji and different objects. **日本語を書いています** means “is writing Japanese”; **絵を描いています** means “is drawing a picture.”

**Notice 聞く.** 音楽（おんがく）を聞いています means “is listening to music”; 先生に聞いています can mean “is asking the teacher.” The particle and object help you choose the meaning.

## Six locations and one important particle

| Word | Reading | Meaning | In a sentence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 教室 | きょうしつ | classroom | 教室で絵を描いています。 — Drawing in the classroom. |
| 図書室 | としょしつ | reading room / school library room | 図書室で本を読んでいます。 — Reading in the reading room. |
| 台所 | だいどころ | kitchen | 台所でお茶を飲んでいます。 — Drinking tea in the kitchen. |
| 庭 | にわ | garden / yard | 庭で写真を撮っています。 — Taking photos in the garden. |
| 公園 | こうえん | park | 公園で休んでいます。 — Resting in the park. |
| 部屋 | へや | room | 部屋で勉強しています。 — Studying in the room. |

The particle **で** marks *where an activity happens*. **を** marks a direct object when the verb takes one.

- **ミナさんは図書室で本を読んでいます。** Mina is reading a book in the reading room. 図書室**で** = where; 本**を** = what.
- **ユキさんは教室で絵を描いています。** Yuki is drawing a picture in the classroom.
- **ケンさんは部屋で日本語を勉強しています。** Ken is studying Japanese in his room.

**Common mix-up:** 図書室**を**本**で**読んでいます swaps the roles of the particles and is not the intended sentence. Keep **place + で**, **object + を**.

## Meaning in context: now versus regularly

Compare these two natural sentences:

> 今、ミナさんは本を読んでいます。  
> *Mina is reading a book now.*

> ミナさんは毎日（まいにち）本を読みます。  
> *Mina reads books every day.*

**今** (“now”) plus **読んでいます** points to the action in progress. **毎日** (“every day”) plus **読みます** describes a habit. At this stage, we use 〜ています for actions currently happening; in wider Japanese, this form can also describe resulting states or habits, so it is not always equivalent to English “-ing.”

## Second reading: a quick message from the park

> アキ：今、どこにいますか。  
> リオ：公園（こうえん）にいます。ベンチで休んでいます。  
> アキ：サラさんは何をしていますか。  
> リオ：サラさんは公園で音楽（おんがく）を聞いています。  
> アキ：そうですか。私は家（いえ）で昼（ひる）ごはんを食べています。

**English:** Aki: Where are you now? Rio: I'm in the park. I'm resting on a bench. Aki: What is Sara doing? Rio: Sara is listening to music in the park. Aki: I see. I'm eating lunch at home.

**Important contrast:** **公園にいます** locates a person (“is at the park”); **公園で音楽を聞いています** locates an *action* (“is listening to music at the park”). The different particles have different jobs.

## Guided retrieval: cover, recall, check

1. Cover the English column above. What is **描いています**? Reveal: “is drawing.”
2. A classmate is **writing** in the classroom. Which kanji fits: **書** or **描**? Answer: **書**, as in 教室で日本語を書いています.
3. Someone is taking a break in the park. Complete **公園___休んでいます**. Answer: **で**.
4. Is **毎日、本を読みます** happening at this exact moment? No: it describes a repeated habit.
5. Ask “What is Sara doing?”: **サラさんは何をしていますか。**

Try the interactive questions below *before* reviewing the tables again. They test vocabulary recognition and contextual interpretation, not pronunciation.

## Independent mini-scene (ungraded writing)

Invent a three-person workshop. Write **three to five short Japanese sentences**. Say where each person is and what they are doing *now*. Use at least three different activity expressions and two locations. You can reuse vocabulary, but create your own combination.

**Model:**

> 今、教室でアキさんは絵を描いています。  
> ミナさんは図書室で本を読んでいます。  
> 私は部屋で日本語を勉強しています。

**English:** Now Aki is drawing a picture in the classroom. Mina is reading a book in the reading room. I'm studying Japanese in my room.

**Self-review rubric:** Check (1) person, (2) appropriate location + で, (3) object + を where needed, (4) correct 〜ています expression, and (5) a clear “now” context. No automatic score is assigned to your original writing.

**Optional speaking:** Read your mini-scene aloud twice, then ask **今、何をしていますか。** and answer with one sentence. Speaking is optional, unrecorded and ungraded. No verified model audio is supplied for this L02 written vocabulary lesson.

## Review

Retrieve the pairs **書く / 書いています**, **描く / 描いています**, **読む / 読んでいます**, **撮る / 撮っています**, and the location marker **で**. In the next lesson, you will explain how 〜ています is formed and used, beyond memorizing these useful expressions.
