## A message that makes a real plan

You and a fictional classmate are arranging lunch after class. You will **type two to five connected Japanese sentences**, then write a reply. This is a writing lesson: a short recognition check supports the task, but **your own composition is self-reviewed, not machine-graded**. Use a Japanese IME or paste Japanese text from your own notes.

### 1. Read the model before writing

**Invitation from あき to れん**

> <span lang="ja">れんさん、土曜日にいっしょに昼ご飯を食べませんか。駅の近くの喫茶店はどうですか。十二時に駅で会いましょう。</span>

“Ren, would you like to have lunch together on Saturday? How about the café near the station? Let's meet at the station at twelve.”

**Reply from れん**

> <span lang="ja">いいですね。土曜日の十二時に駅で会いましょう。楽しみです。</span>

“That sounds good. Let's meet at the station at twelve on Saturday. I'm looking forward to it.”

The invitation proposes **Saturday**, **lunch at a café**, and **a noon meeting at the station**. The reply accepts and repeats the time and meeting point. <span lang="ja">楽しみです</span> (たのしみです) expresses positive anticipation.

### 2. Three forms, three jobs

| Pattern | Example | Meaning and writing job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">〜ませんか</span> | <span lang="ja">いっしょに食べませんか。</span> | “Would you like to eat together?” Polite invitation, despite negative-question form. |
| <span lang="ja">〜ましょう</span> | <span lang="ja">十二時に会いましょう。</span> | “Let's meet at twelve.” Proposes a joint action. |
| <span lang="ja">〜にします</span> | <span lang="ja">私はお茶にします。</span> | “I'll choose tea.” States a selection, not a general invitation. |

A joint choice can also be <span lang="ja">土曜日にしましょう</span> (“Let's make it Saturday”). In a reply, <span lang="ja">土曜日にします</span> can express the speaker's decision, but <span lang="ja">土曜日にしましょう</span> is more directly collaborative.

**Polite refusal:** <span lang="ja">すみません、土曜日はちょっと。日曜日はどうですか。</span> — “Sorry, Saturday is a bit difficult. How about Sunday?” The unfinished <span lang="ja">ちょっと</span> softens the refusal; it does not give a specific reason.

### 3. Build your message one sentence at a time

**Step A — invitation:** <span lang="ja">日曜日にいっしょに映画館に行きませんか。</span> — “Would you like to go to the cinema together on Sunday?”

**Step B — propose a meeting point:** <span lang="ja">二時に駅で会いましょう。</span> — “Let's meet at the station at two.”

**Step C — offer a choice:** <span lang="ja">映画のあとで喫茶店に行きましょう。</span> — “Let's go to a café after the film.” This is an optional extra; you do not need a long message.

The particles matter. <span lang="ja">駅で会います</span> uses **で** for the place where you meet; <span lang="ja">映画館に行きます</span> uses **に** for the destination. A clock time normally takes **に**: <span lang="ja">二時に</span>. With <span lang="ja">今日</span> or <span lang="ja">明日</span>, do not normally add **に**.

### 4. Controlled editing

Rewrite these fragments on paper or in your draft.

1. Change <span lang="ja">土曜日にいっしょに昼ご飯を食べませんか。</span> to **Sunday**. Model: <span lang="ja">日曜日にいっしょに昼ご飯を食べませんか。</span>
2. Change <span lang="ja">十二時に駅で会いましょう。</span> to **three o'clock**. Model: <span lang="ja">三時に駅で会いましょう。</span>
3. Replace a blunt <span lang="ja">行きません。</span> with a softened refusal and alternative. Model: <span lang="ja">すみません、今日はちょっと。明日はどうですか。</span>

### 5. Guided and independent writing

**Guided:** Write a two-sentence invitation with the frame <span lang="ja">＿＿曜日にいっしょに＿＿ませんか。＿＿時に＿＿で会いましょう。</span> The first blank before <span lang="ja">ませんか</span> must be a suitable **ます-stem** such as <span lang="ja">食べ</span> or <span lang="ja">行き</span>; do not attach <span lang="ja">ませんか</span> to a dictionary-form verb.

**Independent task:** Imagine you are inviting a fictional classmate to lunch or the cinema. Write **2–5 connected sentences**. Include a day, an invitation with <span lang="ja">ませんか</span>, a time and a meeting place. Then write a **1–3 sentence reply** that either accepts or politely declines with an alternative. Do not include your real address or personal contact details. Type in the draft area below; your writing stays in this browser until you choose to clear it.

**Alternative valid invitation:**

> <span lang="ja">みかさん、明日いっしょに食堂で昼ご飯を食べませんか。十二時半に食堂の前で会いましょう。</span>

“Mika, would you like to have lunch together in the cafeteria tomorrow? Let's meet in front of the cafeteria at 12:30.”

**Alternative valid reply:**

> <span lang="ja">すみません、明日は予定があります。金曜日はどうですか。</span>

“Sorry, I have plans tomorrow. How about Friday?”

### 6. Self-review, not an automatic grade

Check **four criteria**: (1) the invitation is recognizable and polite, (2) the day and time are clear and consistent, (3) meeting place **で** and destination **に** are used appropriately, and (4) the reply clearly accepts or softens a decline. Compare your writing with the models; different natural wording is welcome. A complete sentence can omit an explicit subject in Japanese.

The short multiple-choice check below reviews patterns and common mistakes. Its score reflects only those fixed-answer questions, **not** your writing quality or speaking ability. Optional speaking: read your own invitation and reply aloud; no recording or pronunciation evaluation is performed.
