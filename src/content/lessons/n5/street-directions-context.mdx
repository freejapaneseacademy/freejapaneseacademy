## When someone asks the way

**Can-do:** Understand and give basic movement instructions. Imagine a visitor who has arrived near a station and wants to find the library. The visitor does not need an entire city map: they need a short, clear sequence of instructions and a recognizable landmark.

In this **written context lesson**, you will identify *who* is speaking, *why* they are speaking and *which clues* help someone find a destination. Listening to actual street instructions is reserved for L06; there is no audio or pronunciation grade here.

## 1. Scene: Outside a station

A visitor, **Mika**, is standing at the exit of a train station. A local resident, **Sato**, is nearby. Mika wants to go to the public library, but she does not know which street to take. Sato can see a traffic light and a park on the way.

| Role | Need or knowledge |
| --- | --- |
| Mika, visitor | Wants to find the library; asks for help |
| Sato, local resident | Knows the route; gives instructions |
| Destination | 図書館 (としょかん), library |
| Landmarks | 信号 (しんごう), traffic light; 公園 (こうえん), park |

**Purpose:** This is a request for directions, not an invitation to travel together. Sato explains the route; the conversation does not say Sato accompanies Mika.

## 2. Read the original exchange

**Mika:** <span lang="ja">すみません。図書館はどこですか。</span>  
(すみません。としょかんは どこですか。)  
“Excuse me. Where is the library?”

**Sato:** <span lang="ja">この道をまっすぐ行ってください。</span>  
(この みちを まっすぐ いってください。)  
“Please go straight along this road.”

**Sato:** <span lang="ja">信号で右に曲がってください。</span>  
(しんごうで みぎに まがってください。)  
“Please turn right at the traffic light.”

**Sato:** <span lang="ja">図書館は公園の左にあります。</span>  
(としょかんは こうえんの ひだりに あります。)  
“The library is to the left of the park.”

**Mika:** <span lang="ja">ありがとうございます。</span>  
(ありがとうございます。)  
“Thank you.”

**Sato:** <span lang="ja">どういたしまして。</span>  
(どういたしまして。)  
“You're welcome.”

This is a fictional instructional dialogue, not a verified recording or a real map.

## 3. Notice the clues

| Expression | Reading | What it tells you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">すみません</span> | すみません | A polite way to get someone's attention |
| <span lang="ja">どこですか</span> | どこですか | Asks where something is |
| <span lang="ja">道</span> | みち | Road or path |
| <span lang="ja">まっすぐ</span> | まっすぐ | Straight ahead |
| <span lang="ja">信号</span> | しんごう | Traffic light |
| <span lang="ja">右</span> | みぎ | Right |
| <span lang="ja">左</span> | ひだり | Left |
| <span lang="ja">公園</span> | こうえん | Park |
| <span lang="ja">図書館</span> | としょかん | Library |
| <span lang="ja">あります</span> | あります | Says where a nonliving place or thing is located |

The two uses of **で** and **に** are not interchangeable here. **信号で** identifies the place where you turn; **右に** gives the direction of the turn. **公園の左にあります** locates the library relative to the park. L03 will examine these forms systematically.

## 4. Reconstruct the route from evidence

Read Sato's lines in order:

1. **この道をまっすぐ** — continue straight on this road.
2. **信号で右に** — turn right at the traffic light.
3. **公園の左にあります** — find the library to the left of the park.

The instructions do **not** specify a distance, a street name, a second turn or a travel time. Do not add any of those details when answering comprehension questions.

**Perspective matters:** “Right” is the traveler's right as they follow the route. “To the left of the park” describes the library's position relative to that landmark; it is not necessarily a command to turn left.

## 5. Compare questions and instructions

**図書館はどこですか。** is a location question: “Where is the library?” **まっすぐ行ってください。** is an instruction: “Please go straight.” **図書館は公園の左にあります。** is a statement of location: “The library is to the left of the park.”

A learner may recognize **左** and mistakenly turn left at the traffic light. But the instruction says **右に曲がってください**. Read the whole clause, not just a direction word.

**Language note:** The expression **行ってください** is a polite request based on the て-form. You can understand the complete phrase now without needing to master verb conjugation yet.

## 6. Guided practice

Without looking back, answer these in English. Then find the exact Japanese phrase that supports each answer.

- Who needs help? **Mika**, the visitor who asks **図書館はどこですか**.
- Where should she turn? **At the traffic light**, supported by **信号で右に曲がってください**.
- Which way should she turn? **Right**, supported by **右に**.
- Where is the library relative to the park? **To the left**, supported by **公園の左にあります**.
- Does Sato promise to walk with Mika? **No such promise is stated.**

## 7. Supported writing and optional oral rehearsal

Write two or three connected Japanese sentences for a fictional visitor: ask where the library is, tell them to go straight and give its position beside the park. Use the model below for self-review.

> <span lang="ja">図書館はどこですか。まっすぐ行ってください。図書館は公園の左にあります。</span>  
> (としょかんは どこですか。まっすぐ いってください。としょかんは こうえんの ひだりに あります。)

Check that you distinguish a question, an instruction and a location statement. The writing is not automatically graded. You may rehearse aloud privately; no microphone access or pronunciation evaluation is provided.

## Review

You can now identify the purpose and participants of a directions exchange and follow three stated clues without inventing extra details. The next lesson builds your navigation and landmark vocabulary; the later listening lesson assesses comprehension from real licensed audio.
