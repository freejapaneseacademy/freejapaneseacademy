## Your mission: report on the community center

You are helping prepare a short activity update for a **fictional community center**. Look at the caption board, then read the coordinator's conversation. At the end you can ask **何（なに）をしていますか** (“What are [they] doing?”) and respond with **〜ています** (“[someone] is doing …”). This is a **written** checkpoint. The authentic listening task belongs to L06 and is assessed separately.

## Grammar: from action to ongoing activity

The pattern is **person は + place で + object を + verb て-form + います**. In a question, **何をしていますか** asks what action is in progress.

| Dictionary form | て-form | Ongoing action | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 読（よ）む | 読んで | 読んでいます | is reading |
| 書（か）く | 書いて | 書いています | is writing |
| 食（た）べる | 食べて | 食べています | is eating |
| 話（はな）す | 話して | 話しています | is talking |
| する | して | しています | is doing |
| 見（み）る | 見て | 見ています | is watching / looking |

**で** marks the place where an action occurs: **図書室（としょしつ）で本（ほん）を読んでいます** (is reading a book in the library). **を** marks the direct object: **本を読んでいます**. **は** marks the topic, as in **田中（たなか）さんは**.

Do not confuse **読みます** (reads / will read, polite nonpast) with **読んでいます** (is reading, in this scene). Also, **〜ています** can describe a continuing state with some verbs (for example, **知（し）っています**, “knows”); in today's activity scenes it describes actions currently underway. Do not assume every verb ending in 〜ています means a visible movement.

## Original photo-caption board: Saturday, 10 a.m.

These are **text captions for fictional photographs**, not photographs of real people.

> **写真（しゃしん）①　図書室（としょしつ）**  
> 田中（たなか）さんは図書室で本（ほん）を読（よ）んでいます。  
> *Tanaka is reading a book in the library.*

> **写真②　教室（きょうしつ）**  
> 山田（やまだ）さんは教室で日本語（にほんご）を書（か）いています。  
> *Yamada is writing Japanese in the classroom.*

> **写真③　台所（だいどころ）**  
> 佐藤（さとう）さんは台所で昼（ひる）ごはんを作（つく）っています。  
> *Sato is making lunch in the kitchen.*

> **写真④　庭（にわ）**  
> ミカさんは庭で花（はな）を見（み）ています。  
> *Mika is looking at flowers in the garden.*

Read for three pieces of evidence: **who** (田中さん), **where** (図書室で), and **what** (本を読んでいます). The particle **で** belongs to the location, not to the person. A caption states what the person is doing at the pictured moment; it does not establish their permanent job or routine.

## Original conversation: updating the noticeboard

> 係（かかり）の人（ひと）：今（いま）、田中（たなか）さんは何（なに）をしていますか。  
> 学生（がくせい）：図書室（としょしつ）で本（ほん）を読（よ）んでいます。  
> 係の人：山田（やまだ）さんは？  
> 学生：教室（きょうしつ）で日本語（にほんご）を書（か）いています。  
> 係の人：佐藤（さとう）さんは台所（だいどころ）にいますか。  
> 学生：はい。台所で昼（ひる）ごはんを作（つく）っています。

**Translation:** Coordinator: What is Tanaka doing now? Student: Reading a book in the library. Coordinator: And Yamada? Student: Writing Japanese in the classroom. Coordinator: Is Sato in the kitchen? Student: Yes. Making lunch in the kitchen.

**Notice the distinction:** **台所にいます** tells us *where Sato is* (existence/location). **台所で作っています** tells us *where Sato is doing an action*. The question **何をしていますか** asks for the action, not just the place.

## Guided rehearsal: repair and explain

1. **田中さんは図書室に本を読んでいます。** → **田中さんは図書室で本を読んでいます。** Use **で** for the action location.
2. **山田さんは日本語を書きています。** → **山田さんは日本語を書いています。** 書く becomes **書いて**, not 書きて.
3. **佐藤さんは昼ごはんを作ります。** → **佐藤さんは昼ごはんを作っています。** The second sentence explicitly describes an ongoing action.
4. **何をしていますか。** → **花を見ています。** The answer supplies an action; **庭にいます** alone answers a different question.

## Independent writing: your activity update

Write **three to five connected Japanese sentences** about an imaginary school club or community event. Include at least **two people**, **two action locations**, and **two different 〜ています verbs**. You may invent names and activities. Model:

> 今（いま）、学校（がっこう）でイベントがあります。  
> アキさんは教室（きょうしつ）で本（ほん）を読（よ）んでいます。  
> ケンさんは庭（にわ）で花（はな）を見（み）ています。  
> 私（わたし）は教室で日本語（にほんご）を書（か）いています。

**Translation:** There is an event at school now. Aki is reading a book in the classroom. Ken is looking at flowers in the garden. I am writing Japanese in the classroom.

Self-check your work using five criteria: **(1)** the people are identifiable; **(2)** each action location uses **で**; **(3)** direct objects use **を** where appropriate; **(4)** each verb has the correct て-form + **います**; **(5)** the sentences describe one coherent moment. This writing is ungraded; there is no automatic Japanese composition or pronunciation assessment.
