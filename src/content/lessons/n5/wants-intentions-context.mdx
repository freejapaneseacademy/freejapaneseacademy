## A weekend-plan board

Imagine a **fictional community center** where two friends, みな and そうた, are choosing a Saturday activity. A noticeboard advertises a book fair and a small art workshop. This is a first encounter with **wants and intentions**: you are not expected to conjugate every verb yet.

**Can-do:** Understand what a person wants to obtain, what they want to do, and where they intend to go for that activity.

### Scene: three clues

- **Who?** みな and そうた, friends deciding how to spend Saturday.
- **Where?** A community center near a library.
- **Why?** They are comparing a book fair with an art workshop and explaining their wishes.

### Original conversation

> **みな:** 土曜日（どようび）、何（なに）をしたいですか。  
> *What do you want to do on Saturday?*
>
> **そうた:** 新（あたら）しい本（ほん）がほしいです。図書館（としょかん）の近（ちか）くの本（ほん）の市（いち）に行（い）きたいです。  
> *I want a new book. I want to go to the book fair near the library.*
>
> **みな:** 私（わたし）は絵（え）を描（か）きたいです。午後（ごご）、ワークショップに行（い）きます。  
> *I want to draw. In the afternoon, I'm going to a workshop.*
>
> **そうた:** いいですね。私（わたし）も絵（え）を見（み）たいです。  
> *That sounds good. I want to look at art too.*

The book fair and workshop are fictional. A **本の市（ほんのいち）** is a book market/fair in this story; the conversation does not assert that such an event actually exists.

## Notice the three meanings

| Form | Japanese | Meaning | What it answers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **N がほしいです** | 新しい本がほしいです。 | I want a new book. | What item do you want? |
| **Verb stem + たいです** | 絵を描きたいです。 | I want to draw. | What action do you want to do? |
| **Place に行きます** | ワークショップに行きます。 | I'm going to the workshop. | Where are you going? |

**ほしい** expresses a desire for a thing: **本がほしいです**. The desired thing commonly takes **が** in this beginner pattern. **描きたい** comes from the ます-stem of **描きます** (draw): 描き + たい. It describes a desired action. The object of that action can take **を**: **絵を描きたいです**.

**〜に行きます** can simply mark a destination: **ワークショップに行きます** (“I'm going to the workshop”). Later in this chapter, you will learn how to express a **purpose of going** with **verb stem + に行きます**, for example **本を買いに行きます** (“I'm going to buy a book”). Do not confuse the destination noun **ワークショップに** with the purpose verb **買いに**.

### Reading aids for the key expressions

- **どようび** — Saturday; **なにをしたいですか** — what do you want to do?
- **あたらしいほん** — new book; **ほしいです** — want (an item).
- **えをかきたいです** — want to draw a picture; **みにいきます** — go to see (a later purpose-pattern example).
- **ごご** — afternoon; **いきます** — go; **わたしも** — I too.

**Important nuance:** 〜たい and ほしい typically describe the speaker's own wishes, or ask the listener about theirs. Do not automatically state a third person's private desires as certain. For a beginner report, say **そうたさんは「本がほしいです」と言いました** (“Sota said, ‘I want a book’”) rather than treating his wish as something you can independently know.

## Guided discovery

1. Find the exact words **本がほしいです**. Does Sota want an object or an action?
2. Find **絵を描きたいです**. What activity does Mina want to do?
3. Compare **本の市に行きたいです** with **ワークショップに行きます**. One describes a desired trip; the other states a plan.
4. Find **私も**. Which person's interest is being added to the conversation?
5. Cover the English translations. Re-read the Japanese and point to the phrase supporting each answer.

### Two more original mini-exchanges

> **A:** お茶（ちゃ）がほしいですか。  
> *Would you like some tea?*  
> **B:** はい、お茶がほしいです。  
> *Yes, I'd like some tea.*

> **A:** 日曜日（にちようび）、何をしたいですか。  
> *What do you want to do on Sunday?*  
> **B:** 映画（えいが）を見（み）たいです。  
> *I want to watch a movie.*

The first asks about an **item**; the second asks about an **action**. In ordinary polite service interactions, other expressions may be more natural than directly asking ほしいですか; here friends are speaking informally but using polite endings.

## Independent comprehension

Without looking at the English lines, write three short notes in English or Japanese: **(1)** what Sota wants to obtain, **(2)** what Mina wants to do, and **(3)** where Mina says she will go. Then try one fictional sentence of your own, such as **水（みず）がほしいです** (“I want water”) or **本を読（よ）みたいです** (“I want to read a book”).

**Self-review (ungraded):** Did you distinguish item from action? Did you identify the speaker? Did you cite the Japanese phrase? Did you avoid assuming that a stated plan and a wish are identical?
