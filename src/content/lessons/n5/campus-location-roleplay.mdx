## Mission: help a visitor find a room

You are helping a new student find the school library and health room. In this **optional** lesson, practise two original, fictional conversations aloud, then swap roles. The written scripts below are models, not claims that a particular audio recording has been checked. If the related L06 model recordings are verified and available, you can listen to those separately before rehearsing. You can complete this page without a microphone, account, or audio.

### What the location words point to

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning in this conversation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ここ | ここ | here, near the speaker |
| そこ | そこ | there, near the listener |
| あそこ | あそこ | over there, away from both |
| どこ | どこ | where |
| 図書室 | としょしつ | school library room |
| 保健室 | ほけんしつ | school health room |
| 受付 | うけつけ | reception desk |
| 入口 | いりぐち | entrance |
| 二階 | にかい | second floor |
| 一階 | いっかい | first floor |

**Pattern:** <span lang="ja">図書室は どこですか。</span> — “Where is the library room?” Answer <span lang="ja">図書室は あそこです。</span> — “The library room is over there.” In <span lang="ja">二階に あります</span>, **に** marks the location where something exists; **あります** is used for a place or thing. Do not say <span lang="ja">図書室は 二階ですか</span> when you mean “Is the library on the second floor?” without context: it may be understood, but <span lang="ja">図書室は 二階に ありますか。</span> explicitly asks whether it is located there.

### Scene A: the reception desk

**Setting:** The visitor stands at the school entrance. The attendant stands at the reception desk nearby; the library room is farther down the corridor, away from both.

| Role | Japanese | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visitor | すみません。図書室は どこですか。 | Excuse me. Where is the library room? |
| Attendant | 図書室は あそこです。 | The library room is over there. |
| Visitor | あそこですか。ありがとうございます。 | Over there? Thank you. |
| Attendant | はい。どういたしまして。 | Yes. You're welcome. |

The visitor repeats **あそこ** to confirm the distant location. **そこ** would suggest a location near the person being addressed, so it would not match this scene.

### Scene B: the health room

**Setting:** A visitor is standing right next to the health-room door. The attendant is several steps away. Both can see the door near the visitor.

| Role | Japanese | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visitor | すみません。保健室は どこですか。 | Excuse me. Where is the health room? |
| Attendant | そこです。あなたの となりです。 | It's there, next to you. |
| Visitor | ここですか。 | Here? |
| Attendant | はい。そこです。 | Yes, there. |

The attendant says **そこ** because the room is near the **listener**. The visitor says **ここ** because the same room is near the **speaker**. This change is not a contradiction: the words depend on who is speaking.

### Guided three-pass rehearsal

1. **Notice:** Read Scene A and point away from both speakers when you say <span lang="ja">あそこ</span>. Say the question slowly, with a natural pause after <span lang="ja">すみません</span>.
2. **Swap:** Read Scene B first as the visitor, then as the attendant. Notice how <span lang="ja">そこ</span> changes to <span lang="ja">ここ</span> when the speaker changes.
3. **Adapt:** Replace <span lang="ja">保健室</span> with <span lang="ja">受付</span>. Imagine that the reception desk is near the visitor. Ask <span lang="ja">受付は どこですか。</span> and reply <span lang="ja">そこです。</span> Then switch roles again.

For a more precise place statement, try <span lang="ja">図書室は 二階に あります。</span> (“The library room is on the second floor”). Do not infer a floor from Scene A: the original dialogue does **not** say which floor the library is on.

### Optional self-review

After speaking, check four things: (1) I asked **どこですか** clearly; (2) I used **ここ／そこ／あそこ** according to the speaker's viewpoint; (3) I swapped roles and changed the location word where needed; (4) I used a polite opening or closing. This is **self-review, not a pronunciation grade**. The written recognition questions below test the language patterns, not oral proficiency.

### Independent mini-challenge

Imagine a visitor at the entrance asks where the cafeteria is. Write two lines: a polite question with <span lang="ja">食堂</span> (しょくどう), and an answer saying the cafeteria is far from both people. Suggested model: <span lang="ja">すみません。食堂は どこですか。</span> / <span lang="ja">食堂は あそこです。</span> You may speak your answer too. Do not enter real personal details.
