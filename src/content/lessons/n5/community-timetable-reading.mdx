## A schedule at a neighborhood community center

**Can-do:** Understand dates, times and basic counters. You are helping a new participant choose a workshop from a fictional Japanese community-center timetable. All names, dates and participant counts are invented for this reading lesson. The weekdays match **November 2026**.

### 1. Read the headings first

| Heading | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 日にち | ひにち | date |
| 時間 | じかん | time |
| 活動 | かつどう | activity |
| 場所 | ばしょ | place |
| 人数 | にんずう | number of people |
| 午前 | ごぜん | a.m. |
| 午後 | ごご | p.m. |
| 教室 | きょうしつ | classroom |
| 調理室 | ちょうりしつ | cooking room |
| 〜人 | 〜にん | people counter (with special readings for one and two) |

**Date reading:** 11月12日 is **じゅういちがつ じゅうににち** (November 12). 13日 is **じゅうさんにち**, and 14日 is **じゅうよっか**. Do not read 14日 as *じゅうよんにち*. **午前9時** is 9 a.m.; **午後3時** is 3 p.m. **午前10時半** means 10:30 a.m. **12人** is **じゅうににん**, twelve people. For one and two people, use **一人（ひとり）** and **二人（ふたり）**.

### 2. Original reading: 町のセンターの予定表

The following is a **written** timetable, not an audio recording. The letters A–F are evidence labels.

| 行 | 日にち | 時間 | 活動（かつどう） | 場所（ばしょ） | 人数（にんずう） |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | 11月12日（木） | 午前9時 | 日本語の会話 | 教室A | 12人 |
| **B** | 11月12日（木） | 午後1時 | 折り紙 | 教室B | 8人 |
| **C** | 11月13日（金） | 午前10時半 | 書道 | 教室A | 6人 |
| **D** | 11月13日（金） | 午後2時 | 料理 | 調理室 | 10人 |
| **E** | 11月14日（土） | 午前11時 | 絵の会 | 教室B | 5人 |
| **F** | 11月14日（土） | 午後3時 | 日本語の会話 | 教室A | 9人 |

**English support (read the Japanese first):** A: November 12, Thursday, 9 a.m., Japanese conversation, classroom A, 12 people. B: November 12, Thursday, 1 p.m., origami, classroom B, 8 people. C: November 13, Friday, 10:30 a.m., calligraphy, classroom A, 6 people. D: November 13, Friday, 2 p.m., cooking, cooking room, 10 people. E: November 14, Saturday, 11 a.m., painting group, classroom B, 5 people. F: November 14, Saturday, 3 p.m., Japanese conversation, classroom A, 9 people.

**Useful activity words:** 日本語の会話（にほんごのかいわ） = Japanese conversation; 折り紙（おりがみ） = origami; 書道（しょどう） = calligraphy; 料理（りょうり） = cooking; 絵の会（えのかい） = painting group. These are activity names, not verbs.

### 3. Follow the row, not your guess

**Worked example 1:** “Where is origami?” Find **折り紙** in row **B**, then read across to **場所**: **教室B**. The evidence is **B — 折り紙 | 教室B**.

**Worked example 2:** “Which activity starts at 10:30?” Find **午前10時半** in row **C**, then read the activity column: **書道**. **半** is half an hour, not an extra hour.

**Worked example 3:** “Is origami free?” The table does not include a fee column. The correct response is **書いてありません** (it is not written). This does *not* mean the activity costs money, or that it is free.

### 4. Guided practice

Cover the English support. Find the Japanese evidence for these requests:

1. Which activity is on Friday at 2 p.m.? **D — 11月13日（金） | 午後2時 | 料理**.
2. How many people are listed for Saturday's painting group? **E — 絵の会 | 5人**.
3. On which day is the second Japanese conversation session? **F — 11月14日（土）**.

Notice that the two **日本語の会話** rows have different dates, times and participant counts. The activity name alone cannot answer “when?” or “how many?”

### 5. Independent reading check

Answer ten questions below using only the Japanese table. For each question, identify the row letter before choosing. After submitting, inspect the cited evidence and distractor explanations. The score is formative practice, not an official JLPT score.

### 6. Optional writing and oral rehearsal

Write two short Japanese sentences about different rows. A model is **書道は11月13日の午前10時半です。料理は午後2時です。** (“Calligraphy is at 10:30 a.m. on November 13. Cooking is at 2 p.m.”) The second sentence assumes the Friday cooking entry in context. You can also read your sentences aloud voluntarily. This page does not record, assess pronunciation or provide audio.

**Self-check:** Are your dates and times copied accurately? Did you use **午前** or **午後** correctly? Does each sentence match its row? Are you avoiding invented prices and other unstated details? Writing and speaking are ungraded self-review.
