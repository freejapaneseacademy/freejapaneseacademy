## Where is everything?

You are helping prepare a small community reading room. You need to find a dictionary, a teacher and a cat that sometimes visits the garden. Japanese often expresses these situations with an **existence verb**, not a direct translation of English “is.”

### 1. Two verbs for being present

| Japanese | Reading | Used for | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">あります</span> | arimasu | Inanimate things, including books, keys, rooms and plants | <span lang="ja">ほんが あります。</span> — There is a book. |
| <span lang="ja">います</span> | imasu | People and animals | <span lang="ja">せんせいが います。</span> — A teacher is here / present. |

A tree is living biologically, but Japanese normally uses <span lang="ja">あります</span> for plants when stating their existence. The practical beginner distinction is **people and animals** versus **other things**. These verbs describe existence or presence; they are not interchangeable with the polite noun ending <span lang="ja">です</span>.

### 2. The sentence pattern

**<span lang="ja">[place] に [thing/person] が あります／います。</span>**

- <span lang="ja">へやに つくえが あります。</span> (Heya ni tsukue ga arimasu.) — There is a desk in the room.
- <span lang="ja">きょうしつに せんせいが います。</span> (Kyōshitsu ni sensei ga imasu.) — There is a teacher in the classroom.
- <span lang="ja">にわに ねこが います。</span> (Niwa ni neko ga imasu.) — There is a cat in the garden.
- <span lang="ja">つくえの うえに じしょが あります。</span> (Tsukue no ue ni jisho ga arimasu.) — There is a dictionary on the desk.

**に** marks the location where something exists. **が** identifies what or who exists. Contrast <span lang="ja">じしょは へやに あります。</span> — “As for the dictionary, it is in the room.” The **は** version starts from an already identifiable dictionary; the **が** version naturally introduces what is present. Both can be grammatical in context, so do not memorize “が is always correct, は is always wrong.”

### 3. Questions and negative answers

Add **か** to ask whether something is there.

- <span lang="ja">へやに かぎが ありますか。</span> — Is there a key in the room?
- <span lang="ja">はい、あります。</span> — Yes, there is.
- <span lang="ja">いいえ、ありません。</span> — No, there isn't.
- <span lang="ja">としょかんに ともだちが いますか。</span> — Is your friend in the library?
- <span lang="ja">いいえ、いません。</span> — No, they aren't.

Use <span lang="ja">ありません</span> for the negative of <span lang="ja">あります</span>, and <span lang="ja">いません</span> for the negative of <span lang="ja">います</span>. When asking **where** an already known object is, you can use <span lang="ja">じしょは どこに ありますか。</span> — “Where is the dictionary?”

### 4. Original conversation: setting up the reading room

**Mika:** <span lang="ja">へやに じしょが ありますか。</span> — Is there a dictionary in the room?

**Haru:** <span lang="ja">はい。つくえの うえに あります。</span> — Yes. It's on the desk.

**Mika:** <span lang="ja">せんせいは どこに いますか。</span> — Where is the teacher?

**Haru:** <span lang="ja">せんせいは としょかんに います。</span> — The teacher is in the library.

**Mika:** <span lang="ja">にわに ねこが いますか。</span> — Is there a cat in the garden?

**Haru:** <span lang="ja">いいえ、いません。でも、にわに きが あります。</span> — No, there isn't. But there is a tree in the garden.

The **dictionary** is on the desk, the **teacher** is in the library, and there is **no cat** in the garden. The tree uses <span lang="ja">あります</span>, even though it is a plant. Read the Japanese evidence before answering; don't infer a cat from the mere mention of a garden.

### 5. Common errors to catch

**Verb mismatch:** <span lang="ja">せんせいが あります</span> is not the ordinary way to say a teacher is present. Use <span lang="ja">せんせいが います</span>. Conversely, a book normally takes <span lang="ja">あります</span>.

**Particle reversal:** <span lang="ja">へやが ほんに あります</span> does not mean “There is a book in the room.” Write <span lang="ja">へやに ほんが あります。</span>

**Don't use を for the location** in this existence pattern. The location takes <span lang="ja">に</span>. Other verbs and meanings may require different particles.

### 6. Practise, then create

**Controlled:** Complete <span lang="ja">にわに いぬが ____。</span> for “There is a dog in the garden.” Answer: <span lang="ja">います</span>. For a chair, use <span lang="ja">いすが あります</span> instead.

**Guided:** Imagine a room with a desk and a teacher, and a garden with a tree. Say three true sentences using <span lang="ja">に</span>, <span lang="ja">が</span> and the right existence verb.

**Independent writing:** Write **three connected sentences** about an imaginary classroom: one inanimate object, one person or animal, and one yes/no question. Model: <span lang="ja">きょうしつに ほんが あります。きょうしつに せんせいが います。へやに かぎが ありますか。</span> Check each noun's category, the に/が roles, and the final か. This is learner self-review, not automatically graded writing.

**Optional speaking:** Read the six-turn exchange aloud, then change one location and one object. Speaking is optional and no microphone or pronunciation score is required.
