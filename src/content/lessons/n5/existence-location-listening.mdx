## Two places, two short conversations

A visitor is searching for a notebook in a **study room**. Later, a student asks about a teacher and a bag in a **classroom**. These are separate fictional settings. Your goal is to understand **what exists, what does not, and where something is**, from slow, clear speech—not to identify kanji by sound.

### Listen for these words

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 机（つくえ） | つくえ | desk |
| 上（うえ） | うえ | on top of |
| ノート | ノート | notebook |
| 青い（あおい） | あおい | blue |
| 赤い（あかい） | あかい | red |
| 教室（きょうしつ） | きょうしつ | classroom |
| 先生（せんせい） | せんせい | teacher |
| かばん | かばん | bag |
| 廊下（ろうか） | ろうか | hallway |

**Sound and meaning:** Listen for **あります** when something inanimate exists, and **います** for a person. **ありません** means an inanimate thing is not there. The particle **に** introduces a location, and **が** introduces the thing or person whose existence is being stated. These are meaning cues; do not score yourself on spelling while listening.

### Track A — a notebook on the desk

On your first play, identify the item being sought. On the second, listen for the color in the positive answer. The visitor then asks about **another color**; the staff member's **いいえ** changes the answer. Keep the blue and red notebooks separate.

### Track B — a teacher and a bag

On your first play, identify **who** is in the classroom. Then listen for the bag. Does the final answer place it in the classroom, or somewhere else? The two sentences in the last turn form a correction: **ありません** followed by **あります**.

### Three-pass listening routine

1. Play each track without opening the transcript. Identify the general topic.
2. Replay and write short notes: *desk — item/color/absent color*; *classroom — person/bag location*.
3. Answer all six questions from the audio. After checking, open the speaker-by-speaker transcripts to compare what you heard. Opening a transcript marks the attempt **transcript-assisted**, even if you close it again.

### Grammar reminder (not an answer key)

**部屋にいすがあります。** means “There is a chair in the room.” **部屋に先生がいます。** means “There is a teacher in the room.” The location takes **に**; the chair or teacher takes **が**. A negative answer may change the topic to **は**, as in **いすはありません**. Use the spoken context to interpret each question rather than assuming every item exists.

### After listening — write and check

Write **three short Japanese sentences** about an imaginary classroom. Include a person with **います**, an object with **あります**, and one negative statement with **ありません**. For example: **教室に学生がいます。机の上に本があります。かばんはありません。** Check that the human takes **います**, the book takes **あります**, and the negative is consistent with your imaginary room. This writing is self-reviewed, not machine-scored.

**Optional speaking:** Ask **教室に先生がいますか。** and respond **はい、います。** or **いいえ、いません。** Rehearse only after the licensed recordings are available; no microphone, native-speaker assessment or pronunciation grade is implied.

### Self-check

Can you hear the contrast between **あります** and **います**? Did you notice both negative corrections? Can you name the hallway as the bag's location without reading the transcript? Replay as needed and review your evidence.
