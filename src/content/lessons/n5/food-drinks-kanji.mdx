## Read what is on the café table

**Can-do:** Name common foods and express simple preferences. Today you will connect the **written forms** of everyday food and drink words to their kana readings and meanings. This is a *reading and writing* lesson, not a test of spoken pronunciation. Japanese kanji can have multiple readings; learn each character **inside a real word**.

Imagine that a student writes this short note after visiting a café:

> 今日（きょう）は、友（とも）だちと昼（ひる）ご飯（はん）を食（た）べます。わたしは水（みず）を飲（の）みます。友だちはお茶（ちゃ）が好（す）きです。

**Meaning:** Today I will eat lunch with a friend. I will drink water. My friend likes tea. This is an original fictional note. In everyday writing, 昼ご飯 is also often written 昼ごはん.

## Eight character–word connections

| Kanji | Word with reading | Meaning | What to notice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 食 | 食べます（たべます） | eat / will eat (polite) | 食 is read **た** here; べます is okurigana |
| 飲 | 飲みます（のみます） | drink / will drink (polite) | 飲 is read **の** here; みます follows |
| 水 | 水（みず） | water | The whole word is みず |
| 茶 | お茶（おちゃ） | tea | お is a polite prefix; 茶 is ちゃ |
| 好 | 好き（すき） | like / fond of | 好 is read す here; き follows |
| 飯 | ご飯（ごはん） | cooked rice; meal | 飯 is はん in this word; ご is a prefix |
| 昼 | 昼（ひる） | daytime / noon | In 昼ご飯（ひるごはん）, it means lunch |
| 友 | 友だち（ともだち） | friend | 友 is とも; だち is written in hiragana |

**Read the word, not just the character.** For example, 食 has other readings in other words. This lesson asks you to recognize **食べます（たべます）**, not to memorize all readings of 食. Similarly, 茶 is ちゃ in お茶; don't assume every compound uses that reading.

## Observe the shapes and meaning

- **食** (eat) and **飲** (drink) appear in action words. The kana endings distinguish 食べます and 飲みます.
- **水** (water) and **茶** (tea) name drinks. **お茶** uses the polite お; **水** normally does not need it.
- **好** appears in **好きです**. In the basic preference pattern, the liked thing takes **が**: **お茶が好きです。** (“I like tea.”)
- **飯** appears in **ご飯**. **ご飯を食べます。** (“I eat rice / a meal.”) The object of the eating action takes **を**.
- **昼** in **昼ご飯** tells you which meal; **友** in **友だち** tells you who is involved.

**Writing note:** Use a Japanese keyboard or IME if available. Typing たべます and converting to 食べます is fine; copying by hand is also fine. Keep the kana endings: **食ます** and **飲ます** are not the polite forms taught here. You do not need to master stroke order to complete this lesson.

## A second café message

**みき:** 昼（ひる）ご飯（はん）は何（なに）を食（た）べますか。  
**そら:** パンを食（た）べます。水（みず）を飲（の）みます。  
**みき:** わたしはお茶（ちゃ）が好（す）きです。

**Meaning:** Miki asks what Sora will eat for lunch. Sora will eat bread and drink water. Miki says she likes tea. Notice that the final sentence **does not say** Miki ordered tea: a preference and an order are different facts.

### Controlled → guided → independent practice

1. **Controlled recognition:** Cover the readings in the table. Match **水**, **茶**, **食**, and **飲** to みず, ちゃ, た, and の *in the words taught above*. Uncover to check.
2. **Guided retrieval:** Copy **水を飲みます。** (“I drink water.”) Change 水 to お茶. Your sentence becomes **お茶を飲みます。** Keep **を** because this describes an action.
3. **Meaning check:** Compare **お茶が好きです** (“I like tea”) and **お茶を飲みます** (“I drink tea”). One describes a preference; the other an action. Do not interchange **が** and **を** here.
4. **Independent use:** Write two short Japanese sentences for a fictional café visit: one about a food you eat and one about a drink you like. Example: **ご飯を食べます。水が好きです。** Read each sentence back and verify its particle and kana endings.
5. **Optional oral rehearsal:** Read the two sentences aloud using the kana guide; no microphone recording or pronunciation grade is required. Listening to a real recorded ordering exchange belongs to L06.

## Self-check before moving on

Can you identify the word **友だち** in the note? Can you explain why **昼ご飯** is lunch, not dinner? Can you distinguish **食べます** from **飲みます** even if both occur next to 水 or ご飯? If not, return to the word table and practice with the quiz below.
