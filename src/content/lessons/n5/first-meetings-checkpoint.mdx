## A first-meeting checkpoint

At the welcome desk for a language exchange, you meet four new people. You need to introduce yourself, distinguish a person's country from their nationality, and politely correct a mistaken occupation. This checkpoint combines **six language questions, four reading questions and four listening questions**. Budget about 28 minutes, including a review of mistakes.

### 1. Language toolkit

| Japanese | Reading / meaning | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">わたしは エマです。</span> | *Watashi wa Ema desu.* — I'm Emma. | は marks the topic; です makes the statement polite. |
| <span lang="ja">たなかさんは せんせいですか。</span> | Is Tanaka a teacher? | Final か turns the statement into a question. |
| <span lang="ja">いいえ。せんせいじゃありません。</span> | No. (I am) not a teacher. | じゃありません is polite noun-predicate negation. |
| <span lang="ja">がくせいです。</span> | (I am) a student. | The topic can be omitted when understood. |
| <span lang="ja">にほんごのせんせい</span> | Japanese-language teacher | の connects the subject taught to the role. |
| <span lang="ja">ちゅうごくじん</span> | Chinese person | じん expresses nationality; ちゅうごく alone means China. |

**Watch the spelling:** The topic particle <span lang="ja">は</span> is pronounced *wa*, even though the same kana in a regular word is usually *ha*. Do not add <span lang="ja">か</span> to an affirmative sentence unless you mean to ask a question. A question such as “Are you a teacher?” does **not** prove that the person is a teacher; the answer may reject the guess.

### 2. Read the original welcome-desk cards

These four profiles are fictional, created for this checkpoint. Read each one, then point to the exact Japanese word that supports your answer.

| Card | Name | Nationality | Occupation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | <span lang="ja">エマ</span> | <span lang="ja">アメリカじん</span> | <span lang="ja">がくせい</span> |
| B | <span lang="ja">たなか</span> | <span lang="ja">にほんじん</span> | <span lang="ja">にほんごのせんせい</span> |
| C | <span lang="ja">リー</span> | <span lang="ja">ちゅうごくじん</span> | <span lang="ja">がくせい</span> |
| D | <span lang="ja">マリア</span> | <span lang="ja">スペインじん</span> | <span lang="ja">かいしゃいん</span> |

Read a card as a short identity statement. For example, <span lang="ja">エマさんは アメリカじんです。がくせいです。</span> means “Emma is American. (She is) a student.” The second sentence leaves out the topic because it remains Emma. **Do not infer** that nationality determines occupation.

### 3. Listening evidence from the chapter

The listening section reuses the two original recordings from **N5-M01-C01-L06**; it does not synthesize or substitute new audio. In dialogue A, listen for Emma's introduction and Tanaka's confirmation. In dialogue B, listen for Lee's nationality and the correction of a mistaken occupation. Listen without a transcript first, then use the transcript to check the evidence. The interactive checkpoint will not unlock listening questions unless the real recordings are playable.

If the audio has not been generated and verified, complete the ten written questions now and return to the listening section later. **Reading the transcript alone does not count as listening practice.**

### 4. Guided writing and optional speaking

Write three short connected sentences about a *fictional* person using a name, a nationality and an occupation. For example:

<span lang="ja">マリアさんは スペインじんです。せんせいじゃありません。かいしゃいんです。</span>

“Maria is Spanish. She is not a teacher. She is a company employee.”

Check yourself: Is the topic clear? Is each statement polite? Does the negative sentence really contradict the false occupation? Optional speaking: read your three sentences aloud once, then change the occupation and correct yourself. No recording or pronunciation score is required.

### 5. Review and spaced repetition

After submitting, read the evidence shown beside each incorrect answer. For each weak item, make a private review card with the Japanese phrase on one side and its function or meaning on the other. Revisit the card tomorrow, then after several days. A wrong answer is a study signal, not a formal JLPT score.
