## First, then: describing a sequence

**Can-do:** Explain two ordinary actions in Japanese: “Wash your hands and eat,” or “After reading, write.” The **て-form** is a linking form, not a tense. In a sequence, the final verb normally carries the sentence's tense and politeness.

**Today's fictional scene:** At a community cooking class, a volunteer gives a short instruction card. You will learn the form before reading the card, then check whether each instruction has the intended order.

### 1. See the pattern before memorizing it

**Group 2:** Remove the dictionary-form **る** and add **て**: 食べる（たべる）→ **食べて** (eat); 見る（みる）→ **見て** (look). **起きる（おきる）→ 起きて** (get up). Do not write 食べりて.

**Group 1:** The final kana determines the change. Learn these sound families as whole pairs:

| Dictionary form | て-form | Rule | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 書く（かく） | 書いて（かいて） | く → いて | write |
| 泳ぐ（およぐ） | 泳いで（およいで） | ぐ → いで | swim |
| 話す（はなす） | 話して（はなして） | す → して | speak |
| 待つ（まつ） | 待って（まって） | つ → って | wait |
| 買う（かう） | 買って（かって） | う → って | buy |
| 帰る（かえる） | 帰って（かえって） | る → って | return |
| 読む（よむ） | 読んで（よんで） | む → んで | read |
| 遊ぶ（あそぶ） | 遊んで（あそんで） | ぶ → んで | play |
| 死ぬ（しぬ） | 死んで（しんで） | ぬ → んで | die |

The **て / で** distinction matters: 読む becomes **読んで**, not 読んて. Group 1 verbs ending in **る**, such as 帰る, do not follow the Group 2 pattern.

**Important exception:** 行く（いく）→ **行って（いって）**, not 行いて. Irregular verbs: **する → して** (do), **来る（くる）→ 来て（きて）** (come). In 来て the reading is **きて**.

### 2. Connect actions with 〜て

**手（て）を洗（あら）って、朝（あさ）ごはんを食（た）べます。** — “I wash my hands and eat breakfast.” Here 洗う → 洗って; the final 食べます expresses a polite nonpast sentence. The two actions are presented in order, but **〜て** alone can also connect closely related actions or states; context matters.

**図書館（としょかん）に行（い）って、本（ほん）を読（よ）みます。** — “I go to the library and read a book.” The destination is marked by **に**, and the book as object by **を**. The first verb is **行って**, not 行いて.

**昨日（きのう）、駅（えき）に行（い）って、切符（きっぷ）を買（か）いました。** — “Yesterday I went to the station and bought a ticket.” The final **買いました** marks the sequence as past; **行って** does not itself mean “went.”

### 3. Make “after doing” explicit with 〜てから

Use **verb て-form + から** when you want to say **after completing A, do B**.

**本を読んでから、ノートに書きます。** — “After reading the book, I write in my notebook.” The first action precedes the second. Compare **本を読んで、ノートに書きます。** — “I read the book and write in my notebook.” Both may describe a sequence, but **〜てから** highlights the “after” relationship.

**手を洗ってから、料理（りょうり）を始（はじ）めます。** — “After washing my hands, I start cooking.” This is an original instructional example. **から** attaches to **洗って**, not to the dictionary form 洗う. Do not write **洗うから** to mean “after washing”; **洗うから** usually introduces a reason or a different context.

### 4. Read the original cooking-class card

> **土曜日（どようび）の料理教室（りょうりきょうしつ） — Saturday cooking class**
>
> ① 九時に教室（きょうしつ）に来（き）てください。
>
> ② 手を洗ってから、野菜（やさい）を切（き）ります。
>
> ③ 野菜を切って、なべに入（い）れます。
>
> ④ 料理を作（つく）ってから、みんなで食べます。

English: “1. Please come to the classroom at nine. 2. After washing your hands, cut the vegetables. 3. Cut the vegetables and put them in the pot. 4. After making the food, everyone eats together.”

**Evidence check:** Which action must happen before cutting? The card says **手を洗ってから、野菜を切ります**. Which form is a request rather than a linked sequence? **来てください**. Here **〜てください** means “please do”; it is not interchangeable with **〜てから**. Requests are studied more fully in Chapter 03.

### 5. Guided changes and common mistakes

- **読む → 読んで**: change む to んで. **本を読んでから、寝（ね）ます。** — “After reading a book, I go to bed.”
- **書く → 書いて**: change く to いて. **名前（なまえ）を書いて、出（だ）します。** — “Write your name and submit it.”
- **行く → 行って**: memorize the exception. **学校（がっこう）に行って、勉強（べんきょう）します。** — “I go to school and study.”
- **帰る → 帰って**: Group 1, not Group 2. **家（いえ）に帰ってから、休（やす）みます。** — “After returning home, I rest.”

**Avoid these errors:** 読みて ✗ → 読んで ✓; 食べって ✗ → 食べて ✓; 行いて ✗ → 行って ✓; 来て is read **きて**, not くて. Also, do not translate every て as “and then”: **見てください** is a polite request, and the meaning of a て-clause depends on its construction.

### 6. Independent use

Write **three short Japanese sentences** about a fictional morning. Include one Group 1 て-form, one Group 2 or irregular て-form, and at least one **〜てから**. Compare with this model **after** you draft:

> 朝七時に起きて、顔（かお）を洗います。朝ごはんを食べてから、学校に行きます。学校に行って、友だちと話します。

“I get up at seven and wash my face. After breakfast, I go to school. I go to school and talk with a friend.” Your own times and actions may differ.

**Self-review:** Did you choose the right て/で ending? Did you use 行って and 来て correctly? Does 〜てから show the order you intended? Is the last verb polite? Reading, writing and optional spoken rehearsal are ungraded; the interactive check below scores only the written recognition questions.

### 7. Review

Remember **Group 2: remove る + て**, **Group 1: sound-family changes**, **する → して**, **来る → 来て**, and **行く → 行って**. Use **〜て** to connect, and **〜てから** to emphasize “after doing.” Read your own three sentences aloud if you wish; no audio, microphone assessment or pronunciation verification is provided here.
