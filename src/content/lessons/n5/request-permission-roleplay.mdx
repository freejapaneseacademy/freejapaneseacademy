## Mission: make a polite request, then switch roles

In two fictional settings, you are a visitor and a helper. Read each line, rehearse it aloud **only if you want to**, then swap roles. This optional lesson never requires microphone access or a recording. The related L06 listening lesson is the chapter's audio study; **no verified audio is embedded or claimed here**. Use its model recordings only when independently available and verified.

### Four useful speech acts

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning and use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ちょっと手伝ってください。 | ちょっと てつだって ください | Please help me for a moment. A polite request, not a question about permission. |
| ここに座ってもいいですか。 | ここに すわっても いいですか | May I sit here? A question asking permission. |
| はい、座ってもいいですよ。 | はい、すわっても いいですよ | Yes, you may sit. A friendly affirmative response. |
| ここで写真を撮ってはいけません。 | ここで しゃしんを とっては いけません | Taking photos here is prohibited. A rule, not simply a preference. |

The て-form is the bridge: **手伝う → 手伝って**, **座る → 座って**, **撮る → 撮って**, **使う → 使って**. In a request, **〜てください** asks another person to act. **〜てもいいですか** asks whether an action is permitted; **〜てもいいです** grants permission. **〜てはいけません** communicates prohibition. These expressions are not interchangeable.

**Politeness note:** 〜てください is a normal polite request, but can sound directive depending on the relationship and situation. Add すみません or ちょっと for a softer opening. To ask for help rather than give a command, **手伝ってもらえますか** is another useful, slightly more advanced option.

### Scene A — preparing a classroom display

**Situation:** A student asks a classmate for help and checks whether a pen may be used. The classmate agrees.

| Speaker | Japanese | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Student | すみません。ちょっと手伝ってください。 | Excuse me. Please help me for a moment. |
| Classmate | はい、いいですよ。 | Yes, of course. |
| Student | このペンを使ってもいいですか。 | May I use this pen? |
| Classmate | はい、使ってもいいですよ。 | Yes, you may use it. |
| Student | ありがとうございます。 | Thank you. |

**Notice:** 使ってもいいですか is a permission question; the classmate's 使ってもいいですよ grants permission. The exchange does not establish who owns the pen.

### Scene B — community-hall photography rules

**Situation:** A visitor wants to take a photo inside a fictional community hall. A staff member explains the rule and suggests a different location.

| Speaker | Japanese | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visitor | すみません。ここで写真を撮ってもいいですか。 | Excuse me. May I take photos here? |
| Staff | すみません。ここで写真を撮ってはいけません。 | Sorry. Taking photos here is not allowed. |
| Visitor | そうですか。外で撮ってもいいですか。 | I see. May I take them outside? |
| Staff | はい、外で撮ってもいいですよ。 | Yes, you may take them outside. |
| Visitor | わかりました。ありがとうございます。 | Understood. Thank you. |

The prohibition applies to **ここで** (here, inside the hall). The staff explicitly allows photos **外で** (outside). Do not overgeneralize the rule to all locations. **で** marks where an action happens; **に** marks the destination or target in expressions such as ここに座る.

### Three rehearsal passes

1. **Observe:** Read Scene A once, following the Japanese and English lines. Notice that each question receives a relevant answer.
2. **Switch:** Say the student lines and then the classmate lines. Repeat Scene B first as visitor, then as staff. Pauses are fine; accuracy matters more than speed.
3. **Adapt:** Imagine a library where eating is prohibited but reading is allowed. Try: **ここで食べてもいいですか。** (“May I eat here?”) → **いいえ、ここで食べてはいけません。** (“No, eating here is not allowed.”) Then **ここで本を読んでもいいですか。** (“May I read here?”) → **はい、読んでもいいですよ。** (“Yes, you may read here.”) These are *fictional* rules, not claims about real libraries.

### Common repair points

- **撮ってください** means “Please take a photo,” not “May I take a photo?” Use **撮ってもいいですか** for permission.
- **使ってはいけませんか** is a negative question about prohibition, not the ordinary direct way to ask permission at this level. Prefer **使ってもいいですか**.
- **はい、使ってはいけません** is a confusing reply to a positive permission question. To refuse clearly, say **いいえ、使ってはいけません** or **すみません、使わないでください** depending on the intended rule/request.
- Do not use the dictionary form directly before ください in this pattern: **手伝うください** is incorrect; **手伝ってください** is correct.

### Independent mini-dialogue

Choose one fictional scenario: borrowing a pencil in class or asking about photography at an exhibition. Write or speak **four short turns**: a polite opening, a request or permission question, an appropriate reply, and a thank-you. Compare with the scenes above. An alternative valid response might grant permission with **どうぞ** (“Go ahead”) or politely refuse with **すみません、だめです** (“Sorry, that's not allowed”), depending on the scenario.

### Self-review, not a speaking grade

Check whether you (1) used the right て-form, (2) distinguished requests from permission questions, (3) responded consistently with permission or prohibition, (4) switched roles with polite openings and closings, and (5) respected the location specified by the rule. You can rehearse silently. The interactive check below scores **written recognition only**; it does not assess spoken fluency, pronunciation or cultural competence.
