## A plan across three days

**Can-do:** Say what happened yesterday and what is planned. Imagine two classmates looking at a calendar before a study session. To understand their conversation, you first need to recognize *when* each activity happens. This lesson focuses on written vocabulary. The following grammar lesson explains past polite verbs in detail.

## 1. Twelve expressions in context

| Expression | Kana reading | Meaning | Original example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">昨日</span> | きのう | yesterday | <span lang="ja">昨日、勉強しました。</span> — I studied yesterday. |
| <span lang="ja">今日</span> | きょう | today | <span lang="ja">今日は学校に行きます。</span> — I am going to school today. |
| <span lang="ja">明日</span> | あした | tomorrow | <span lang="ja">明日、図書館に行きます。</span> — I will go to the library tomorrow. |
| <span lang="ja">先週</span> | せんしゅう | last week | <span lang="ja">先週、本を読みました。</span> — I read a book last week. |
| <span lang="ja">今週</span> | こんしゅう | this week | <span lang="ja">今週、日本語を勉強します。</span> — I am studying Japanese this week. |
| <span lang="ja">来週</span> | らいしゅう | next week | <span lang="ja">来週、友だちに会います。</span> — I will meet a friend next week. |
| <span lang="ja">今朝</span> | けさ | this morning | <span lang="ja">今朝、六時に起きました。</span> — I got up at six this morning. |
| <span lang="ja">今晩</span> | こんばん | this evening | <span lang="ja">今晩、家で勉強します。</span> — I will study at home this evening. |
| <span lang="ja">朝</span> | あさ | morning | <span lang="ja">朝、パンを食べます。</span> — I eat bread in the morning. |
| <span lang="ja">昼</span> | ひる | daytime / noon | <span lang="ja">昼にごはんを食べます。</span> — I eat a meal at noon. |
| <span lang="ja">夜</span> | よる | night / evening | <span lang="ja">夜、本を読みます。</span> — I read a book at night. |
| <span lang="ja">予定</span> | よてい | plan / schedule | <span lang="ja">明日の予定は何ですか。</span> — What are your plans for tomorrow? |

Read the kana before guessing from the kanji. **昨日** is きのう, not a simple reading of each character in isolation. **今日** is きょう, **明日** is あした in these examples (あす is another reading), and **今朝** is けさ. These are whole-word readings worth memorizing.

## 2. Time words and verb endings work together

- <span lang="ja">昨日、学校で勉強しました。</span> (きのう、がっこうでべんきょうしました。) — I studied at school yesterday. **昨日** locates the action in the past; **しました** is the polite past affirmative.
- <span lang="ja">昨日、テレビを見ませんでした。</span> (きのう、テレビをみませんでした。) — I did not watch TV yesterday. **ませんでした** is polite past negative.
- <span lang="ja">明日、学校で勉強します。</span> (あした、がっこうでべんきょうします。) — I will study at school tomorrow. **ます** is nonpast and can express a future plan when the time is clear.
- <span lang="ja">来週、映画を見ます。</span> (らいしゅう、えいがをみます。) — I will watch a film next week.

**Important:** Japanese does not have a separate future tense ending corresponding to English “will.” **明日** or **来週** makes the future interpretation clear. Conversely, **昨日、勉強します** is a mismatch when you mean “I studied yesterday”; use **勉強しました**.

## 3. Morning, noon and night

**朝** (あさ) is morning in general; **今朝** (けさ) means *this morning*. **昼** (ひる) refers to daytime/noon; **夜** (よる) is evening/night, and **今晩** (こんばん) means *this evening/tonight*. **今朝** may refer to an already completed action even though it includes “this”; choose the verb ending according to whether the action is complete.

Compare <span lang="ja">今朝、パンを食べました。</span> — “I ate bread this morning” — with <span lang="ja">今晩、パンを食べます。</span> — “I will eat bread tonight.” A clock time often takes **に** (<span lang="ja">七時に</span>), but broad relative expressions such as **昨日**, **今日**, **明日**, **先週** and **来週** normally do not take **に** when used adverbially.

## 4. A fictional calendar note

> <span lang="ja">ゆきのメモ</span> — Yuki's note
>
> <span lang="ja">昨日、図書館で日本語を勉強しました。</span> — Yesterday I studied Japanese at the library.
>
> <span lang="ja">今朝、七時に起きました。</span> — This morning I got up at seven.
>
> <span lang="ja">今日、学校で友だちに会います。</span> — Today I am meeting a friend at school.
>
> <span lang="ja">明日、家で本を読みます。</span> — Tomorrow I will read a book at home.
>
> <span lang="ja">来週、映画を見ます。</span> — Next week I will watch a film.

**Evidence check:** When did Yuki study at the library? Find **昨日**. What will Yuki do next week? Find **来週、映画を見ます**. The note does not tell us whether Yuki watched a film last week; do not infer it.

## 5. Avoid common confusions

**先週** (last week) and **来週** (next week) point in opposite directions. **今日** (today) is not automatically past or future: the verb and context tell you whether an event is completed or planned. **予定** refers to a schedule or plan, not to a completed action. **今晩** is about tonight, while **夜** can describe nights generally.

## 6. Controlled → guided → independent practice

First, cover the English meanings and recall the readings of **昨日**, **今日**, **明日**, **今朝**, **先週**, and **来週**. Next, fill these gaps: “yesterday” → <span lang="ja">＿＿、本を読みました。</span>; “tomorrow” → <span lang="ja">＿＿、本を読みます。</span>; “next week” → <span lang="ja">＿＿、友だちに会います。</span>. Answers: **昨日**, **明日**, **来週**.

Finally, write two sentences about a fictional student's schedule: one completed action yesterday and one plan tomorrow. Model: <span lang="ja">昨日、家で勉強しました。明日、図書館に行きます。</span> — “Yesterday I studied at home. Tomorrow I will go to the library.” Check the time expression, verb ending and particle. This writing is self-reviewed, not automatically graded.
