## Welcome to the hobby club

**Can-do preview:** You can introduce a hobby you like, understand a classmate's preference, and recognize a simple statement about what someone can do. The scene is fictional. Read for *who* likes *what*, not for a word-by-word translation.

> 先生（せんせい）：みなさん、こんにちは。好（す）きなことを話（はな）しましょう。  
> ミカ：私（わたし）は音楽（おんがく）が好（す）きです。ピアノも好（す）きです。  
> ケン：私（わたし）はスポーツが好（す）きです。サッカーが好（す）きです。  
> 先生：ミカさんはサッカーが好（す）きですか。  
> ミカ：いいえ、サッカーはあまり好（す）きではありません。でも、見（み）るのは好（す）きです。  
> 先生：ケンさんはピアノができますか。  
> ケン：いいえ、できません。でも、ギターは少（すこ）しできます。

**English:** Teacher: Hello, everyone. Let's talk about things we like. Mika: I like music. I also like piano. Ken: I like sports. I like soccer. Teacher: Mika, do you like soccer? Mika: No, I don't like soccer very much. But I like watching it. Teacher: Ken, can you play piano? Ken: No, I can't. But I can play guitar a little.

The teacher is asking about **preferences**, then about **ability**. These are different questions. Saying you like something does **not** automatically mean you can do it well.

## Useful expressions to notice

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning in this scene |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 好きです | すきです | like / am fond of |
| 音楽が好きです | おんがくがすきです | I like music |
| サッカーが好きですか | サッカーがすきですか | Do you like soccer? |
| あまり好きではありません | あまりすきではありません | don't like very much |
| できます | できます | can do / am able to |
| できません | できません | cannot do |
| 少し | すこし | a little |
| ピアノ | ピアノ | piano |
| ギター | ギター | guitar |

**Pattern 1 — preference:** 私は音楽**が**好きです。 (“I like music.”) Here **が** marks what is liked. 好き is a *na-adjective*, not the English verb “to like.” In polite Japanese, **好きです** is a complete predicate.

**Pattern 2 — negative preference:** サッカーは**あまり好きではありません**。 (“I don't like soccer very much.”) The **は** highlights soccer as the topic under contrast; **あまり** normally accompanies a negative expression. This is softer than a blunt declaration of dislike.

**Pattern 3 — ability preview:** ギターは少し**できます**。 (“I can play guitar a little.”) With activities such as instruments, **できます** can mean “can play/do.” More detailed ability constructions come later. It is **not** interchangeable with **好きです**.

## A second club sign-up card

> 名前（なまえ）：ユイ  
> 好きなこと：読書（どくしょ）と絵（え）  
> ひとこと：本（ほん）が好（す）きです。絵（え）を描（か）くことも好（す）きです。でも、ダンスはあまり好（す）きではありません。

**English:** Name: Yui. Interests: reading and pictures/art. Message: I like books. I also like drawing pictures. But I don't like dancing very much.

**Notice:** **絵を描くこと** turns “drawing pictures” into an activity you can talk about as something you like. You do not need to produce this longer pattern yet. For now, you can confidently say **本が好きです** or **音楽が好きです**.

## Meaning check: three common confusions

1. **好きです ≠ 上手です.** 好きです means you *like* it; 上手（じょうず）です means someone is *skilled* at it. Avoid calling yourself 上手 as a casual boast; you can simply say what you like or can do.
2. **できます ≠ 好きです.** ケンさんはギターが少しできます could be said even if Ken has not told us whether he likes guitar.
3. **あまり好きではありません** is a polite, softened negative preference; it does not mean “I absolutely hate it.”

## Guided rehearsal

Read the club dialogue again. **Who likes soccer?** Ken. **Who likes watching soccer but does not particularly like soccer itself?** Mika. **Who can play a little guitar?** Ken. Point to the exact Japanese sentence before revealing the English.

Now replace the hobby in the frame **私は___が好きです**. Try **本（ほん）**, **音楽（おんがく）**, and **スポーツ**. Say it quietly or type it; no microphone is needed.

**Short question-answer model:**

> A：何（なに）が好（す）きですか。  
> B：音楽（おんがく）が好（す）きです。Aさんは？  
> A：私（わたし）は本（ほん）が好（す）きです。

**English:** A: What do you like? B: I like music. How about you? A: I like books.

## Independent use (ungraded)

Write **two to four short lines** introducing yourself to a fictional club. Include one thing you like, optionally one thing you do not like very much, and optionally an activity you can do. You may use invented details.

**Model:** 私はユキです。音楽が好きです。サッカーはあまり好きではありません。ピアノが少しできます。  
**English:** I'm Yuki. I like music. I don't like soccer very much. I can play piano a little.

**Self-check:** Does **が** follow the liked thing? Does **あまり** go with a negative? Are you keeping liking and ability separate? Other natural answers are possible. Your writing and optional oral rehearsal are **not automatically graded**.
