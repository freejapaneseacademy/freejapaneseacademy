## Travel is about two different questions

**Can-do:** Explain transport choices and who accompanies you. Imagine three friends arranging a Saturday outing. Someone asks **どうやって行きますか。** (“How will you get there?”) and another asks **だれと行きますか。** (“Who are you going with?”). A vehicle answers the first question; a person answers the second.

This is a **written vocabulary lesson**, not a listening test. Kana readings support your own rehearsal; no verified recording or pronunciation assessment is supplied.

## 1. Twelve useful words and expressions

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning | Usage note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">バス</span> | バス | bus | a road vehicle used by many passengers |
| <span lang="ja">電車</span> | でんしゃ | train | a train, commonly used for rail travel |
| <span lang="ja">地下鉄</span> | ちかてつ | subway | an underground urban railway |
| <span lang="ja">自転車</span> | じてんしゃ | bicycle | a bicycle, usually with で for travel |
| <span lang="ja">タクシー</span> | タクシー | taxi | a hired car |
| <span lang="ja">徒歩</span> | とほ | on foot | a formal noun for walking, often on signs |
| <span lang="ja">友達</span> | ともだち | friend | a friend or friends, depending on context |
| <span lang="ja">家族</span> | かぞく | family | family members as a group |
| <span lang="ja">母</span> | はは | my mother | used for one's own mother when speaking to outsiders |
| <span lang="ja">父</span> | ちち | my father | used for one's own father when speaking to outsiders |
| <span lang="ja">一人で</span> | ひとりで | alone | by oneself, without a companion |
| <span lang="ja">いっしょに</span> | いっしょに | together | together, often used with と |

**Reading caution:** 電車 is でんしゃ; 自転車 is じてんしゃ. The shared 車 does not make the whole words interchangeable. 地下鉄 (ちかてつ) means subway, a more specific kind of rail transport. 徒歩 (とほ) is common in signs and route information; in conversation **歩いて行きます** (“go on foot / walk there”) is often more natural.

**Family words:** When speaking to someone outside your family, **母 (はは)** and **父 (ちち)** refer to *your own* mother and father. **お母さん** and **お父さん** are common ways to address someone or refer politely to another person's parents. Do not treat these as universal substitutes.

## 2. Vehicle or companion?

In this chapter, **で** marks a means of transport: <span lang="ja">バスで行きます。</span> (“I go by bus.”) **と** marks a companion: <span lang="ja">友達と行きます。</span> (“I go with a friend.”)

Put them together:

- <span lang="ja">友達と電車で行きます。</span> (ともだちと でんしゃで いきます。) — “I go with a friend by train.”
- <span lang="ja">母とバスで行きます。</span> (ははと バスで いきます。) — “I go by bus with my mother.”
- <span lang="ja">一人で自転車で行きます。</span> (ひとりで じてんしゃで いきます。) — “I go alone by bicycle.”

The two instances of で in the last sentence have different roles: **一人で** describes doing something alone; **自転車で** describes the transport. It is grammatical, though you may instead say **自転車で一人で行きます**. You will practise the particle contrasts systematically in L03.

## 3. Mini-conversation: Saturday plans

**Aki:** <span lang="ja">土曜日、どうやって公園に行きますか。</span> (どようび、どうやって こうえんに いきますか。) — “How will you go to the park on Saturday?”

**Ken:** <span lang="ja">電車で行きます。友達と行きます。</span> (でんしゃで いきます。ともだちと いきます。) — “I'll go by train. I'll go with a friend.”

**Aki:** <span lang="ja">そうですか。私は一人で自転車で行きます。</span> (そうですか。わたしは ひとりで じてんしゃで いきます。) — “I see. I'll go alone by bicycle.”

**Check the evidence:** Ken's means is **電車で**, his companion is **友達と**. Aki's means is **自転車で**, and **一人で** tells you no one accompanies her. None of these lines tells you the departure time.

## 4. Similar words, different meaning

**一人で** (alone) contrasts with **友達といっしょに** (together with a friend). You can say **友達と行きます** without いっしょに; adding it emphasizes being together. Avoid **友達で行きます** when you mean “with a friend”: that incorrectly treats the friend like a transport means.

**徒歩で行きます** is understandable in a travel itinerary, but **歩いて行きます** is a common conversational choice. Do not say **歩くで行きます**. This lesson introduces the whole phrase; the conjugation will be explained in a later verb-form lesson.

## 5. Guided retrieval

Cover the Japanese column above. Recall **バス**, **地下鉄**, **自転車**, **友達**, **家族**, and **一人で** from the English meanings. Then cover the English column and explain each Japanese word without translating character by character.

Complete the meaning in each sentence before checking the model:

1. “I go by taxi.” → <span lang="ja">タクシーで行きます。</span>
2. “I go with my father.” → <span lang="ja">父と行きます。</span>
3. “I go alone.” → <span lang="ja">一人で行きます。</span>
4. “I go with my family by bus.” → <span lang="ja">家族とバスで行きます。</span>

Notice that a means of transport and a companion can both appear in one sentence without competing for the same particle.

## 6. Independent writing and optional oral rehearsal

Write **three short Japanese sentences** about a fictional trip: a destination, a means of transport and either a companion or being alone. Try including both **と** and **で** in one sentence. For example:

> <span lang="ja">日曜日に図書館に行きます。友達とバスで行きます。帰りは一人で帰ります。</span>
>
> (にちようびに としょかんに いきます。ともだちと バスで いきます。かえりは ひとりで かえります。)
>
> “On Sunday I'll go to the library. I'll go with a friend by bus. On the way back, I'll return alone.”

Self-check: Is the transport followed by **で**? Is the companion followed by **と**? Does **一人で** really mean without companions? Is the destination clear? This writing is **self-reviewed, not automatically scored**. You may read your own sentences aloud, but no pronunciation score is assigned.
