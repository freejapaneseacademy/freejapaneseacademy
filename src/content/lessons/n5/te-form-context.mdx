## A morning with two steps

**Can-do:** Understand a simple sequence of actions and begin connecting your own actions with the **て-form**. This is the **context and first-exposure** lesson for N5 Module 06, Chapter 02. You do not need to memorize every conjugation rule yet; the later grammar lesson teaches the complete pattern set.

Imagine two students preparing for a morning community-center class. A volunteer posts a short routine card, and one student explains what they do before leaving home. Japanese often uses the **て-form** to connect actions rather than repeating a separate polite sentence for each action.

### Step 1 — Read the routine card

**あさの じゅんび — Morning preparation**

1. <ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>って、<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べます。  
   *Wash your hands, then eat breakfast.*
2. <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでから、<ruby>学校<rt>がっこう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。  
   *After reading a book, go to school.*

The first action is **手を洗う** (wash your hands). In a connected sentence it becomes **洗って**. The final verb **食べます** makes the sentence polite. The second line uses **読んでから** to make the order especially explicit: *after reading*.

**Notice:** The written card is an original learning scenario, not a real public notice. Its short instructions are examples of how actions can be sequenced; they do not prescribe a universal Japanese morning routine.

### Step 2 — Hear the meaning in a printed conversation

**A:** <ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>をしますか。  
*What do you do in the morning?*

**B:** <ruby>顔<rt>かお</rt></ruby>を<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>って、<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べます。  
*I wash my face and then eat breakfast.*

**A:** それから？  
*And after that?*

**B:** <ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてから、<ruby>学校<rt>がっこう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。  
*After eating breakfast, I go to school.*

The same verb **食べる** appears as **食べます** when it ends a polite statement and as **食べて** when it links to another action. The phrase **〜てから** stresses that the second action happens **after** the first. These are **printed reading models**, not recorded listening clips.

### Step 3 — Recognize action words

| Dictionary form | Reading | Linking form | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 洗う | あらう | 洗って（あらって） | wash |
| 食べる | たべる | 食べて（たべて） | eat |
| 読む | よむ | 読んで（よんで） | read |
| 書く | かく | 書いて（かいて） | write |
| 行く | いく | 行って（いって） | go |
| 見る | みる | 見て（みて） | see/watch |
| する | する | して | do |
| 来る | くる | 来て（きて） | come |

The て-form does **not** always literally end in て: **読む → 読んで** ends in **で**. Nor can you simply attach て to a dictionary form: **読むて** is incorrect. **行く → 行って** is a special case to remember. We will study the complete Group 1, Group 2, and irregular patterns in L03.

### Step 4 — Understand the difference

- **顔を洗って、朝ごはんを食べます。** *I wash my face and eat breakfast.* The actions are presented in sequence in this context, although 〜て can also connect actions without emphasizing strict chronology.
- **顔を洗ってから、朝ごはんを食べます。** *After washing my face, I eat breakfast.* Here **から** explicitly marks the first action as completed before the next.
- **顔を洗います。朝ごはんを食べます。** *I wash my face. I eat breakfast.* Two complete polite sentences, without a linking て-form.

**Common misconception:** **食べて** is not automatically past tense, and **食べてから** is not the same as **食べましたから**. The latter uses a different form and can introduce a reason depending on context. The sequence meaning comes from the linking construction.

### Step 5 — Controlled noticing

Without checking the table, identify the first action in each line. Then check the bold explanation.

1. **本を読んで、寝ます。** *I read a book and go to bed.* First: **読む**.
2. **手を洗ってから、食べます。** *After washing my hands, I eat.* First: **洗う**.
3. **学校に行って、勉強します。** *I go to school and study.* First: **行く**.
4. **名前を書いてから、入ります。** *After writing my name, I enter.* First: **書く**.

Here **寝ます（ねます）** means *go to bed / sleep*, **勉強します（べんきょうします）** means *study*, and **入ります（はいります）** means *enter*. They are supporting vocabulary, not required new conjugation targets.

### Step 6 — Guided production

Choose a pair of actions from the table. Keep the second verb polite. For example:

- **本を読んで、朝ごはんを食べます。** *I read a book and eat breakfast.*
- **手を洗ってから、本を読みます。** *After washing my hands, I read a book.*

Try changing just one element. Say or type **顔を洗って、学校に行きます** (*I wash my face and go to school*). Check that **洗って** links the first action and **行きます** ends the sentence.

### Step 7 — Independent mini-task

Write **two or three** short Japanese sentences about a fictional or real morning. Use at least one **〜て** link and one **〜てから** sequence. If writing kanji is difficult, use kana: **かおをあらって、あさごはんをたべます。** is acceptable for this first-exposure activity.

Compare your work against this model:

**朝、顔を洗って、朝ごはんを食べます。朝ごはんを食べてから、本を読みます。**  
*In the morning, I wash my face and eat breakfast. After eating breakfast, I read a book.*

**Self-review (ungraded):** Are the actions in a sensible order? Is the first verb in the correct linking form? Is the final verb polite? Does 〜てから accurately mean *after*? Can you explain the meaning in English? This is not an automatically graded writing assessment.
