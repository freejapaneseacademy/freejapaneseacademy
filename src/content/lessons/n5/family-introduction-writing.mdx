## A family photograph for a class display

**Can-do:** Describe family members and simple relationships in two to five connected Japanese sentences. This is a writing lesson: no recording or speech score is required. Use an invented family rather than real personal information if you prefer.

**Situation:** Mina is preparing a small caption for a classroom display. The names and relationships are fictional.

| Person | Relationship to Mina | Detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| えみ | 母（はは） — mother | 先生（せんせい） — teacher |
| たけし | 兄（あに） — older brother | 学生（がくせい） — student |
| そうた | 弟（おとうと） — younger brother | 名前（なまえ） — name is Sota |

## 1. Read a complete model

> これは私（わたし）の家族（かぞく）の写真（しゃしん）です。  
> こちらは私の母（はは）です。  
> 母の名前（なまえ）はえみです。  
> 母は先生（せんせい）です。

**Meaning:** “This is a photograph of my family. This is my mother. My mother's name is Emi. My mother is a teacher.”

Each sentence contributes a different fact. The name **えみ** is in kana, so no unprovided kanji reading is assumed. The fourth sentence is supported by the fictional chart, not an inference from the photograph.

## 2. Build accurate relationships with の

**私の母** = my mother; **母の名前** = mother's name; **私の家族の写真** = a photo of my family. The particle **の** links nouns in many ways; it does not always express legal ownership.

Compare:

- **こちらは私の兄です。** — This is my older brother.
- **兄の名前はたけしです。** — My older brother's name is Takeshi.
- **弟の名前はそうたです。** — My younger brother's name is Sota.

The particle **は** marks the topic and is written は but pronounced *wa* here. Keep **です** at the end of a polite nominal sentence. You do not need to add 私の to every sentence once the family context is clear.

**Respectful references:** When describing your own mother to someone outside the family, **母（はは）** is a standard choice. When referring respectfully to someone else's mother, **お母さん（おかあさん）** is common. In a friendly introduction, **こちら** politely directs attention to the person; **その人** refers to a person identifiable in context but may sound less courteous in an introduction. Neither form tells you a person's relationship without supporting information.

## 3. Guided composition

Follow the steps and compare the evidence.

1. Start with the photo: **これは私の家族の写真です。**
2. Choose a person: **こちらは私の兄です。**
3. Add a name: **兄の名前はたけしです。**
4. Add a known fact: **兄は学生です。**

**Another correct model:**

> こちらは私の弟です。  
> 名前はそうたです。

“Here is my younger brother. His name is Sota.” Because the topic is already clear, the second sentence can omit 弟の. For a new, unrelated person, specify whose name it is.

**Common mistake:** **私の母はえみの名前です** does not mean “My mother's name is Emi.” The correct pattern is **母の名前はえみです**. The topic is the *name*, not the mother.

## 4. Independent writing task

Write **two to five connected Japanese sentences** introducing one or two fictional family members. You may use Mina's chart or invent a harmless example. Include an introduction, a relationship, and at least one name or stated fact. Do not invent a profession from a name or a photo alone.

Use the private writing editor below. Your text is **not sent to a teacher or graded automatically**; saving stores it locally on this device when browser storage is available. You can compare your work to the models without treating an open-ended answer as automatically correct.

### Self-review rubric (ungraded)

Check these four criteria:

- **Relationship:** Does 母, 父, 兄, 弟 or another family word correctly identify the person relative to the speaker?
- **Noun links:** Does の correctly connect the person to the name, family or photograph?
- **Sentence form:** Are は and です used naturally in the polite introduction?
- **Evidence and clarity:** Are your facts supported by the fictional chart or clearly marked as invented, and can a reader tell who each sentence is about?

Revise one sentence after reviewing. You can optionally read it aloud for your own practice; no pronunciation assessment is performed.
