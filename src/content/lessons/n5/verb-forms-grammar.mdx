## Two ways to name the same action

At a community Japanese class, Mika reads a board headed **今日の活動（きょうのかつどう）— Today's activities**. The board lists **読む（よむ）**, **書く（かく）**, and **話す（はなす）**. Her teacher says:

> 今日は本（ほん）を読みます。 — Today we will read a book.  
> 名前（なまえ）を書きます。 — We will write our names.  
> 日本語（にほんご）を話します。 — We will speak Japanese.

The board uses **dictionary forms**; the teacher uses **polite nonpast ます forms**. Both name the same actions. Dictionary form is also a plain nonpast form in sentences; it does **not** inherently mean “to …” or past tense. Context decides whether nonpast describes a habit, present situation or future plan.

## 1. Spot the form and the group

| Dictionary form | Polite form | Meaning | Group |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 読む（よむ） | 読みます（よみます） | read | Godan (Group 1) |
| 書く（かく） | 書きます（かきます） | write | Godan (Group 1) |
| 話す（はなす） | 話します（はなします） | speak | Godan (Group 1) |
| 飲む（のむ） | 飲みます（のみます） | drink | Godan (Group 1) |
| 買う（かう） | 買います（かいます） | buy | Godan (Group 1) |
| 見る（みる） | 見ます（みます） | see/watch | Ichidan (Group 2) |
| 食べる（たべる） | 食べます（たべます） | eat | Ichidan (Group 2) |
| 起きる（おきる） | 起きます（おきます） | get up | Ichidan (Group 2) |
| する（する） | します（します） | do | Irregular (Group 3) |
| 来る（くる） | 来ます（きます） | come | Irregular (Group 3) |
| 帰る（かえる） | 帰ります（かえります） | return home | Godan (Group 1) |
| 行く（いく） | 行きます（いきます） | go | Godan (Group 1) |

**Reading tip:** These kanji spell familiar actions; kana in parentheses gives the reading. Pay attention to the changing kana in each pair. This lesson checks recognition and conversion, not handwriting accuracy or pronunciation.

## 2. Godan (Group 1): change the final kana to its い-row partner

For many godan verbs, change the final dictionary-form kana from the **う-row** to the corresponding **い-row**, then add **ます**.

| Change | Example | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| む → み | 飲む → 飲みます | drink |
| く → き | 書く → 書きます | write |
| す → し | 話す → 話します | speak |
| う → い | 買う → 買います | buy |
| る → り | 帰る → 帰ります | return home |

**Try it:** Cover the right column. Convert **読む**, **書く**, and **買う**. Then reveal: **読みます・書きます・買います**.

The ます form is a polite *nonpast* form, not a past tense. For example, **毎日（まいにち）本を読みます** means “I read a book every day,” while **明日（あした）本を読みます** can mean “I will read a book tomorrow.”

## 3. Ichidan (Group 2): remove る and add ます

With an ichidan verb, the stem remains unchanged:

> 食べる（たべる） → 食べます（たべます） — eat  
> 見る（みる） → 見ます（みます） — see / watch  
> 起きる（おきる） → 起きます（おきます） — get up

**Common misconception:** “Every verb ending in る is ichidan.” This is false. **帰る（かえる） → 帰ります（かえります）** is godan. So is **走る（はしる） → 走ります（はしります）** (“run”). Learn the group alongside a new る-ending verb; its spelling alone does not always settle the question.

## 4. Two important irregular verbs

> する → します — do  
> 来る（くる） → 来ます（きます） — come

Notice the reading of **来** changes: **くる** in the dictionary form, **きます** in the polite form. Do not produce *くます*.

These forms appear in ordinary plans:

> 明日（あした）、勉強（べんきょう）します。 — I'll study tomorrow.  
> 友達（ともだち）が来ます。 — A friend is coming.

**Register note:** **本を読む** is a natural plain nonpast statement, while **本を読みます** is polite. Do not automatically switch all conversations to plain form; choose register for the relationship and situation.

## 5. Guided action-board practice

Read the following fictional class plan.

> 午前九時（ごぜんくじ）に本を読みます。  
> 午前十時（ごぜんじゅうじ）に日本語を書きます。  
> 午後一時（ごごいちじ）に友達と勉強します。

Meaning: At 9 a.m., we read a book. At 10 a.m., we write Japanese. At 1 p.m., we study with friends. The timetable is invented for learning.

Find each polite verb (**読みます・書きます・勉強します**) and write its dictionary form (**読む・書く・勉強する**). The compound **勉強する** uses the irregular verb **する**.

Now make a second plan using **食べる**, **飲む**, and **帰る**. Model:

> 昼（ひる）にパンを食べます。 — I eat bread at lunchtime.  
> 水（みず）を飲みます。 — I drink water.  
> 午後五時（ごごごじ）に家（いえ）に帰ります。 — I return home at 5 p.m.

The destination **家に** is different from the action object **水を**; the verb form does not change the particle's role.

## 6. Independent retrieval and writing

Without looking at the table, write **three to five short connected Japanese sentences** about a study day. Include one godan, one ichidan, and one irregular polite verb. Underline each ます form and write its dictionary form beside it. Compare your work with the model above.

Self-review (ungraded): (1) Are all three groups represented? (2) Does each dictionary form correspond to the same action? (3) Are ます forms polite and nonpast? (4) Have you checked any る-ending verbs against their group?

For listening preparation, read the three model sentences slowly, then cover them and recall the actions. **No recorded audio is provided or required for this written grammar lesson**; the chapter's L06 supplies authentic listening practice separately.
