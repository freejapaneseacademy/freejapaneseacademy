## Your mission: help a visitor find the library

A visitor is standing outside a fictional station. You are writing a short, polite message explaining how to reach the library. In this invented town, the route is **station → straight ahead → turn right at the traffic light → library on the left**. The park is beside the library.

> 駅（えき）から、まっすぐ行（い）ってください。  
> 信号（しんごう）を右（みぎ）に曲（ま）がってください。  
> 図書館（としょかん）は左（ひだり）にあります。

“Please go straight from the station. Please turn right at the traffic light. The library is on the left.”

These sentences describe a fictional route, not directions to a real library. The first two tell the visitor **what to do**; the last tells them **where the destination is**.

## 1. Choose a useful direction expression

| Japanese | Reading | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 駅から | えきから | From the station; から marks the starting point |
| まっすぐ行ってください | まっすぐいってください | Please go straight |
| 信号を右に曲がってください | しんごうをみぎにまがってください | Please turn right at the traffic light |
| 左に曲がってください | ひだりにまがってください | Please turn left |
| 図書館は左にあります | としょかんはひだりにあります | The library is on the left |
| 公園のとなりにあります | こうえんのとなりにあります | It is next to the park |

**〜てください** is a polite request to perform an action. **〜にあります** describes where a place or inanimate object is located. The same particle **に** appears in **右に曲がる** (turn right) and **左にあります** (be on the left), but the verbs express different functions.

## 2. Write instructions in route order

A reader needs a clear **start**, **movement**, **turn**, and **destination**. Japanese normally omits the subject when the context already makes it clear who is moving.

1. **駅から、まっすぐ行ってください。** — Please go straight from the station.
2. **信号を右に曲がってください。** — Please turn right at the traffic light.
3. **図書館は左にあります。** — The library is on the left.
4. **図書館は公園のとなりにあります。** — The library is beside the park.

The location in the fourth sentence is relative to the park. In a real-world route, make sure landmarks are actually visible and that the instructions match the map; these are practice facts only.

### Common writing repairs

| Less suitable | Better | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 右を曲がってください。 | 右に曲がってください。 | 右に marks the direction of the turn; a street or corner can be marked with を. |
| 図書館は左をあります。 | 図書館は左にあります。 | あります uses に for the location. |
| 信号に右に曲がってください。 | 信号を右に曲がってください。 | 信号を is the beginner-friendly route instruction at the traffic light. |
| 図書館にまっすぐあります。 | 図書館は左にあります。 | まっすぐ normally modifies motion here; use a clear relative location for あります. |

**Important nuance:** **右にあります** means “is on the right” relative to a viewpoint or route. It does **not** mean “turn right.” Use **右に曲がってください** to request the turn.

## 3. Guided composition

**Controlled substitution:** Start with **信号を右に曲がってください。** Change **右** to **左**. You should get **信号を左に曲がってください。** Now change **図書館は左にあります。** to say the library is on the right: **図書館は右にあります。**

**Rebuild a route:** A visitor starts at the station, goes straight, turns left at the traffic light, and finds the park on the right. Write three sentences, then compare:

> 駅から、まっすぐ行ってください。  
> 信号を左に曲がってください。  
> 公園は右にあります。

**Independent task:** Write **two to five connected sentences** guiding a visitor from the station to the library or park. Choose **one** route and keep your directions internally consistent. Use Japanese punctuation **。** and kana when you cannot recall a kanji. Do not combine a left turn from one model with a right turn from another without updating the destination description.

## 4. Review your writing

Check four things: **(1)** your start and destination are clear, **(2)** the route order is consistent, **(3)** you distinguish action **〜てください** from location **〜にあります**, and **(4)** your direction words and particles are accurate. Compare your text with the workshop model; more than one coherent route is possible.
