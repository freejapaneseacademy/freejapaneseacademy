## The situation: meeting family at a school event

You are welcoming visitors to a school exhibition. Your classmate Mika introduces her younger sister; later, you need to explain who a person in a photograph is. By the end, you can introduce a family member in a short, polite sentence, and understand which person a relationship belongs to.

### 1. の links two nouns

In **AのB**, noun A specifies noun B. In this chapter it often tells us whose relative B is:

- <span lang="ja">わたしの母（はは）</span> — my mother (when speaking about one's own mother to someone outside the family)
- <span lang="ja">ミカさんの妹（いもうと）</span> — Mika's younger sister
- <span lang="ja">田中（たなか）さんのお父（とう）さん</span> — Tanaka's father (respectful reference)

**Order matters.** <span lang="ja">ミカさんの妹</span> means *Mika's younger sister*, not *the younger sister's Mika*. **の** is a noun-linking particle; it is not the topic marker **は**. A complete polite sentence can combine both: <span lang="ja">こちらは ミカさんの妹です。</span> — This is Mika's younger sister.

### 2. こちら for a polite introduction

<span lang="ja">こちらは わたしの姉（あね）です。</span> — This is my older sister.

Here **こちら** politely points out or introduces a person near the speaker. It can also mean *this direction/this way* in other contexts, so do not memorize it as a word that *always* means a person. In a formal introduction, **こちらは…です** is more courteous than using the casual demonstrative **これ** for a person. Avoid <span lang="ja">これは わたしの姉です</span> when politely presenting someone; **これ** normally points to things, not people.

**Register note:** When introducing your own mother or father to an outsider, conventional humble/neutral terms include **母（はは）** and **父（ちち）**. For someone else's parent, respectful **お母（かあ）さん** and **お父（とう）さん** are natural. These are usage conventions, not a claim that families must address one another in a single way.

### 3. その人 refers to a person already identified

A friend points to a person in a picture. You ask:

<span lang="ja">その人（ひと）は だれですか。</span> — Who is that person?

<span lang="ja">その人は ミカさんのお兄（にい）さんです。</span> — That person is Mika's older brother.

**その人** is useful when the person is already in focus, especially in a photo or description. It is not automatically the most polite way to address a person standing in front of you. For a respectful in-person reference, **こちらの方（かた）** may be preferable; for this lesson, use **こちら** when presenting someone and **その人** when referring to a person in a photograph. **その** needs a following noun: <span lang="ja">その人</span>, not <span lang="ja">そのは</span>.

### Original dialogue: at the exhibition

| Speaker | Japanese | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mika | <span lang="ja">こちらは わたしの妹、ユイです。</span> | This is my younger sister, Yui. |
| Visitor | <span lang="ja">はじめまして、ユイさん。</span> | Nice to meet you, Yui. |
| Mika | <span lang="ja">こちらは わたしの母です。</span> | This is my mother. |
| Visitor | <span lang="ja">はじめまして。</span> | Nice to meet you. |
| Visitor (looking at a photograph) | <span lang="ja">その人は だれですか。</span> | Who is that person? |
| Mika | <span lang="ja">その人は わたしの兄（あに）です。</span> | That person is my older brother. |

The person introduced as **妹** is Yui; the person in the photograph is Mika's **兄**. The dialogue is fictional original written material, not a recording.

### Compare and repair

| Learner's attempt | Better Japanese | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">妹のミカさん</span> (intended: Mika's sister) | <span lang="ja">ミカさんの妹</span> | Put the person whose relative is described before の. |
| <span lang="ja">そのは わたしの兄です。</span> | <span lang="ja">その人は わたしの兄です。</span> | その modifies a noun; 人 supplies the noun. |
| <span lang="ja">これは わたしの母です。</span> (formal introduction) | <span lang="ja">こちらは わたしの母です。</span> | こちら is more appropriate for presenting a person. |

### Guided writing and speaking

**Observe:** <span lang="ja">こちらは わたしの弟（おとうと）です。</span> — This is my younger brother.

**Recombine:** Change **弟** to **姉**. Model: <span lang="ja">こちらは わたしの姉です。</span> — This is my older sister.

**Transfer:** Imagine a fictional friend named Aki and a photo of Aki's father. Write two sentences: <span lang="ja">その人は アキさんのお父さんです。アキさんのお父さんは 先生（せんせい）です。</span> — That person is Aki's father. Aki's father is a teacher. Other natural sentences may be valid. Self-check the order of **の**, the family term and the predicate ending; open writing is not automatically graded.

**Optional oral rehearsal:** Say <span lang="ja">こちらは わたしの妹です。</span> slowly, then point to a fictional photo and ask <span lang="ja">その人は だれですか。</span>. This is silent/written-model practice with no recorded audio and no pronunciation score. The chapter's L06 provides the actual listening task.
