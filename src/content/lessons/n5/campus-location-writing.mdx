## A note for a new student

You are helping a new student find places in a fictional school. Your goal is to write **two to five connected, polite Japanese sentences**. Use the imaginary building below; no real location details are needed.

### 1. Read the building information

At this school, **the library is on the second floor**, **the classroom is beside the library**, and **the reception desk is near the entrance on the first floor**. You and your visitor are standing at reception. The library is away from both of you.

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 図書館 | としょかん | library |
| 教室 | きょうしつ | classroom |
| 受付 | うけつけ | reception desk |
| 入口 | いりぐち | entrance |
| 二階 | にかい | second floor |
| ここ / そこ / あそこ | ここ / そこ / あそこ | here / there near you / over there |
| どこ | どこ | where |
| あります / います | あります / います | exists (thing) / is present (person) |

**Model dialogue**

**Visitor:** <span lang="ja">図書館は どこですか。</span> — Where is the library?

**You:** <span lang="ja">図書館は 二階です。</span> — The library is on the second floor.

**You:** <span lang="ja">教室も 二階です。</span> — The classroom is also on the second floor.

**You:** <span lang="ja">受付は ここです。</span> — Reception is here.

The first sentence is a question; the next three provide information. **も** means “also,” and replaces **は** in this sentence.

### 2. How location sentences work

**Place + は + location + です** identifies where a place is: <span lang="ja">図書館は あそこです。</span> — “The library is over there.” You can also say <span lang="ja">図書館は 二階です。</span> — “The library is on the second floor.”

**Thing + は + place + に + あります** states where an inanimate thing exists: <span lang="ja">本は 教室に あります。</span> — “The book is in the classroom.” The particle **に** marks the location of existence.

**Person + は + place + に + います** uses **います** for a person or animal: <span lang="ja">先生は 図書館に います。</span> — “The teacher is in the library.” Do not use **あります** for the teacher.

**Perspective matters.** <span lang="ja">ここ</span> is near the speaker; <span lang="ja">そこ</span> is near the listener; <span lang="ja">あそこ</span> is away from both. In a written exchange, establish where the speakers stand. **どこ** asks where: <span lang="ja">トイレは どこですか。</span> — “Where is the restroom?”

**Common mistake:** <span lang="ja">本は 教室です。</span> identifies the book as a classroom, rather than locating it. Use <span lang="ja">本は 教室に あります。</span> to say the book is in the classroom. For a facility's location, <span lang="ja">図書館は 二階です。</span> is natural.

### 3. Controlled practice

Replace the location in <span lang="ja">受付は ここです。</span> with <span lang="ja">あそこ</span> when the desk is far from both people. The result is <span lang="ja">受付は あそこです。</span> — “Reception is over there.”

Change <span lang="ja">本は 教室に あります。</span> to locate a teacher instead of a book. Use <span lang="ja">先生は 教室に います。</span> — “The teacher is in the classroom.” Notice that the location stays the same but the verb changes.

### 4. Guided reconstruction

Put these parts in order: <span lang="ja">図書館 / は / どこ / ですか</span>. Answer: <span lang="ja">図書館は どこですか。</span> (“Where is the library?”)

Now assemble <span lang="ja">本 / は / 図書館 / に / あります</span>. Answer: <span lang="ja">本は 図書館に あります。</span> (“The book is in the library.”)

Try rewriting both without looking at the answers. Check **は** after the topic, **に** after the location of existence, and the polite sentence ending.

### 5. Independent writing

You are at reception with a new student. Write **two to five connected sentences** in Japanese. Ask where the library is; state that it is on the second floor; mention that reception is here; and optionally say where a book or teacher is. You can write a short two-speaker exchange or a connected description.

**One possible model** (not the only correct answer):

> <span lang="ja">図書館は どこですか。</span> — Where is the library?
>
> <span lang="ja">図書館は 二階です。</span> — The library is on the second floor.
>
> <span lang="ja">受付は ここです。</span> — Reception is here.
>
> <span lang="ja">本は 図書館に あります。</span> — The book is in the library.

The model uses four sentences. You may choose other plausible room locations, but make your imagined map consistent. You do not have to copy the model.

### 6. Self-review rubric

| Criterion | Check your own writing |
| --- | --- |
| Communication | Does your text locate a campus facility or object in 2–5 connected sentences? |
| Location words | Are ここ/そこ/あそこ justified by the stated speaker perspective? Does どこ ask a question? |
| Particles and verbs | Does は mark the topic? Does に mark the location with あります/います? Is the verb appropriate for a thing or person? |
| Polite clarity | Are sentence endings and punctuation readable, with です/ですか or あります/います? |

Use the writing workshop to type your own sentences, compare with the model, and revise. **The workshop does not grade free writing or check pronunciation.** Its separate multiple-choice questions assess written-form recognition. Saving a draft is optional and stays only in your browser.

### 7. Transfer

Imagine that you and your visitor have walked to the library. Now <span lang="ja">図書館は ここです。</span> (“The library is here”) is appropriate because you are standing there. Explain in English why <span lang="ja">あそこ</span> would no longer describe that same library from your new viewpoint. You may read your Japanese aloud privately, but there is no speaking evaluation; recorded listening practice is a separate L06 activity.
