## Mission: ask, answer, switch

Imagine you are helping at a **fictional community center**. A friend asks what people are doing *right now*. You can speak the lines aloud, whisper, or rehearse silently. Speaking is optional; no microphone or recording is required. The chapter's L06 listening lesson is the separate source of model audio when its authentic recordings are available and verified. This page does **not** claim to contain recorded or pronunciation-reviewed audio.

### Notice the pattern

**今、何をしていますか。** (いま、なにを していますか。) — What are you doing now?

**今、本を読んでいます。** (いま、ほんを よんでいます。) — I'm reading a book now.

**〜ています** attaches to the **て-form**, not the dictionary form: 読む → 読んで → 読んでいます; 書く → 書いて → 書いています; 食べる → 食べて → 食べています; する → して → しています. In this chapter, use it for an activity underway at the time of speaking. In broader Japanese, 〜ています can also express a resulting state or a repeated activity; context determines the reading.

| Japanese | Reading | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 何をしていますか。 | なにを していますか | What are you doing? |
| 図書館で本を読んでいます。 | としょかんで ほんを よんでいます | I'm reading a book at the library. |
| 台所で昼ご飯を作っています。 | だいどころで ひるごはんを つくっています | I'm making lunch in the kitchen. |
| 公園で友だちと話しています。 | こうえんで ともだちと はなしています | I'm talking with a friend at the park. |

**Particles:** **で** marks where an action takes place (図書館で); **を** marks the direct object (本を); **と** marks a companion (友だちと). **に** is not a general substitute for the action-location particle で. The topic or subject may be omitted when obvious from context.

### Scene A — at the library

A student calls a friend during a fictional study break.

| Speaker | Japanese | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | もしもし。今、何をしていますか。 | Hello. What are you doing now? |
| B | 図書館で日本語の本を読んでいます。 | I'm reading a Japanese book at the library. |
| A | そうですか。だれと勉強していますか。 | I see. Who are you studying with? |
| B | 一人で勉強しています。 | I'm studying alone. |

**Reading support:** 図書館（としょかん）library; 日本語（にほんご）Japanese; 一人（ひとり）one person/alone. Here **一人で** means *alone*, not an action location.

### Scene B — at the community kitchen

Two volunteers are preparing food at a fictional community event.

| Speaker | Japanese | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | 田中さんは今、何をしていますか。 | What is Tanaka doing now? |
| B | 台所で野菜を切っています。 | Tanaka is cutting vegetables in the kitchen. |
| A | 佐藤さんは？ | And Sato? |
| B | 佐藤さんはお茶を入れています。 | Sato is making tea. |
| A | ありがとうございます。 | Thank you. |

**Reading support:** 田中（たなか）Tanaka; 佐藤（さとう）Sato; 台所（だいどころ）kitchen; 野菜（やさい）vegetables; 切る（きる）to cut; 入れる（いれる）to put in, or to prepare tea in お茶を入れる.

The second answer does not specify where Sato is. Do not invent a location when reporting the scene.

### Rehearsal ladder

1. **Observe:** Read both dialogues with the translations. Underline the activity verb in each answer.
2. **Echo:** Read A's question and B's answer with a natural pause. Then swap roles. There is no timing target or automatic pronunciation judgment.
3. **Change one detail:** Replace 図書館で本を読んでいます with 公園で友だちと話しています. Keep **で** for the place and **と** for the companion.
4. **Independent turn:** Imagine a person drawing a picture in a classroom. Ask **今、何をしていますか。** and answer **教室で絵を描いています。** (きょうしつで えを かいています。 — I'm drawing a picture in the classroom.) Then invent a different truthful *fictional* activity and switch speakers.

### Common repairs

- **読んでいます**, not **読むています**: form the て-form first.
- **図書館で読んでいます**, not **図書館に読んでいます** when the library is the place of the reading action.
- **本を読んでいます** says *reading a book*, while **本を書いています** says *writing a book*. A small verb change alters the message.
- **何をしていますか** asks about the activity. **どこで勉強していますか** asks where the studying occurs. Answer the question actually asked.
- **今、何をしていますか** can sound direct in some contexts; a friendly opening such as **すみません** or **今、ちょっといいですか** may be more considerate.

### Self-review: no speaking grade

After rehearsing, check: (1) the verb is in the correct て-form + います, (2) the answer matches the question, (3) any place uses で appropriately, (4) objects and companions use を and と as needed, and (5) you can switch roles and speak politely. You may rehearse silently. The interactive quiz below scores **written recognition only**, not speaking proficiency, pronunciation, fluency, or JLPT performance.
