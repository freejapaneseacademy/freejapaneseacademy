## Mission: leave a two-day note

**Chapter Can-do:** Say what happened yesterday and what is planned. Imagine that you are writing a short message for a fictional community-center study partner. Your note should contain **two to five connected Japanese sentences**: at least one sentence about yesterday, and at least one about tomorrow. You can use kana when you do not yet know a kanji. The writing workshop below saves drafts **only when you choose Save**, on this device; it does not send your text to a teacher.

### Original model: Yui's library plan

> 昨日（きのう）の午後（ごご）、図書室（としょしつ）へ行（い）きました。  
> 日本語（にほんご）の本（ほん）を読（よ）みました。  
> でも、漢字（かんじ）は勉強（べんきょう）しませんでした。  
> 明日（あした）の朝（あさ）、漢字を勉強します。  
> 午前（ごぜん）十時（じゅうじ）に図書室へ行きます。

**Meaning:** Yesterday afternoon, I went to the library room. I read a Japanese book. However, I did not study kanji. Tomorrow morning, I will study kanji. At 10 a.m., I will go to the library room.

The five sentences form one coherent note: a completed visit, a missed activity, and a plan to follow up. These are fictional details, not a real calendar or a report about a real person.

## 1. Pick the time before choosing the ending

| Meaning | Japanese | Why it fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yesterday I went. | 昨日、行きました。（きのう、いきました。） | **ました** is polite past affirmative. |
| Yesterday I did not go. | 昨日、行きませんでした。（きのう、いきませんでした。） | **ませんでした** is polite past negative. |
| Tomorrow I will go. | 明日、行きます。（あした、いきます。） | **ます** is polite nonpast; 明日 makes the future meaning clear. |
| Tomorrow I will not go. | 明日、行きません。（あした、いきません。） | **ません** is polite nonpast negative. |

Japanese polite nonpast does not have a separate English-style “will” ending. The time word **明日** makes a future plan clear. **昨日、行きます** is not the ordinary way to report a completed trip; use **行きました**. Conversely, **明日、行きました** clashes with the intended future plan.

For the verb **勉強します** (“study”), the four forms are **勉強します・勉強しません・勉強しました・勉強しませんでした**. For **読みます** (“read”), they are **読みます・読みません・読みました・読みませんでした**. Keep the verb stem with its correct ending; do not form a past negative by simply attaching でした to **読みました**.

## 2. Add time and place naturally

**昨日の午後** means “yesterday afternoon”; **明日の朝** means “tomorrow morning.” The particle **の** connects the day with the part of the day. **午前十時に** marks a specific clock time, so **に** follows 十時. With broad time words like 昨日 and 明日, **に** is normally unnecessary: **昨日、図書室へ行きました。**

For an activity's object, use **を**: **本を読みました** (“I read a book”). For a destination, **へ** is natural: **図書室へ行きます** (“I will go to the library room”). The directional particle **へ** is pronounced **え**, and the object particle **を** is pronounced **お**.

**でも** (“but/however”) connects a contrasting sentence in casual explanatory writing. You may omit it and still make a coherent note. **昨日は勉強しませんでした。明日は勉強します。** contrasts yesterday and tomorrow using **は**.

## 3. Controlled rewriting

Start with **昨日、本を読みました。** Change the activity to “did not read”: **昨日、本を読みませんでした。** Keep **を**; only the verb polarity changes.

Start with **明日、学校へ行きます。** Change the time to yesterday: **昨日、学校へ行きました。** You must change **both** the time word and verb ending.

Start with **明日の朝、勉強します。** Change the plan to a negative: **明日の朝、勉強しません。** The time is still future; **ません** is not a past negative.

## 4. Guided reconstruction: Sora's note

Sora gives you four fictional facts: **yesterday morning** went to the library; **yesterday evening** did not read a book; **tomorrow afternoon** will read a book; **tomorrow at 3 p.m.** will study Japanese. Write four short Japanese sentences before revealing the workshop model.

One possible answer:

> 昨日の朝、図書室へ行きました。  
> 昨日の夜、本を読みませんでした。  
> 明日の午後、本を読みます。  
> 明日の午後三時に日本語を勉強します。

**English:** Yesterday morning I went to the library room. Last night I did not read a book. Tomorrow afternoon I will read a book. Tomorrow at 3 p.m. I will study Japanese.

Other natural word orders are possible. In particular, **午後三時** means 3 p.m.; **明日の午後三時に** specifies tomorrow at 3 p.m. The model is a comparison aid, not an exact-match key.

## 5. Write your own fictional two-day message

Write **two to five** connected Japanese sentences about a fictional learner's yesterday and tomorrow. Include a past affirmative **ました** or past negative **ませんでした**, a future plan with **ます** or **ません**, at least one day expression, and a recognizable activity. If you want extra challenge, use both a positive and negative past form. Avoid personal identifying information.

### Four-criterion self-review rubric

1. **Connected message:** Two to five sentences describe the same person's yesterday and tomorrow.
2. **Time agreement:** 昨日 pairs with past forms, and 明日 pairs with nonpast forms when describing plans.
3. **Sentence structure:** Objects use を, destinations use へ, and specific clock times can use に.
4. **Meaning and readability:** A reader can tell what happened and what is planned; kana or supported kanji are readable.

Use the interactive editor to write, explicitly save, compare with the guided model, and revise. **No automatic grade is assigned to your original Japanese prose.** The separate eight-question recognition check measures specific grammar and reading decisions, not writing proficiency.

## 6. Reading, listening preparation, and optional speaking

For reading review, find every **昨日** and **明日** in the models and underline the matching verb ending. For listening preparation, ask a partner to read your written sentences slowly and identify whether each refers to yesterday or tomorrow; this page supplies **no recorded listening assessment**. For optional speaking, read your own draft aloud twice without recording. No pronunciation evaluation is claimed.

## Takeaway

A short, clear two-day message uses **昨日 + polite past** for what happened and **明日 + polite nonpast** for what is planned. Check both the day and the verb ending before you send a note.
