## Mission: help find a person and a missing bag

At a fictional community classroom, a visitor wants to find the teacher. Later, another student looks for a bag. Read both original dialogues, practise each part aloud, and switch roles. This is **optional speaking**; you can complete it silently and without a microphone. The related listening lesson L06 provides the chapter's audio study; **this page does not claim its recordings have been independently reviewed or licensed for reuse**.

### The essential distinction

| Form | Reading | Function | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| います | います | A person or animal exists/is present | 先生が います。 |
| あります | あります | A thing exists/is present | かばんが あります。 |
| に | に | Marks the place of existence | 教室に |
| が | が | Marks what or who is present | 先生が |
| どこ | どこ | Where | どこに いますか。 |
| 教室 | きょうしつ | Classroom | 教室に |
| 図書室 | としょしつ | School library room | 図書室に |
| 机 | つくえ | Desk | 机の 上に |
| 上 | うえ | On top of | 机の 上に |

The core pattern is **place に + person/animal が います** or **place に + thing が あります**. In <span lang="ja">教室に 先生が います。</span>, the teacher is the person present and the classroom is the location. <span lang="ja">教室に 先生が あります。</span> is wrong for an ordinary person. Conversely, <span lang="ja">机の 上に かばんが います。</span> is wrong for a bag. These patterns express existence or location, not ownership.

### Scene A — looking for the teacher

**Setting:** A visitor asks the receptionist about a teacher. The receptionist knows the teacher is in the classroom.

| Role | Japanese | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visitor | すみません。先生は どこに いますか。 | Excuse me. Where is the teacher? |
| Receptionist | 先生は 教室に います。 | The teacher is in the classroom. |
| Visitor | 教室ですか。ありがとうございます。 | In the classroom? Thank you. |
| Receptionist | はい。 | Yes. |

Here **は** sets up the already-mentioned teacher as the topic. Compare <span lang="ja">教室に 先生が います。</span>, which introduces the fact that a teacher is present there. Both are natural in their respective contexts. Do not assume which teacher or which classroom beyond the stated scene.

### Scene B — looking for a bag

**Setting:** A student has misplaced a bag. The helper sees it on top of a desk in the library room.

| Role | Japanese | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Student | すみません。かばんは どこに ありますか。 | Excuse me. Where is the bag? |
| Helper | 図書室に あります。机の 上に あります。 | It's in the library room. It's on the desk. |
| Student | 机の 上ですか。ありがとうございます。 | On the desk? Thank you. |
| Helper | はい。 | Yes. |

**机の 上** is a location phrase: literally “the desk's top.” The helper does not say who owns the bag. The question uses **あります** because a bag is an inanimate object.

### Three-pass speaking practice

1. **Notice:** Read Scene A while pointing to the teacher's imagined location. Pause naturally after <span lang="ja">すみません。</span> and notice <span lang="ja">どこに いますか。</span>.
2. **Swap:** Play the visitor, then the receptionist; repeat Scene B as student and helper. Keep the verbs matched to the referent when switching roles.
3. **Adapt:** Imagine a cat in the classroom and a book on the desk. Try <span lang="ja">教室に ねこが います。</span> (“There is a cat in the classroom”) and <span lang="ja">机の 上に 本が あります。</span> (“There is a book on the desk”). A cat is an animal, so it takes **います**.

For additional contrast, ask <span lang="ja">図書室に だれが いますか。</span> (“Who is in the library room?”) versus <span lang="ja">図書室に なにが ありますか。</span> (“What is in the library room?”). These ask different things. Avoid claiming a person is in the library in Scene B: only the bag's location is stated.

### Optional independent challenge

Imagine your friend asks about a dog in the garden and a notebook on a table. Produce two short exchanges, each with a question and answer. Suggested models: <span lang="ja">犬は どこに いますか。庭に います。</span> (“Where is the dog? It's in the garden.”) and <span lang="ja">ノートは どこに ありますか。テーブルの 上に あります。</span> (“Where is the notebook? It's on the table.”). The location is part of this fictional prompt, not a real-world fact.

### Self-review, not a speech score

Check whether you (1) used **います** for people/animals; (2) used **あります** for objects; (3) put **に** after the location and understood **が** for the entity present; and (4) switched roles while maintaining a polite exchange. Speaking aloud is optional; no voice recording or pronunciation grading is performed. The quiz below measures **written recognition**, not speaking ability.
