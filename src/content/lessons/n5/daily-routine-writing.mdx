## Your mission: write a day in four lines

**Chapter can-do:** Describe a usual day using polite present verbs. You are writing a *habitual* routine, not yesterday's diary. Imagine a fictional student, みお (Mio), who wants to introduce a typical weekday to a new classmate. Write **two to five connected Japanese sentences**. Your composition is **not automatically graded**; the recognition questions check specific forms separately.

### Read an original model

> わたしは毎朝（まいあさ）七時（しちじ）に起（お）きます。  
> 八時（はちじ）にパンを食（た）べます。  
> 九時（くじ）に学校（がっこう）へ行（い）きます。  
> 夜（よる）はテレビを見（み）ません。本（ほん）を読（よ）みます。

**Meaning:** I get up at seven every morning. I eat bread at eight. I go to school at nine. At night I do not watch television; I read a book.

This model contains **five sentences** (the final line has two). It is an invented schedule, not a factual claim about anyone. You can use kana-only spelling where a kanji is unfamiliar.

## 1. Choose the action and its polite ending

| Dictionary meaning | Polite affirmative | Polite negative | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| get up | 起きます（おきます） | 起きません（おきません） | 七時に起きます。 — I get up at seven. |
| eat | 食べます（たべます） | 食べません（たべません） | パンを食べます。 — I eat bread. |
| drink | 飲みます（のみます） | 飲みません（のみません） | 水（みず）を飲みます。 — I drink water. |
| go | 行きます（いきます） | 行きません（いきません） | 学校へ行きます。 — I go to school. |
| read | 読みます（よみます） | 読みません（よみません） | 本を読みます。 — I read a book. |
| watch | 見ます（みます） | 見ません（みません） | テレビは見ません。 — I do not watch TV. |

**ます** is polite *nonpast*: with 毎朝 (“every morning”) or 毎日 (“every day”), it describes a habit. **ません** is polite nonpast negative. Do not use **ました** for a normal repeated present-day routine: **ました** indicates past tense.

## 2. Put the small words in the right places

**Clock time + に + action:** 七時に起きます。 (“I get up at seven.”) The particle **に** marks the specific time. **毎朝** and **毎日** normally do not need に: 毎朝七時に起きます。 (“Every morning, I get up at seven.”)

**Object + を + action:** パンを食べます。 (“I eat bread.”) **を** is written *wo* in some romanization systems but pronounced *o* as a particle. 本を読みます means “read a book,” not “write a book.”

**Destination + へ + 行きます:** 学校へ行きます。 (“I go to school.”) **へ** indicates direction. Compare 学校で勉強します (“I study at school”): **で** marks the place of the action.

**Contrast with は:** テレビは見ません。本を読みます。 (“I don't watch TV. I read books.”) **は** highlights TV as the topic/contrast; **テレビを見ません** is also grammatical when no contrast is intended.

## 3. Controlled rewriting

Start with **七時に起きます。** Change 七時 to 六時（ろくじ）. The answer is **六時に起きます。** Notice that に stays after the new time.

Start with **水を飲みます。** Make it negative. The answer is **水を飲みません。** Keep を because water is still the direct object of 飲む.

Start with **本を読みます。** Change the object to 新聞（しんぶん, newspaper）. The answer is **新聞を読みます。** The action remains reading.

## 4. Guided reconstruction

Mio's friend なお (Nao) gives these imaginary details: gets up at **6 a.m.**, drinks **water at 7 a.m.**, goes to **school at 8 a.m.**, does **not watch TV** at night. Write four Japanese sentences before opening the model in the interactive workshop.

A suitable response is:

> 六時に起きます。  
> 七時に水を飲みます。  
> 八時に学校へ行きます。  
> 夜はテレビを見ません。

The subject “I” is understood from context; it need not be repeated in each sentence. There are other valid ways to express these facts, so this is a comparison model, **not** an exact-match grading key.

## 5. Independent writing: a fictional usual day

Choose a character and write **two to five** short Japanese sentences. Include (a) at least one specific clock time with に, (b) at least one direct object with を, (c) a polite affirmative ます action, and (d) a polite negative ません action. You may write about a fictional student who gets up at seven, eats rice at eight, reads a book at night, and does not watch TV. Avoid real personal details.

### Four-criterion self-review

1. **Content and connection:** Are there two to five sentences that describe one coherent usual day, rather than unrelated events?
2. **Time and object particles:** Is に placed after a specific clock time and を after a direct object?
3. **Polite forms:** Are your habitual affirmative verbs in ます and at least one negative in ません, without accidentally switching to past tense?
4. **Meaning and clarity:** Can another learner identify the actions and times, and is the Japanese consistent with your intended meaning?

Type your draft below. Save it explicitly on this device, compare it with the model, then revise. There is no automatic Japanese writing score or human correction service.

## 6. Review and optional oral rehearsal

Complete the eight recognition questions to check particles, verb endings and meaning. For reading practice, underline every clock time and its に in your draft. For listening preparation, have a partner read your own model sentences and check whether you can recognize the activities without seeing the text; **no recorded audio or scored listening task is provided in this writing lesson**. You may read your draft aloud for optional speaking rehearsal; pronunciation is not evaluated.
