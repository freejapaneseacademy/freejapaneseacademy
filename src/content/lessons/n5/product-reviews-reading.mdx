## Reading mission

Four fictional reviewers describe food, a bag and a room. These are original teaching texts, not real endorsements. Read each Japanese review first, then check the English support.

### Review A — café pudding

> このプリンは あまくて、おいしいです。でも、少し 高いです。

**English support:** The pudding is sweet and tasty, but a little expensive.

### Review B — lunch curry

> このカレーは からいです。あまくないです。とても おいしいです。

**English support:** The curry is spicy, not sweet, and very tasty.

### Review C — small bag

> このかばんは 小さいです。でも、安いです。色（いろ）は 青（あお）いです。

**English support:** The bag is small but inexpensive; its color is blue.

### Review D — library room

> この部屋（へや）は きれいで、しずかです。大きくないです。

**English support:** The room is clean and quiet, and not large.

## Read for facts, contrasts and negatives

In A, **あまくて、おいしいです** describes the pudding as sweet and delicious. **でも、少し 高いです** adds a criticism: it is a little expensive. A positive taste review and a negative price judgment can both be true.

In B, **からいです** means spicy, while **あまくないです** means *not sweet*. Neither means *not tasty*: **とても おいしいです** says the curry is very tasty. Do not mistake a negative adjective for a negative review of the entire dish.

In C, **小さいです** describes the bag's size, **安いです** its price evaluation and **青いです** its color. No exact yen price, material or brand is stated. A reader must not invent them.

In D, **きれいで、しずかです** joins two な-adjectives meaning clean and quiet. **大きくないです** means *not large*. It does not necessarily mean *very small*.

## Grammar cues while reading

| Japanese | Meaning | Reading clue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| あまい → あまくないです | sweet → not sweet | い-adjective negative: い → くない |
| 大きい → 大きくないです | large → not large | Same negative pattern |
| あまくて、おいしいです | sweet and tasty | くて connects い-adjectives |
| きれいで、しずかです | clean and quiet | で connects な-adjectives |
| 少（すこ）し 高（たか）いです | a little expensive | 少し modifies 高い |
| とても おいしいです | very tasty | とても intensifies the description |

**Common mistakes:** 「あまいくない」 is incorrect; use **あまくない**. **きれい** is a な-adjective despite ending in い, so use **きれいで**, not 「きれいくて」. **安い** says inexpensive, not necessarily poor quality.

## Controlled evidence practice

Find the Japanese phrase for each fact before revealing the answer in this paragraph. “The pudding is a little expensive” → **少し 高いです** (A). “The curry is not sweet” → **あまくないです** (B). “The room is not large” → **大きくないです** (D). These are direct quotations, not guesses.

## Guided practice: stated or unstated?

Make two columns labelled **stated** and **not stated**. For C, *small*, *inexpensive* and *blue* are stated; the exact price and brand are not. For D, *clean*, *quiet* and *not large* are stated; whether people are studying inside is not.

Then complete the ten evidence questions below. For every answer, locate the exact Japanese phrase in one review. Feedback will explain the textual evidence.

## Independent reading and writing transfer

Choose one review and write two or three connected Japanese sentences summarizing it. Example: **このカレーは からいです。あまくないです。でも、おいしいです。** Compare your own summary with the original and check: (1) correct object, (2) correct adjective forms, (3) no invented facts. The writing is self-reviewed and not automatically scored.

Optionally read your sentences aloud; this lesson does not assess pronunciation or claim a recording. Recorded comparison listening belongs to the separate L06 lesson.

**Takeaway:** Identify the topic, interpret contrasts and negatives, and cite the exact words supporting your answer.
