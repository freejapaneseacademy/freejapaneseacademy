## What is in the room?

**Can-do:** say that an object or person exists in a place. In this chapter, you will learn how to name what is present and where it is. This vocabulary lesson is a **written recognition and retrieval** activity; it does not test listening or verified pronunciation.

Imagine a fictional study room. Mika is looking for her bag, a teacher is helping students, and a cat is resting in a nearby room. The words below help you say **who** or **what** is there, and **where**.

## 1. People and animals: います

| Word | Reading | Meaning | Usage cue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">人</span> | ひと | person | People use います, not あります. |
| <span lang="ja">先生</span> | せんせい | teacher | A teacher is a person; use います. |
| <span lang="ja">学生</span> | がくせい | student | A student is a person; use います. |
| <span lang="ja">友だち</span> | ともだち | friend | Use います for a friend. |
| <span lang="ja">猫</span> | ねこ | cat | Animals such as cats use います. |
| <span lang="ja">犬</span> | いぬ | dog | Animals such as dogs use います. |

Use **います** (imasu) to express the presence of people and animals. For example, <span lang="ja">教室に 先生が います。</span> (きょうしつに せんせいが います。) means “There is a teacher in the classroom.” In this pattern, **に** marks the location, **が** marks the person or thing whose existence is stated, and **います** expresses presence.

**友だち** (ともだち) means a friend. It can refer to a friend of any gender; it is not limited to a classmate. **先生** is commonly a teacher or an honorific title for certain professionals; in this school scene it means *teacher*. These context clues matter more than a rigid one-to-one translation.

## 2. Things: あります

| Word | Reading | Meaning | Usage cue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">本</span> | ほん | book | An inanimate object; use あります. |
| <span lang="ja">かばん</span> | かばん | bag | An inanimate object; use あります. |
| <span lang="ja">机</span> | つくえ | desk | Furniture; use あります. |
| <span lang="ja">いす</span> | いす | chair | Furniture; use あります. |

Use **あります** (arimasu) for the presence of inanimate things. <span lang="ja">部屋に かばんが あります。</span> (へやに かばんが あります。) means “There is a bag in the room.” A bag is not a living creature, so います would be incorrect for this ordinary meaning.

**机** (つくえ) is a desk or table used for work or study; **いす** is a chair. A desk and chair may stand together, but they are not interchangeable words.

## 3. Places: where something exists

| Word | Reading | Meaning | Usage cue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">教室</span> | きょうしつ | classroom | Place marked by に in the existence pattern. |
| <span lang="ja">部屋</span> | へや | room | Place marked by に in the existence pattern. |

**教室** (きょうしつ) names a classroom; **部屋** (へや) means a room more generally. Compare <span lang="ja">教室に 学生が います。</span> (きょうしつに がくせいが います。 — “There is a student in the classroom.”) and <span lang="ja">部屋に 机が あります。</span> (へやに つくえが あります。 — “There is a desk in the room.”)

The simple existence pattern is **place に + person/animal/thing が + います／あります**. The location phrase can come first. The next grammar lesson will explore this structure in greater depth; here, notice which nouns go with which verb.

## 4. Four short scenes

**Scene A — Before class.** <span lang="ja">教室に 先生が います。学生も います。</span> (きょうしつに せんせいが います。がくせいも います。 — “There is a teacher in the classroom. There is a student too / there are students too.”) Japanese nouns do not normally require a singular/plural ending; the context determines the number.

**Scene B — Looking for belongings.** <span lang="ja">部屋に かばんが あります。本も あります。</span> (へやに かばんが あります。ほんも あります。 — “There is a bag in the room. There is also a book / there are also books.”) The second sentence uses **も** for “also,” replacing が in this simple pattern.

**Scene C — A pet.** <span lang="ja">部屋に 猫が います。</span> (へやに ねこが います。 — “There is a cat in the room.”) Even though the cat is not a person, it is an animal, so use います.

**Scene D — Furniture.** <span lang="ja">教室に 机が あります。いすも あります。</span> (きょうしつに つくえが あります。いすも あります。 — “There is a desk in the classroom. There is also a chair / there are also chairs.”)

## 5. A common confusion

Do not choose the existence verb based on whether a noun is important, moving, or close to you. Choose it based on the kind of referent in the normal physical-presence sense: **先生が います**, **猫が います**, but **本が あります**. The expression <span lang="ja">先生が あります</span> is not an ordinary way to say that a teacher is present. Similarly, **部屋** is a *place noun*; it can be the location marked with に, or itself be the thing whose existence is described in a wider context.

For written pronunciation support, remember that **が** is pronounced *ga* and **に** is *ni*. Kana readings in this lesson are study cues, not an audio recording or pronunciation assessment.

## 6. Guided retrieval and writing

First, cover the English meanings in the tables. Recall **先生**, **猫**, **本**, **机**, **教室** and **部屋** from their kana readings. Then reverse the task: given the English words, write the Japanese nouns without looking.

Next, complete these with the right verb: <span lang="ja">教室に 学生が ____。</span> (**います**) and <span lang="ja">部屋に 本が ____。</span> (**あります**). Explain *why* each answer differs.

**Independent task:** Write two connected sentences about a fictional room. Include one person or animal, one object, a location ending in に, and the appropriate existence verb. A model is <span lang="ja">部屋に 友だちが います。かばんも あります。</span> (へやに ともだちが います。かばんも あります。 — “There is a friend in the room. There is also a bag.”) In your second sentence, the location is understood from the first.

Self-check: (1) Did you choose the correct noun? (2) Does に mark the location? (3) Does います describe the person/animal and あります describe the object? (4) Can someone understand the room you describe? This writing is self-reviewed, not automatically graded.
