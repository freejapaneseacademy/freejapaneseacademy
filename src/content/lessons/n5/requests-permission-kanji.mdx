## A notice at the community learning room

Imagine a small Japanese community learning room. At the door, staff post a short sign. You already know how to make polite requests with **〜てください**, ask permission with **〜てもいいですか**, and state prohibitions with **〜てはいけません**. Today, use six common kanji to read those messages more confidently.

**By the end**, you can recognize the characters in real words rather than guessing from their shapes alone. The sign and dialogues below are original teaching examples, not actual building or emergency instructions.

### The notice — first read

> **学習室（がくしゅうしつ）のお知らせ**  
> ① 入（い）り口（ぐち）で手（て）を洗（あら）ってください。  
> ② 水（みず）を飲（の）んでもいいです。  
> ③ この部屋（へや）で火（ひ）を使（つか）ってはいけません。  
> ④ 出（で）るときは、ドアを閉（し）めてください。

**Meaning:** (1) Please wash your hands at the entrance. (2) You may drink water. (3) You must not use fire in this room. (4) Please close the door when leaving.

The sign contains two requests, one statement of permission, and one prohibition. Read the **ending** before deciding which function the sentence serves.

## Six characters in useful words

| Kanji | Word and reading | Meaning in this context | Notice clue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 手 | 手（て） | hand | 手を洗ってください |
| 入 | 入り口（いりぐち） | entrance | 入り口で |
| 口 | 入り口（いりぐち） | opening; entrance in this compound | 入り口で |
| 水 | 水（みず） | water | 水を飲んでもいいです |
| 火 | 火（ひ） | fire | 火を使ってはいけません |
| 出 | 出る（でる） | to leave or go out | 出るときは |

**Reading note:** A kanji can have several readings. Here, **入** is **い** in 入り口, **口** is **ぐち** in 入り口, and **出** is **で** in 出る. Do not assume a single pronunciation works in every compound. For example, **入口（いりぐち）** is also commonly written without the okurigana **り**, but this lesson keeps **入り口** for transparency.

**Shape clues:** 手 has four strokes and a vertical stroke through the middle; 水 has a central vertical form with strokes spreading outward; 火 has a central shape with strokes like rising flames. These are memory aids, not reliable rules for guessing unknown characters. Learn each character together with its actual word and reading.

## How meaning and grammar work together

**Request:** 入り口で手を洗ってください。 (*いりぐちで てを あらってください*) — “Please wash your hands at the entrance.” The particle **で** marks where the action happens; **を** marks what is washed. **〜てください** is a polite request or instruction.

**Permission:** 水を飲んでもいいです。 (*みずを のんでもいいです*) — “You may drink water.” A learner could ask **水を飲んでもいいですか。** (*みずを のんでもいいですか*) — “May I drink water?” The final **か** makes this a question. The statement on the sign grants permission; it does not ask a question.

**Prohibition:** この部屋で火を使ってはいけません。 (*このへやで ひを つかってはいけません*) — “You must not use fire in this room.” The **は** in **〜てはいけません** belongs to the prohibition pattern. Do not confuse it with **〜てもいいです**: these have opposite practical meanings.

**Leaving:** 出るときは、ドアを閉めてください。 (*でるときは ドアを しめてください*) — “When leaving, please close the door.” **出る** is a dictionary-form verb modifying **とき** (“when”). The requested action is **閉めてください**, not **出てください**.

### Mini dialogue

> 学生（がくせい）：先生（せんせい）、水を飲んでもいいですか。  
> 先生：はい、いいですよ。でも、火を使ってはいけません。  
> 学生：わかりました。ありがとうございます。

**English:** Student: “Teacher, may I drink water?” Teacher: “Yes, that's fine. But you must not use fire.” Student: “Understood. Thank you.”

Notice that **いいですよ** responds to a permission question, while **〜てはいけません** announces a rule. The teacher's response is not a request to use fire.

## Guided recognition: look, read, interpret

1. Cover the readings in the table. Find **水** in the sign, say **みず**, and identify what is allowed.
2. Point to **火** and read **ひ**. Identify the exact prohibition ending **使ってはいけません**.
3. Circle **入** and **口** together in **入り口**. Read the *whole word* **いりぐち**, not two isolated English glosses.
4. Compare **入り口** with **出る**. The first names an entrance; the second describes going out.
5. Locate **手** and read **て**. Say the whole request **手を洗ってください**.

**Common mix-up:** **入る（はいる）** means “enter,” but **入り口（いりぐち）** is pronounced *いりぐち*, not *はいりくち*. Reading changes across words. Also, **火（ひ）** is fire, while **日（ひ）** can mean day or sun; they are different characters. Use the surrounding sentence as evidence.

## Independent reading and writing

Without looking at the English meanings, copy the four Japanese lines from the sign. Underline each of the six target kanji wherever it occurs. Then write two new short notices for the same fictional room:

- **水を飲んでもいいです。** — You may drink water.
- **入り口で手を洗ってください。** — Please wash your hands at the entrance.

For an extra challenge, change the first to a question by adding **か** and explain who would naturally say it. Read your own lines aloud if useful; this is *not* a recorded pronunciation test.

**Self-review (ungraded):** Did you use the intended kanji in the correct word? Is the reading accurate? Is **〜てください** a request, **〜てもいいですか** a permission question, and **〜てはいけません** a prohibition? Did you keep **を** and **で** where needed? If unsure, return to the sign and compare character by character.
