## Two voices, two everyday situations

In the first original recording, a class leader announces Saturday activities. In the second, two cooking-club members discuss who does what. Both are **fictional learning scenarios**, spoken by approved synthetic Japanese voices rather than real people.

Your task is to hear **actions and stated details**, not to identify written characters. Begin with the transcripts hidden. Replay each track until you can identify the topic. Then listen for a time, person, place or duration. You may use the slower 0.8× playback option.

### Before listening: meaning cues

| Verb pair | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 読む → 読みます | よむ → よみます | read |
| 書く → 書きます | かく → かきます | write |
| 見る → 見ます | みる → みます | watch / see |
| 切る → 切ります | きる → きります | cut |
| 作る → 作ります | つくる → つくります | make |
| 飲む → 飲みます | のむ → のみます | drink |

These pairs are useful *recognition tools*, not answers to the recordings. **切る** and **作る** are Group 1 verbs despite ending in る. In contrast, **見る** is Group 2. Compare **切ります** with **見ます**: dropping る indiscriminately produces incorrect forms.

## Listening sequence

**First pass — main idea:** Listen to track A without text. Is it a class announcement or a shopping conversation? Then listen to track B: is it about cooking activities or directions? These are informal orientation questions, not scored.

**Second pass — audible details:** For A, note the activity order and the spoken number for a duration. For B, listen for **まず** (first), a person's name and **最後に** (finally). The final question asks for the time as spoken; do not infer morning or afternoon when it is not stated.

**Third pass — independent comprehension:** Answer the eight questions in the listening activity. The player permits replays. An unaided attempt is recorded locally only after both recordings have been started and all questions answered. If you reveal a transcript before submitting, the interface switches to **assisted practice** and does not record a blind listening score.

## Understanding the form contrast

The announcer uses **読みます**, a polite nonpast sentence form, and **読む時間**, where the dictionary form modifies **時間** (“time for reading”). The cooking-club speaker similarly says **切ります** and then **切る仕事** (“the cutting task”). This is a meaningful grammatical contrast: the same action is expressed in two sentence roles.

Compare the following *new practice examples*, not transcript clues:

- **新聞を読みます。**（しんぶんをよみます。）— I read / will read a newspaper (polite).
- **新聞を読む時間です。**（しんぶんをよむじかんです。）— It's time to read the newspaper.
- **野菜を切ります。**（やさいをきります。）— I cut / will cut vegetables.
- **野菜を切る仕事です。**（やさいをきるしごとです。）— It's the vegetable-cutting task.

The **ます** form is not automatically past tense. The polite past of **読みます** is **読みました**. Do not confuse them when listening.

## After your first score

Reveal the Japanese transcript and English translation for each track. Replay the corresponding lines and locate the *exact spoken evidence* for any missed question. The transcript is provided for verification and accessibility, not as a shortcut to an unaided score.

**Optional speaking:** Pause after one sentence and repeat at a comfortable pace. This is self-directed shadowing, not a graded pronunciation test; no microphone is requested.

**Writing transfer (ungraded):** Write two to four short sentences about an imaginary activity club. Use at least two different polite verbs and one time expression. Model: **日曜日に本を読みます。それから、お茶を飲みます。** (“On Sunday I read a book. Then I drink tea.”) Check whether your verbs match their dictionary forms.
