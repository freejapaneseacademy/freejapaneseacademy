## A missing notebook at reception

You are helping return belongings after class. The speaker, the listener and a distant shelf can each have different objects. **Distance is relative to the people speaking**, not an intrinsic property of the object.

### 1. Three stand-alone pointing words

| Japanese | Reading | Typical reference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">これ</span> | kore | this one, near the speaker |
| <span lang="ja">それ</span> | sore | that one, near the listener |
| <span lang="ja">あれ</span> | are | that one over there, away from both |

At the counter, the receptionist holds an umbrella: <span lang="ja">これは かさです。</span> — “This is an umbrella.” The student points to a notebook beside the receptionist: <span lang="ja">それは ノートですか。</span> — “Is that a notebook?” Both point to a bag on the far shelf: <span lang="ja">あれは かばんです。</span> — “That over there is a bag.”

The speaker's viewpoint can change after the other person takes a turn. In ordinary conversation, それ can also refer back to something already mentioned; the location table is the beginner's physical-pointing model.

### 2. Before a noun: この・その・あの

| Stand-alone | Must be followed by a noun | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| これ | この (*kono*) | <span lang="ja">この ペン</span> — this pen |
| それ | その (*sono*) | <span lang="ja">その ノート</span> — that notebook near you |
| あれ | あの (*ano*) | <span lang="ja">あの かばん</span> — that bag over there |

**Do not write** <span lang="ja">これ ペン</span> for “this pen.” Say <span lang="ja">この ペン</span> or use a complete sentence: <span lang="ja">これは ペンです。</span> (“This is a pen.”) Likewise, <span lang="ja">そのは</span> is not a complete noun phrase.

### 3. Ownership with の

Place the owner before <span lang="ja">の</span>: <span lang="ja">ミナさんの ノート</span> means “Mina's notebook.” The particle expresses a relationship; in these lost-and-found examples it marks ownership.

- <span lang="ja">この ノートは ミナさんのです。</span> — This notebook is Mina's.
- <span lang="ja">その けしゴムは だれのですか。</span> — Whose eraser is that (near you)?
- <span lang="ja">わたしのです。</span> — It's mine.
- <span lang="ja">あの かばんは レンさんのです。</span> — That bag over there is Ren's.

In <span lang="ja">ミナさんのです</span>, the noun (for example ノート) is understood from context. Compare <span lang="ja">ミナさんです</span> (“It is Mina / I am Mina,” depending on context), which does **not** express possession.

### 4. Read an original exchange

The receptionist is holding a pencil. A notebook is beside the student, and a bag is on a shelf across the room.

**Receptionist:** <span lang="ja">これは だれの えんぴつですか。</span> — Whose pencil is this?

**Student:** <span lang="ja">それは わたしのです。</span> — That one is mine.

**Receptionist:** <span lang="ja">その ノートも あなたのですか。</span> — Is that notebook near you also yours?

**Student:** <span lang="ja">いいえ。ミナさんのです。</span> — No. It's Mina's.

**Receptionist:** <span lang="ja">あの かばんは だれのですか。</span> — Whose bag is that over there?

**Student:** <span lang="ja">レンさんのです。</span> — It's Ren's.

**Evidence check:** The pencil belongs to the student; the notebook belongs to Mina; the bag belongs to Ren. The receptionist's これ becomes the student's それ because they are viewing the same pencil from different positions.

### 5. Controlled → guided → independent practice

**Controlled:** Complete: “___ ペン” for a pen in your hand → <span lang="ja">この ペン</span>. For “this one is a pen,” use <span lang="ja">これは ペンです。</span>. Explain why the forms differ: one modifies a noun; the other replaces it.

**Guided:** A notebook is beside your partner. Ask whose it is: <span lang="ja">その ノートは だれのですか。</span> Your partner answers: <span lang="ja">わたしのです。</span> Switch roles. Now imagine a bag on a distant shelf and change その to あの.

**Independent writing:** Draw or imagine a desk with three labeled locations: near me, near you, and far away. Write **three complete Japanese sentences** identifying objects and **one ownership question**, then answer it. Check that every この・その・あの has a noun immediately after it and every だれの asks for an owner. Use the dialogue as a model, but change the objects and owners.

**Reading self-check:** In the dialogue, underline the Japanese evidence for Mina's notebook and Ren's bag. Do not infer that the notebook belongs to the student just because it is near them.
