## Your speaking mission

Imagine joining a fictional international community class. Introduce yourself in **two to five short sentences**, then ask a partner about school, work or hometown. This is optional speaking practice: you may rehearse silently or read aloud without a microphone. The two chapter L06 recordings are candidate AivisSpeech models; playback and pronunciation must be verified before public release. **No speaking grade is generated.**

## 1. Build a polite profile in four steps

| Purpose | Japanese with reading support | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Greet | はじめまして。 | Nice to meet you. |
| Name | わたしは 小林（こばやし）あきです。 | I'm Aki Kobayashi. |
| Age | 十九歳（じゅうきゅうさい）です。 | I'm nineteen. |
| Hometown | 出身（しゅっしん）は 京都（きょうと）です。 | I'm from Kyoto. |
| Role | 大学生（だいがくせい）です。 | I'm a university student. |

**です** makes a polite noun sentence; it does not mean “am” in every possible context. **は** introduces a topic, and its particle reading is *wa*. After introducing yourself, Japanese often omits repeated **わたしは**. **十九歳** is *じゅうきゅうさい*; **二十歳** is commonly *はたち*. An age is optional in real life—do not feel obliged to share personal details.

**Common mistake:** **京都出身です** describes origin, while **今（いま）は 東京（とうきょう）に 住（す）んでいます** describes present residence. A person's hometown need not be their current city.

## 2. Scene A — two students at orientation

Use the first original L06 track as a shadowing model. Listen, pause after each turn, then repeat. Switch roles on your second pass.

> **Woman:** はじめまして。わたしは小林あきです。十九歳です。
>
> **Man:** はじめまして。田中けんです。小林さんは大学生ですか。
>
> **Woman:** はい、大学生です。出身は京都です。
>
> **Man:** そうですか。わたしも大学生です。出身は大阪です。

**Meaning:** Aki says she is nineteen. Ken asks whether she is a university student. Aki says yes and that she is from Kyoto. Ken says he is also a university student, but is from Osaka. **も** means “also” here: the shared fact is being a student, not having the same hometown.

**Rehearsal:** Repeat the woman's turns, then the man's. On a third pass, try answering **大学生ですか** without reading. Do not claim you heard the scene if the WAV is unavailable; use the printed dialogue as ungraded reading rehearsal instead.

## 3. Scene B — a corrected assumption

> **Man:** 佐藤（さとう）さんは会社員（かいしゃいん）ですか。
>
> **Woman:** いいえ、会社員じゃありません。高校（こうこう）の先生（せんせい）です。
>
> **Man:** そうですか。出身は東京ですか。
>
> **Woman:** いいえ、出身は名古屋（なごや）です。今は東京に住んでいます。

**Meaning:** Sato is not a company employee; she is a high-school teacher. She is from Nagoya and currently lives in Tokyo. **いいえ** rejects the guess. **じゃありません** is a polite conversational negative of a noun predicate; **ではありません** is a more formal alternative.

**Rehearsal:** First ask the man's two questions. Switch to Sato's role and answer both with a calm correction. Notice that a negative answer is followed by a positive, useful fact.

## 4. Invent a safe, fictional introduction

Make up a name, age and place; you never need to disclose your own identity. Try this *new written model* (it has no claimed recording):

> A: はじめまして。わたしは みかです。大阪出身です。大学生です。
>
> B: みかさんは 二十歳ですか。
>
> A: いいえ、二十一歳（にじゅういっさい）です。
>
> B: そうですか。よろしくお願いします。

**English:** “Nice to meet you. I'm Mika. I'm from Osaka. I'm a university student.” “Mika, are you twenty?” “No, I'm twenty-one.” “I see. Pleased to meet you.”

Create a three- or four-turn variation. Use **ですか** to ask, **いいえ** to correct, and **よろしくお願いします** to close. If you prefer, type the dialogue on paper or practise silently.

## 5. Self-review without a speech score

Check four things: (1) you introduce a fictional name and one true *within-the-story* profile fact; (2) your topic **は** and polite **です** are in appropriate positions; (3) your hometown and residence are not confused; (4) you can switch roles and respond to **ですか** politely. A recording is optional, private and initiated only by you. It is not uploaded or analysed.

The eight questions below check **written preparation and meaning**, not your pronunciation, listening comprehension or JLPT level. Review one scene tomorrow, then revisit the chapter's listening lesson for genuine audio-only comprehension.
