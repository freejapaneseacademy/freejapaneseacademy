## Mission: check what is in the room

Imagine helping a new student find a teacher, a book and a bag. In this checkpoint you will say **who or what exists**, **where**, and **what is not there**. The classroom diagram below is a fictional reading scene. The two listening dialogues are separate fictional settings; do not combine their facts.

### 1. Review the existence pattern

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 先生（せんせい） | せんせい | teacher |
| 猫（ねこ） | ねこ | cat |
| 本（ほん） | ほん | book |
| 辞書（じしょ） | じしょ | dictionary |
| 机（つくえ） | つくえ | desk |
| 廊下（ろうか） | ろうか | hallway |
| あります | あります | exists (an inanimate thing) |
| います | います | exists/is present (a person or animal) |

**Place に + thing/person が + existence verb** is a useful beginner pattern:

- <span lang="ja">教室に先生がいます。</span> — There is a teacher in the classroom.
- <span lang="ja">机の上に本があります。</span> — There is a book on the desk.
- <span lang="ja">窓の近くに猫がいます。</span> — There is a cat near the window.

**に** identifies the location. **が** identifies what or who exists. Do not swap them in this pattern. **あります** applies to objects such as a book; **います** applies to people and animals. A plant is normally described with **あります** when stating its existence, despite being biologically alive: the distinction is grammatical usage, not simply biological life.

For a negative response, <span lang="ja">かばんはありません。</span> means “There is no bag,” while <span lang="ja">先生はいません。</span> means “The teacher is not here.” **は** often marks the already discussed item in a correction; it does not mean that が is always wrong in affirmative statements.

### 3. Listen for an item, then a correction

The chapter's L06 audio uses two other scenes. **Track A** is a study room: someone asks whether a notebook is on a desk. A blue notebook is present, but a red notebook is not. **Track B** is a classroom conversation: a teacher is present, but a bag is not in that classroom; it is in the hallway.

First listen without revealing the transcripts. Replay to identify **あります・います・ありません** and listen for the correction **いいえ**. Answer the listening questions before opening the transcripts; revealing them marks the attempt transcript-assisted. **No listening score is available until both actual recordings can play.** Reading a transcript alone is not listening.

### 4. Guided writing and optional speaking

Write **four connected Japanese sentences** describing an imaginary classroom: one person, one animal, one object and one object that is absent. Model to consult *after* your attempt:

> <span lang="ja">教室に先生がいます。窓の近くに猫がいます。机の上に本があります。かばんはありません。</span>

Self-review: (1) Does each person/animal take います? (2) Does each object take あります? (3) Does に introduce a location? (4) Does が introduce a new entity? (5) Does the negative statement match your imaginary room? This open writing is **not automatically graded**.

Optional roleplay: Ask <span lang="ja">教室に先生がいますか。</span> and answer <span lang="ja">はい、います。</span> or <span lang="ja">いいえ、いません。</span> No microphone starts automatically. Do not infer pronunciation accuracy from reading aloud.
