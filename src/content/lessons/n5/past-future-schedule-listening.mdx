## Two schedules: what happened and what is planned

In **Track A**, two fictional people discuss what one person did yesterday. In **Track B**, two people discuss tomorrow's plans. Your job is to hear the main topic and pick out **stated facts**, not to guess from familiar words in a question. These are original educational scripts performed by synthetic voices when the approved recordings are available.

### Before listening: useful clues

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 昨日 | きのう | yesterday |
| 昨日の朝 | きのうのあさ | yesterday morning |
| 午後 | ごご | afternoon / p.m. |
| 明日 | あした | tomorrow |
| 明日の朝 | あしたのあさ | tomorrow morning |
| 図書館 | としょかん | library |
| 勉強しました | べんきょうしました | studied |
| 勉強しませんでした | べんきょうしませんでした | did not study |
| 映画を見ました | えいがをみました | watched a film |
| 買い物をします | かいものをします | go shopping |
| 本を読みます | ほんをよみます | read a book |

**ました** marks a polite past affirmative action: **見ました** (“watched”). **ませんでした** marks polite past negative: **勉強しませんでした** (“did not study”). For plans with **明日**, polite nonpast **行きます** can mean “will go,” while **しません** can mean “will not do.” The time expression gives the intended timeframe; the nonpast form alone does not encode a special future tense.

**で** marks where an activity takes place (**図書館で勉強しました**), **に** can mark a specific time (**九時に**), **へ** marks direction (**公園へ行きます**), and **と** marks accompaniment (**友達と**). Avoid confusing a question (“Did you study?”) with the speaker's negative answer.

### Track A — yesterday

Listen once for the general topic. On a second pass, find where the speaker studied in the morning. On a third pass, listen carefully to the afternoon answer: the speaker **did not study**, but did another activity with a friend. Notice how **ませんでした** contrasts with **見ました**.

### Track B — tomorrow

Listen first for the general plan. Next, identify the time for the park visit. Finally, listen to the afternoon contrast: a question asks about shopping, but the reply says no and names a different activity at home. Do not assume that a mentioned activity is planned just because it appears in the question.

### Three-pass practice

1. Play each track without revealing its transcript. Say whether it concerns yesterday or tomorrow.
2. Replay and note one time or location, one positive action and one explicitly negative action.
3. Answer the six questions below. Reveal the transcripts only after attempting blind listening; revealing one labels that attempt **transcript-assisted**, even if you hide it again.

The listening score is disabled until both authentic recordings are playable. A committed synthesis job is not proof of recorded audio.

### Guided writing: yesterday and tomorrow

Create three sentences about an imaginary schedule. Include one past affirmative, one past negative, and one tomorrow plan. For example:

**昨日、家で本を読みました。午後はテレビを見ませんでした。明日は図書館へ行きます。**

“Yesterday I read a book at home. In the afternoon I did not watch TV. Tomorrow I will go to the library.”

Self-check: Did you use **ました** for an action that happened, **ませんでした** for one that did not happen, and **ます** for a plan framed by **明日**? Are **で** and **へ** used for the activity location and destination respectively? This writing task is for learner self-review, not an automatically graded composition.

### Optional speaking

Once the genuine audio is ready, replay **昨日、何をしましたか。** and **明日は何をしますか。** Shadow the phrases, then answer with your own imaginary past action and future plan. No microphone permission or recording is required; pronunciation is not automatically graded.
