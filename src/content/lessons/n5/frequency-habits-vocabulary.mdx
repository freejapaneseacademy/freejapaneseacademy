## How often do you do it?

**Chapter Can-do:** Explain how often you do familiar activities. Imagine a small school-club survey. One student asks about reading, exercise and weekend routines. First learn to recognize the expressions, then use them in short, accurate statements. This is a **written vocabulary lesson**; the L06 habit survey will provide authentic listening practice once licensed recordings are ready.

## 1. Twelve expressions in context

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning in this context | Original example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">いつも</span> | いつも | always / habitually | <span lang="ja">いつも朝ごはんを食べます。</span> — I always eat breakfast. |
| <span lang="ja">よく</span> | よく | often | <span lang="ja">よく図書館で勉強します。</span> — I often study at the library. |

| <span lang="ja">運動します</span> | うんどうします | exercise | <span lang="ja">よく運動します。</span> — I exercise often. |

**Read the whole word:** 毎日 is **まいにち**, 毎朝 is **まいあさ**, 毎晩 is **まいばん**, 週末 is **しゅうまつ**, 習慣 is **しゅうかん**. The kanji in these compounds help recognition; do not assume every kanji has one fixed pronunciation. **運動します** and **散歩します** are polite verb forms built from nouns and **します**.

## 2. Frequency is not an exact number

**いつも** describes an always/regularly repeated habit; **よく** means often; **ときどき** means sometimes. They do not specify a precise count per week. **毎日** means every day, which is a calendar-based expression rather than a vague frequency estimate. **毎朝** and **毎晩** locate a repeated action in the morning or evening. **週末** names the weekend; by itself it does not say whether an activity happens every weekend.

Compare these original sentences:

- <span lang="ja">毎日、日本語を勉強します。</span> (まいにち、にほんごをべんきょうします。) — I study Japanese every day.
- <span lang="ja">よく日本語を勉強します。</span> (よくにほんごをべんきょうします。) — I often study Japanese.
- <span lang="ja">ときどき日本語を勉強します。</span> (ときどきにほんごをべんきょうします。) — I sometimes study Japanese.

The first statement is specific about *every day*; the other two give approximate frequency.

## 3. Negative patterns: not much versus not at all

For this beginner chapter, pair **あまり** with a negative predicate: <span lang="ja">あまり運動しません。</span> — I do not exercise much. Pair **ぜんぜん** with a negative predicate: <span lang="ja">ぜんぜん運動しません。</span> — I do not exercise at all. The second is stronger. **あまり運動します** and **ぜんぜん運動します** are not the target patterns taught here. In some contemporary informal contexts ぜんぜん can occur with affirmative expressions, but do not generalize that usage to these N5 examples.

**Contrast with いつも:** <span lang="ja">いつもは運動しません。</span> means “I do not usually exercise” in a contrastive context. It is not a substitute for **ぜんぜん運動しません**, which says “I do not exercise at all.”

## 4. Fictional club survey: Rina's habits

> <span lang="ja">りなさんのメモ</span> — Rina's notes
>
> <span lang="ja">毎朝、七時に起きます。</span> — Every morning I get up at seven.
>
> <span lang="ja">毎日、日本語を勉強します。</span> — I study Japanese every day.
>
> <span lang="ja">よく図書館で本を読みます。</span> — I often read books at the library.
>
> <span lang="ja">週末、ときどき友だちと散歩します。</span> — On weekends I sometimes take walks with friends.
>
> <span lang="ja">テレビはあまり見ません。</span> — I do not watch much television.
>
> <span lang="ja">コーヒーはぜんぜん飲みません。</span> — I do not drink coffee at all.

**Evidence check:** Which habit happens every day? Find **毎日、日本語を勉強します**. Does Rina never watch television? **No**: **あまり見ません** says “not much,” not “never.” The note gives no exact number of library visits; **よく** does not mean a fixed count.

## 5. Verbs and particles with habits

**本を読みます** marks the book with **を**; **図書館で読みます** uses **で** for where reading happens. **友だちと散歩します** uses **と** for a companion. **七時に起きます** uses **に** for the clock time. Frequency words commonly come before the action, as in **よく本を読みます**. A beginner can safely use these models without treating word order as completely rigid.

**Common errors:** **あまり** does not mean “never”; **ぜんぜん〜ません** does. **毎朝** is every morning, not this morning (**今朝／けさ**). **週末** is the weekend, not next week (**来週／らいしゅう**). **習慣** is the noun “habit,” not a frequency adverb.

## 6. Controlled → guided → independent practice

**Controlled recall:** Cover the English column and read **毎日**, **毎朝**, **毎晩**, **週末**, **習慣** aloud using the kana. Then choose which phrase fits: “I never drink coffee” → **ぜんぜん飲みません**; “I do not drink much coffee” → **あまり飲みません**.

**Guided substitution:** Change only the frequency in <span lang="ja">よく散歩します。</span> to **ときどき散歩します。** Now make a negative statement with **あまり散歩しません。** Explain how the meaning changes.

**Independent transfer:** Write two sentences about a *fictional* student, one affirmative and one negative. Model: <span lang="ja">毎晩、本を読みます。テレビはあまり見ません。</span> Check that **あまり** has a negative verb. Different truthful fictional answers are possible; this writing is self-reviewed, not machine graded.

**Optional speaking rehearsal:** Read the survey note aloud, hiding the English after the first pass. No audio recording, automatic pronunciation score or listening grade is provided in this L02.
