## Your mission: help three classmates get to the right places

Three classmates are travelling to a library, station and park. Read their fictional plans and explain where each person is going, how they travel, and whether they are coming toward someone or returning home. This checkpoint reviews Chapter 01; it is **not** an official JLPT examination.

### 1. Review the movement verbs

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning | Viewpoint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">行きます</span> | いきます | go | Movement toward a destination, generally away from the speaker's current position |
| <span lang="ja">来ます</span> | きます | come | Movement toward the speaker or another established reference point |
| <span lang="ja">帰ります</span> | かえります | return | Go back to home or another place of belonging |

Compare <span lang="ja">わたしは図書館（としょかん）に行きます。</span> (“I am going to the library”) with <span lang="ja">友（とも）だちは図書館に来ます。</span> (“My friend is coming to the library”), said by someone **already at the library**. The same journey may be described differently depending on whose viewpoint is established. <span lang="ja">帰ります</span> emphasizes returning, not merely moving to any destination.

### 2. Destination versus means

- <span lang="ja">駅（えき）に行きます。</span> — I go to the station. **に** marks the destination.
- <span lang="ja">公園（こうえん）へ行きます。</span> — I go toward/to the park. **へ**, pronounced **え** as a particle, marks direction or destination.
- <span lang="ja">電車（でんしゃ）で行きます。</span> — I go by train. **で** marks the means of travel.
- <span lang="ja">バスで公園に行きます。</span> — I go to the park by bus. The transport and destination each have their own particle.
- <span lang="ja">歩（ある）いて駅に行きます。</span> — I walk to the station. **歩いて** is a useful whole expression for “on foot.”

**Common error:** <span lang="ja">電車に図書館で行きます</span> swaps the particles and does not mean “go to the library by train.” Use <span lang="ja">電車で図書館に行きます。</span> Remember that **に** can have other functions in Japanese, but here it marks the travel destination.

### 3. Read an original route plan

The following plan is fictional. Read it first without the English notes, then find the exact evidence for each question.

| Person | Japanese route plan |
| --- | --- |
| たけし (Takeshi) | <span lang="ja">電車で図書館に行きます。</span> |
| ゆき (Yuki) | <span lang="ja">歩いて駅に行きます。駅からバスで学校（がっこう）に行きます。</span> |
| みか (Mika) | <span lang="ja">バスで公園に行きます。公園から家（いえ）に帰ります。</span> |

**English meaning:** Takeshi goes to the library by train. Yuki walks to the station and then goes to school by bus. Mika goes to the park by bus, then returns home from the park. <span lang="ja">から</span> marks the starting point (“from”), while <span lang="ja">に</span> marks the destination in these examples.

**Evidence strategy:** For “Who goes by train?” locate <span lang="ja">電車で</span>. For “Where does Mika return?” locate <span lang="ja">家に帰ります</span>. A familiar word in an incorrect answer does not make it correct: verify the entire clause.

### 5. Guided writing and optional speaking

Write **two to five connected Japanese sentences** about a fictional trip. Include a destination, transport method, and a return or arrival from a clear viewpoint.

Model: <span lang="ja">土曜日（どようび）に電車で図書館に行きます。友だちは図書館に来ます。午後（ごご）四時（よじ）に家に帰ります。</span>

“On Saturday I go to the library by train. My friend comes to the library. At four in the afternoon I return home.”

Before comparing your draft, check: (1) destination with に or へ; (2) transport with で; (3) 行きます versus 来ます from the intended viewpoint; (4) 帰ります for returning; (5) connected sentences with polite endings. Your writing is self-reviewed, **not** automatically graded.

Optional speaking: read the model aloud, then change 図書館 to 公園. If verified chapter audio is available, shadow a short line; otherwise practise without claiming a model pronunciation. No microphone is activated or pronunciation grade assigned.
