## Read a plan, not just isolated words

**Can-do:** Explain where someone is going, how they travel, and when they return. A route plan is a useful real-world text because it combines short sentences, place names, times and transport. This lesson is **written reading comprehension**; no listening or pronunciation score is claimed.

First, skim the plan for the order of events. Then reread and underline the *exact Japanese phrase* that proves each answer. Do not guess from familiar vocabulary alone.

## 1. Before reading: four signposts

| Expression | Reading | What it signals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">午前九時</span> | ごぜんくじ | 9 a.m.; a time clue |
| <span lang="ja">駅へ行きます</span> | えきへいきます | going to the station |
| <span lang="ja">電車で行きます</span> | でんしゃでいきます | travelling by train |
| <span lang="ja">家に帰ります</span> | いえにかえります | returning home |

Remember: **へ** (pronounced え) and **に** can mark a destination; **で** can mark a means of transport. In a route plan, **来ます** means someone comes toward the reference location. **帰ります** signals a return to home or another usual base.

## 2. Original route plan: Mei's Saturday

Read this notice as if Mei sent it to her friend Haru. The timetable and details are fictional.

> ### 土曜日の予定（どようびの よてい）— Saturday's plan
>
> <span lang="ja">午前九時に、メイは家を出ます。</span>  
> ごぜん くじに、メイは いえを でます。  
>
> <span lang="ja">九時半に駅へ行きます。駅までは歩いて行きます。</span>  
> くじはんに えきへ いきます。えきまでは あるいて いきます。  
>
> <span lang="ja">駅でハルが来ます。二人は電車で図書館の近くの駅へ行きます。</span>  
> えきで ハルが きます。ふたりは でんしゃで としょかんの ちかくの えきへ いきます。  
>
> <span lang="ja">午前十時に図書館に行きます。そこで本を読みます。</span>  
> ごぜん じゅうじに としょかんに いきます。そこで ほんを よみます。  
>
> <span lang="ja">正午に近くの公園へ行きます。公園でお弁当を食べます。</span>  
> しょうごに ちかくの こうえんへ いきます。こうえんで おべんとうを たべます。  
>
> <span lang="ja">午後二時に、メイはバスで家に帰ります。</span>  
> ごご にじに、メイは バスで いえに かえります。

### English meaning (check after your first read)

At 9 a.m., Mei leaves home. At 9:30 she goes to the station, walking there. Haru arrives at the station. Together they take a train to a station near the library. At 10 a.m. they go to the library and read books there. At noon they go to a nearby park and eat boxed lunches. At 2 p.m. Mei returns home by bus.

## 3. Follow the route

| Time | Japanese evidence | Event |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 9:00 a.m. | <span lang="ja">午前九時に…家を出ます</span> | Mei leaves home |
| 9:30 a.m. | <span lang="ja">九時半に駅へ行きます</span> | Mei goes to the station |
| Before 10:00 a.m. | <span lang="ja">電車で図書館の近くの駅へ行きます</span> | They take a train toward the library area |
| 10:00 a.m. | <span lang="ja">午前十時に図書館に行きます</span> | They visit the library |
| Noon | <span lang="ja">正午に近くの公園へ行きます</span> | They go to the park |
| 2:00 p.m. | <span lang="ja">午後二時に…バスで家に帰ります</span> | Mei returns by bus |

**Important distinction:** The text does **not** say that the library itself is reached directly by train. It says they take a train to a **station near the library** and then go to the library. Reading the destination carefully prevents an overconfident inference.

## 4. How to prove an answer

**Question:** How does Mei get to the first station?  
**Evidence:** <span lang="ja">駅までは歩いて行きます。</span> (“She walks as far as the station.”)  
**Answer:** On foot.

**Question:** Who arrives at the station?  
**Evidence:** <span lang="ja">駅でハルが来ます。</span> (“Haru comes to the station.”)  
**Answer:** Haru, not Mei. The subject marker **が** points to Haru.

**Question:** What does Mei do at 2 p.m.?  
**Evidence:** <span lang="ja">午後二時に、メイはバスで家に帰ります。</span>  
**Answer:** She goes home by bus.

These are **stated facts**, not assumptions. If the plan doesn't specify what Haru does after lunch, the correct response is “not stated.”

## 5. Useful reading contrasts

- <span lang="ja">駅でハルが来ます</span>: **で** here marks the place where Haru arrives/appears in the scene, not transport. Compare <span lang="ja">電車で行きます</span>, where **で** marks the vehicle.
- <span lang="ja">家を出ます</span>: **を** marks the place departed from with 出ます; it does not mean the house is the object being carried.
- <span lang="ja">図書館の近くの駅</span>: the station **near** the library, not a station inside the library.
- <span lang="ja">午前十時</span> (10 a.m.) and <span lang="ja">午後二時</span> (2 p.m.) are different parts of the day. **正午** means noon.
- <span lang="ja">歩いて行きます</span> uses the walking verb form; don't write <span lang="ja">歩いてで行きます</span>.

## 6. Guided rereading

Cover the English meaning and answer in order: (1) Where does Mei go at 9:30? (2) Who meets her? (3) What do they do at the library? (4) Where do they eat? (5) How does Mei return?

Then find these five evidence strings in the Japanese plan: **駅へ行きます**, **ハルが来ます**, **本を読みます**, **公園でお弁当を食べます**, **バスで家に帰ります**. Notice that **公園で** marks the location of eating, while **バスで** marks the means of returning.

## 7. Independent transfer

Write three short sentences about a different day. Use one destination, one transport choice and one return. For example:

<span lang="ja">日曜日に公園へ行きます。自転車で行きます。午後三時に家に帰ります。</span>  
にちようびに こうえんへ いきます。じてんしゃで いきます。ごご さんじに いえに かえります。  
“On Sunday I'll go to the park. I'll go by bicycle. At 3 p.m. I'll return home.”

Self-check: Is each destination paired with **へ/に**? Is each vehicle paired with **で**? Is your return expressed with **帰ります**? This writing is optional and not automatically graded.

## Review

A good route-plan reader uses **time + subject + destination + travel means + action** as a checklist. In the quiz, read the Japanese evidence before choosing. Later, L06 will practise understanding movement information from authentic audio rather than printed text.
