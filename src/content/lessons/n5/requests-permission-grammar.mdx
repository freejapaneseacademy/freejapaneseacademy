## One verb, three intentions

**Can-do:** In a classroom or public space, ask someone to do something, ask whether you may do something, and understand or state a simple rule. This lesson develops the grammar introduced in L01. All situations below are fictional.

At a community-center reading room, a volunteer says:

- <span lang="ja"><ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでください。</span> — *Please read the book.*
- <span lang="ja"><ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでもいいですか。</span> — *May I read the book?*
- <span lang="ja">ここで<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んではいけません。</span> — *You must not read books here.*

The action is the same, **読む** (*read*), but the endings change the speaker's intention. The first addresses a listener, the second asks about the speaker's action, and the third states a rule.

## 1. Start with the correct て-form

The て-form is the attachment point. It may end in **て** or **で**. Keep that sound when adding the expression.

| Dictionary form | て-form | Request | English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 書く（かく） | 書いて（かいて） | 書いてください | Please write |
| 読む（よむ） | 読んで（よんで） | 読んでください | Please read |
| 待つ（まつ） | 待って（まって） | 待ってください | Please wait |
| 食べる（たべる） | 食べて（たべて） | 食べてください | Please eat |
| する | して | してください | Please do it |
| 来る（くる） | 来て（きて） | 来てください | Please come |

Notice **読んでください**, not 読んてください. The て-form of 読む is 読んで. For a more detailed conjugation review, revisit the previous chapter's grammar lesson.

## 2. Request: verb て-form + ください

**Pattern:** <span lang="ja">[verb て-form] + ください。</span>

A teacher tells the class:

<span lang="ja"><ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いてください。</span> — *Please write your name.*

A visitor asks for assistance:

<span lang="ja">ちょっと<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ってください。</span> — *Please wait a moment.*

This is a polite **request**, but it is fairly direct and may sound like an instruction. It does not itself ask permission. The person expected to perform the action is normally the listener.

**Important verb distinction:** <span lang="ja"><ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>てください</span> means *Please look*; <span lang="ja"><ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>せてください</span> means *Please show me*. The latter comes from 見せる (*to show*), not 見る (*to look*).

## 3. Permission question: verb て-form + もいいですか

**Pattern:** <span lang="ja">[verb て-form] + もいいですか。</span>

A student asks:

<span lang="ja">ここに<ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>ってもいいですか。</span> — *May I sit here?*

A visitor asks:

<span lang="ja"><ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>ってもいいですか。</span> — *May I use the dictionary?*

**も** belongs to this construction; do not omit it. Compare:

- <span lang="ja">座ってください。</span> — *Please sit down.* (request to someone else)
- <span lang="ja">座ってもいいですか。</span> — *May I sit down?* (permission question)

A friendly answer may be <span lang="ja">はい、いいですよ。</span> (*Yes, that's fine.*). A refusal might be <span lang="ja">すみません、ここではだめです。</span> (*Sorry, not here.*). Asking does **not** mean the answer is yes.

**Related affirmative statement:** <span lang="ja">ここに座ってもいいです。</span> — *You may sit here.* Removing **か** changes the question into a permission statement; it does not create a request.

## 4. Prohibition: verb て-form + はいけません

**Pattern:** <span lang="ja">[verb て-form] + はいけません。</span>

A sign in a fictional reading room says:

<span lang="ja">ここで<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてはいけません。</span> — *You must not eat here.*

Another says:

<span lang="ja"><ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>撮<rt>と</rt></ruby>ってはいけません。</span> — *You must not take photographs.*

This is a **firm prohibition**, not just a suggestion. It is different from <span lang="ja">食べないでください</span> (*Please don't eat*), which is phrased as a negative request. In casual speech you may hear shorter forms, but learn the complete polite form here.

**Particle trap:** In <span lang="ja">ここで食べてはいけません</span>, **で** after ここ marks the place where eating occurs. The **て** in 食べて is part of the verb form. In <span lang="ja">読んではいけません</span>, the **で** is part of 読んで, not a location particle.

## 5. Compare the grammar, not just the English

| Intended meaning | Japanese | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Please open the door | ドアを開けてください。 | Ask the listener to open it |
| May I open the door? | ドアを開けてもいいですか。 | Ask permission for your action |
| Do not open the door | ドアを開けてはいけません。 | State a prohibition |

A common error is using **〜てください** when you mean *May I ...?* That changes the actor and can make you sound as though you are instructing someone else. Another error is interpreting **〜てはいけません** as *Please don't ...* in every setting; it is stronger, typically expressing a rule or ban.

## 6. Guided practice: three situations

Read each situation, choose the correct ending, then check the explanation.

1. A librarian wants a visitor to wait: <span lang="ja">ここで待って＿＿。</span> **ください**. The librarian requests the visitor's action.
2. You want to drink water: <span lang="ja">水を飲んで＿＿。</span> **もいいですか**. You ask permission.
3. A notice prohibits running: <span lang="ja">ここで走って＿＿。</span> **はいけません**. The notice states a rule.

Read the full Japanese sentences aloud only if comfortable; there is **no audio track or pronunciation grade** in this grammar lesson.

## 7. Independent use: a three-line exchange

Imagine a fictional study room. Write or type three lines: ask whether you may use a dictionary, answer with permission, and ask a classmate to write a name. You may use kana instead of kanji. Compare your response with this model after attempting it:

<span lang="ja">A：辞書を使ってもいいですか。</span> — *May I use the dictionary?*  
<span lang="ja">B：はい、いいですよ。</span> — *Yes, that's fine.*  
<span lang="ja">B：名前を書いてください。</span> — *Please write your name.*

**Self-check (ungraded):** Did you use もいいですか for the question, a clear permission response, and ください for the request? Are the て-forms **使って** and **書いて** correct? You may revise freely; no automatic writing grade is assigned.
