## Write a message before meeting at a café

**Can-do:** Name common foods and express simple preferences. You are writing a short, friendly message to a fictional classmate about lunch. By the end, you will produce **2–5 connected Japanese sentences**, not just select correct words.

**Situation:** You and あき (Aki) plan to eat together. Tell Aki what you like, what you plan to eat and drink, and who you will eat with. Use the polite **です／ます** style. No personal details are needed.

## Step 1 · Notice a complete model

> あきさん、こんにちは。  
> わたしはパンが好きです。  
> 昼（ひる）ご飯（はん）にパンを食（た）べます。  
> 水（みず）を飲（の）みます。  
> 友（とも）だちと食（た）べます。

**English:** Hello, Aki. I like bread. I eat / will eat bread for lunch. I drink / will drink water. I eat / will eat with a friend.

This message is an original fictional example. Japanese present polite forms such as 食べます can describe a habitual action or a future plan; the context determines which. The model is a plan for lunch, not a claim about what someone already ate.

| Writing purpose | Japanese pattern | What it says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Say what you like | パン**が好きです**。 | I like bread. |
| Name an eating action | パン**を食べます**。 | I eat / will eat bread. |
| Name a drinking action | 水**を飲みます**。 | I drink / will drink water. |
| Name a companion | 友だち**と**食べます。 | I eat / will eat with a friend. |

**Important contrast:** **パンが好きです** does not mean you ordered or ate bread. **パンを食べます** describes eating. With **好き**, use **が** for the thing liked in this beginner pattern; with **食べます／飲みます**, use **を** for the food or drink being consumed.

## Step 2 · Build your own sentences

Choose from these words; you may use other familiar food words too.

| Food | Reading | Drink | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| パン (bread) | パン | 水 (water) | みず |
| ご飯 (cooked rice / meal) | ごはん | お茶 (tea) | おちゃ |
| りんご (apple) | りんご | コーヒー (coffee) | コーヒー |
| サンドイッチ (sandwich) | サンドイッチ | ジュース (juice) | ジュース |

Use the sentence frames below as **scaffolding**, not a compulsory exact answer:

1. わたしは＿＿が好きです。 (I like __.)
2. 昼ご飯に＿＿を食べます。 (For lunch I eat / will eat __.)
3. ＿＿を飲みます。 (I drink / will drink __.)
4. 友だちと食べます。 (I eat / will eat with a friend.)

**Guided transformation:** Start with **水を飲みます。** Replace 水 with お茶 → **お茶を飲みます。** The particle **を** stays. Now change the meaning from an action to a preference → **お茶が好きです。** The particle changes because the sentence function changes.

## Step 3 · Repair common mistakes

| Draft | Better sentence | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| パンを好きです。 | パンが好きです。 | The liked item takes が in this pattern. |
| 水が飲みます。 | 水を飲みます。 | The drink is the object of 飲みます. |
| 友だちを食べます。 | 友だちと食べます。 | と marks the companion; を would make the friend the object eaten. |
| パンを食ます。 | パンを食べます。 | Keep the kana ending べます. |

**Writing note:** Type Japanese with an IME or paste kana/kanji you know. You can use **ひるごはん**, **みず**, **たべます** and **のみます** when you cannot convert to kanji. This lesson evaluates meaning and basic sentence construction, not handwriting stroke order.

## Step 4 · Write, compare, revise

Write **2–5 connected sentences** as a café message to Aki. Include **a food preference**, **an eating or drinking action**, and **a companion using と**. The interactive writing workspace below keeps your draft in this browser when storage is available. Reveal the sample **after** you have tried your own version; it is one possible answer, not the only acceptable answer.

### Self-review rubric (no automatic writing grade)

Check each criterion yourself after comparing with the sample:

- **Meaning:** I name a food or drink I like and a food/drink action.
- **Particles:** I use が with 好き and を with 食べます／飲みます.
- **Connection:** I use と for a companion, and the sentences make sense together.
- **Form:** I use polite です／ます, with correct kana endings and readable punctuation.

A complete self-review is not proof of independent Japanese proficiency. Rewrite one sentence that did not meet your criteria.

One supported version is **お茶が好きです。水を飲みます。友だちと食べます。** If you choose to read it aloud, treat that as optional rehearsal, not scored pronunciation.
