## Mission: help plan a Saturday activity club

You are helping a teacher read a fictional activity board and explain the planned actions to a classmate. First review the verb forms, then read the Japanese board without translating every word. Finally complete **16 written questions** and an ungraded writing task. The people and schedule are invented for this checkpoint.

### 1. The two useful nonpast styles

The **dictionary form** is the headword form used in dictionaries and many plain-style contexts. The **ます form** is polite. Neither form by itself means past tense. With a Saturday plan, both can refer to the future.

| Dictionary form | Reading | Polite nonpast | Meaning | Group |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 読む | よむ | 読みます（よみます） | read | 1 / godan |
| 書く | かく | 書きます（かきます） | write | 1 / godan |
| 飲む | のむ | 飲みます（のみます） | drink | 1 / godan |
| 帰る | かえる | 帰ります（かえります） | return | 1 / godan |
| 切る | きる | 切ります（きります） | cut | 1 / godan |
| 食べる | たべる | 食べます（たべます） | eat | 2 / ichidan |
| 見る | みる | 見ます（みます） | see / watch | 2 / ichidan |
| する | する | します | do | 3 / irregular |
| 来る | くる | 来ます（きます） | come | 3 / irregular |

**Group 1:** the final u-row kana changes to its corresponding i-row kana before ます: 読**む** → 読**み**ます, 書**く** → 書**き**ます. **Group 2:** remove る and add ます: 食べる → 食べます. **Group 3:** memorize する → します and 来る（くる）→ 来ます（きます）.

**Common trap:** not every る verb is Group 2. 帰る and 切る are Group 1, while 見る and 食べる are Group 2. Also, **見ます** is nonpast; **見ました** is past. A polite form does not automatically mean a future action.

### 2. Original reading: Saturday activity board

Read the Japanese column first. A dictionary form on a notice can label an activity without making a complete polite sentence.

| Time | Activity label | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 午前九時（ごぜんくじ） | 本（ほん）を読む（よむ） | Read a book |
| 午前十時（ごぜんじゅうじ） | 日本語（にほんご）を書く（かく） | Write Japanese |
| 午前十一時（ごぜんじゅういちじ） | お茶（ちゃ）を飲む（のむ） | Drink tea |
| 午後一時（ごごいちじ） | 映画（えいが）を見る（みる） | Watch a film |

**Teacher:** 土曜日（どようび）、何（なに）をしますか。 — What will you do on Saturday?

**Yui:** 午前九時に本を読みます。 — I'll read a book at 9 a.m.

**Aki:** 午前十時に日本語を書きます。それから、お茶を飲みます。 — I'll write Japanese at 10 a.m. Then I'll drink tea.

**Teacher:** 映画も見ますか。 — Will you watch a film too?

**Aki:** はい、午後一時に見ます。 — Yes, I'll watch it at 1 p.m.

Find **本を読む** on the board and **本を読みます** in the conversation. The object marker を and the action remain the same; only the form changes. **午前** means a.m. and **午後** means p.m.

### 3. Reading for evidence, not guessing

Before the quiz, point to the exact line for each claim: reading is at nine, writing is at ten, drinking tea is at eleven, and watching a film is at one in the afternoon. If you confuse 読む（よむ） with 飲む（のむ）, compare the kanji and their objects: **本を読む** versus **お茶を飲む**.

A useful sound-alike contrast is **切る（きる）** “cut” versus **着る（きる）** “wear”; they are different verbs, and **切ります** and **着ます** have different group patterns. **来る（くる）** “come” is another word entirely.

### 4. Guided transformation

Cover the polite-form column above. Say or write the polite forms for **読む・書く・食べる・帰る・来る**. Then check: **読みます・書きます・食べます・帰ります・来ます（きます）**. Reverse the exercise: start from the polite form and reconstruct the dictionary headword. Be especially careful with 来ます.

### 6. Independent writing and optional oral rehearsal

Write **two to five connected Japanese sentences** describing a fictional Saturday or Sunday. Use at least three different verbs from the table, one time expression, and consistent polite forms.

**Model:** 土曜日（どようび）に本（ほん）を読みます。午前十時（ごぜんじゅうじ）に日本語（にほんご）を書（か）きます。午後一時（ごごいちじ）に映画（えいが）を見（み）ます。

*English:* On Saturday I will read a book. At 10 a.m. I will write Japanese. At 1 p.m. I will watch a film.

Self-check: (1) two to five connected sentences; (2) a clear time; (3) at least three appropriate verbs; (4) correct ます forms; (5) natural particles such as を and に. Compare with the model but do not assume that a different correct answer is wrong. This writing is **not automatically graded**. Optionally read your sentences aloud; no microphone recording or pronunciation grade is implied.
