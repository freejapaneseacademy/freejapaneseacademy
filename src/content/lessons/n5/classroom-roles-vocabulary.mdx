## Welcome to the language-school reception

**Can-do:** Ask and answer who, what, which and where questions. You are visiting a fictional language school and want to find your teacher, identify another student and locate the classroom. This L02 word-study lesson prepares vocabulary for the fuller question grammar in L03.

At the desk, you see this short exchange:

> A: あの人はだれですか。  
> B: たなかせんせいです。  
> A: きょうしつはどこですか。  
> B: ２かいです。

**Meaning:** “Who is that person over there?” “That's Teacher Tanaka.” “Where is the classroom?” “It's on the second floor.” The reply **２かいです** names a floor, not a person's identity. The scene and names are invented for practice.

## Vocabulary: people and places

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning / use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 人（ひと） | ひと | person; あの人 = that person over there |
| せんせい（先生） | せんせい | teacher; respectful role/title |
| がくせい（学生） | がくせい | student |
| クラスメート | クラスメート | classmate; a person in your class |
| ともだち（友だち） | ともだち | friend; not automatically every classmate |
| うけつけ（受付） | うけつけ | reception / reception desk |
| きょうしつ（教室） | きょうしつ | classroom |
| じむしつ（事務室） | じむしつ | office / administrative room |
| なまえ（名前） | なまえ | name |
| ２かい（二階） | にかい | second floor |

Kanji are **recognition support** here, not a handwriting test. 先生 is read せんせい, and 教室 is read きょうしつ. The long vowel in きょう is written with small ょ and う. クラスメート is a katakana loanword; the ー lengthens the preceding vowel.

## Useful question words

| Question word | Meaning | Typical answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| だれ | who | a person: たなかさん |
| なん／何 | what | a thing or category: ほん |
| どこ | where | a location: きょうしつ / ２かい |
| どれ | which one | one item selected from several |

**だれ** asks for a person, while **どこ** asks for a location. **どれ** stands on its own as “which one?”; when modifying a noun, **どの + noun** is used instead. These are first-use patterns, not a complete explanation of every particle.

**Compare:**

- **あの人はだれですか。** — Who is that person over there?
- **せんせいはどこですか。** — Where is the teacher?
- **これはなんですか。** — What is this?
- **どれですか。** — Which one is it?

**Common confusion:** A teacher is a person, but asking *where the teacher is* still takes **どこ**. Choose the question word based on the missing information, not just the noun.

## Roles, titles and the word も

**たなかさんはせんせいです。** — Tanaka is a teacher. **エマさんはがくせいです。** — Emma is a student. **わたしもがくせいです。** — I am a student too.

In the final sentence, **も** means “also/too.” It takes the place of the topic particle は in this simple pattern: **わたしも** rather than **わたしはも**. You will analyze this more fully in L03. Use **せんせい** for an actual teacher or a suitable professional title, not for every adult or classmate.

## Three-stage practice

**1. Recognition:** Cover the English column. Read せんせい, がくせい, クラスメート, うけつけ and きょうしつ, then say whether each is a person or place. Check the table.

**2. Guided retrieval:** A visitor asks for a person at reception. Choose **だれ** for identity and **どこ** for location. Complete aloud or on paper: 「あの人は___ですか。」 and 「きょうしつは___ですか。」 The answer to both blanks is not the same.

**3. Independent application:** Invent two fictional classmates and one teacher. Write three short labels using words from the table. Then write a question about someone's identity and a separate question about a location. Check that the question word matches the information sought. Do not share real classmates' personal details.

## Reading, writing and listening transfer

Read the original reception dialogue once without English. Write **がくせい**, **きょうしつ** and **クラスメート** from memory; compare small kana and the katakana long-vowel mark. For listening preparation, practice tracking whether a future spoken question requests a person or a place; **no recording or listening accuracy is scored in this written lesson**. Optionally rehearse the model lines aloud without recording yourself.
