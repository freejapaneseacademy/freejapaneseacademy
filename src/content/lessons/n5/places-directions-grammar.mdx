## Finding your way around a community center

You have just arrived at a community center. A volunteer is at the entrance; you need the library, restroom and cafeteria. By the end, you can ask where each facility is and understand a short reply.

### 1. Three places, three viewpoints

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning in a physical-pointing conversation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">ここ</span> | koko | here, near the speaker |
| <span lang="ja">そこ</span> | soko | there, near the listener |
| <span lang="ja">あそこ</span> | asoko | over there, away from both |
| <span lang="ja">どこ</span> | doko | where (question word) |

The volunteer stands at reception and says <span lang="ja">うけつけは ここです。</span> — “Reception is here.” You stand near the restroom; the volunteer tells you <span lang="ja">トイレは そこです。</span> — “The restroom is there, near you.” Both point across the courtyard: <span lang="ja">しょくどうは あそこです。</span> — “The cafeteria is over there.”

These words locate **places**. Compare the previous chapter's <span lang="ja">これ・それ・あれ</span>, which identify **things**. Do not say <span lang="ja">これは としょかんです</span> merely to mean “The library is here”; use <span lang="ja">としょかんは ここです。</span>

### 2. Ask: どこですか

The frame is <span lang="ja">[place] は どこですか。</span> — “Where is [place]?”

- <span lang="ja">としょかんは どこですか。</span> — Where is the library?
- <span lang="ja">トイレは どこですか。</span> — Where is the restroom?
- <span lang="ja">しょくどうは どこですか。</span> — Where is the cafeteria?

Answer <span lang="ja">としょかんは あそこです。</span> — “The library is over there.” The subject can be omitted when obvious: <span lang="ja">あそこです。</span> — “Over there.”

**Do not confuse** <span lang="ja">どこ</span> (“where”) with <span lang="ja">だれ</span> (“who”). A room is not a person.

### 3. Location with に and あります

A different, useful sentence pattern is <span lang="ja">[thing/place] は [location] に あります。</span> — “[Thing/place] is located at [location].”

- <span lang="ja">としょかんは にかいに あります。</span> — The library is on the second floor.
- <span lang="ja">トイレは いっかいに あります。</span> — The restroom is on the first floor.
- <span lang="ja">じしょは つくえの うえに あります。</span> — The dictionary is on the desk.

<span lang="ja">に</span> marks the location where something exists. The existence verb <span lang="ja">あります</span> is appropriate for inanimate things and facilities; people are normally located with <span lang="ja">います</span>, taught systematically in the next chapter. This is **not** a rule that every English “at/in” must become に: actions often take other particles.

### 4. A short original exchange

The volunteer is at reception. The visitor is standing beside the restroom. The library is on floor two, and the cafeteria is across the courtyard.

**Visitor:** <span lang="ja">すみません。としょかんは どこですか。</span> — Excuse me. Where is the library?

**Volunteer:** <span lang="ja">としょかんは にかいに あります。</span> — The library is on the second floor.

**Visitor:** <span lang="ja">トイレは どこですか。</span> — Where is the restroom?

**Volunteer:** <span lang="ja">そこです。</span> — There, near you.

**Visitor:** <span lang="ja">しょくどうも にかいですか。</span> — Is the cafeteria also on the second floor?

**Volunteer:** <span lang="ja">いいえ。しょくどうは あそこです。</span> — No. The cafeteria is over there.

**Reading evidence:** The phrase <span lang="ja">にかいに あります</span> supports “second floor”; <span lang="ja">そこです</span> points to the restroom near the visitor. Do not infer that the cafeteria is on floor two.

### 5. Guided practice and your own directions

**Controlled:** Complete <span lang="ja">トイレは ___ です。</span> when the restroom is near your listener. Answer: <span lang="ja">そこ</span>. To ask the question instead, say <span lang="ja">トイレは どこですか。</span>

**Guided:** Imagine a two-floor center: reception and restroom on floor one; library on floor two; cafeteria across the courtyard. Ask a partner where each place is. Model: <span lang="ja">としょかんは どこですか。にかいに あります。</span>

**Independent writing:** Create a fictional three-place building guide in **three connected Japanese sentences**, including one <span lang="ja">どこですか</span> question and at least one answer with <span lang="ja">に あります</span>. Example: <span lang="ja">トイレは どこですか。トイレは いっかいに あります。としょかんは にかいです。</span> Self-check: Is the location word appropriate to the viewpoint? Does に immediately follow the location in the existence pattern? Is the question marked with か? This is self-review, not a graded composition.

**Optional speaking:** Read the original dialogue aloud, swap visitor and volunteer roles, and repeat with your own fictional building. No microphone or pronunciation grade is required.
