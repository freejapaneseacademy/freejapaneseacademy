## Your mission: a mini review in Japanese

You have visited a **fictional café** and looked at its small gift corner. Write **2–5 short, connected Japanese sentences** describing its food, atmosphere and an object. Give at least one positive description and one negative description. This is your own writing: the practice tool will **not** pretend to grade free Japanese composition.

**Can-do:** Describe food, places and objects with adjectives. Read the model, build a sentence, compose independently, then revise using a transparent self-review rubric.

## 1. Your fictional scene

The café is quiet. The cake is delicious but small. The gift corner has a new blue bag; it is expensive. These are invented details for practice, not a real business review.

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">ケーキ</span> | ケーキ | cake |
| <span lang="ja">カフェ</span> | カフェ | café |
| <span lang="ja">かばん</span> | かばん | bag |
| <span lang="ja">おいしい</span> | おいしい | delicious (い-adjective) |
| <span lang="ja">小さい</span> | ちいさい | small (い-adjective) |
| <span lang="ja">新しい</span> | あたらしい | new (い-adjective) |
| <span lang="ja">高い</span> | たかい | expensive / high (い-adjective) |
| <span lang="ja">しずか</span> | しずか | quiet (な-adjective) |
| <span lang="ja">きれい</span> | きれい | clean / beautiful (な-adjective) |

**Important:** The final sound い does **not** always mean an い-adjective. <span lang="ja">きれい</span> is a **な-adjective**. A café can be <span lang="ja">しずか</span> (quiet) or <span lang="ja">にぎやか</span> (lively), depending on the scene.

## 2. Two ways to use an adjective

**Before a noun:** <span lang="ja">おいしいケーキ</span> — delicious cake. An い-adjective attaches directly. <span lang="ja">しずかなカフェ</span> — quiet café. A な-adjective uses **な** before a noun.

**At the end of a polite sentence:**

- <span lang="ja">このケーキはおいしいです。</span> — This cake is delicious.
- <span lang="ja">このカフェはしずかです。</span> — This café is quiet.

The topic particle **は** is written は but pronounced わ. In <span lang="ja">しずかです</span>, do **not** add な before です. The pattern <span lang="ja">しずかなカフェ</span> needs な because a noun follows.

## 3. Say what is *not* true

For an **い-adjective**, remove the final い and add **くないです**:

- <span lang="ja">小さい → 小さくないです</span> (ちいさい → ちいさくないです) — not small.
- <span lang="ja">高い → 高くないです</span> (たかい → たかくないです) — not expensive.

For a **な-adjective**, add **じゃないです** after the stem:

- <span lang="ja">しずか → しずかじゃないです</span> — not quiet.
- <span lang="ja">きれい → きれいじゃないです</span> — not clean / beautiful.

A more formal alternative is <span lang="ja">ではありません</span>. At this level, practise one consistent polite style. Do not write <span lang="ja">おいしいじゃないです</span> to mean “not delicious”; that is not the taught plain negative of this い-adjective. Also, <span lang="ja">暑い（あつい）</span> describes hot weather, while <span lang="ja">熱い（あつい）</span> describes hot soup or another hot object.

## 4. Observe an original four-sentence model

> <span lang="ja">このカフェはしずかです。</span> — This café is quiet.
>
> <span lang="ja">ケーキはおいしいです。</span> — The cake is delicious.
>
> <span lang="ja">でも、ケーキは大きくないです。</span> — But the cake is not big.
>
> <span lang="ja">あの新しいかばんは高いです。</span> — That new bag over there is expensive.

**Reading notes:** <span lang="ja">大きい（おおきい）</span> means big; <span lang="ja">大きくない</span> means not big. <span lang="ja">でも</span> links a contrasting thought. <span lang="ja">あの</span> points to an object away from both speaker and listener. This model describes a place, food and an object, with a polite negative.

## 5. Build a sentence before drafting

Recombine the pieces. Try to say the full Japanese sentence before looking back:

1. Café + quiet → <span lang="ja">このカフェは＿＿です。</span> (Answer: <span lang="ja">しずか</span>)
2. Delicious + cake → <span lang="ja">＿＿ケーキです。</span> (Answer: <span lang="ja">おいしい</span>)
3. Cake + not small → <span lang="ja">このケーキは＿＿です。</span> (Answer: <span lang="ja">小さくない</span>)
4. Beautiful + shop → <span lang="ja">＿＿店です。</span> (Answer: <span lang="ja">きれいな</span>; 店 = みせ, shop)

Check the difference between a **noun modifier** and a **sentence-ending predicate**. The quiz below checks controlled sentence choices, not your original composition.

## 6. Write, compare, revise

Write **2–5 connected Japanese sentences** about the fictional café and gift corner. Include **one description of food**, **one description of a place or object**, **both adjective classes**, and **at least one negative**. You may change the fictional details, but keep your statements mutually consistent. A useful plan is: place → food → contrast → object.

After your first draft, reveal the model and rubric in the interactive practice. Check whether you used (1) connected 2–5 sentences, (2) accurate い-adjective forms, (3) accurate な-adjective forms, (4) an understandable polite negative, and (5) clear food/place/object descriptions. Revise one sentence. These are **self-checks**, not a teacher grade. Your writing stays in the browser page and is not sent to a server.

**Optional speaking:** Read your revised sentences aloud at a comfortable pace, pausing after each sentence. No microphone, automatic pronunciation score or unverified audio is needed. Listening to comparisons belongs to L06 and uses actual audio only when licensed and verified.

## 7. Takeaway

An **い-adjective** modifies a noun directly and becomes **〜くないです** in the negative. A **な-adjective** takes **な** before a noun, but not before sentence-ending **です**; its negative is **〜じゃないです**. Use both patterns to make a short, meaningful review of food, places and objects.
