## At the welcome table

**Can-do:** Introduce yourself and identify another person's basic details. In this writing lesson you will create a **fictional** first-meeting introduction. Use invented names and occupations; do not enter your real name or personal information.

Imagine a student meeting a classmate at a language-school orientation. Your goal is **two to five connected Japanese sentences**: a name, a country or occupation, and optionally a polite question or negative statement. The focus is clear communication, not fancy kanji or handwriting speed.

## 1. Observe a short model

> はじめまして。私はエマです。アメリカから来ました。学生です。よろしくお願いします。

**Meaning:** Nice to meet you. I'm Emma. I came from the United States. I'm a student. Nice to meet you / I look forward to knowing you.

The expression **アメリカから来ました** is an optional model phrase; you are not required to produce its verb form in this chapter. You can meet the objective using the familiar noun pattern **私はエマです。私は学生です。** The topic **は** is written *ha* but pronounced *wa* when used as a particle. Japanese often omits an understood topic, as in **学生です**.

A second model with a question:

> 私はケンです。日本人です。あなたは学生ですか。

**Meaning:** I'm Ken. I'm Japanese. Are you a student? **日本人（にほんじん）** describes nationality, whereas **日本（にほん）** names the country. In a real conversation, repeating **あなた** may feel unnecessary; when the other person's name is known, **エマさんは学生ですか** is a more specific question.

## 2. Build from a word bank

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 私 | わたし | I |
| 名前 | なまえ | name |
| 日本 | にほん | Japan |
| アメリカ | アメリカ | United States |
| 学生 | がくせい | student |
| 先生 | せんせい | teacher |
| 会社員 | かいしゃいん | company employee |
| です | です | polite noun-predicate ending |
| じゃありません | じゃありません | polite noun-predicate negative |
| か | か | question ending |

**Controlled recombination:** Fill each blank mentally or on paper, then compare with the answer below.

1. 私＿エマです。 (I am Emma.)
2. 私は学生＿＿。 (I am a student.)
3. 田中さんは先生です＿。 (Is Tanaka a teacher?)
4. 私は先生＿＿＿＿＿＿。 (I am not a teacher.)

**Answers:** 1 **は**, 2 **です**, 3 **か**, 4 **じゃありません**. The negative pattern is **先生じゃありません**, not **先生ですじゃありません**. In more formal contexts, **ではありません** is also a valid polite negative; today's model uses **じゃありません**.

## 3. Guided composition

Use the fictional profile below. You may choose a different fictional name.

> 名前：ミナ  
> 国：日本  
> 仕事：学生

A supported two-sentence version is **私はミナです。学生です。** A three-sentence version can be **私はミナです。日本人です。学生です。** The country field says **日本** but does **not** establish nationality. Do not infer **日本人** from it. A safe version is **私はミナです。学生です。** You can add a question such as **田中さんは先生ですか。**

**Meaningful revision:** Compare **私は学生ですか。** (“Am I a student?”) with **私は学生です。** (“I am a student.”). One final **か** changes the speech act. Similarly, **私は先生じゃありません。** means “I am not a teacher,” not “I am a teacher.”

## 4. Independent writing and learner self-review

Write **two to five connected sentences** as a fictional introduction in the editor below. Include a name statement and at least one country, nationality or occupation statement. Add a polite question or a negative statement if you can. You may type entirely in kana and katakana if a kanji is unfamiliar, but check that the words remain understandable.

Use the model after writing, not before, to notice differences. This is **ungraded free composition**: no automatic system can reliably judge every natural alternative. The four checks in the editor are **your own self-review**, not a teacher's mark.

**Rubric:** (1) Does the text communicate a fictional name and another basic detail? (2) Are topic **は** and polite endings **です / じゃありません** used appropriately? (3) Is **か** present only when you intend a question? (4) Are your two to five sentences readable and logically connected? Each check is a yes/not-yet reflection, not a numerical proficiency score.

## 5. Recognition check and next steps

Complete the eight written questions after drafting. These check constrained forms, not the quality of your original writing. Retry errors and revise your saved text. For an optional speaking extension, read your own introduction slowly; no microphone or recording is required. The dedicated L08 speaking lesson will use approved model audio.

**Review tomorrow:** Without looking at the model, write **私は___です。** and **___さんは学生ですか。** with fictional names. Explain aloud why **は** is the topic marker and **か** marks a question. No third-party text, images or audio are needed for this written lesson.
