## At the school reception desk

A student has left a bag in the classroom. A receptionist puts several objects on a table. Your task is to **name and identify** the objects before asking whose they are. These examples are original written teaching material; they are not recordings or pronunciation assessments.

## 1. Eight words to recognize

| Japanese | Reading cue | Meaning | Where you might see it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">かさ</span> | *kasa* | umbrella | A forgotten umbrella at reception. |
| <span lang="ja">ほん</span> | *hon* | book | A book on the classroom desk. |
| <span lang="ja">ノート</span> | *nōto* | notebook | A notebook used for class notes. |
| <span lang="ja">ペン</span> | *pen* | pen | A pen for writing. |
| <span lang="ja">えんぴつ</span> | *enpitsu* | pencil | A pencil for writing or drawing. |
| <span lang="ja">けしゴム</span> | *keshigomu* | eraser | An eraser for pencil marks. |
| <span lang="ja">かばん</span> | *kaban* | bag | A bag holding school supplies. |
| <span lang="ja">じしょ</span> | *jisho* | dictionary | A dictionary for looking up words. |

**Script note:** ノート and ペン are written in katakana because they are loanwords. けしゴム combines hiragana and katakana. A word's script is not a reliable guide to who owns the object. The long vowel mark **ー** in ノート extends the preceding vowel; written cues are not a substitute for listening.

## 2. Words inside useful sentences

| Japanese | Natural English |
| --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">これは ほんです。</span> | This is a book. |
| <span lang="ja">それは ノートですか。</span> | Is that a notebook? |
| <span lang="ja">この ペンは ミナさんのです。</span> | This pen is Mina's. |
| <span lang="ja">あの かばんは だれのですか。</span> | Whose bag is that over there? |
| <span lang="ja">この けしゴムは わたしのです。</span> | This eraser is mine. |

**これ** means “this one” and stands alone; **この** must come before a noun, as in **この ペン**. The particle **の** can identify an owner in this setting. It is not restricted to legal ownership in every Japanese sentence.

## 3. Two words that are easy to confuse

**ノート** means a notebook, not an English “note” or message. **ほん** means a book. A student can write notes *in* a ノート while reading a ほん.

**ペン** means a pen; **えんぴつ** means a pencil. If someone asks for a **けしゴム**, they need an eraser, not a pencil. In Japan, a school pencil eraser is commonly called けしゴム.

## 4. Read a short original exchange

**Aki (receptionist):** <span lang="ja">これは ミナさんの ノートですか。</span> — Is this Mina's notebook?

**Ren (student):** <span lang="ja">いいえ。ミナさんの ノートじゃありません。</span> — No. It isn't Mina's notebook.

**Aki:** <span lang="ja">この かばんは だれのですか。</span> — Whose bag is this?

**Ren:** <span lang="ja">わたしのです。ありがとうございます。</span> — It's mine. Thank you.

Notice the difference between the **object** (ノート or かばん) and the **owner** (ミナさん or わたし). Ren rejects an ownership claim about the notebook, then claims the bag. Do not infer that the notebook belongs to Ren.

## 5. Controlled → guided → independent practice

**Controlled:** Cover the English meanings in the word table. Point to the Japanese word for “umbrella,” “eraser,” and “dictionary.” Reveal the table to check: かさ, けしゴム, じしょ.

**Guided:** At reception, you see a pencil and a notebook. Complete the phrases: <span lang="ja">この ___ は わたしのです。</span> (This pencil is mine.) → **えんぴつ**. <span lang="ja">それは ___ ですか。</span> (Is that a notebook?) → **ノート**.

**Independent:** Imagine two objects you brought to class. Write two Japanese sentences using **この + object + は + owner + のです** or **これは + object + です**. For example, <span lang="ja">この ペンは わたしのです。</span> (“This pen is mine.”) Check that **この** has a noun after it and that the object name matches what you intend.

## 6. Practice and review

Use the eight interactive questions below to retrieve the meanings without looking at the table. Read each explanation after answering. Retry missed words after a short break. This is written vocabulary recognition; it does not verify your pronunciation or listening ability.
