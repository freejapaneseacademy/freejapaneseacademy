## Where should we meet?

**Can-do:** Make basic comparisons and select one option. Imagine choosing a place for a study-group outing. These three places are fictional. Read the Japanese descriptions first; use the translations only to check your understanding.

### Key words

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 駅 | えき | station |
| 歩いて | あるいて | on foot |
| 静か | しずか | quiet |
| にぎやか | にぎやか | lively |
| 広い | ひろい | spacious |
| 席 | せき | seat |
| 入場料 | にゅうじょうりょう | admission fee |
| 無料 | むりょう | free of charge |
| 近い | ちかい | near |
| 多い | おおい | numerous; many |

### Original Japanese comparison chart

| Card | Place | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | みどり図書館 | <span lang="ja">駅から歩いて五分です。静かです。席は二十あります。</span> |
| **B** | あおカフェ | <span lang="ja">駅から歩いて二分です。にぎやかです。席は十あります。</span> |
| **C** | さくら公園 | <span lang="ja">駅から歩いて十分です。広いです。入場料は無料です。</span> |

**English support:** A: The library is a five-minute walk from the station. It is quiet and has twenty seats. B: The café is a two-minute walk from the station. It is lively and has ten seats. C: The park is a ten-minute walk from the station. It is spacious and admission is free.

### Reading the evidence

**駅から歩いて五分** means five minutes on foot *from* the station. **から** identifies the starting point. The numbers are **二分** (にふん, two minutes), **五分** (ごふん, five minutes), and **十分** (じゅっぷん, ten minutes). The café is the shortest walk among these three.

**より** marks the comparison baseline: <span lang="ja">あおカフェはみどり図書館より駅に近いです。</span> The café is closer to the station than the library. Another pattern highlights the preferred side: <span lang="ja">みどり図書館よりあおカフェのほうが駅に近いです。</span> The café is closer than the library.

For a group of three: <span lang="ja">三つの中で、あおカフェが一番近いです。</span> Of the three, the café is closest. **一番** here does not mean the closest venue in the entire city.

### Worked example

Question: Which place is quieter, the café or library? The chart says **A — 静かです** and **B — にぎやかです**. Therefore the library is the quieter option: <span lang="ja">あおカフェよりみどり図書館のほうが静かです。</span> Do not assume that the park is quiet: its description only says it is spacious.

Question: Which place has more seats? **A — 席は二十あります** versus **B — 席は十あります**. The library has more seats. The park's seat count is not stated.

### Guided reading

Find the exact evidence for these three facts: (1) the café's walk is two minutes, (2) the library has twenty seats, (3) the park has free admission. Answers: **B — 歩いて二分**, **A — 席は二十あります**, **C — 入場料は無料です**.

### Common mistakes

**無料** applies to admission, not to every product or service at the park. **広い** does not establish how many seats exist. **静か** and **にぎやか** describe different atmospheres; choosing one depends on your purpose. **どちら** asks which of two options, while **どれ** is suitable for choosing among three.

### Writing and optional speaking

Write two to five Japanese sentences choosing between two of the places. Compare a factual feature and explain your preference. Example: <span lang="ja">カフェは駅から近いです。でも、図書館のほうが静かです。わたしは図書館がいいです。静かなところで勉強したいからです。</span> The café is close to the station, but the library is quieter. I prefer the library because I want to study somewhere quiet.

Self-check without a numerical grade: Did you compare the correct places? Is your evidence actually stated? Did you use **より** and **のほうが** appropriately? Optionally read your sentences aloud with a partner; no recording or pronunciation assessment is performed.
