## Your writing mission: a community-center activity update

You are helping a fictional community center prepare a short update about what people **are doing now**. Write **two to five connected Japanese sentences**. Say who is doing what, and where at least one activity is taking place. These are invented people and events, not real photographs or live observations.

### Model A: a busy afternoon

> 今（いま）、公民館（こうみんかん）でみんなが活動（かつどう）しています。  
> 田中（たなか）さんは図書室（としょしつ）で本（ほん）を読（よ）んでいます。  
> 佐藤（さとう）さんは教室（きょうしつ）で日本語（にほんご）を勉強（べんきょう）しています。  
> 山田（やまだ）さんは台所（だいどころ）で昼（ひる）ご飯（はん）を作（つく）っています。

**English:** Right now, everyone is taking part in activities at the community center. Tanaka is reading a book in the library room. Sato is studying Japanese in the classroom. Yamada is making lunch in the kitchen.

Each sentence describes an action in progress. **で** identifies the place where the action happens; **を** marks its object. A person doing an action is commonly introduced with **は**.

### Model B: ask and answer

> A：今（いま）、何（なに）をしていますか。  
> B：教室（きょうしつ）で絵（え）を描（か）いています。  
> A：友達（ともだち）は何をしていますか。  
> B：友達は庭（にわ）で写真（しゃしん）を撮（と）っています。

**English:** A: What are you doing now? B: I am drawing a picture in the classroom. A: What is your friend doing? B: My friend is taking photos in the garden.

**何をしていますか** asks what activity someone is doing. The answer can omit **私（わたし）は** when the subject is obvious. **今** makes the current-time context explicit.

## Build the progressive carefully

| Dictionary form | て-form | Ongoing activity | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 読む（よむ） | 読んで | 読んでいます | is reading |
| 書く（かく） | 書いて | 書いています | is writing |
| 撮る（とる） | 撮って | 撮っています | is taking (photos) |
| 食べる（たべる） | 食べて | 食べています | is eating |
| 勉強する（べんきょうする） | 勉強して | 勉強しています | is studying |

Attach **います** to the **て-form**, not the dictionary form: **読んでいます**, not **読むています**. The polite question **何をしていますか** uses **か**; the statement **本を読んでいます** does not.

**Important nuance:** 〜ています does not always mean an action is physically unfolding at this instant. Some verbs can describe a continuing state or habit (for example, 住んでいます, “lives”). In *this* activity-report task, the context and 今 signal ongoing actions. Do not automatically translate every 〜ています sentence as “right now.”

## Guided writing ladder

**1. Name the actor.** Start with **田中さんは**. For yourself, use **私は** if needed.

**2. Add a location.** **図書室で** marks the place of reading. **図書室を** would not correctly mark the action location here.

**3. Add the object and ongoing verb.** **本を読む** becomes **本を読んでいます**. Combine: **田中さんは図書室で本を読んでいます。**

**4. Connect another observation.** **それから** (“and then”) suggests a time sequence; for simultaneous activities, **また** (“also”) or a new sentence is often more natural. For example: **また、佐藤さんは教室で勉強しています。**

**5. Repair common errors.** Change **本が読んでいます** to **本を読んでいます** when the person is reading a book. Change **教室に勉強しています** to **教室で勉強しています** to indicate the place of studying. Change **写真を撮りています** to **写真を撮っています**.

## Independent composition

Imagine a school club, library room, park or community center. Write **2–5 connected Japanese sentences** about what at least two people are doing now. Include **今**, at least one **location + で**, and at least two correct **〜ています** verbs. You may ask **何をしていますか** as part of a short exchange, but your final text must still describe activities. Avoid real names or identifying details.

**Another possible model:**

> 今、公園（こうえん）で友達（ともだち）が遊（あそ）んでいます。  
> ミカさんは写真を撮っています。  
> ケンさんはベンチで本を読んでいます。  
> 私は二人（ふたり）を見（み）ています。

**English:** Now friends are playing in the park. Mika is taking pictures. Ken is reading a book on a bench. I am watching the two of them.

## Five-point self-review

1. **Meaning:** Are your sentences about activities happening in the chosen scene?
2. **Verb forms:** Did you use て-form + います correctly (読んでいます, 撮っています)?
3. **Particles:** Does **で** mark the action location, and **を** the object where appropriate?
4. **Clarity:** Can the reader tell who is doing each action? Are names and subjects consistent?
5. **Connected output:** Did you write two to five related, punctuated Japanese sentences?
