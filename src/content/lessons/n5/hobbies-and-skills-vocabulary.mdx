## A club introduction

Imagine you are at a fictional community-center club fair. You want to find people who enjoy the same activities as you. Your goal is not to claim you are good at everything you like: **liking something** and **being skilled at it** are different ideas.

Read these two original introductions. Names and events are fictional.

> アキさん：私は音楽（おんがく）が好きです。特（とく）に歌（うた）が好きです。でも、歌（うた）はあまり上手（じょうず）じゃありません。ピアノが少（すこ）しできます。

**English:** Aki: “I like music. I especially like songs/singing. But I'm not very good at singing. I can play a little piano.”

> レンさん：私はスポーツが好きです。水泳（すいえい）が得意（とくい）です。料理（りょうり）はあまり好きじゃありません。でも、料理（りょうり）を習（なら）っています。

**English:** Ren: “I like sports. I'm good at swimming. I don't particularly like cooking. But I'm learning to cook.”

**Notice:** Aki likes singing but does not claim to be skilled at it. Ren enjoys sports but does not particularly enjoy cooking. **得意** is an additional way to describe one's strength; this lesson focuses mainly on **上手** and **下手**, with the next grammar lesson explaining their nuances.

## Ten useful hobby and skill expressions

| Japanese (reading) | English | Natural combination |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 音楽（おんがく） | music | 音楽が好きです — I like music |
| 歌（うた） | song; singing in context | 歌が好きです — I like songs/singing |
| ピアノ | piano | ピアノが少しできます — I can play a little piano |
| 読書（どくしょ） | reading (as a hobby) | 読書が好きです — I like reading |
| 映画（えいが） | film / movie | 映画が好きです — I like films |
| 写真（しゃしん） | photograph; photography in context | 写真を撮（と）ります — I take photos |
| 料理（りょうり） | cooking; cuisine | 料理をします — I cook |
| 水泳（すいえい） | swimming | 水泳が好きです — I like swimming |
| スポーツ | sports | スポーツが好きです — I like sports |
| 絵（え） | picture; drawing in context | 絵を描（か）きます — I draw pictures |

**Word study:** 読書 is the noun “reading as a hobby”; 本（ほん）を読（よ）みます means “I read a book.” 写真 is a photo, while 写真を撮ります is the action “take photos.” Similarly, 料理 can name the activity or food, depending on context.

## Talk about preference, not ability

- **音楽が好きです。**（おんがくがすきです。） — I like music.
- **料理はあまり好きじゃありません。**（りょうりはあまりすきじゃありません。） — I don't particularly like cooking.
- **水泳が上手です。**（すいえいがじょうずです。） — [Someone] is good at swimming.
- **絵が下手です。**（えがへたです。） — [Someone] is not good at drawing.
- **ピアノが少しできます。**（ピアノがすこしできます。） — [Someone] can play a little piano.

**が** often marks the object of 好き and ability expressions; **は** can introduce a topic or contrast. **あまり** with a negative expression softens the preference (“not particularly”). It does not mean “very” in this sentence.

**Pragmatic note:** Saying **私は上手です** about your own skill can sound self-congratulatory. A more modest answer is **少しできます** (“I can do a little”), or **まだ上手じゃありません** (“I'm not good yet”). **下手** can sound blunt when judging another person's ability; use it cautiously.

## Guided retrieval: a hobby-card exchange

> ミオ：趣味（しゅみ）は何（なん）ですか。 — What's your hobby?
>
> ソラ：読書（どくしょ）です。映画（えいが）も好きです。 — Reading. I also like films.
>
> ミオ：スポーツは好きですか。 — Do you like sports?
>
> ソラ：はい、水泳（すいえい）が好きです。でも、あまり上手じゃありません。 — Yes, I like swimming. But I'm not very good at it.

**Try it:** Cover the English and find the Japanese evidence for Sora's hobby, second interest, and attitude toward swimming. Then swap **読書** for **写真** and **水泳** for **料理**. Notice that liking an activity does not tell you whether you can do it well.

## Common confusions

**好き** is a preference, not a skill rating. **上手** evaluates skill, not enjoyment. **できます** says that an activity is possible or within someone's ability; it does not automatically mean “expert.” For a concrete verb, Japanese can use **verb dictionary form + ことができます**, introduced later. Here **ピアノができます** is a familiar, broad ability expression.

The adjective **好き** behaves as a な-adjective in this pattern, not as the English verb “like”: say **音楽が好きです**, not **音楽を好きます**. The particle in **写真を撮ります** is different because 撮る is a verb taking an object.

## Independent practice

Write two to four sentences for a **fictional** club profile. Include (1) one hobby you like, (2) a second interest or a gentle dislike, and (3) an honest ability statement if you wish. You need not disclose personal details.

**Model:** 私は映画が好きです。読書も好きです。ピアノが少しできます。 — “I like films. I also like reading. I can play a little piano.”

**Self-check rubric (ungraded):** Did you use **が好きです** for liking, keep **あまり** with a negative expression, and distinguish liking from skill? Are your sentences polite and understandable? Rehearse the profile aloud only if you want; no recording or pronunciation score is required.
