## A café visit after class

**Chapter Can-do:** Name common foods and express simple preferences. In this opening context lesson, you will understand a short, fictional exchange at a café, identify who is speaking, and tell the difference between *liking* a food and *actually ordering* it. The next lessons will build your vocabulary and grammar.

Two classmates, **あき (Aki)** and **れん (Ren)**, stop at a small café after class. A staff member takes their orders. This is an original learning scenario, not a transcript of a real restaurant.

### Original dialogue

**店員（てんいん）:** いらっしゃいませ。  
**あき:** こんにちは。メニューをお願いします。  
**店員:** はい、どうぞ。  
**れん:** あきさん、パンが好きですか。  
**あき:** はい、パンが好きです。でも、今日はごはんを食べます。  
**れん:** そうですか。わたしはパンを食べます。  
**店員:** お飲み物（のみもの）は？  
**あき:** お茶（ちゃ）をお願いします。  
**れん:** コーヒーと水（みず）をお願いします。  
**店員:** かしこまりました。

**English meaning, turn by turn:** The server welcomes the customers. Aki asks for a menu, which the server hands over. Ren asks Aki whether she likes bread. Aki says yes, but today she will eat rice/a rice dish. Ren says he will eat bread. When the server asks about drinks, Aki requests tea; Ren requests coffee and water. The server acknowledges the order.

**Reading help:** 店員（てんいん） staff member; 今日（きょう） today; 食べます（たべます） eat / will eat; 飲み物（のみもの） drink or beverage; お茶（おちゃ） tea; 水（みず） water. The written conversation includes polite service expressions for recognition, not for memorization yet.

## Who says what?

| Speaker | Role | Japanese evidence | What we know |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 店員 | Server | いらっしゃいませ。 | Welcomes the customers |
| あき | Customer | メニューをお願いします。 | Requests a menu |
| れん | Customer and classmate | パンが好きですか。 | Asks about Aki's preference |
| あき | Customer | 今日はごはんを食べます。 | Plans to eat rice/a rice dish today |
| れん | Customer | パンを食べます。 | Plans to eat bread |
| あき | Customer | お茶をお願いします。 | Orders tea |
| れん | Customer | コーヒーと水をお願いします。 | Orders coffee and water |

**Evidence boundary:** We know Aki likes bread, but she chooses rice today. We do **not** know whether Ren likes coffee or whether the café sells desserts. A stated order is not automatically a lifelong preference.

## Notice three useful patterns

### 1. A preference: パンが好きです

**パンが好きです。** — “I like bread.” In this beginner pattern, **が** marks the thing liked. **好き（すき）** expresses liking; it is not the verb “eat.” In **パンが好きですか**, **か** makes it a polite question. You can understand the sentence without producing every variation yet.

### 2. An action: ごはんを食べます

**ごはんを食べます。** — “I eat / will eat rice (or a rice meal).” The particle **を** marks the object of the eating action. In this context, **今日は** (“today, as for today”) supports a choice for today's meal. **食べます** is a polite non-past form and can refer to a present habit or a future action depending on context.

Compare **パンが好きです** (a preference) with **パンを食べます** (an action). **パンを好きです** is not the standard beginner pattern taught here; do not substitute **を** for **が**.

### 3. Two items: コーヒーと水

**コーヒーと水** means “coffee and water.” **と** joins these two named items. In the order **コーヒーと水をお願いします**, the server is being asked for both drinks. This use of **と** is not the same as the companion use in **友だちと** (“with a friend”).

**Politeness note:** **〜をお願いします** is a polite request useful for a menu or drinks. **いらっしゃいませ** and **かしこまりました** are common service expressions; learners can recognize them without using them as customer greetings.

## Supported reading practice

1. **First pass — situation.** Hide the English meaning. Identify the line spoken by the server first. Who is likely working at the café?
2. **Second pass — choices.** Underline **ごはんを食べます**, **パンを食べます**, **お茶をお願いします**, and **コーヒーと水をお願いします**. Make a two-column list for Aki and Ren.
3. **Third pass — inference boundary.** Does **パンが好きです** prove Aki orders bread today? No. Her next sentence begins **でも** (“but”) and says **今日はごはんを食べます**.
4. **Guided substitution.** Change **パンが好きです** to **お茶が好きです** (“I like tea”). Change **パンを食べます** to **ごはんを食べます** (“I will eat rice”). The particle changes with the pattern, not simply with the food.
5. **Independent transfer.** Write two short lines for a fictional customer: one preference and one choice for today. Model: **お茶が好きです。でも、今日は水をお願いします。** (“I like tea. But today, water please.”) Compare your particles and meanings; multiple original answers are possible.

## Reading, writing, listening preparation, and optional speaking

Read the dialogue again with the English covered, using the kana supports. Copy the two lines that contain **と** and **が**; say what each particle contributes. For optional speaking, rehearse the customer role aloud or with a partner.
