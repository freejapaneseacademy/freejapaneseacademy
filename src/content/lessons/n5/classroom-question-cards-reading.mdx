## Reading mission: four classroom Q&A cards

You are helping a new student at a fictional school reception desk. Read each card to answer **who**, **what**, **which one** and **where**. The conversations and names below are original fictional teaching examples, not real school records. This is a written reading activity, not a listening or pronunciation assessment.

| Card | Setting | Japanese exchange | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | うけつけ | A: すみません。たなか先生はどこですか。 B: たなか先生はきょうしつです。 | Where is Teacher Tanaka? — In the classroom. |
| B | きょうしつ | A: あの人はだれですか。 B: さとうさんです。さとうさんも先生です。 | Who is that person? — Sato. Sato is also a teacher. |
| C | つくえ | A: これは何ですか。 B: クラスのめいぼです。 | What is this? — The class roster. |
| D | うけつけ | A: どれがさとうさんのペンですか。 B: あおいペンです。 | Which is Sato's pen? — The blue pen. |

**Reading help:** 先生（せんせい） is teacher; 教室（きょうしつ） is classroom; 人（ひと） is person; 何（なん） is what; 青い（あおい） is blue. The cards use kana for most words so you can focus on meaning. In Card A, the natural full sentence would often be **たなか先生はきょうしつにいます** (“Teacher Tanaka is in the classroom”). The shorter **たなか先生はきょうしつです** can be understood as a context-dependent answer to “Where is Teacher Tanaka?”, but it is less explicit about physical presence.

## Step 1: Match the question word to its job

| Japanese | Reading | What it requests | Card |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| だれ | dare | a person's identity: **who?** | B |
| 何 | なん | an object's identity: **what?** | C |
| どこ | doko | a location: **where?** | A |
| どれ | dore | one choice among visible options: **which one?** | D |

**Don't mix up どれ and どこ.** If someone is pointing to several pens, **どれ** asks which pen. If someone is looking for a teacher's room, **どこ** asks where. **どれ** stands alone as a pronoun; **どのペン** (“which pen”) places どの before a noun. The cards use **どれが…ですか** to select an item.

## Step 2: Read what the card actually says

In Card B, **さとうさんも先生です** means “Sato is also a teacher.” The particle **も** adds an “also/too” relationship; it does not say that Sato is the *only* teacher. Card A names Tanaka, and Card B names Sato; do not assume they are in the same room.

In Card D, **あおいペンです** gives the color of the selected pen. It says **nothing** about the pen's price or who bought it. The English gloss “Sato's pen” is supported by the preceding question **さとうさんのペン** (“Sato's pen”).

## Worked example: cite the answer, not a guess

**Question:** Where is Teacher Tanaka? **Answer:** In the classroom. **Evidence:** 「たなか先生はきょうしつです。」 in Card A. This is enough to identify the location in the exchange. A statement about Sato in Card B cannot establish Tanaka's location.

## Guided rehearsal

First cover the English meanings. For each card, underline the question word, then locate the answer after **B:**. Ask yourself whether the response names a person, object, location or selected option. Read Card B again and explain what **も** adds. Finally, read Card D and say one thing the card **does not** tell you.

## Independent reading and review

Complete the ten questions below without relying on the English glosses when possible. Check the evidence after submission. A correct choice with the wrong evidence is worth reviewing again. Afterwards, write two new Q&A pairs using **だれ** and **どこ** and compare them with the card patterns. Written composition is self-reviewed; no automatic writing grade or human review is claimed.

**Takeaway:** Locate the exact Japanese phrase first. Keep the four question words distinct, and never infer details absent from the card.
