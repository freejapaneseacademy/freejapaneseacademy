## A café and a stationery shop

You and a friend are choosing a snack, then buying a notebook. A word such as **おいしい** describes taste, while **安い** describes price. By the end you can recognize which quality someone is describing, not just translate an isolated word. This is original written study material; it is not a recording or pronunciation evaluation.

## 1. Twelve description words in context

| Japanese | Meaning | Example | English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">おいしい</span> | tasty / delicious | <span lang="ja">このパンはおいしいです。</span> | This bread is delicious. |
| <span lang="ja">まずい</span> | tastes bad | <span lang="ja">このスープはまずいです。</span> | This soup tastes bad. |
| <span lang="ja">あまい</span> | sweet | <span lang="ja">このケーキはあまいです。</span> | This cake is sweet. |
| <span lang="ja">からい</span> | spicy | <span lang="ja">このカレーはからいです。</span> | This curry is spicy. |
| <span lang="ja">しょっぱい</span> | salty | <span lang="ja">このスープはしょっぱいです。</span> | This soup is salty. |
| <span lang="ja">大きい</span> | big | <span lang="ja">このかばんは大きいです。</span> | This bag is big. |
| <span lang="ja">小さい</span> | small | <span lang="ja">このかばんは小さいです。</span> | This bag is small. |
| <span lang="ja">高い</span> | expensive / high | <span lang="ja">このとけいは高いです。</span> | This watch is expensive. |
| <span lang="ja">安い</span> | inexpensive | <span lang="ja">このノートは安いです。</span> | This notebook is inexpensive. |
| <span lang="ja">いい</span> | good | <span lang="ja">この店はいいです。</span> | This shop is good. |
| <span lang="ja">きれい</span> | clean / beautiful | <span lang="ja">このへやはきれいです。</span> | This room is clean. |
| <span lang="ja">しずか</span> | quiet | <span lang="ja">このカフェはしずかです。</span> | This café is quiet. |

**Reading help:** 大きい = おおきい, 小さい = ちいさい, 高い = たかい, 安い = やすい. These readings belong to the complete words; the kanji are not isolated pronunciation rules. しずか is often written 静か and きれい is often written 綺麗, but kana is enough for today's recognition work.

## 2. Notice the meaning in the situation

At the café, Yui says: <span lang="ja">このケーキはあまいです。でも、おいしいです。</span> — “This cake is sweet. And it's delicious.” Sweetness is a taste; **おいしい** is Yui's positive evaluation. Sweet food is not automatically delicious to everyone.

At the stationery shop, Ken says: <span lang="ja">このノートは小さいです。でも、安いです。</span> — “This notebook is small. But it's inexpensive.” The first description is size; the second is price. **高い** can mean “high” or “expensive”: <span lang="ja">このかばんは高いです。</span> means the bag is expensive in a shopping context.

## 3. Short conversation: Which place should we choose?

**Yui:** <span lang="ja">このカフェはしずかですか。</span> — Is this café quiet?

**Ken:** <span lang="ja">はい。しずかです。ケーキもおいしいです。</span> — Yes, it is quiet. The cake is delicious too.

**Yui:** <span lang="ja">でも、ケーキは高いですか。</span> — But is the cake expensive?

**Ken:** <span lang="ja">いいえ。あまり高くないです。</span> — No. It isn't very expensive.

**Yui:** <span lang="ja">じゃあ、ここにしましょう。</span> — Then let's choose this place.

**Evidence check:** They like the quiet setting and cake; Ken says the cake is *not very expensive*, not that it is free. The negative form **高くない** is previewed here; its formation is taught systematically in Lesson 03.

## 4. Two adjective families: a useful preview

Many adjectives end in **い**, such as **おいしい・あまい・大きい・高い**. They can directly precede a noun: <span lang="ja">おいしいケーキ</span> (delicious cake).

**きれい** looks as though it ends in い, but it is a **な-adjective**. Say <span lang="ja">きれいなへや</span> (a clean/beautiful room), not *きれいいへや*. **しずか** is also a な-adjective: <span lang="ja">しずかなカフェ</span> (a quiet café). At the end of a polite sentence, both families can appear before です: <span lang="ja">このへやはきれいです。</span> and <span lang="ja">このパンはおいしいです。</span>

**いい** means good, but its negative is **よくない**, not *いくない*. Do not guess negative forms from the final sound alone. In Lesson 03 you will practice negatives in detail.

## 5. Common interpretation mistakes

**からい** describes spicy heat, not the temperature of a hot drink. For hot temperature, Japanese commonly uses **あつい**. **まずい** is a strong negative judgment of taste; when speaking to someone who prepared the food, a softer expression is often more considerate. **きれい** can mean beautiful or clean depending on context; the room sentence may suggest cleanliness, but the word itself is broader.

**高い** and **大きい** do not mean the same thing: a small item can be expensive. Likewise **安い** does not automatically mean low quality.

## 6. Controlled → guided → independent practice

**Controlled retrieval:** Cover the English column. Find the Japanese for “salty,” “small,” “quiet,” and “inexpensive.” Check: **しょっぱい・小さい・しずか・安い**.

**Guided contrast:** A notebook costs little but has a large cover. Choose the correct words: <span lang="ja">このノートは（安い／からい）です。大きいです。</span> Answer: **安い** because the context concerns price, not taste.

**Independent task:** Write three short product or food comments using the model <span lang="ja">この___は___です。</span> Try one taste word, one size word, and one evaluation word. Example: <span lang="ja">このパンはおいしいです。</span> Check that the chosen adjective actually describes the noun.

**Optional speaking:** Read the café conversation aloud, alternating roles, then replace ケーキ with パン. This is ungraded rehearsal; no recorded pronunciation has been checked.
