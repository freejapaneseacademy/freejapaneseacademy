## Mission: make a plan everyone can accept

Two fictional classmates, Mika and Haru, are deciding where and when to meet after class. This chapter checkpoint brings together **invitations, polite refusals, suggestions and final decisions**. The twelve scored items assess **written grammar and reading**; listening and independent writing are separate ungraded activities.

### 1. Retrieve the useful patterns

| Function | Japanese | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Invite | <span lang="ja">いっしょに食べませんか。</span> | Would you like to eat together? |
| Accept | <span lang="ja">いいですね。ぜひ。</span> | Sounds good. I'd love to. |
| Decline softly | <span lang="ja">すみません、今日はちょっと……。</span> | Sorry, today is a little difficult. |
| Suggest | <span lang="ja">カフェに行きましょう。</span> | Let's go to the café. |
| Decide | <span lang="ja">カフェにします。</span> | I'll choose the café. |
| Propose a time | <span lang="ja">二時に会いましょうか。</span> | Shall we meet at two? |

**Form and meaning:** <span lang="ja">食べませんか</span> (tabemasen ka) is an invitation, even though it contains a negative verb ending. <span lang="ja">食べましょう</span> (tabemashō) suggests doing something together. <span lang="ja">カフェにします</span> (kafe ni shimasu) indicates a choice; <span lang="ja">カフェにしましょう</span> (kafe ni shimashō) proposes making that choice together.

A refusal such as <span lang="ja">今日はちょっと……</span> (kyō wa chotto…) is deliberately indirect. It usually means the invitation is difficult to accept, **not** that the speaker agrees. Respond kindly rather than pressing for an explanation.

### 2. Read an original meeting message

**Class group message — Wednesday evening**

> <span lang="ja">ミカ：金曜日の授業のあとで、いっしょに晩ごはんを食べませんか。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">ハル：いいですね。でも、金曜日はアルバイトがあります。土曜日なら大丈夫です。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">ミカ：わかりました。土曜日の六時はどうですか。駅の前のレストランに行きましょう。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">ハル：すみません。あのレストランは土曜日は休みです。学校のとなりのカフェはどうですか。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">ミカ：いいですね。じゃあ、土曜日の六時にカフェで会いましょう。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">ハル：はい。土曜日の六時ですね。</span>

**Meaning, line by line:** Mika invites Haru to dinner after Friday's class. Haru likes the idea but works part-time on Friday and can meet Saturday. Mika proposes Saturday at six and a restaurant in front of the station. Haru says that restaurant is closed on Saturday and suggests the café next to the school. Both agree to meet at the café at six on Saturday.

**Reading support:** <span lang="ja">金曜日（きんようび）</span> = Friday; <span lang="ja">土曜日（どようび）</span> = Saturday; <span lang="ja">授業（じゅぎょう）</span> = class; <span lang="ja">駅（えき）の前（まえ）</span> = in front of the station; <span lang="ja">学校（がっこう）のとなり</span> = next to the school; <span lang="ja">六時（ろくじ）</span> = six o'clock; <span lang="ja">休み（やすみ）</span> = closed / day off in this context.

### 3. Prove your answers with text

Ask three questions before you choose: **Who proposes it? What changes? What is the final agreed plan?** For example, the evidence for the final time is <span lang="ja">土曜日の六時にカフェで会いましょう</span> and Haru's confirmation <span lang="ja">土曜日の六時ですね</span>. Do not confuse the original Friday invitation with the final Saturday decision.

Try a mini-retrieval exercise without looking above: (1) What makes Friday difficult? (2) Why not the station restaurant? (3) Which place is chosen? Then reopen the message and underline <span lang="ja">アルバイトがあります</span>, <span lang="ja">土曜日は休みです</span>, and <span lang="ja">学校のとなりのカフェ</span>.

### 6. Independent writing and optional speaking

Write **two to five connected Japanese sentences** that invite a friend, respond to a scheduling problem, propose an alternative and confirm a meeting time. Your friend may accept or decline. Use the writing box after the quiz and compare with this model:

<span lang="ja">土曜日にカフェに行きませんか。土曜日はちょっと……。日曜日なら大丈夫です。じゃあ、日曜日の二時に会いましょう。</span>

“Would you like to go to a café on Saturday? Saturday is difficult. Sunday works for me. Then let's meet Sunday at two.”

**Self-review rubric (not machine-graded):** Check (1) invitation with ませんか, (2) clear acceptance or polite refusal, (3) a realistic alternative, (4) a final day/time or place that matches the response, and (5) natural particles and polite sentence endings. Several different answers can be correct.

Optionally role-play both speakers aloud with a partner. No microphone starts automatically, and no pronunciation score is generated. Review missed items on a later day and revisit the chapter's authentic listening when it is ready.
