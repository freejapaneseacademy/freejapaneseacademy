## Mission: understand a friend's normal day

Imagine you are preparing to meet a Japanese-speaking friend after class. You need to distinguish **what she does**, **what she does not do**, and **when**. This checkpoint combines grammar, reading and listening; it is formative practice, not a certified language test.

### 1. Review the patterns

| Pattern | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 毎朝六時半に起きます。 | まいあさ ろくじはんに おきます | I get up at 6:30 every morning. |
| 水を飲みます。 | みずを のみます | I drink water. |
| テレビを見ません。 | テレビを みません | I do not watch television. |
| 学校に行きます。 | がっこうに いきます | I go to school. |
| 図書館で勉強します。 | としょかんで べんきょうします | I study at the library. |

**ます** is polite nonpast affirmative; **ません** is polite nonpast negative. Neither by itself indicates a past event. **を** marks the direct object of an action such as reading, drinking or eating. **に** marks a specific time (**七時に**) or a destination (**学校に**). **で** marks the location of an action (**図書館で勉強します**). The recurring expression **毎朝** normally appears without time に.

Compare **七時に食べます** (eat at seven) with **パンを食べます** (eat bread): the particles tell you whether a phrase names the time or the object. The polite past negative is **ませんでした**, not **ません**; this checkpoint tests normal present habits.

### 2. Original reading: Yui's weekday note

This fictional note is independent of the two chapter recordings.

> <span lang="ja">わたしはゆいです。毎朝六時半に起きます。七時に朝ご飯を食べます。朝は水を飲みます。八時に図書館に行きます。図書館で日本語を勉強します。夜は本を読みます。でも、夜はテレビを見ません。</span>

**Translation:** My name is Yui. Every morning I get up at 6:30. I eat breakfast at seven. In the morning I drink water. At eight I go to the library. I study Japanese at the library. At night I read books. But I do not watch television at night.

**Evidence strategy:** Underline the shortest Japanese phrase that answers each question. **六時半に起きます** establishes the waking time, **水を飲みます** identifies the drink, and **夜はテレビを見ません** gives the negative action. The contrastive **は** in **夜は** highlights nighttime; it is not the direct-object marker.

### 3. Listening: two chapter conversations

**Track A** is a before-school exchange about waking up, breakfast and television. **Track B** is an after-work exchange about coming home, making dinner and nighttime drinks. These are different people from Yui's written note.

Listen once without looking at the transcript. Listen again for the time and negative verb. You can reveal a transcript afterward; doing so labels your attempt **transcript-assisted**. Four listening questions unlock only after **both actual recordings can play**. Reading a transcript alone never earns a listening score.

### 4. Write your own connected routine

Write **four Japanese sentences** about a usual day: one wake-up time, one food or drink with **を**, one destination with **に**, and one negative habit ending in **ません**.

Model:

> <span lang="ja">毎朝七時に起きます。朝ご飯にパンを食べます。八時に学校に行きます。夜はコーヒーを飲みません。</span>

Self-review: (1) your verbs are habitual, not past; (2) clock times use に; (3) direct objects use を; (4) the negative ends in ません. The writing is **self-assessed**, not automatically graded. Optional speaking: read your sentences aloud and rehearse a question **何時に起きますか。** No microphone starts automatically; no pronunciation grade is claimed.
