## Your task: read the activity board

At a community Japanese class, students are choosing activities for Saturday. A teacher writes a short board and asks who wants to join. **Your goal today is to understand the activities**, not to memorize every conjugation rule. The board and conversation are original fictional learning materials.

### Saturday activity board — 土曜日（どようび）の活動（かつどう）

| Time | Japanese | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 午前九時（ごぜんくじ） | 本（ほん）を読む（よむ） | Read a book |
| 午前十時（ごぜんじゅうじ） | 日本語（にほんご）を書く（かく） | Write Japanese |
| 午前十一時（ごぜんじゅういちじ） | お茶（ちゃ）を飲む（のむ） | Drink tea |
| 午後一時（ごごいちじ） | 映画（えいが）を見る（みる） | Watch a film |

The board uses **dictionary forms**: 読む, 書く, 飲む, 見る. These are the forms normally used as dictionary headwords. A dictionary form is *not automatically future tense*: context determines whether a nonpast verb describes a habit, present general fact, or future plan.

### Short exchange

> **先生（せんせい）:** 土曜日、何（なに）をしますか。  
> **ゆい:** 午前九時に本を読みます。  
> **先生:** あきさんは？  
> **あき:** 午前十時に日本語を書きます。それから、お茶を飲みます。  
> **先生:** 映画も見ますか。  
> **あき:** はい、午後一時に見ます。

**English meaning**

- **Teacher:** What will you do on Saturday?
- **Yui:** I'll read a book at 9 a.m.
- **Teacher:** And you, Aki?
- **Aki:** I'll write Japanese at 10 a.m. After that, I'll drink tea.
- **Teacher:** Will you watch a film too?
- **Aki:** Yes, I'll watch one at 1 p.m.

**Notice:** The board says **読む**, but Yui says **読みます**. Both describe reading; the difference is sentence style. In this classroom conversation, **ます** forms make polite statements and questions.

## 1. What changes and what stays the same?

| Dictionary form | Reading | Polite form | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 読む | よむ | 読みます（よみます） | read |
| 書く | かく | 書きます（かきます） | write |
| 飲む | のむ | 飲みます（のみます） | drink |
| 見る | みる | 見ます（みます） | see / watch |
| 食べる | たべる | 食べます（たべます） | eat |
| する | する | します | do |
| 来る | くる | 来ます（きます） | come |

**読む → 読みます:** a common Group 1 (五段・ごだん) pattern changes the final **む** to **み** before ます. **書く → 書きます** changes **く** to **き**. **飲む → 飲みます** follows the same む → み pattern.

**見る → 見ます** and **食べる → 食べます** are Group 2 (一段・いちだん) examples: remove **る** and add **ます**. Not every verb ending in **る** is Group 2. For example, **帰る（かえる）→ 帰ります（かえります）** is Group 1. You will practise the full classification in the following lessons.

**する → します** and **来る（くる）→ 来ます（きます）** are irregular (Group 3). In particular, 来る is read **くる**, but 来ます is read **きます**. Do not guess **くます**.

## 2. Read meaning before naming the group

On the board, **本を読む** is an activity label. In the dialogue, **本を読みます** is a complete polite statement. The object marker **を** stays the same. The verb form changes; the activity does not.

Compare:

- **映画を見る。**（えいがをみる。）— Watch a film / I watch films (plain nonpast, depending on context).
- **映画を見ます。**（えいがをみます。）— I watch a film / I will watch a film (polite nonpast, depending on context).
- **映画を見ました。**（えいがをみました。）— I watched a film (polite past).

**見ます is not 見ました.** The **ました** ending marks past, which the board does not use.

### Common traps

**飲む** is pronounced **のむ**, not **よむ**. **読む（よむ）** means read. Similar endings do not mean identical actions.

**見る → 見ます** removes る, but **帰る → 帰ります** does not. Avoid the shortcut “all る-verbs drop る.”

The expression **何をしますか** asks what someone will do in this Saturday context. **します** comes from **する**; it does not mean “see.”

## 3. Controlled noticing

Without looking at the English column, point to the board item for **writing Japanese**. Then find its polite form in Aki's reply: **書きます**. Do the same for reading, drinking and watching.

Cover the polite-form column and try to supply **読みます**, **書きます**, **飲みます**, **見ます**. Check each against the table. This is a guided rehearsal, not an automatic score.

## 4. Independent transfer

A friend writes **日曜日（にちようび）に本を読みます。** (“I read / will read a book on Sunday.”) Write the activity as a short dictionary-form label: **本を読む**. Now write one polite sentence of your own using **食べます** or **見ます**, with a real or imagined object.

Model: **日曜日に映画を見ます。** — “I'll watch a film on Sunday.” The model is only one possible answer; your free writing is not machine-graded here.

## 5. Review and next step

Return to the board and explain, in your own words, the difference between **読む** and **読みます**. Both are nonpast forms; one is dictionary/plain, the other polite.
