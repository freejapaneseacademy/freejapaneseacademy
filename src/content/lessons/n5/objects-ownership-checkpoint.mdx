## Mission: help at the school lost-and-found

A class has left notebooks, bags and umbrellas behind. You are helping a staff member identify each item and return it to the correct person. In this chapter checkpoint, you will **point out an object and ask whose it is**, read four short original notices, and distinguish owners in two conversations. This is original academy practice, not an official JLPT examination.

### 1. Choose the right demonstrative

| Standalone (no following noun) | Before a noun | Typical reference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">これ</span> — this one | <span lang="ja">このノート</span> — this notebook | Near the speaker |
| <span lang="ja">それ</span> — that one | <span lang="ja">そのかさ</span> — that umbrella | Near the listener or previously mentioned |
| <span lang="ja">あれ</span> — that one over there | <span lang="ja">あのかばん</span> — that bag over there | Away from both |

The physical viewpoint matters: when a staff member holds an umbrella, the staff member might call it **これ**, while the student facing the counter might call it **それ**. A previously mentioned object can also be referred to with **それ** even without a literal near/far contrast.

Do not write <span lang="ja">これノート</span> to mean “this notebook”: **これ** is a noun-like standalone word. Use <span lang="ja">このノート</span>, or say <span lang="ja">これはノートです。</span> (“This is a notebook.”)

### 2. Ask whose object it is

- <span lang="ja">このかばんはだれのですか。</span> — Whose bag is this?
- <span lang="ja">佐藤（さとう）さんのです。</span> — It is Sato's (bag).
- <span lang="ja">あれは田中（たなか）さんのかさです。</span> — That over there is Tanaka's umbrella.
- <span lang="ja">いいえ、そのかさは山本（やまもと）さんのです。</span> — No, that umbrella is Yamamoto's.

**の** connects a person or other noun to a related noun: <span lang="ja">田中さんのノート</span> (“Tanaka's notebook”). When the object is already clear, <span lang="ja">田中さんのです</span> means “It's Tanaka's.” **だれの** asks “whose?”; **だれ** alone asks “who?” and does not express ownership.

### 3. Read four original lost-and-found notices

Read each notice before answering the reading questions. The notices are fictional. Some words are recognition-only supports: **あずかっています** means “we are holding [an item]”, **職員室（しょくいんしつ）** means staff room, and **机（つくえ）の上（うえ）** means on the desk.

| Notice | Japanese text | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | <span lang="ja">青（あお）いノートをあずかっています。</span> | Which object and color? |
| B | <span lang="ja">赤（あか）いかさは山本さんのです。</span> | Who is the owner? |
| C | <span lang="ja">このかばんは職員室にあります。</span> | Where is the bag? |
| D | <span lang="ja">佐藤さんのノートは机の上です。</span> | Whose notebook? |

English check: A says a blue notebook is being held; B identifies Yamamoto's red umbrella; C places this bag in the staff room; D locates Sato's notebook on the desk. Notice that the person who *finds* an object is not necessarily its owner.

### 5. Guided writing and optional speaking

Write **three connected Japanese sentences** about a fictional lost item. Include an object, a question about its owner, and a response. Model:

<span lang="ja">これは青いノートです。このノートはだれのですか。田中さんのです。</span>

“This is a blue notebook. Whose notebook is this? It's Tanaka's.”

**Self-review rubric:** (1) Does **これ** stand alone? (2) Does **この** come before the noun? (3) Does **だれの** ask ownership? (4) Is the response with **のです** consistent with the object? (5) Is the viewpoint clear? This writing is self-assessed, not machine-graded.

Optional: read your exchange aloud, changing the object to a bag or umbrella. If genuine audio is available, shadow one short turn. No microphone is activated and no pronunciation grade is assigned.
