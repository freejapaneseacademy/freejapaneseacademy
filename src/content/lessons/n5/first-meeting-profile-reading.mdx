## Welcome to the language exchange

**Can-do:** Introduce yourself and identify another person's basic details. At a fictional international language exchange, you read three short profile cards before meeting their writers. Your task is not to translate every word: locate a fact and point to the **exact Japanese evidence**. All people and details below are invented for this lesson.

After this lesson you can identify a person's name, country, work or study role, and a stated nationality, while leaving unstated details unknown.

## 1. Read the profile labels

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 名前 | なまえ | name |
| 国 | くに | country |
| 仕事 | しごと | job / occupation |
| 学生 | がくせい | student |
| 先生 | せんせい | teacher |
| 会社員 | かいしゃいん | company employee |
| 日本人 | にほんじん | Japanese person / nationality |
| アメリカ人 | アメリカじん | American person / nationality |
| ではありません / じゃありません | — | is not / am not (polite negative) |

The topic particle **は** is pronounced *wa*, even though it is written は. In **私は学生です。** (*わたしは がくせいです*), the speaker says “I am a student.” In **私は先生じゃありません。**, the speaker says “I am not a teacher.” The more formal **ではありません** is also a polite negative. **か** at the end of a sentence marks a question: **学生ですか。** “Are you a student?”

## 2. Original profile card A — 佐藤さん

> **名前：** 佐藤（さとう）みき  
> **国：** 日本（にほん）  
> **仕事：** 先生（せんせい）  
> **メッセージ：** 私（わたし）は日本人（にほんじん）です。英語（えいご）の先生です。

**Meaning:** Name: Miki Sato. Country: Japan. Occupation: teacher. Message: “I am Japanese. I teach English / I am an English teacher.”

The **evidence** for her occupation is **仕事：先生** and the more specific **英語の先生です**. The **evidence** for her nationality is **私は日本人です**. The country field alone would not be enough to establish nationality, but her message states it explicitly. Her age is **not given**.

## 3. Original profile card B — リナさん

> **名前：** リナ  
> **国：** カナダ  
> **仕事：** 学生（がくせい）  
> **メッセージ：** 私は学生です。先生じゃありません。日本語（にほんご）を勉強（べんきょう）しています。

**Meaning:** Name: Rina. Country: Canada. Occupation: student. Message: “I am a student. I am not a teacher. I am studying Japanese.”

**Evidence:** **仕事：学生** supports “student,” while **先生じゃありません** explicitly rules out “teacher.” **日本語を勉強しています** describes what she is studying; it does not identify her nationality. The card does not state that she is Canadian by nationality, only that the country field is Canada.

## 4. Original profile card C — アレックスさん

> **名前：** アレックス  
> **国：** アメリカ  
> **仕事：** 会社員（かいしゃいん）  
> **メッセージ：** 私はアメリカ人です。学生ではありません。よろしくおねがいします。

**Meaning:** Name: Alex. Country: United States. Occupation: company employee. Message: “I am American. I am not a student. Nice to meet you.”

**Evidence:** **仕事：会社員** identifies the occupation; **私はアメリカ人です** explicitly states nationality; **学生ではありません** denies student status. Neither the person's company nor hometown is specified.

## 5. A short question-and-answer card

> A: 佐藤さんは学生ですか。  
> B: いいえ、学生じゃありません。先生です。  
> A: リナさんは先生ですか。  
> B: いいえ、先生じゃありません。学生です。

**Meaning:** A asks if Sato is a student; B says no, Sato is a teacher. A asks if Rina is a teacher; B says no, Rina is a student.

Notice the **topic** before は. In the reply **先生です**, the topic can be omitted because the speakers already know who is being discussed. Do not accidentally transfer the second answer to the first person. Here, **いいえ** introduces a negative reply, while **じゃありません** supplies the negative statement.

## 6. Find evidence before answering

Use this three-step method:

1. **Find the named person.** Is the question about 佐藤, リナ or アレックス?
2. **Locate the right field or sentence.** For a job, look for **仕事**; for nationality, look for **〜人です**.
3. **Quote only what the card supports.** If the question asks for Rina's nationality, the accurate answer is **not stated**, because **国：カナダ** is a country field, not an explicit nationality sentence.

**Controlled practice:** Read **仕事：会社員**. What is Alex's occupation? *Company employee.* Quote **仕事：会社員** as evidence.

**Guided practice:** Compare **リナ：先生じゃありません** with **佐藤：先生です**. Who is a teacher? *Sato.* The negative ending changes the meaning.

**Independent practice:** Without looking at the English glosses, answer: “Which person explicitly says they are American?” *Alex.* Find and copy **私はアメリカ人です**.

## 7. Reading check and follow-up

Complete the ten evidence-based questions below. The score measures this local reading practice, **not** an official JLPT result. When you miss a question, revisit the specific phrase rather than memorizing the letter of an option.

For optional unscored writing, choose one person and write two supported Japanese statements. Example: **リナさんは学生です。先生じゃありません。** Compare your sentences with the original card. Do not invent a missing age, employer or nationality.

**Review:** です states a polite nominal fact, **じゃありません / ではありません** negates one, and **か** asks. A reliable reader distinguishes **what is written** from **what might be true**. The next lesson's listening task uses audio; this written reading lesson requires no recording.
