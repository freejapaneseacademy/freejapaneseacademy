## A conversation before class

**Can-do for this chapter:** Describe a usual day using polite present verbs. Today's task is to **understand** a typical-day conversation before producing your own descriptions in later lessons.

Two fictional classmates, あや (Aya) and けん (Ken), meet at school before class. Aya wants to learn what Ken usually does. **まいにち** (“every day”) establishes a habitual routine rather than a one-time appointment. The times and activities below belong to this invented scenario.

### Original dialogue

**あや:** おはようございます。けんさん、まいにち何をしますか。  
**けん:** あさ七時におきます。八時にパンを食べます。  
**あや:** 学校へ行きますか。  
**けん:** はい、九時に学校へ行きます。学校で日本語を勉強します。  
**あや:** よる、テレビを見ますか。  
**けん:** いいえ、テレビは見ません。本を読みます。

**English meaning:** Aya says good morning and asks what Ken does every day. Ken gets up at seven in the morning and eats bread at eight. Aya asks whether he goes to school. Ken says yes: he goes at nine and studies Japanese at school. Aya asks whether he watches television at night. He says no, he does not watch television; he reads a book instead.

### Who, where and why?

| Context clue | Evidence | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speakers | あや / けん | Two classmates taking turns |
| Setting | 学校（がっこう） | A school; the exchange happens before class |
| Purpose | まいにち何をしますか | Aya asks about Ken's usual routine |
| Morning | 七時におきます / 八時にパンを食べます | Ken gets up at 7 and eats bread at 8 |
| Later | 九時に学校へ行きます | Ken goes to school at 9 |
| Evening | テレビは見ません。本を読みます | He does not watch TV; he reads a book |

**Evidence discipline:** The dialogue does **not** say what Aya does every day, how Ken travels, or what time he sleeps. Do not infer those facts.

## Notice the sentence signals

**Polite nonpast:** **食べます** means “eat” or “will eat” depending on context. With **まいにち**, it describes a habit: **八時にパンを食べます** (“I eat bread at eight”). **見ません** is the polite negative nonpast: **テレビは見ません** (“I don't watch television”). Neither form by itself means “ate yesterday.”

**を** marks the direct object of an action: **パンを食べます** (eat bread), **本を読みます** (read a book). The particle **を** is normally pronounced *o*.

**に** marks a specific clock time in **七時におきます** (“get up at seven”). **学校へ行きます** uses **へ** for direction (“go to school”), while **学校で勉強します** uses **で** for the place where studying occurs. Those two are contextual supports; this chapter will focus especially on **ます／ません・を・に**.

**A common trap:** Do not interpret **テレビは見ません** as “Ken watches TV.” The negative ending changes the fact. Also do not claim that **九時** is his wake-up time: **おきます** occurs with **七時**.

## Practice in three stages

**Controlled retrieval:** Find the exact Japanese phrase that tells you when Ken gets up, and the phrase that tells you what he does at night. Point to the verb endings.

**Guided reconstruction:** Complete this English outline without looking at the translation: “Ken gets up at ___, eats ___ at ___, and goes to ___ at ___.” Then check against the Japanese dialogue.

**Independent transfer:** Write two short Japanese sentences about an *imagined* typical morning using the models **七時におきます。** and **八時にパンを食べます。** You may change the clock time or food. These are ungraded sentences; check the verb ending and whether **に** follows the time and **を** follows the food.

## Reading, listening preparation and optional speaking

Read the dialogue once for the main topic and again to underline time expressions. For listening preparation, ask a partner to read the original dialogue aloud while you hide the text, then reveal it to check what you understood. **There is no recorded audio or scored listening task in this L01.** Optionally rehearse Aya's questions and Ken's answers aloud; no recording or pronunciation score is collected.
