## Optional mission: arrange a weekend trip

You and a classmate are planning a fictional visit to a museum. You want to know **how** your classmate will travel and **with whom**. At the end, you can perform a short exchange in either role. Speaking aloud is optional: reading silently or typing is equally valid. This page does not request microphone access, record you, or score pronunciation.

The chapter's L06 is the separate authentic listening lesson. Only use its audio when the licensed WAVs and quality manifest have actually been verified. The written dialogues here are **not recordings**.

## 1. Your conversation toolkit

| Purpose | Japanese | Kana reading | English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ask how | 何で行きますか。 | なんで いきますか | How will you go? (By what means?) |
| Answer by train | 電車で行きます。 | でんしゃで いきます | I'll go by train. |
| Ask with whom | だれと行きますか。 | だれと いきますか | Who will you go with? |
| Answer with a friend | 友達と行きます。 | ともだちと いきます | I'll go with a friend. |
| Answer alone | 一人で行きます。 | ひとりで いきます | I'll go alone. |
| Say destination | 博物館に行きます。 | はくぶつかんに いきます | I'll go to the museum. |

**Three different relationships:** **電車で** marks the vehicle used; **友達と** marks the accompanying person; **博物館に** marks the destination. These particles cannot be swapped freely. **一人で** is an established expression meaning “alone,” not “by means of one person.”

**Note:** 何で can also be read **なんで** meaning “why” in other contexts. Here, with a transport answer and the explicit “by what means” setting, it asks how. You can also ask **どうやって行きますか** to make “how” explicit.

## 2. Model A: going together

> **さき：** 日曜日（にちようび）、博物館（はくぶつかん）に行（い）きますか。  
> Saki: Are you going to the museum on Sunday?
>
> **れん：** はい、行きます。  
> Ren: Yes, I am.
>
> **さき：** 何（なん）で行きますか。  
> Saki: How will you get there?
>
> **れん：** 地下鉄（ちかてつ）で行きます。  
> Ren: I'll go by subway.
>
> **さき：** だれと行きますか。  
> Saki: Who will you go with?
>
> **れん：** 妹（いもうと）と行きます。  
> Ren: I'll go with my younger sister.

**Find the evidence:** 地下鉄で answers **how**; 妹と answers **with whom**. Both answers can use 行きます without repeating the destination.

## 3. Model B: going alone, then meeting a friend

> **ゆい：** 土曜日（どようび）、公園（こうえん）へ行きますか。  
> Yui: Are you going to the park on Saturday?
>
> **たく：** はい。自転車（じてんしゃ）で行きます。  
> Taku: Yes. I'll go by bicycle.
>
> **ゆい：** だれと行きますか。  
> Yui: Who are you going with?
>
> **たく：** 一人（ひとり）で行きます。でも、公園で友達（ともだち）に会（あ）います。  
> Taku: I'll go alone. But I'll meet a friend at the park.
>
> **ゆい：** そうですか。  
> Yui: I see.

**Important nuance:** Going **alone** and meeting someone **at the destination** are compatible. 公園で marks the location of the meeting action, not the means of travel. **会います** (あいます, meet) is recognition-only here.

## 4. Guided rehearsal: change one detail at a time

**Round A — Means.** Ask **どうやって行きますか** and reply **バスで行きます**. Replace バス with **電車（でんしゃ）**, **地下鉄（ちかてつ）** or **自転車（じてんしゃ）**. Do not attach と to a vehicle to mean “by.”

**Round B — Companion.** Ask **だれと行きますか**. Answer **母（はは）と行きます**, **友達と行きます**, or **一人で行きます**. If you are alone, do not invent a companion.

**Round C — Combine.** Try **友達と電車で図書館（としょかん）に行きます** — “I'll go to the library by train with a friend.” Japanese allows the companion and transport phrases to be reordered in many contexts, while their particles stay attached.

## 5. Independent role switch

Choose a fictional destination: **図書館** (library), **公園** (park), or **博物館** (museum). Choose a means: **バス**, **電車**, **地下鉄**, **自転車**, or **歩（ある）いて** (on foot). Choose a companion: **友達**, **家族（かぞく）**, or **一人で**.

First play the questioner and ask **どこへ行きますか**, **どうやって行きますか**, and **だれと行きますか**. Then play the traveler and give three relevant answers. Switch roles once. If walking, say **歩いて行きます**, not **歩くで行きます**.

## 6. Written knowledge check and recap

The twelve questions below check written recognition of the expressions just taught. They are not listening or oral-fluency tests. You may retry, and successful browser-local storage saves your written attempt.

**Remember:** **何で／どうやって** asks how, **だれと** asks with whom, **vehicle + で** indicates means, **person + と** indicates company, and **一人で** means alone. A clear, short exchange is more valuable than a memorized long speech.
