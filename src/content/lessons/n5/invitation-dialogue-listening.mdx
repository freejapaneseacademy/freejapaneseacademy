## An invitation can change

Imagine two classmates deciding where to eat after class. The first suggestion is not always the final decision. In Japanese, listen for **〜ませんか** (an invitation), **〜ましょう** (let's do it), and **〜にしましょう** (let's choose that option).

You will hear **two different original conversations**. Track A is about lunch and a closed restaurant; Track B is about finding a day and time to meet. Each uses two licensed synthetic Japanese voices. The transcripts are available **after you choose to reveal them**, but reading them before answering changes your attempt to assisted practice.

## Before listening: a few useful sounds

- **いっしょに** — together. Listen for this near an invitation.
- **〜ませんか** — “Would you like to…?” The rising question is an invitation, not a negative statement.
- **〜はどうですか** — “How about…?” A way to offer an option.
- **休み（やすみ）** — closed / a day off. In Track A it describes a shop, not a person.
- **〜にしましょう** — “Let's settle on…”. The particle に marks the chosen option.
- **〜はちょっと** — literally “as for …, a little…”. In a refusal context it can gently indicate difficulty without a direct いいえ.

These are **pre-listening vocabulary reminders**, not the answers to the seven questions. You still need to hear the specific place, day and time.

## First pass: listen without reading

For **Track A**, listen for the first proposed location, what makes it unsuitable, and the location chosen instead. Try to follow the sequence rather than translating every word.

For **Track B**, notice that the first suggested day is not accepted. Listen for the alternative day and the time that both speakers confirm.

Use the audio players and the seven questions below. Start each track before attempting a blind score. You may slow playback to 0.8× and replay as often as needed.

## Check the evidence after answering

Reveal the Japanese and English transcript for each track. Compare what you thought you heard with the speakers' actual words. In Track A, notice how **じゃあ** signals a new plan. In Track B, notice how **〜なら大丈夫です** accepts a proposed alternative.

**Optional speaking:** Read one short invitation aloud, such as 「日曜日に会いませんか。」 Then reply 「いいですね。」 or politely decline 「日曜日はちょっと……。」 This is ungraded; no recording or microphone is needed.

**Independent writing:** Write a fictional two-line exchange inviting a classmate to lunch. Include one invitation with **〜ませんか** and a reply with **〜ましょう** or **〜にします**. Self-check the verb ending, the chosen location, and the polite register. Do not treat the listening score as a pronunciation grade.

## Review

You should now be able to follow an invitation, identify a changed plan, and distinguish a gentle refusal from an agreement. If you missed a question, replay the specific line before moving on to the chapter's writing lesson.
