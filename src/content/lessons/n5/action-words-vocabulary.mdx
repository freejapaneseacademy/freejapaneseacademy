## Everyday actions have two useful forms

**Can-do:** recognize a Japanese verb in its **dictionary form** and connect it with the polite **ます form**. A dictionary form is the basic form used to look up a verb. It is not inherently rude: it appears in dictionaries, plain-style conversation and many grammatical patterns. The ます form is a useful polite choice when speaking with someone you do not know well.

Imagine a fictional community center. Aki reads the activity board and tells a visitor what people do there. The same action may appear in different forms on a word list and in a polite conversation.

> <span lang="ja">アキ：ここで 本を 読みます。お茶も 飲みます。</span>  
> あき：ここで ほんを よみます。おちゃも のみます。  
> Aki: “We read books here. We also drink tea.”

The dictionary entries for the two actions are **読む（よむ）** and **飲む（のむ）**, not 読みます and 飲みます.

## 1. Learn the action words

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">食べる</span> | たべる | to eat | <span lang="ja">食べます</span> | Group 2 |
| <span lang="ja">見る</span> | みる | to see/watch | <span lang="ja">見ます</span> | Group 2 |
| <span lang="ja">起きる</span> | おきる | to get up | <span lang="ja">起きます</span> | Group 2 |
| <span lang="ja">行く</span> | いく | to go | <span lang="ja">行きます</span> | Group 1 |
| <span lang="ja">書く</span> | かく | to write | <span lang="ja">書きます</span> | Group 1 |
| <span lang="ja">読む</span> | よむ | to read | <span lang="ja">読みます</span> | Group 1 |
| <span lang="ja">話す</span> | はなす | to speak | <span lang="ja">話します</span> | Group 1 |
| <span lang="ja">飲む</span> | のむ | to drink | <span lang="ja">飲みます</span> | Group 1 |
| <span lang="ja">帰る</span> | かえる | to return home | <span lang="ja">帰ります</span> | Group 1 |
| <span lang="ja">する</span> | する | to do | <span lang="ja">します</span> | Group 3 |
| <span lang="ja">来る</span> | くる | to come | <span lang="ja">来ます（きます）</span> | Group 3 |
| <span lang="ja">買う</span> | かう | to buy | <span lang="ja">買います</span> | Group 1 |

**Group 1** verbs generally change their final *u*-row sound to the corresponding *i*-row sound before ます: **読む → 読みます**, **書く → 書きます**, **買う → 買います**. **Group 2** verbs commonly remove final る and add ます: **食べる → 食べます**, **見る → 見ます**. **Group 3** contains the irregular verbs **する → します** and **来る（くる）→ 来ます（きます）**. These are useful first patterns, not a complete classification algorithm.

## 2. Meaning in short scenes

**At a café.** <span lang="ja">水を 飲みます。</span> (みずを のみます。) — “I drink water / will drink water.” **飲む** is drinking, while **食べる** is eating. The object marker **を** identifies what is consumed.

**At the library.** <span lang="ja">本を 読みます。</span> (ほんを よみます。) — “I read / will read a book.” **読む** is reading written material; **見る** means seeing or watching. A person may **見る** a film but **読む** a book.

**On the way home.** <span lang="ja">家に 帰ります。</span> (いえに かえります。) — “I go home / will return home.” The dictionary form is **帰る**, which belongs to Group 1 even though it ends in -える. Do not assume every -える or -いる verb is Group 2.

**At the station.** <span lang="ja">友だちが 来ます。</span> (ともだちが きます。) — “A friend is coming / will come.” **来る（くる）** has the polite reading **きます**; it is not くります. Compare **行く（いく）→ 行きます（いきます）**, “to go.”

## 3. Three ways to remember the pairs

First, say the **meaning** from the dictionary form: <span lang="ja">書く</span> (かく) means “write.” Second, read the polite partner: <span lang="ja">書きます</span> (かきます). Third, notice the change: く → きます. Try **話す → 話します** (はなす → はなします), where す becomes します. This is not the irregular verb する; the two look similar in polite form but have different dictionary forms.

For **起きる → 起きます** (おきる → おきます), remove る. For **する → します**, memorize the pair. **Dictionary form** and **polite form** name the same action; the choice changes how the sentence is expressed, not the action itself.

## 4. Controlled retrieval

Cover the polite-form column. For each dictionary form, predict the polite form and then reveal it: **買う**, **読む**, **見る**, **帰る**, **来る**. Next cover the English meanings and retrieve “buy,” “read,” “see,” “return home,” and “come.”

Now compare these pairs: **読む／飲む** (read/drink), **行く／来る** (go/come), and **食べる／見る** (eat/see). Say what changes in meaning before thinking about verb groups.

## 5. Guided practice: the activity board

A fictional notice says:

> <span lang="ja">土曜日：本を 読みます。お茶を 飲みます。午後、映画を 見ます。</span>  
> どようび：ほんを よみます。おちゃを のみます。ごご、えいがを みます。  
> “Saturday: We read books. We drink tea. In the afternoon, we watch a film.”

Write the **dictionary forms** of the three activities: ______, ______, ______. Then answer: which activity involves a film? Which one involves a drink?

**Model:** 読む、飲む、見る. The film activity is **見る**; the drink activity is **飲む**. The polite notice uses 読みます、飲みます、見ます.

## 6. Independent use and review

Choose a fictional day and write three short polite Japanese sentences using actions from the table. You may use **本を 読みます。** (“I read a book”), **お茶を 飲みます。** (“I drink tea”), and **家に 帰ります。** (“I return home”) as models. Underline the polite verb in each sentence and write its dictionary form beside it. Check: (1) correct action meaning, (2) correct kana/kanji reading, (3) correct ます form, and (4) appropriate object or destination particle.

This lesson checks **written vocabulary and form recognition**. It does not include recorded listening, microphone practice, pronunciation verification, or a speaking grade. The next lesson develops verb-group rules more systematically.
