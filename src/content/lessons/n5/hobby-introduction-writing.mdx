## Your mission: introduce yourself to a hobby circle

A fictional community hobby circle asks new members for a short introduction. Write **two to five connected Japanese sentences** saying what you like, what you do not particularly like, and something you can do or are good at. This is a **writing and typing** lesson; no real personal details are needed. Invent a character if you prefer.

**Can-do:** Discuss likes, dislikes and broad abilities. This lesson moves from reading hobby profiles to producing a short connected introduction.

## Model A: a new club member

> はじめまして。私（わたし）はミオです。  
> 音楽（おんがく）が好（す）きです。とくに、歌（うた）を歌（うた）うのが好（す）きです。  
> でも、ダンスはあまり好（す）きではありません。  
> ピアノはあまり上手（じょうず）ではありません。

**English:** Nice to meet you. I'm Mio. I like music. I especially like singing. But I don't particularly like dancing. I'm not very good at piano.

**Notice the contrast:** 好き describes preference; 上手 describes skill. Someone can love singing without being good at it. **あまり** commonly pairs with a negative expression here to soften “not particularly.” **でも** links contrasting sentences.

## Model B: an activity exchange

> A：趣味（しゅみ）は何（なん）ですか。  
> B：本（ほん）を読（よ）むのが好（す）きです。  
> A：日本語（にほんご）で日記（にっき）を書（か）けますか。  
> B：はい、少（すこ）し書（か）けます。でも、まだ上手（じょうず）ではありません。

**English:** A: What is your hobby? B: I like reading books. A: Can you write a diary in Japanese? B: Yes, I can write a little. But I'm not good at it yet.

The question **書けますか** uses a potential verb form, included only for recognition. This chapter previews ability with **できます**: **日本語で日記を書くことができます** means “I can write a diary in Japanese.” You do not need to master potential forms now. The beginner-friendly contrast is **好き** (“like”) versus **上手** (“good at”) versus **できます** (“can do”).

## Build accurate sentences

| What you want to say | Japanese model | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I like music. | 音楽が好きです。 | A noun being liked takes が. |
| I like reading books. | 本を読むのが好きです。 | Verb phrase + の turns the activity into a noun-like unit. |
| I don't particularly like dancing. | ダンスはあまり好きではありません。 | は marks the contrasted topic; あまり + negative softens it. |
| I'm good at piano. | ピアノが上手です。 | Skill is different from preference. |
| I'm not good at singing. | 歌はあまり上手ではありません。 | A modest, polite self-description. |
| I can play piano. | ピアノを弾くことができます。 | Dictionary-form verb + ことができます expresses ability. |

**Grammar focus 1 — particle が:** **音楽が好きです** is the standard beginner pattern, not **音楽を好きです**. In **本を読むのが好きです**, the **を** belongs to **読む**, while **が** marks the *whole activity* being liked.

**Grammar focus 2 — activity + の:** **歌うのが好きです** means “I like singing.” Do not write **歌うが好きです**: insert **の** after the dictionary-form verb.

**Grammar focus 3 — negatives and tact:** **好きではありません** is a polite negative. **下手（へた）です** means “unskilled / bad at” and can sound blunt about another person. Prefer **まだ上手ではありません** when speaking modestly about your own skills. Never assume that liking something implies skill.

**Grammar focus 4 — ability preview:** **泳（およ）ぐことができます** = “I can swim.” **泳ぐのが上手です** = “I am good at swimming.” Being able to do an activity and being skilled at it are not the same claim. **できます** can also stand alone when the activity is clear from context.

## Guided writing ladder

1. **Open with a setting or identity.** Try **私はケンです。** (“I'm Ken.”) or **私の趣味は読書です。** (“My hobby is reading.”).
2. **State a preference.** Choose a noun: **音楽が好きです。** Or a verb phrase: **絵（え）を描（か）くのが好きです。** (“I like drawing pictures.”)
3. **Add a contrast.** **でも、ダンスはあまり好きではありません。** (“But I don't particularly like dancing.”)
4. **Describe skill without overclaiming.** **ピアノはまだ上手ではありません。** (“I'm not good at piano yet.”) Or **料理（りょうり）が上手です。** (“I'm good at cooking.”)
5. **Connect and proofread.** Use **とくに** (“especially”) or **でも** (“but”) where they fit the meaning. Keep your 2–5 sentences about the same fictional person.

### Repair these common mistakes

- **音楽を好きです。** → **音楽が好きです。** The liked noun takes **が** in this pattern.
- **本を読むが好きです。** → **本を読むのが好きです。** The action needs **の**.
- **ピアノが好きです** does **not** mean “I'm good at piano.” For that, use **ピアノが上手です**.
- **歌うことが上手です** is not the target pattern here; write **歌うのが上手です** or **歌が上手です**.
- **あまり好きです** does not express “don't particularly like.” Use **あまり好きではありません**.

## Independent writing challenge

Imagine you are joining the circle. Write **2–5 connected Japanese sentences** that include (1) one hobby you like, (2) a contrasting preference or dislike, and (3) one statement about skill or ability. You may use kana instead of unfamiliar kanji. Do not enter personal identifying details. Type into the draft box below and compare with the model **after** attempting your own sentences.

**Possible model (not the only answer):**

> 私（わたし）はユウです。  
> 本（ほん）を読（よ）むのが好（す）きです。  
> でも、ダンスはあまり好（す）きではありません。  
> 日本語（にほんご）で短（みじか）い文（ぶん）を書（か）くことができます。  
> まだ上手（じょうず）ではありませんが、毎日（まいにち）練習（れんしゅう）しています。

**English:** I'm Yuu. I like reading books. But I don't particularly like dancing. I can write short sentences in Japanese. I'm not good at it yet, but I practise every day.

The final model sentence contains **が** meaning “but,” a useful optional extension; you may write shorter sentences instead.

## Five-criterion revision rubric

Check **meaning** (your statements match your intended hobbies), **grammar** (が好き / が上手 / ことができます), **activity phrasing** (verb + のが好き), **tone** (polite, tactful skill descriptions) and **cohesion** (2–5 connected sentences with clear references). Creative writing is **self-reviewed, not machine-graded**. The separate eight-question recognition check below tests understanding of the models and grammar, not the quality of your composition.
