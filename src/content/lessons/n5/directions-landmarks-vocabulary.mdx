## Finding your way in Japanese

**Can-do:** Understand and give basic movement instructions. Imagine you are leaving a station and asking how to reach a post office. You need to recognize direction words and landmarks before you can follow the route. This lesson develops **written recognition and retrieval**; the later listening lesson uses authentic recorded instructions.

## 1. Your navigation word bank

| Japanese | Reading | English | What to notice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">右</span> | みぎ | right | Look for this word in a route |
| <span lang="ja">左</span> | ひだり | left | Look for this word in a route |
| <span lang="ja">まっすぐ</span> | まっすぐ | straight ahead | Look for this word in a route |
| <span lang="ja">道</span> | みち | road / way | Look for this word in a route |
| <span lang="ja">角</span> | かど | corner | A corner, not necessarily an intersection |
| <span lang="ja">交差点</span> | こうさてん | intersection | Roads meet or cross here |
| <span lang="ja">信号</span> | しんごう | traffic light | Look for this word in a route |
| <span lang="ja">横断歩道</span> | おうだんほどう | pedestrian crossing | Look for this word in a route |
| <span lang="ja">駅</span> | えき | station | Look for this word in a route |
| <span lang="ja">銀行</span> | ぎんこう | bank | Look for this word in a route |
| <span lang="ja">郵便局</span> | ゆうびんきょく | post office | Look for this word in a route |
| <span lang="ja">隣</span> | となり | next to | Usually linked to another place with の |

Read the words aloud for personal practice. **右** (みぎ) and **左** (ひだり) are opposites. **まっすぐ** is an adverb meaning “straight ahead,” not a place name. **道** (みち) means road or way, while **交差点** (こうさてん) is an intersection and **角** (かど) is a corner.

**Kanji reading strategy:** The kanji in **銀行** (ぎんこう) and **郵便局** (ゆうびんきょく) form whole place names. Learn each complete reading rather than guessing a pronunciation from one character. **信号** (しんごう) is a traffic signal; **横断歩道** (おうだんほどう) is a pedestrian crossing. Cross only where it is safe and permitted; the vocabulary is not a substitute for road-safety rules.

## 2. A short route from the station

**Visitor:** <span lang="ja">すみません。郵便局はどこですか。</span> (すみません。ゆうびんきょくは どこですか。) — “Excuse me. Where is the post office?”

**Local:** <span lang="ja">この道をまっすぐ行ってください。</span> (この みちを まっすぐ いってください。) — “Please go straight along this road.”

**Local:** <span lang="ja">信号を右に曲がってください。</span> (しんごうを みぎに まがってください。) — “Please turn right at the traffic light.”

**Local:** <span lang="ja">郵便局は銀行の隣にあります。</span> (ゆうびんきょくは ぎんこうの となりに あります。) — “The post office is next to the bank.”

**Visitor:** <span lang="ja">ありがとうございます。</span> — “Thank you.”

The visitor is seeking the **郵便局**, not the **銀行**. The bank is a reference landmark. The route is straight, then right at the signal. These are **fictional** directions, not real-world navigation advice.

## 3. Direction or destination?

A direction answers *which way?*; a landmark answers *what place?* Compare:

- <span lang="ja">右に曲がります。</span> (みぎに まがります。) — “Turn right” / “I turn right” (polite statement).
- <span lang="ja">左に曲がります。</span> (ひだりに まがります。) — “Turn left” / “I turn left.”
- <span lang="ja">まっすぐ行きます。</span> (まっすぐ いきます。) — “Go straight” / “I go straight.”
- <span lang="ja">駅の隣に銀行があります。</span> (えきの となりに ぎんこうが あります。) — “There is a bank next to the station.”

**〜にあります** tells you where an inanimate thing or place is located. **銀行の隣** means “next to the bank”; the の connects the reference place to 隣. Do not use **右** as a substitute for **隣**: “on the right” and “next to” are not the same relationship.

**Usage note:** In everyday instructions, <span lang="ja">右に曲がってください</span> is a polite request to turn right. The form **〜てください** is supplied here as a useful whole expression; a later lesson will explain how to form it.

## 4. Guided retrieval

Cover the Japanese column of the table. Say the Japanese for “left,” “traffic light,” “intersection,” “post office” and “next to.” Then reverse the exercise: cover the English column and explain **右**, **角**, **横断歩道**, **駅** and **銀行**. Do not assume that a word you recognize is already one you can retrieve without help.

Try these three route clues before reading the answers:

1. <span lang="ja">まっすぐ行きます。</span> — Which way? **Straight ahead.**
2. <span lang="ja">銀行の隣にあります。</span> — Relative to what? **The bank.**
3. <span lang="ja">信号を左に曲がります。</span> — Which direction at the signal? **Left.**

## 5. Independent use

Write three short Japanese sentences describing a **fictional** route from a station to a post office. Include one direction, one landmark and one location relationship. A possible model is:

> <span lang="ja">駅からまっすぐ行きます。信号を左に曲がります。郵便局は銀行の隣にあります。</span>
>
> (えきから まっすぐ いきます。しんごうを ひだりに まがります。ゆうびんきょくは ぎんこうの となりに あります。)
>
> “Go straight from the station. Turn left at the traffic light. The post office is next to the bank.”

Self-check: Did you distinguish 左 from 右? Is your landmark identifiable? Did you use の before 隣? Does にあります describe a location rather than an action? Are the three sentences consistent? Free writing is **not automatically graded**. Optionally read the model aloud for personal rehearsal; no recording or pronunciation evaluation is provided.

## Review

Direction words (**右**, **左**, **まっすぐ**) help someone move. Landmarks (**駅**, **銀行**, **郵便局**, **信号**) help them recognize where they are. **隣** describes a relationship between places. Test your written recall below, then move on to grammar for directions.
