## A question at the community art club

**Can-do:** Talk about a hobby you enjoy, describe another person's skill, and say whether you can do an activity. These are three *different* meanings, even when English conversations mix them together.

> 先生（せんせい）：みなさん、何（なに）が好（す）きですか。  
> アキ：私（わたし）は絵（え）が好（す）きです。でも、絵（え）はあまり上手（じょうず）ではありません。  
> 先生：そうですか。リナさんは？  
> リナ：私（わたし）は音楽（おんがく）が好（す）きです。ピアノが少（すこ）しできます。  
> アキ：リナさんはピアノが上手（じょうず）ですか。  
> リナ：いいえ、まだあまり上手（じょうず）ではありません。

**English:** Teacher: What do you all like? Aki: I like drawing/pictures. But I'm not very good at drawing. Teacher: I see. How about you, Rina? Rina: I like music. I can play piano a little. Aki: Are you good at piano? Rina: No, I'm not very good yet.

Notice that **アキ likes art but does not claim to be skilled**. Rina can play piano a little but does not claim to be very good. The dialogue is original and fictional.

## Step 1 — Likes are preferences

**Noun + が好きです**: 本（ほん）が好きです。 *I like books.*

好き（すき） is a **na-adjective**, not a Japanese transitive verb meaning “like.” The liked item commonly takes **が**. You may set a contrast with **は**: サッカーはあまり好きではありません。 *I don't like soccer very much.* The negative form is **好きではありません** (conversational **好きじゃありません** is also possible).

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 音楽が好きです。 | おんがくがすきです | I like music. |
| 絵が好きですか。 | えがすきですか | Do you like pictures/art? |
| 本はあまり好きではありません。 | ほんはあまりすきではありません | I don't like books very much. |
| スポーツが大好きです。 | スポーツがだいすきです | I really love sports. |

**Guided choice:** To ask what someone likes, choose **何が好きですか** rather than **何を好きですか** for the introductory pattern.

## Step 2 — 上手 and 下手 describe skill

**Noun + が上手です** describes being good at an activity; **noun + が下手です** describes being unskilled at it.

> ミカさんは料理（りょうり）が上手（じょうず）です。  
> *Mika is good at cooking.*

> 私（わたし）は歌（うた）があまり上手（じょうず）ではありません。  
> *I'm not very good at singing.*

> ケンさんはダンスが下手（へた）です。  
> *Ken is poor at dancing.*

**Pragmatic note:** 上手 is common when complimenting another person. Calling yourself **上手です** can sound boastful in an ordinary modest introduction; **まだあまり上手ではありません** (“I'm not very good yet”) is often more natural. **下手** can be blunt when said about someone else, so use care. **上手ではありません** is a gentler alternative.

**Form table:**

| Positive | Polite negative | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 好きです | 好きではありません | like / do not like |
| 上手です | 上手ではありません | skilled / not skilled |
| 下手です | 下手ではありません | unskilled / not unskilled |

All three work as **na-adjective predicates** with です. Do not conjugate them as if they were **い-adjectives**: **上手くないです** is not the beginner target form here (上手く is an adverbial form, but this lesson practices 上手ではありません).

## Step 3 — できます expresses ability

**できます** means “can do / is possible”; **できません** means “cannot.” With a familiar activity noun, **ピアノができます** means “can play piano.” The sentence **日本語（にほんご）が少しできます** can mean “I can use/speak a little Japanese.”

> 私はピアノができます。 *I can play piano.*  
> 私はピアノができません。 *I cannot play piano.*  
> 兄（あに）はテニスができます。 *My older brother can play tennis.*

**Be precise:** **できます** alone does not tell us whether the speaker enjoys the activity or performs it expertly. “I like piano” (**ピアノが好きです**), “I'm good at piano” (**ピアノが上手です**), and “I can play piano” (**ピアノができます**) answer different questions. A more general verb-ability construction (dictionary-form verb + ことができます) is for later study.

## Step 4 — Ask the matching question

| Question | Answer | What you learn |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 何が好きですか。 | 音楽が好きです。 | Preference |
| ピアノが上手ですか。 | いいえ、あまり上手ではありません。 | Skill |
| テニスができますか。 | はい、少しできます。 | Ability |

The polite question particle **か** turns the statement into a question. When the topic is clear, Japanese naturally omits 私は and repeated nouns.

**Guided rehearsal:** Read these in order, then decide which meaning each communicates: **絵が好きです** (preference), **絵が上手です** (skill), **絵ができます** (ability to do art/draw, depending on context). For a clear claim about drawing, **絵が描（か）けます** is more precise, but that potential form is beyond today's required production.

## Common mistakes to repair

1. **「音楽を好きです」** → **音楽が好きです**. In this beginner pattern, が marks the liked item.
2. **「私はピアノが好きです。だから、上手です。」** does not follow logically: liking something does not prove skill.
3. **「下手です」** is not the negative of 好きです. **好きではありません** expresses not liking; **下手です** expresses low skill.
4. **「ピアノができます」** does not mean “I am playing piano now.” **ピアノを弾（ひ）いています** expresses the ongoing action.

## Independent practice — a friendly introduction

Imagine joining a fictional hobby group. Write **three or four polite Japanese sentences**: one hobby you like, one ability you have or lack, and one gentle comment about your own skill. Invent all details; no personal information is needed.

**Possible model:**

> 私（わたし）は音楽（おんがく）が好（す）きです。ピアノが少（すこ）しできます。でも、まだあまり上手（じょうず）ではありません。友（とも）だちは歌（うた）が上手（じょうず）です。

**English:** I like music. I can play a little piano. But I'm not very good yet. My friend is good at singing.

**Self-check rubric (not automatically graded):** (1) Does が mark the liked hobby? (2) Is skill distinct from preference? (3) Is できます/できません used only for ability? (4) Are polite endings consistent? (5) Is the comment about another person's skill respectful? Your own natural alternatives may differ from the model.
