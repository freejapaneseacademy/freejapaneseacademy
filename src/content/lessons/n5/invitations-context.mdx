## After Japanese class: where shall we eat?

**Chapter can-do:** Invite someone and accept or decline simply. Today you will *interpret* an invitation and a decision in context. Later lessons teach you to produce these forms independently.

In this **original fictional written dialogue**, classmates **Mina**, **Haru**, and **Sora** are leaving a Japanese class on Saturday. They are deciding whether to eat lunch together. This is written reading material, **not a recording**.

| Speaker | Japanese | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mina | <span lang="ja">きょう、いっしょに 昼ごはんを 食べませんか。</span> | Would you like to eat lunch together today? |
| Haru | <span lang="ja">いいですね。食べましょう。</span> | Sounds good. Let's eat. |
| Sora | <span lang="ja">すみません。きょうは ちょっと……。また こんど。</span> | Sorry, today is a little difficult… Another time. |
| Mina | <span lang="ja">わかりました。じゃあ、ハルさん、どこで 食べましょうか。</span> | I understand. Well then, Haru, where shall we eat? |
| Haru | <span lang="ja">えきの ちかくの カフェは どうですか。</span> | How about the café near the station? |
| Mina | <span lang="ja">いいですね。そのカフェに します。</span> | Sounds good. I'll choose that café. |
| Haru | <span lang="ja">じゃあ、十二時に カフェで 会いましょう。</span> | Then let's meet at the café at twelve. |

**Reading support:** <span lang="ja">昼ごはん（ひるごはん）</span> = lunch; <span lang="ja">食べます（たべます）</span> = eat; <span lang="ja">駅（えき）</span> = station; <span lang="ja">近く（ちかく）</span> = nearby; <span lang="ja">十二時（じゅうにじ）</span> = twelve o'clock; <span lang="ja">会います（あいます）</span> = meet.

## 1. Who wants what?

Mina invites **both classmates** to lunch. Haru accepts; Sora politely declines for today. Mina and Haru then choose a café near the station and agree to meet there at noon. **Do not infer that Sora will join them:** <span lang="ja">また こんど</span> indicates another occasion, not acceptance of today's plan.

The setting matters. These are classmates making an informal plan in **polite Japanese**; no one is ordering food from a waiter yet. The invitation is a request for company, not a command.

## 2. Three patterns, three jobs

| Pattern | Example | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">〜ませんか</span> | <span lang="ja">いっしょに 食べませんか。</span> — Would you like to eat together? | Invites someone; despite the negative-looking form, it is not a statement that someone does not eat |
| <span lang="ja">〜ましょう</span> | <span lang="ja">食べましょう。</span> — Let's eat. | Suggests or agrees to a shared action |
| <span lang="ja">〜に します</span> | <span lang="ja">そのカフェに します。</span> — I'll choose that café. | Announces a choice among possibilities |

The **か** in <span lang="ja">食べませんか</span> marks an invitation question. <span lang="ja">食べません</span> alone can mean “do not eat / will not eat” in polite speech, but <span lang="ja">食べませんか</span> is commonly a friendly invitation. A response of <span lang="ja">いいですね</span> is positive here because Haru immediately adds <span lang="ja">食べましょう</span>.

**Important distinction:** <span lang="ja">そのカフェに します</span> chooses the café. It does **not** mean “I am going to the café” (which could be <span lang="ja">そのカフェに 行きます</span>). The verb <span lang="ja">します</span> here expresses a decision.

## 3. A gentle decline is still a decline

<span lang="ja">すみません。きょうは ちょっと……。</span> is a context-sensitive, indirect way to decline. Literally, <span lang="ja">ちょっと</span> means “a little,” but an unfinished response after an apology can suggest that the invitation is difficult to accept. Here, <span lang="ja">また こんど</span> (“another time”) confirms the intended decline. **Do not translate ちょっと alone as “no” in every situation.**

Compare these short responses:

- <span lang="ja">はい、行きましょう。</span> — Yes, let's go. **Acceptance.**
- <span lang="ja">すみません。きょうは 行けません。</span> — Sorry, I can't go today. **Explicit decline.** (The potential form <span lang="ja">行けません</span> is for recognition only here.)
- <span lang="ja">また こんど お願いします。</span> — Another time, please. **Defers the invitation.**

## 4. Read for evidence, not guesses

Ask yourself: **Who** proposed lunch? **Who** accepted? **Where** and **when** will the two classmates meet? Quote the shortest phrase supporting each answer.

| Question | Exact Japanese evidence | Conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who invites? | <span lang="ja">いっしょに 昼ごはんを 食べませんか</span> (Mina) | Mina proposes lunch together |
| Does Haru accept? | <span lang="ja">いいですね。食べましょう</span> | Yes |
| Does Sora join today? | <span lang="ja">きょうは ちょっと……。また こんど</span> | No |
| Which place? | <span lang="ja">そのカフェに します</span> | The café near the station |
| When to meet? | <span lang="ja">十二時に カフェで 会いましょう</span> | At noon, at the café |

Notice <span lang="ja">十二時に</span> marks a time and <span lang="ja">カフェで</span> marks the meeting location. These are context clues; full particle review comes later.

## 5. Guided rehearsal

**A.** Cover the English translations. Read Mina's first line and identify the invitation ending. Is she asking Haru and Sora to join her, or saying that she never eats lunch?

**B.** Read Sora's response and underline both <span lang="ja">すみません</span> and <span lang="ja">また こんど</span>. Why is it incorrect to count Sora as an attendee?

**C.** Change the plan while preserving the function. Replace <span lang="ja">そのカフェ</span> with <span lang="ja">このレストラン</span> (“this restaurant”) in <span lang="ja">このレストランに します</span> (“I'll choose this restaurant”). This is a decision, not an invitation.

## 6. Try it yourself

Imagine you are meeting a classmate after class. Write **two or three short Japanese lines**: one invitation, one acceptance *or* polite decline, and one choice of place if the invitation is accepted. You may reuse the sentence frames below; your work is **not automatically graded**.

- <span lang="ja">いっしょに 昼ごはんを 食べませんか。</span> — Would you like to have lunch together?
- <span lang="ja">いいですね。食べましょう。</span> — Sounds good. Let's eat.
- <span lang="ja">このカフェに します。</span> — I'll choose this café.

**Self-check:** Is the invitation a question? Does the reply clearly accept or decline? Is <span lang="ja">に します</span> used for a choice rather than movement? Does each Japanese line mean what your English gloss says?

**Optional speaking:** Read Mina and Haru's lines aloud at a comfortable pace. This is an ungraded rehearsal; no recording, audio model, or pronunciation verification is supplied in this written L01 lesson.
