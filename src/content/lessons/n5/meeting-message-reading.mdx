## Can-do: Read a plan and find the evidence

A classmate sends a message about meeting after class. By the end of this lesson, you can find **the day, time, meeting point, and later destination** in an original Japanese exchange. This is **reading practice**: no audio or listening score is implied. All names, places, and messages below are fictional.

### Before you read: a compact word guide

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 土曜日 | どようび | Saturday |
| 午後 | ごご | afternoon / p.m. |
| 二時 | にじ | two o'clock |
| 学校 | がっこう | school |
| 図書館 | としょかん | library |
| 前 | まえ | in front of |
| 駅 | えき | station |
| 喫茶店 | きっさてん | coffee shop |
| そのあと | そのあと | after that |
| ちょっと | ちょっと | a little; in a refusal, often a soft hesitation |

Read the whole word, not each kanji in isolation. **学校の前** means *in front of the school*. **図書館の前** means *in front of the library*. In the messages, **で** marks where the action of meeting takes place, while **に** can mark a specific time.

## 1. Original meeting-message exchange

Read once without looking at the English guide. Notice who proposes a plan, who cannot come, and what the two remaining classmates finally agree to.

> **あや → けん、みほ**  
> **土曜日の午後二時に、学校の前で会いませんか。**  
> **そのあと、駅の近くの喫茶店に行きましょう。**
>
> **けん → あや、みほ**  
> **いいですね。でも、学校の前は人が多いです。図書館の前はどうですか。**
>
> **みほ → あや、けん**  
> **すみません。土曜日はちょっと……。また今度お願いします。**
>
> **あや → けん**  
> **わかりました。では、土曜日の午後二時に図書館の前で会いましょう。**
>
> **けん → あや**  
> **はい。喫茶店は「さくら」にしましょう。**

### English meaning, message by message

**Aya:** Would you like to meet in front of the school at 2 p.m. on Saturday? After that, let's go to a coffee shop near the station.

**Ken:** Sounds good. But there are many people in front of the school. How about in front of the library?

**Miho:** Sorry. Saturday is a little difficult… Another time, please.

**Aya:** Understood. Then let's meet in front of the library at 2 p.m. on Saturday.

**Ken:** Yes. Let's choose the coffee shop “Sakura.”

The translation is a reading aid. For the questions, go back to the **Japanese evidence**.

## 2. Read in two passes

**First pass — find the plan.** Look for the date/time expression **土曜日の午後二時**, the meeting verb **会いましょう**, and the location followed by **で**. Do not confuse the café destination, marked by **に行きましょう**, with the meeting point.

**Second pass — track changes.** Aya first suggests **学校の前**. Ken proposes **図書館の前** because **学校の前は人が多いです**. Aya's later message confirms **図書館の前で会いましょう**. The *last confirmed meeting place* is the library entrance, not the school.

**Track participants.** Miho writes **土曜日はちょっと……。また今度お願いします**. This is a context-dependent polite decline, not an acceptance. Aya then addresses only Ken; the final meeting plan is between Aya and Ken.

## 3. Evidence notebook

| Question | Evidence in the Japanese message | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| When do they meet? | **土曜日の午後二時に** | Saturday at 2 p.m. |
| Where do they finally meet? | **図書館の前で会いましょう** | In front of the library |
| Why change the place? | **学校の前は人が多いです** | The school front is crowded |
| Who cannot attend? | **土曜日はちょっと……** (Miho's message) | Miho |
| Which café is chosen? | **喫茶店は「さくら」にしましょう** | Sakura |
| Is Sakura the meeting point? | **そのあと…喫茶店に行きましょう** | No, they go afterward |

**Reference words:** **そのあと** points to the time *after the meeting*. **では** signals Aya's response to Ken's suggested change. **「さくら」** is the name of the café, not the name of a participant.

## 4. Common reading traps

- **First plan vs. final plan:** The first place (**学校の前**) is superseded by **図書館の前**. Always read the replies.
- **Invitation vs. decision:** **会いませんか** asks *Would you like to meet?*; **会いましょう** confirms *Let's meet*. Neither automatically tells you that every recipient agreed.
- **Meeting place vs. destination:** **図書館の前で会う** means meet in front of the library; **喫茶店に行く** means go to the café. The verbs and particles matter.
- **Soft decline:** **土曜日はちょっと……** does not literally say “I cannot,” but with **すみません** and **また今度**, it communicates a polite refusal.

## 5. Guided practice

Cover the answers above and find the exact line that proves each statement:

1. **Ken changes the proposed meeting point.** Locate **図書館の前はどうですか**.
2. **Aya accepts the change.** Locate **図書館の前で会いましょう**.
3. **Miho does not join.** Locate **また今度お願いします**.
4. **The café is chosen after the meeting plan.** Locate **喫茶店は「さくら」にしましょう**.

Now complete the interactive evidence questions. Choose an answer **only when you can point to a phrase** in the original Japanese.

## 6. Independent writing and optional speaking

Write a **two- to four-sentence fictional reply** to Aya as Ken. State a meeting time and place, and make a café choice. Example:

> 土曜日の午後二時に会いましょう。  
> 図書館の前で会いましょう。  
> 喫茶店は「さくら」にしましょう。

Self-review your draft: Does the time match the plan? Is the meeting location distinct from the later café? Does **に** mark the time and **で** mark the place where people meet? Is **にしましょう** used for choosing the café? Free writing is **not automatically graded**.

You may optionally read the message aloud and compare your phrasing to the printed model. This lesson provides no recorded voice, microphone assessment or pronunciation score. The chapter's L06 handles authentic listening separately.

## Recap

To read an invitation exchange, follow **who speaks**, **what changes**, and **which statement is final**. Cite the Japanese words that prove your answer. This skill transfers to everyday group messages, class plans and simple invitations.
