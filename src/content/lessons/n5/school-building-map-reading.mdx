## A visitor needs to find the right room

**Can-do:** Say where common places and objects are. In this reading lesson you will follow a fictional school's **two-floor building map** and a short written information sheet. You can answer from labels even if you cannot yet give complex directions. All information here is invented for practice; this is not a real school map.

Read the map **before** answering. The words <span lang="ja">一階</span> (いっかい, first floor) and <span lang="ja">二階</span> (にかい, second floor) are important: do not confuse a room number with a floor number.

## 1. Original building map: さくら学校

The following is a **text map**, not a scale drawing. Each column is a labeled location; the middle column shows the connecting corridor and stairs. The entrance is on the first floor. The positions are fixed facts of this fictional map.

| Floor | West side (西) | Middle (中央) | East side (東) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **二階 (にかい)** | **図書室** (としょしつ, library room) | **階段・廊下** (かいだん・ろうか, stairs and corridor) | **教室** (きょうしつ, classroom) |
| **一階 (いっかい)** | **保健室** (ほけんしつ, health room) | **入口・階段・廊下** (いりぐち・かいだん・ろうか, entrance, stairs and corridor) | **事務室** (じむしつ, office) |

**Map key:** <span lang="ja">西</span> (にし) = west; <span lang="ja">中央</span> (ちゅうおう) = center; <span lang="ja">東</span> (ひがし) = east. These are **labels on the map**, not instructions to turn. <span lang="ja">入口</span> (entrance) and <span lang="ja">出口</span> (exit) are different words; this map labels an entrance but **does not label an exit**.

## 2. Read the written building guide

> **さくら学校の あんない** (Sakura School guide)
>
> <span lang="ja">入口は 一階の 中央です。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">事務室は 一階の 東です。保健室は 一階の 西です。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">図書室は 二階の 西です。教室は 二階の 東です。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">階段は 一階と 二階に あります。</span>

**Line-by-line reading support:**

- <span lang="ja">入口は 一階の 中央です。</span> — The entrance is in the center of the first floor.
- <span lang="ja">事務室は 一階の 東です。</span> — The office is on the east side of the first floor.
- <span lang="ja">保健室は 一階の 西です。</span> — The health room is on the west side of the first floor.
- <span lang="ja">図書室は 二階の 西です。</span> — The library room is on the west side of the second floor.
- <span lang="ja">教室は 二階の 東です。</span> — The classroom is on the east side of the second floor.
- <span lang="ja">階段は 一階と 二階に あります。</span> — There are stairs on the first and second floors.

Notice how **の** links a floor and a location: <span lang="ja">二階の 西</span> means “the west side of the second floor.” In the last line, **に** marks the location of the stairs with **あります**. In the shorter location sentence <span lang="ja">図書室は 二階の 西です</span>, no に is needed before です.

## 3. Find facts, not guesses

A visitor asks: <span lang="ja">図書室は どこですか。</span> (“Where is the library room?”) You can answer <span lang="ja">二階の 西です。</span> (“On the west side of the second floor.”) **Evidence:** <span lang="ja">図書室は 二階の 西です。</span> The map and guide agree.

Another visitor asks: “Where is the cafeteria?” The answer is **not stated**. There is no 食堂 label on this map. A school might have a cafeteria, but this document does not tell us where it is. In reading comprehension, “not shown” is better than inventing a floor.

**A reference trap:** <span lang="ja">ここ</span> means “here” from the speaker's viewpoint; <span lang="ja">そこ</span> often means “there” near the listener or a pointed-to location; <span lang="ja">あそこ</span> means “over there,” away from both. The static map does **not** tell us where the speaker is standing, so it does not establish whether the library is ここ, そこ or あそこ. Use the explicit floor and side instead.

## 4. Guided reading: three visitors

**Visitor A** wants to talk to office staff. Read <span lang="ja">事務室は 一階の 東です。</span> The correct destination is the **first-floor east side**. The evidence is the word <span lang="ja">事務室</span> together with <span lang="ja">一階の 東</span>.

**Visitor B** needs a book. Read <span lang="ja">図書室は 二階の 西です。</span> The destination is the **second-floor west side**. Do not confuse 図書室 with 教室; both are on floor two, but on different sides.

**Visitor C** needs the health room. Read <span lang="ja">保健室は 一階の 西です。</span> The destination is the **first-floor west side**. A second-floor west answer would locate the library instead.

## 5. A mini-dialogue using the map

> A: <span lang="ja">すみません。教室は どこですか。</span> — Excuse me. Where is the classroom?
>
> B: <span lang="ja">二階の 東です。</span> — On the east side of the second floor.
>
> A: <span lang="ja">ありがとうございます。</span> — Thank you.

This is a **written model**. It does not claim a real recording or pronunciation assessment. The map gives a location, not a turn-by-turn route. Do not infer a particular staircase path beyond the fact that stairs appear on both floors.

## 6. Independent practice

Without looking at the English support, cover the guide and answer in Japanese: (a) Where is the office? (b) Where is the library? (c) Which floor has the entrance? (d) Where is the cafeteria? Then uncover the map and cite the Japanese label or sentence supporting each answer.

**Model responses:** (a) <span lang="ja">一階の 東です。</span> (b) <span lang="ja">二階の 西です。</span> (c) <span lang="ja">一階です。</span> (d) <span lang="ja">地図に 書いてありません。</span> (“It is not written on the map.”) The last model is a useful reading response, not a claim that the cafeteria does not exist.

## Review and transfer

Read **floor first, then side**, distinguish **図書室** from **教室**, and use the **exact written evidence**. Answer <span lang="ja">どこですか</span> with a location, but do not guess a speaker's viewpoint from a map. The ten questions below assess written reading comprehension, not listening, speaking or map-navigation speed.
