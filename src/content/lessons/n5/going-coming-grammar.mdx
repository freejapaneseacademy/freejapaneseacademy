## Your mission: explain a Saturday journey

Mei and Haru are planning a library visit. You will learn how Japanese distinguishes **going somewhere**, **coming toward a reference point**, and **returning to a familiar base**. You will also say **where** and **how** a person travels. This is written grammar practice; sound recordings are not required or assessed here.

By the end, describe a trip in three short sentences, then explain why each particle fits. Review the destination and transport words from [L02](/learn/n5/module-05/chapter-01/lesson-02/) first if needed.

## 1. Three verbs, three viewpoints

| Japanese | Kana | Basic function | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">行きます</span> | いきます | go to a destination | <span lang="ja">図書館へ行きます。</span> — I go to the library. |
| <span lang="ja">来ます</span> | きます | come toward the speaker or another established reference point | <span lang="ja">友だちが学校に来ます。</span> — My friend comes to school. |
| <span lang="ja">帰ります</span> | かえります | return to a home/base or previous place | <span lang="ja">家に帰ります。</span> — I go home / return home. |

**Important viewpoint:** <span lang="ja">来ます</span> does not simply mean “go” with a different word. Imagine Mei is **at the school**: <span lang="ja">ハルさんは学校に来ます。</span> (Haru comes to the school) describes Haru moving toward Mei's location. If Mei is at home discussing Haru's trip away to the school, <span lang="ja">学校に行きます</span> (goes to school) is usually more appropriate. Japanese can also use 来る with a reference point established by the conversation, not only the speaker's physical position.

**Return** has a destination too. <span lang="ja">家に帰ります</span> (いえ／うちに かえります) means “return home”; <span lang="ja">学校に帰ります</span> can mean returning to school if school is the relevant base. For a first-time visit to a new library, choose <span lang="ja">図書館に行きます</span>, not 帰ります.

## 2. Destination: へ and に

In ordinary movement sentences, **place + へ + movement verb** and **place + に + movement verb** can both indicate a destination:

- <span lang="ja">公園へ行きます。</span> (こうえんへ いきます。) — I go to the park.
- <span lang="ja">公園に行きます。</span> (こうえんに いきます。) — I go to the park.
- <span lang="ja">学校に来ます。</span> (がっこうに きます。) — [Someone] comes to school.
- <span lang="ja">家へ帰ります。</span> (いえへ かえります。) — I return home.

**へ is pronounced え** when it is the particle. It tends to highlight direction toward a place; **に** often highlights the destination as an endpoint. Both are natural with these movement verbs, so do not mark a learner wrong merely for choosing one where either is possible. **に** also has other functions (for example, marking time); context matters.

## 3. Means of transport: で

To say how you travel, use **vehicle + で**:

- <span lang="ja">バスで図書館へ行きます。</span> (バスで としょかんへ いきます。) — I go to the library by bus.
- <span lang="ja">電車で学校に来ます。</span> (でんしゃで がっこうに きます。) — [Someone] comes to school by train.
- <span lang="ja">自転車で家に帰ります。</span> (じてんしゃで いえに かえります。) — I return home by bicycle.

The particle **で** here indicates the means of travel. It is not the destination marker. Compare <span lang="ja">駅へ行きます</span> (“go to the station”) with <span lang="ja">電車で行きます</span> (“go by train”). They answer different questions.

**On foot:** <span lang="ja">歩いて行きます。</span> (あるいて いきます。 — “I go on foot”) uses a verb form. Do **not** say <span lang="ja">歩いてで行きます</span>. <span lang="ja">徒歩で行きます</span> (とほで いきます) is another possible expression, but the two patterns are not identical word-for-word.

## 4. A new mini-dialogue: where is the speaker?

Mei is waiting **at the library**; Haru is still at home.

> <span lang="ja">メイ：ハルさん、何時に図書館に来ますか。</span>  
> めい：ハルさん、なんじに としょかんに きますか。  
> Mei: “Haru, what time will you come to the library?”
>
> <span lang="ja">ハル：二時に行きます。バスで行きます。</span>  
> はる：にじに いきます。バスで いきます。  
> Haru: “I'll go at two. I'll go by bus.”
>
> <span lang="ja">メイ：わかりました。私は六時に家に帰ります。</span>  
> めい：わかりました。わたしは ろくじに いえに かえります。  
> Mei: “Understood. I'll return home at six.”

**Why do they use different verbs?** Mei says **来ますか** because Haru will move toward the library, where Mei is waiting. Haru says **行きます** because he is departing from home for the library. Both describe the *same trip from different viewpoints*. The time expressions <span lang="ja">二時に</span> and <span lang="ja">六時に</span> use に for a clock time, whereas <span lang="ja">図書館に</span> and <span lang="ja">家に</span> use に for destinations.

## 5. Common errors and corrections

| Avoid | Better | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">バスへ行きます。</span> (when you mean “by bus”) | <span lang="ja">バスで行きます。</span> | で marks transport means; へ would mean toward the bus. |
| <span lang="ja">図書館で行きます。</span> (when you mean “to the library”) | <span lang="ja">図書館に行きます。</span> | The library is the destination, not the vehicle. |
| <span lang="ja">歩いてで行きます。</span> | <span lang="ja">歩いて行きます。</span> | 歩いて already expresses walking as the manner. |
| <span lang="ja">新しい店に帰ります。</span> (first visit) | <span lang="ja">新しい店に行きます。</span> | 帰ります implies returning to a relevant base or previous place. |

These corrections depend on the stated meanings. <span lang="ja">バスへ行きます</span> can be grammatical when you literally walk toward a bus, and <span lang="ja">図書館で</span> is natural for an action *at* a library, such as <span lang="ja">図書館で勉強します</span> (“study at the library”).

## 6. Guided practice before the quiz

Choose the role of each underlined expression:

1. <span lang="ja">電車で公園へ行きます。</span> — 電車で = **means**; 公園へ = **destination**.
2. <span lang="ja">学校に来ます。</span> — 学校に = **destination toward a reference point**.
3. <span lang="ja">家に帰ります。</span> — 家に = **return destination**.
4. <span lang="ja">三時に駅へ行きます。</span> — 三時に = **clock time**; 駅へ = **destination**.

Cover the answers and retrieve them again. Then complete the interactive twelve-item practice. Questions deliberately use context to distinguish **行きます** and **来ます**; there are no trick questions treating へ and に as mutually exclusive where both work.

## 7. Independent writing and optional speaking

Write **three connected sentences** about a fictional Saturday outing: (1) where you will go, (2) how you will travel, and (3) where you will return. Use a Japanese keyboard or IME. Example:

<span lang="ja">土曜日に公園へ行きます。自転車で行きます。午後、家に帰ります。</span>  
どようびに こうえんへ いきます。じてんしゃで いきます。ごご、いえに かえります。  
“On Saturday I'll go to the park. I'll go by bicycle. In the afternoon I'll return home.”

Self-review: Is the destination marked with へ or に? Is the means marked with で? Does 帰ります really mean a return? Is the viewpoint for 来ます established if you use it? Can a reader follow your plan? Several different natural responses are possible, so free writing is **not automatically graded**. You may optionally read your plan aloud; this page does not record or evaluate pronunciation.

**Takeaway:** place + **へ／に** tells where movement ends; vehicle + **で** tells how; **行きます／来ます／帰ります** convey going, coming toward a reference point, and returning. Review any missed patterns later. Independent qualified Japanese and assessment reviews remain pending.
