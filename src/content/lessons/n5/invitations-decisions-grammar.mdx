## A lunchtime decision

You and a classmate have finished studying. You want to invite them to lunch, agree on an activity and choose a place. This chapter's Can-do is **invite someone and accept or decline simply**.

> あき：いっしょに昼ご飯（ひるごはん）を食（た）べませんか。  
> Aki: Would you like to have lunch together?
>
> みお：いいですね。食べましょう。  
> Mio: Sounds good. Let's eat.
>
> あき：カフェとレストラン、どちらがいいですか。  
> Aki: Which would you prefer, the café or the restaurant?
>
> みお：カフェにします。  
> Mio: I'll choose the café.

Notice the progression: **invitation → agreement → decision**. The three expressions do different jobs.

## 1. Invite politely: 〜ませんか

Start with a polite **ます** verb. Replace **ます** with **ませんか** to invite someone to do something together. The negative-looking ending is a conventional invitation here, not simply a statement that the action will not happen.

| Dictionary idea | Polite verb | Invitation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| eat | 食べます（たべます） | 食べませんか（たべませんか） | Would you like to eat? |
| drink | 飲みます（のみます） | 飲みませんか（のみませんか） | Would you like to drink? |
| go | 行きます（いきます） | 行きませんか（いきませんか） | Would you like to go? |
| see/watch | 見ます（みます） | 見ませんか（みませんか） | Would you like to watch/see? |

**いっしょに** means *together*. For example, **いっしょにカフェに行きませんか。** (Would you like to go to a café together?) The destination uses **に** here. **いっしょにお茶（ちゃ）を飲みませんか。** means *Would you like to have tea together?*

**Common mistake:** 食べますか asks *Do you eat?/Will you eat?* depending on context; **食べませんか** is a natural polite invitation. They are not interchangeable in every conversation.

## 2. Accept and propose: 〜ましょう

Replace the **ます** ending with **ましょう** to suggest doing an action together or to accept an invitation:

- **行きましょう。**（いきましょう。）— Let's go.
- **食べましょう。**（たべましょう。）— Let's eat.
- **お茶を飲みましょう。**（おちゃをのみましょう。）— Let's have tea.

An affirmative reply can be **いいですね。行きましょう。** — *That sounds good. Let's go.* **はい、行きます。** (*Yes, I will go*) can also convey agreement, but **行きましょう** explicitly frames the action as shared.

A simple, considerate refusal is **すみません。今日はちょっと……。** — *Sorry, today is a little difficult…* The unfinished **ちょっと** gently signals reluctance; it is not a literal explanation of how much time is available. For a clear refusal, **すみません。今日は行けません。** — *Sorry, I can't go today* — uses a potential form for recognition; you need not produce it yet. Do not answer an invitation with **行きませんか** if you mean “No”; that would sound like another invitation.

## 3. Choose: noun + にします

After comparing options, **[noun] にします** means *I'll choose / I'll go with [noun]*.

- **カフェにします。** — I'll choose the café.
- **このレストランにします。** — I'll go with this restaurant.
- **お茶にします。** — I'll have tea (as my choice).

In a restaurant, **カレーにします。** can mean *I'll have curry*. This **にします** marks a decision, **not** a destination. Compare:

| Japanese | Function | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| カフェに行きませんか。 | Invite | Shall we go to a café? |
| カフェに行きましょう。 | Shared proposal | Let's go to a café. |
| カフェにします。 | Decide | I'll choose the café. |

The identical particle **に** appears in the first and third examples, but the verb changes its role: destination with **行く**, chosen option with **する**.

## 4. A second scene: a polite decline

> けん：土曜日（どようび）にレストランに行きませんか。  
> Ken: Would you like to go to a restaurant on Saturday?
>
> りな：すみません。土曜日はちょっと……。  
> Rina: Sorry, Saturday is a little difficult…
>
> けん：そうですか。じゃあ、日曜日（にちようび）はどうですか。  
> Ken: I see. Then how about Sunday?
>
> りな：いいですね。日曜日にしましょう。  
> Rina: Sounds good. Let's make it Sunday.

**日曜日にしましょう** means *Let's settle on Sunday*; here the **にしましょう** form proposes a shared choice, while **にします** announces the speaker's own decision. This extension helps you understand the dialogue; the core production target remains **にします**.

## 5. Guided substitutions

Try these without looking at the solutions, then check the bold forms.

1. Change **いっしょに食べます** to a polite invitation: **いっしょに食べませんか。**
2. Respond positively to **お茶を飲みませんか。**: **いいですね。飲みましょう。**
3. Choose the restaurant, not the café: **レストランにします。**
4. Decline a Saturday invitation gently: **すみません。土曜日はちょっと……。**
5. Invite a friend to see a movie: **いっしょに映画（えいが）を見ませんか。**

## 6. Write and rehearse (not automatically graded)

Imagine that a friend wants to meet for lunch. Write **three or four short Japanese lines**: one invitation, an acceptance or gentle refusal, and a decision between a café and restaurant. You may use the fictional names あき and みお.

Model:

> あき：いっしょに昼ご飯を食べませんか。  
> みお：いいですね。食べましょう。  
> あき：カフェとレストラン、どちらがいいですか。  
> みお：レストランにします。

**Self-check:** Is your invitation in **ませんか**? Does **ましょう** refer to a shared action? Does **にします** select a noun? Does a refusal remain considerate? Read the lines aloud if you wish, but this written lesson does not contain or score recorded speech.

## Quick recap

**食べませんか** invites; **食べましょう** proposes or accepts shared action; **カフェにします** makes a choice. Check the activity below, then return to the writing task and create your own exchange.
