## Read the details that make a journey possible

**Can-do:** Plan a simple outing and read short itinerary details. A schedule can contain several numbers, places and prices. Kanji help you recognize which number is a *time*, which word is a *station*, and which amount is a *fare*. This is written character study; it does not test pronunciation from audio.

## 1. Ten useful characters in real words

The readings below belong to the **whole words**. A kanji can have other readings in other words, so do not assume one character always makes the same sound.

| Kanji | Word and kana reading | Meaning in this context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">日</span> | <span lang="ja">日曜日</span> (にちようび) | Sunday |
| <span lang="ja">時</span> | <span lang="ja">九時</span> (くじ) | nine o'clock |
| <span lang="ja">分</span> | <span lang="ja">十分</span> (じゅっぷん) | ten minutes |
| <span lang="ja">午</span> | <span lang="ja">午前</span> (ごぜん) / <span lang="ja">午後</span> (ごご) | a.m. / p.m. |
| <span lang="ja">前</span> | <span lang="ja">駅前</span> (えきまえ) | in front of the station |
| <span lang="ja">駅</span> | <span lang="ja">駅</span> (えき) | station |
| <span lang="ja">電</span> | <span lang="ja">電車</span> (でんしゃ) | train |
| <span lang="ja">車</span> | <span lang="ja">電車</span> (でんしゃ) / <span lang="ja">車</span> (くるま) | train / car |
| <span lang="ja">行</span> | <span lang="ja">行きます</span> (いきます) | go |
| <span lang="ja">円</span> | <span lang="ja">三百円</span> (さんびゃくえん) | 300 yen |

These are *lesson-selected* everyday characters, not an official JLPT character list. Notice that **午** is shared by 午前 and 午後, but the second character distinguishes before and after noon. **前** also appears in 駅前 with the concrete meaning “in front of.”

## 2. Read a time, not just a number

<span lang="ja">午前九時十分</span> (ごぜん くじ じゅっぷん) is **9:10 a.m.**. The sequence is **午前/午後 + hour + 時 + minutes + 分**. A written schedule might instead show **9:10**, but the characters explain the units.

Compare <span lang="ja">午前九時</span> (ごぜん くじ, 9:00 a.m.) with <span lang="ja">午後九時</span> (ごご くじ, 9:00 p.m.). Changing 前 to 後 changes the time of day. Here **後** (ご) is a useful contrast character; today's ten-character recognition set centers on 午 and 前.

Minutes do not always have a single fixed reading: <span lang="ja">十分</span> is **じゅっぷん**, while <span lang="ja">五分</span> is **ごふん**. Read the whole time expression rather than mechanically attaching ふん to every number.

## 3. Recognize stations, vehicles and prices

<span lang="ja">駅前で会います。</span> (えきまえで あいます。) — “We meet in front of the station.” The location of the meeting uses **で**.

<span lang="ja">電車で行きます。</span> (でんしゃで いきます。) — “We go by train.” Here **で** expresses the means of travel. The same particle can mark a place where an action happens or the means used; the noun and verb provide the context.

<span lang="ja">切符は三百円です。</span> (きっぷは さんびゃくえんです。) — “The ticket is 300 yen.” **円** is the currency unit. A number without 円 may be a time, a quantity or something else.

The character **車** is read しゃ in 電車 but くるま when used as the standalone word 車. This is why learning **words in context** is more reliable than memorizing a single reading for each character.

## 4. Original fictional outing card

> **日曜日の電車旅行**
>
> <span lang="ja">集合：午前九時、駅前</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">出発：午前九時十分</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">到着：午前十時</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">行き先：市立美術館</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">電車の運賃：片道三百円</span>

Reading support: **日曜日** (にちようび) Sunday; **電車旅行** (でんしゃりょこう) train trip; **集合** (しゅうごう) meeting; **出発** (しゅっぱつ) departure; **到着** (とうちゃく) arrival; **行き先** (いきさき) destination; **市立美術館** (しりつびじゅつかん) municipal art museum; **運賃** (うんちん) fare; **片道** (かたみち) one way.

English: “Sunday train trip. Meet at 9:00 a.m. in front of the station. Depart at 9:10 a.m. Arrive at 10:00 a.m. Destination: municipal art museum. Train fare: 300 yen one way.” This is an invented classroom notice, **not** a real service or price.

Look for **時** and **分** to separate 9:00 from 9:10. Look for **円** to find the fare, and **駅前** to locate the meeting point. **行き先** is the destination, not the departure point.

## 5. Guided character retrieval

Without looking at the table, find the kanji for station (**駅**), train (**電車**), yen (**円**) and o'clock (**時**). Then reread the card and underline the exact phrase supporting each fact: meeting place, departure time, arrival time and one-way fare.

For handwriting, copy **駅・時・円** slowly while saying the whole example word to yourself. Stroke order is not graded here and no stroke animation is implied. For typing, use a Japanese IME to enter **えき**, **でんしゃ** and **さんびゃくえん**, selecting the appropriate conversion; plain kana is a valid practice stage.

## 6. Independent writing and optional speaking

Write **three connected sentences** about a different fictional outing: a meeting time and place, transport to a destination, and a fare or arrival time. Model:

> <span lang="ja">土曜日の午前八時に駅前で会います。電車で公園に行きます。切符は二百円です。</span>
>
> (どようびの ごぜん はちじに えきまえで あいます。でんしゃで こうえんに いきます。きっぷは にひゃくえんです。)
>
> “We meet in front of the station at 8 a.m. Saturday. We go to the park by train. The ticket is 200 yen.”

Self-check: (1) the time includes 時; (2) the meeting place is clear; (3) the transport uses で; (4) 円 follows a fare amount; (5) the details form a plausible plan. Writing is **ungraded**; compare it with the model, not an invented automated correctness score. You may read the model aloud as optional rehearsal; no recording or pronunciation verification is provided.

## Review

You can now identify times, transport, station locations and prices from meaningful kanji-containing words. Complete the written character check below. Next, apply the same recognition to a new travel notice in L05. Authentic itinerary listening belongs to L06.
