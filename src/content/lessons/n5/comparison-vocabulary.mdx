## Mission: choose a place for the club meeting

Two students are deciding between a fictional **small café** and a **large library room** for their study club. Your goal is to understand the words used to compare options, not to memorize a ranking of real places. Read the Japanese first; use the kana readings and translations when needed.

## Word bank: adjectives and choices

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning and usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 大きい | おおきい | big; an い-adjective |
| 小さい | ちいさい | small; an い-adjective |
| 新しい | あたらしい | new; an い-adjective |
| 古い | ふるい | old; an い-adjective; not necessarily bad |
| 高い | たかい | expensive / high; context decides |
| 安い | やすい | inexpensive; not automatically poor quality |
| 近い | ちかい | near; often with a reference point |
| 遠い | とおい | far |
| 静か（な） | しずか（な） | quiet; a な-adjective before a noun |
| にぎやか（な） | にぎやか（な） | lively/bustling; a な-adjective |
| 便利（な） | べんり（な） | convenient; a な-adjective |
| どちら | どちら | which of two (polite/neutral) |
| より | より | than; marks the comparison reference |
| ほうが | ほうが | the option described as more ... |
| 一番 | いちばん | most / number one within a set |

**Read the adjective carefully.** 静かな部屋（しずかなへや） means “a quiet room,” but 部屋は静かです means “the room is quiet.” Do not add な before です. Likewise, 大きい部屋 means “a big room,” not 大きな部屋 as an automatic substitution in this beginner pattern (大きな exists, but is a different attributive form).

## Three useful comparison frames

**AとBと、どちらが adjective ですか。** asks which of two options has the quality. Example:

- カフェと図書館（としょかん）と、どちらが静か（しずか）ですか。 — *Which is quieter, the café or the library?*
- 図書館のほうが静かです。 — *The library is quieter.*

**AはBより adjective です。** explicitly names the comparison reference:

- 図書館はカフェより静かです。 — *The library is quieter than the café.*
- カフェは図書館よりにぎやかです。 — *The café is livelier than the library.*

The option before **は** is being described. The option before **より** is the reference. Reversing them changes the claim. **AのほうがBより adjective** can also combine the two markers: 図書館のほうがカフェより静かです.

**一番** names the highest degree *within a stated or understood group*, not necessarily a universal fact:

- この三（みっ）つの部屋（へや）の中（なか）で、図書館の部屋が一番大きいです。 — *Among these three rooms, the library room is the biggest.*

Use **どちら** for a choice between two. For three or more, **どれ** (“which one?”) is more suitable. 一番 can describe the top choice among three or more; it does not itself mean “than.”

## Read a fictional comparison card

| Feature | カフェ (café) | 図書館の部屋 (library room) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Size | 小さい (small) | 大きい (big) |
| Atmosphere | にぎやか (lively) | 静か (quiet) |
| Distance from school | 近い (near) | 遠い (far) |
| Room fee | 安い (inexpensive) | 高い (expensive) |

These are **invented study examples**, not claims about real facilities. The table supports different choices depending on what matters: the café is nearer and cheaper; the library room is larger and quieter.

## Guided dialogue: two reasonable answers

**ゆき:** カフェと図書館（としょかん）の部屋（へや）と、どちらが静か（しずか）ですか。  
*Yuki: Which is quieter, the café or the library room?*

**けん:** 図書館の部屋のほうが静かです。でも、カフェのほうが近（ちか）いです。  
*Ken: The library room is quieter. But the café is closer.*

**ゆき:** そうですか。カフェは図書館の部屋より安（やす）いですか。  
*Yuki: I see. Is the café less expensive than the library room?*

**けん:** はい、安いです。私（わたし）は近いところが好（す）きです。  
*Ken: Yes, it is. I like places that are nearby.*

**ゆき:** 私は静かなところが好きです。  
*Yuki: I like quiet places.*

There is **no single objectively best option**: Ken values distance, while Yuki values quietness. Notice that 静かな modifies ところ (“place”) and 近い modifies ところ directly.

## Common mistakes to repair

| Intended meaning | Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The café is cheaper than the library room. | カフェは図書館の部屋より安いです。 | カフェより図書館の部屋は安いです。(This reverses the comparison.) |
| The library room is quieter. | 図書館の部屋のほうが静かです。 | 図書館の部屋より静かです。(The comparison reference is missing in this standalone answer.) |
| A quiet café | 静かなカフェ | 静かカフェ |
| Which of the two is closer? | どちらが近いですか。 | 一番が近いですか。 |

The standalone sentence **図書館の部屋より静かです** can be grammatical when its subject is understood from context; it simply does **not** mean “the library room is quieter.” The table above teaches the intended meaning, not an absolute ban on omitted subjects.

## Retrieval and short writing

1. Without looking back, say the opposite pairs: 大きい / 小さい, 新しい / 古い, 高い / 安い, 近い / 遠い.
2. Read the comparison card and finish: **図書館の部屋はカフェより＿＿です。** Possible answers: 大きい or 静か (with です). Explain which row proves your choice.
3. Write **two connected sentences** choosing a place for a fictional club: one comparison using **より** or **のほうが**, followed by **私は〜が好きです**. Model: **カフェは図書館の部屋より近いです。私は近いところが好きです。** (*The café is closer than the library room. I like nearby places.*)
4. Check your writing: Is the compared feature in the table? Did you keep the reference after より? Is な present before a noun such as 静かなところ? Other natural wording is welcome; this open response is self-checked, not automatically graded.

## Reading, listening and optional speaking

For reading, cover the English dialogue and identify **two** comparative facts. For listening awareness, ask a partner to read the original dialogue slowly while you look away; then uncover the text to verify the two facts. This is **not recorded audio**, and no listening score is assigned. If studying alone, read the Japanese aloud once and then silently recall the meaning; do not treat that as an audio test.

Optional speaking: take Yuki's role and ask **どちらが静かですか**; switch to Ken's role and answer **〜のほうが静かです**. Substitute 近い or 安い. No microphone, pronunciation judgment or speaking score is involved.
