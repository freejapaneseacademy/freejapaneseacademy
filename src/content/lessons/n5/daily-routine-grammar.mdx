## A day at the community library

You are helping a new student understand a volunteer's normal weekday. By the end, you can say **what someone does**, **what they do not do**, **what they act on**, and **when**. This is written grammar and reading practice; the chapter's L06 teaches listening to a routine conversation.

### 1. Polite nonpast: ます and ません

Japanese polite verbs ending in **ます** describe habitual actions or future actions, depending on context. **ません** is the corresponding polite nonpast negative. These are **not** past-tense endings.

| Dictionary meaning | Polite affirmative | Polite negative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| eat | 食べます（たべます） | 食べません（たべません） |
| drink | 飲みます（のみます） | 飲みません（のみません） |
| read | 読みます（よみます） | 読みません（よみません） |
| go | 行きます（いきます） | 行きません（いきません） |
| study | 勉強します（べんきょうします） | 勉強しません（べんきょうしません） |

**毎朝（まいあさ）パンを 食べます。** — I eat bread every morning. The word 毎朝 makes the habitual meaning clear. **今日は パンを 食べません。** — I will not eat bread today / I do not eat bread today (depending on context). Do not automatically translate ません as “did not”; the past negative form is different and is taught later.

### 2. The object particle を

In **本を 読みます。**（ほんを よみます。）— “I read a book” — **を** marks the direct object, the thing read. The particle is written を but pronounced **お** in standard modern Japanese.

- **水を 飲みます。**（みずを のみます。）— I drink water.
- **日本語を 勉強します。**（にほんごを べんきょうします。）— I study Japanese.
- **テレビを 見ません。**（テレビを みません。）— I do not watch television.

Not every verb takes a direct object: **学校に 行きます。** — “I go to school” uses **に** to mark the destination, not を for the school. Some motion verbs can take を with a route or departure point, but that is outside this lesson.

### 3. Time particle に — and when to leave it out

A specific clock time usually takes **に**: **7時に 起きます。**（しちじに おきます。）— I get up at seven. **8時半に 学校に 行きます。**（はちじはんに がっこうに いきます。）— I go to school at 8:30. The first に marks time; the second に marks the destination.

Relative or recurring time expressions such as **毎朝（まいあさ）**, **今日（きょう）** and **明日（あした）** normally appear **without** this time に: **毎朝 勉強します。** — I study every morning. **今日 勉強しません。** — I am not studying today. A topic marker は may appear for contrast, but it is not required here.

A natural basic order is **time + object + verb**: **7時に 朝ごはんを 食べます。** — I eat breakfast at seven. Japanese often omits “I” when the subject is understood.

### 4. Original reading: a volunteer's weekday note

The following is a fictional written note by library volunteer さくら. It describes a **usual weekday**, not one specific past day.

> **さくらの 平日の メモ**
>
> 毎朝 6時半に 起きます。7時に パンを 食べます。水を 飲みます。
>
> 8時に 図書館に 行きます。図書館で 日本語を 勉強します。
>
> 昼は 本を 読みます。テレビは 見ません。
>
> 夜 9時に 家で 音楽を 聞きます。

**Meaning:** Sakura gets up at 6:30 every morning, eats bread at seven and drinks water. At eight she goes to the library, where she studies Japanese. At lunchtime she reads books and does not watch television. At nine at night she listens to music at home.

**Notice the evidence:** **6時半に 起きます** tells us when she gets up; **日本語を 勉強します** tells us what she studies; **テレビは 見ません** uses は to contrast television with what she does do. In an ordinary noncontrastive sentence **テレビを 見ません** is also possible. **図書館で** and **家で** mark where an action takes place; do not confuse action-location **で** with destination **に**.

### 5. A short written exchange

**A:** 毎朝 何時に 起きますか。 — What time do you get up every morning?

**B:** 6時半に 起きます。 — I get up at 6:30.

**A:** 朝ごはんを 食べますか。 — Do you eat breakfast?

**B:** はい、パンを 食べます。 — Yes, I eat bread.

**A:** 夜 テレビを 見ますか。 — Do you watch television at night?

**B:** いいえ、見ません。音楽を 聞きます。 — No, I don't. I listen to music.

The question particle **か** follows the polite verb; the answer may omit an already-known object.

### 6. Repair the errors

- **パンが 食べます** is not the basic “eat bread” pattern. Use **パンを 食べます**; を marks the object of 食べます.
- **7時を 起きます** uses an object marker where a clock-time marker belongs. Use **7時に 起きます**.
- **毎朝に 勉強します** unnecessarily attaches time に to 毎朝. Use **毎朝 勉強します**.
- **昨日 食べません** does not express the usual polite past negative “did not eat.” The past negative is **食べませんでした** (introduced in the next chapter). Keep this lesson's ません in nonpast contexts.
- **図書館を 行きます** is not the beginner destination pattern. Use **図書館に 行きます**.

### 7. Practice from controlled to independent

**Controlled:** Complete **水___ 飲みます** with **を**; complete **8時___ 行きます** with **に**. Change **本を 読みます** to its polite nonpast negative: **本を 読みません**.

**Guided:** Use Sakura's note to answer **何時に 図書館に 行きますか。** → **8時に 行きます。** Then answer **テレビを 見ますか。** → **いいえ、見ません。**

**Independent writing:** Write **three to five connected Japanese sentences** about your usual day, including one clock time + に, one object + を, and one polite negative ません. Model: **毎朝 7時に 起きます。8時に 学校に 行きます。夜 本を 読みます。テレビは 見ません。** Check: (1) all actions are habitual, (2) clock times use に, (3) direct objects use を, (4) the negative ends in ません. Your own writing is **not automatically scored**.

**Optional speaking:** Read the short exchange aloud and substitute your real routine. Speak only if comfortable; no microphone, pronunciation scoring or verified recording is part of this lesson.
