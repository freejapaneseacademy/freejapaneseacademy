## At orientation: listen for a profile

Two fictional people meet at a student orientation. Later, a visitor asks a teacher about their job and hometown. These are original academy dialogues, not recordings of real people. You will use **age**, **school or work**, and **place of origin** to build a short mental profile of each speaker.

### What to listen for

- <span lang="ja">十九歳（じゅうきゅうさい）です。</span> — (I am) nineteen years old. The counter **歳** follows the age number.
- <span lang="ja">大学生（だいがくせい）です。</span> — (I am) a university student. A school role is different from someone's age.
- <span lang="ja">出身（しゅっしん）は京都（きょうと）です。</span> — I am from Kyoto. **出身** refers to a place of origin, not necessarily the place someone lives now.
- <span lang="ja">会社員（かいしゃいん）じゃありません。</span> — (I am) not a company employee. The negative tells you to reject a guess.
- <span lang="ja">わたしも大学生です。</span> — I am also a university student. **も** adds a shared fact.

The written examples above prepare vocabulary; **they do not replace listening**. The actual six questions depend on hearing the two original conversations.

### Track A — Two students at orientation

First listen without opening the transcript. Identify Aki's age, Aki's hometown and the one fact she shares with Ken. Pay attention to who says each place name: the same dialogue mentions two different hometowns. Listen a second time and answer the three Track A questions.

### Track B — Profiles at a community class

Someone guesses that Sato works for a company and that Sato is from Tokyo. Listen for the polite negatives <span lang="ja">いいえ</span> and <span lang="ja">じゃありません</span>. Then listen for the actual job and place of origin. The final sentence says where Sato **lives now**, which is different from hometown. You do not need to master the verb <span lang="ja">住んでいます</span> in this lesson; understand its meaning from context.

### Practice sequence

1. Play each authentic recording at a comfortable volume, with its transcript closed. Listen once for the overall purpose.
2. Replay and answer three questions per track, distinguishing **who** supplies each detail.
3. Submit only after both audio files are playable. An unavailable recording locks scoring rather than pretending that printed text is listening.
4. After your first unaided attempt, reveal the Japanese transcript and English meaning to compare exact evidence. Opening a transcript at any point marks the current attempt **assisted**, saved under a separate progress ID.
5. For optional shadowing, pause after one speaker's turn and repeat it aloud. No microphone is activated and no pronunciation score is assigned.

### Contrast: hometown versus residence

<span lang="ja">出身は名古屋です。</span> means “I am from Nagoya.” <span lang="ja">今は東京に住んでいます。</span> means “I live in Tokyo now.” A listener who hears **Tokyo** but ignores the distinction may answer the hometown question incorrectly. Likewise, <span lang="ja">わたしも大学生です</span> confirms a shared school status; it does not imply a shared hometown.

### Follow-up writing and speaking (ungraded)

Write a fictional two-sentence profile using the model <span lang="ja">わたしはミカです。出身は大阪です。</span> (“I am Mika. I am from Osaka.”). Add a school or job statement if you can. Check that **は** marks the topic, **です** closes a polite noun sentence and **も** is used only when the fact really is shared. Read your sentences aloud if comfortable. Free composition and pronunciation are not automatically graded.

### Review

Listen for **age + 歳**, **出身 + place**, **job + です**, and the correction **じゃありません**. A person can live somewhere different from their hometown. These practice scores are not official JLPT results; independent Japanese-language and audio reviews remain pending.
