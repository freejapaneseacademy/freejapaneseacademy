## A classroom after the lesson

Imagine a **fictional** school classroom after a study session. A teacher is still there. A book and a bag have been left behind. You are writing a short note for another student to explain what is where.

> 教室（きょうしつ）に本（ほん）があります。 — There is a book in the classroom.  
> 教室にかばんがあります。 — There is a bag in the classroom.  
> 教室に先生（せんせい）がいます。 — There is a teacher in the classroom.

All three lines share the same location. Only the last line changes the verb, because the teacher is a person.

## 1. Build the existence sentence

**Place + に + thing/person + が + あります／います。**

| Part | Example | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Place | 教室（きょうしつ） | Classroom |
| に | 教室に | Marks the place where something exists |
| Thing or person | 本（ほん）／先生（せんせい） | What or who is present |
| が | 本が／先生が | Marks the entity whose existence is stated |
| Verb | あります／います | Exists / is present |

**あります** is the polite existence verb for ordinary inanimate objects, such as 本 (book), 机（つくえ, desk）, and かばん (bag). **います** is used for people, such as 先生 (teacher), 学生（がくせい, student）, and 人（ひと, person), and generally animals. This is a beginner-friendly distinction; not every metaphorical or special usage follows a simple object/person rule.

Compare:

- **学校（がっこう）に机があります。** — There is a desk at school.
- **学校に学生がいます。** — There is a student at school.
- **部屋（へや）に人がいます。** — There is a person in the room.

The location is not the thing that exists. In **教室に本があります**, 教室に means *in the classroom*, while 本が means *a book* is present. Do not swap these particles.

## 2. Say what is present and ask a question

**教室に先生がいますか。** — Is there a teacher in the classroom? Add **か** after the polite verb. **はい、います。** — Yes, there is. **いいえ、いません。** — No, there isn't. The negative is supplied for recognition; this writing task focuses on affirmative statements.

You can repeat the place for clarity or use **そこ** (“there,” near the listener or at a previously indicated place) when the context makes its reference clear. For this exercise, repeating 教室に is safest and most explicit.

### Common repairs

| Check | Not suitable | Repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Object vs. person | 教室に本がいます。 | 教室に本があります。 |
| Person vs. object | 教室に先生があります。 | 教室に先生がいます。 |
| Place marker | 教室が本にあります。 | 教室に本があります。 |
| Existence marker | 教室に本にあります。 | 教室に本があります。 |

Spaces in English glosses do not correspond to Japanese word boundaries. Japanese is normally written without spaces between these words. Use a Japanese IME and check kanji conversions; kana forms such as きょうしつ and せんせい are acceptable scaffolds.

## 3. Controlled to guided writing

**Controlled substitution:** Copy **教室に本があります。** Change 本 to かばん. Then change 本 to 先生 and adjust the verb. Expected sentences: **教室にかばんがあります。** and **教室に先生がいます。**

**Guided reconstruction:** You have these facts: a desk is at school; a student is in the classroom. Write the two sentences without looking at the models, then compare:

> 学校に机があります。  
> 教室に学生がいます。

**Independent task:** Write **two to five connected Japanese sentences** about the fictional classroom. Include at least one nonliving object and at least one person. Keep the location explicit. You can use 本, かばん, 机, 先生, 学生 and 教室; invent no personal details. The writing workshop below lets you save a draft locally and compare a model.

## 4. Revise your composition

Check **(1)** every sentence has an understandable place, **(2)** に marks that place and が marks the person or object, **(3)** あります and います agree with the referent, and **(4)** spelling and polite endings are clear. Equivalent correct sentences are possible. The rubric is **self-review**, not an automatic Japanese-writing grade.

After revising, optionally read your sentences aloud for fluency practice. This written lesson contains no audio and does not test pronunciation. The eight scored recognition questions check written form selection only; they do not grade your composition or predict a JLPT score.
