## Your writing mission

You are planning a fictional trip from school to a station and then home. **Write 2–5 connected Japanese sentences** about where you go, how you travel, and where you return. Your text is self-reviewed; the separate fixed-answer check only measures recognition of forms. You can use a Japanese IME or paste your own Japanese.

### 1. Notice the movement in a model

**ゆい's travel note**

> <span lang="ja">私は月曜日に学校から駅へ行きます。バスで駅に行きます。友だちは駅に来ます。いっしょに電車で町へ行きます。夕方、私は家に帰ります。</span>

“I go from school to the station on Monday. I go to the station by bus. My friend comes to the station. We go to town together by train. In the evening, I return home.”

The first two sentences deliberately describe the same leg of the journey: the second adds the means of transport. In your own note, you may combine them: <span lang="ja">私は月曜日に学校からバスで駅へ行きます。</span> (“On Monday I go from school to the station by bus.”)

**Reading support:** <span lang="ja">月曜日（げつようび）</span> Monday; <span lang="ja">学校（がっこう）</span> school; <span lang="ja">駅（えき）</span> station; <span lang="ja">電車（でんしゃ）</span> train; <span lang="ja">町（まち）</span> town; <span lang="ja">夕方（ゆうがた）</span> evening; <span lang="ja">家（いえ）</span> home/house. <span lang="ja">友だち（ともだち）</span> means friend.

### 2. Choose the verb from the viewpoint

| Verb | Example | What the writer means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">行きます（いきます）</span> | <span lang="ja">駅へ行きます。</span> | “I go to the station.” Movement toward a destination. |
| <span lang="ja">来ます（きます）</span> | <span lang="ja">友だちはここに来ます。</span> | “My friend comes here.” Movement toward the speaker's or established reference location. |
| <span lang="ja">帰ります（かえります）</span> | <span lang="ja">家に帰ります。</span> | “I return home.” A return to a home or usual base. |

**Viewpoint matters.** From your home, you can say <span lang="ja">友だちが私の家に来ます。</span> (“My friend comes to my home”). If you are going to your friend's home, say <span lang="ja">友だちの家に行きます。</span> (“I go to my friend's home”). Do not translate English “come” mechanically in every situation.

### 3. Three particles with different jobs

| Particle | Example | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">に / へ</span> | <span lang="ja">駅に行きます。／駅へ行きます。</span> | Destination / direction. Here either is natural; <span lang="ja">へ</span> is pronounced *e*. |
| <span lang="ja">で</span> | <span lang="ja">電車で行きます。</span> | Means of transport: by train. |
| <span lang="ja">から</span> | <span lang="ja">学校から行きます。</span> | Starting point: from school. |

A destination is not the means: <span lang="ja">電車に行きます</span> would mean going *to* a train, not traveling *by* train. For walking, <span lang="ja">歩いて行きます（あるいていきます）</span> means “go on foot”; avoid treating <span lang="ja">歩きで</span> as the ordinary beginner transport expression.

### 4. Controlled rewriting

Try each transformation before looking at the model.

1. **Change the destination** from station to school: <span lang="ja">駅へ行きます。</span> → <span lang="ja">学校へ行きます。</span> (“I go to school.”)
2. **Add transport** to <span lang="ja">駅に行きます。</span> → <span lang="ja">バスで駅に行きます。</span> (“I go to the station by bus.”)
3. **Make a return** from <span lang="ja">家に行きます。</span> → <span lang="ja">家に帰ります。</span> (“I return home.”) Both can be grammatical in context, but the second explicitly describes returning.
4. **Make the viewpoint clear:** <span lang="ja">友だちはここに来ます。</span> (“My friend comes here.”) Change <span lang="ja">ここ</span> to <span lang="ja">私の家</span> to specify the reference place: <span lang="ja">友だちは私の家に来ます。</span>

### 5. Guided composition

Fill the blanks with words that make one coherent journey:

> <span lang="ja">私は＿＿曜日に＿＿から＿＿へ行きます。＿＿で行きます。夕方、＿＿に帰ります。</span>

For example: <span lang="ja">私は金曜日に学校から図書館へ行きます。バスで行きます。夕方、家に帰ります。</span> — “On Friday I go from school to the library. I go by bus. In the evening, I return home.”

The omitted destination in <span lang="ja">バスで行きます</span> is understood from the preceding sentence. <span lang="ja">図書館（としょかん）</span> means library.

### 6. Independent writing task

Write **2–5 connected Japanese sentences** about a fictional journey. Include a starting point, destination, transport method, and a return or an arrival involving another person. Use at least **two** movement verbs, and check whether the speaker is at the reference location for <span lang="ja">来ます</span>. If you do not want to use <span lang="ja">来ます</span>, use <span lang="ja">行きます</span> and <span lang="ja">帰ります</span> correctly.

**Alternative model:**

> <span lang="ja">土曜日に私は家から図書館へ行きます。自転車で行きます。友だちは図書館に来ます。午後、私は家に帰ります。</span>

“On Saturday I go from home to the library. I go by bicycle. My friend comes to the library. In the afternoon, I return home.” In this fictional scene, the narrator is already at the library when the friend arrives. <span lang="ja">自転車（じてんしゃ）</span> means bicycle; <span lang="ja">午後（ごご）</span> means afternoon.

### 7. Revise with four criteria

Check (1) **meaning**: the journey is understandable and sentences connect; (2) **verbs**: go, come and return match the viewpoint; (3) **particles**: <span lang="ja">に/へ</span> marks destination, <span lang="ja">で</span> transport and <span lang="ja">から</span> origin; (4) **readability**: places and time expressions are consistent. Compare your text with the two models. Multiple natural answers are valid, and your writing is **not automatically graded**.

Optional oral rehearsal: read your own sentences slowly, then explain the trip in English. No audio is recorded or pronunciation assessed here. The chapter's separate L06 listening lesson is intended for authentic audio comprehension; this writing task does not claim to supply or assess audio.
