## Reading signs in a shared space

**Can-do:** Read short Japanese notices, work out what people are asked or forbidden to do, and point to the words that prove your answer. This lesson uses **original fictional signs** from a community learning centre; they are not actual regulations. No audio is required for this reading slot.

### Before reading: three different meanings

- **〜てください** asks someone to do something: ドアを閉（し）めてください — “Please close the door.”
- **〜てもいいですか** asks whether an action is permitted: ここで写真（しゃしん）を撮（と）ってもいいですか — “May I take a photo here?” It is a **question**, not a statement of permission. **〜てもいいです** without か can grant permission.
- **〜てはいけません** prohibits an action: ここで食（た）べてはいけません — “You must not eat here.” The は is pronounced *wa*.

Notice the contrast between **読（よ）んでください** (“please read”) and **読んではいけません** (“you must not read”). Do not assume every sentence containing a て-form is a request.

### Sign A — Classroom door

> **日本語教室（にほんごきょうしつ） — Japanese class**  
> 授業（じゅぎょう）は午前（ごぜん）十時（じゅうじ）からです。  
> 九時五十分（くじごじゅっぷん）までに教室（きょうしつ）に来（き）てください。  
> 教室では電話（でんわ）で話（はな）してはいけません。

**Translation:** “Japanese class starts at 10 a.m. Please come to the classroom by 9:50. You must not talk on the phone in the classroom.”

**Find and cite:** When must learners arrive? The exact evidence is **九時五十分までに教室に来てください**. **までに** gives a deadline (“by”), unlike **まで** (“until”). What is forbidden? Quote **教室では電話で話してはいけません**. The location phrase **教室では** limits the notice to the classroom.

### Sign B — Library reading corner

> **図書室（としょしつ） — Reading room**  
> 本（ほん）はここで読（よ）んでもいいです。  
> 本を持（も）って帰（かえ）ってはいけません。  
> 読み終（お）わったら、本を棚（たな）に戻（もど）してください。

**Translation:** “You may read books here. You must not take books home. After you finish reading, please return books to the shelf.”

**Find and cite:** What is allowed? **本はここで読んでもいいです** explicitly grants permission. What is prohibited? **本を持って帰ってはいけません**. What must happen after reading? **本を棚に戻してください**. **読み終わったら** means “when/after you finish reading”; the main task is identifying the stated action, not mastering this additional grammar today.

### Sign C — Community hall desk

> **交流（こうりゅう）ホール — Community hall**  
> 受付（うけつけ）で聞（き）いてください。  
> 「ここで写真を撮ってもいいですか。」  
> 係（かかり）の人（ひと）：「はい、写真を撮ってもいいです。でも、フラッシュを使（つか）ってはいけません。」

**Translation:** “Please ask at reception. ‘May I take photos here?’ Staff: ‘Yes, you may take photos. But you must not use flash.’”

**Find and cite:** The visitor's **写真を撮ってもいいですか** is a permission **request**. The staff reply **はい、写真を撮ってもいいです** grants it. The limitation is **フラッシュを使ってはいけません**. Never treat the question alone as proof that photographs are allowed.

### Reading method: find the evidence, not a guess

1. Locate the **place**: 教室, 図書室, or 交流ホール.
2. Underline the **action**: 来る, 話す, 読む, 持って帰る, 戻す, 撮る, or 使う.
3. Look at the **ending**: ください (request), てもいいです (permission), てもいいですか (permission question), or てはいけません (prohibition).
4. Copy the **shortest phrase** that proves the answer. If a sign does not state a fact, answer “not stated” rather than inventing it.

### Guided reading

**Question:** Can a learner take a library book home? **Answer:** No. **Evidence:** 本を持って帰ってはいけません. This is a prohibition, not a polite request.

**Question:** Can visitors use flash in the hall? **Answer:** No. **Evidence:** フラッシュを使ってはいけません. Photography itself is permitted in the dialogue, but flash is not.

**Question:** Does Sign A say that class ends at eleven? **Answer:** **Not stated.** The sign gives only the start time, **午前十時からです**. Do not turn a start time into an invented end time.

### Independent practice

Before opening the interactive questions, write three short answers in your notebook: (1) where should finished books go, (2) who grants photo permission, and (3) what is the arrival deadline? For each, copy one Japanese evidence phrase from the signs. Then compare your phrases with the question feedback.

**Optional writing:** Invent a two-line sign for a fictional study room using one **〜てください** and one **〜てはいけません**. Model: **ここで静（しず）かに読んでください。ここで食べてはいけません。** (“Please read quietly here. Do not eat here.”) Check that each verb uses the correct て-form. This writing is ungraded.

**Optional speaking:** Read one sign aloud slowly and explain its meaning in English. There is no recording, pronunciation grade or verified speech audio in this lesson.

### Takeaway

A permission **question** is not the same as a permission **answer**. Read the ending and cite exact words before deciding what is required, allowed or prohibited. The next chapter activity separately practices listening; today's score measures written comprehension only.
