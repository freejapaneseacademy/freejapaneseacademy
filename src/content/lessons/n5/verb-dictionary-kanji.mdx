## From a word list to real actions

**Can-do:** Recognize dictionary forms and basic verb categories. In a Japanese activity list, you might see **読む** instead of **読みます**. Both can refer to reading, but the dictionary form and the polite ます form look different. This lesson helps you recognize the **kanji within the whole verb** without guessing a character's reading in isolation. No audio or pronunciation grade is offered.

## 1. Ten action kanji in real words

Read each complete word aloud using the kana support. The kanji usually supplies the lexical core, while the following hiragana (called **okurigana**) is part of the verb's written form.

| Character | Dictionary form (reading) | Polite form (reading) | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 食 | <span lang="ja">食べる</span> (たべる) | <span lang="ja">食べます</span> (たべます) | eat |
| 飲 | <span lang="ja">飲む</span> (のむ) | <span lang="ja">飲みます</span> (のみます) | drink |
| 見 | <span lang="ja">見る</span> (みる) | <span lang="ja">見ます</span> (みます) | see, watch |
| 聞 | <span lang="ja">聞く</span> (きく) | <span lang="ja">聞きます</span> (ききます) | listen, ask |
| 読 | <span lang="ja">読む</span> (よむ) | <span lang="ja">読みます</span> (よみます) | read |
| 書 | <span lang="ja">書く</span> (かく) | <span lang="ja">書きます</span> (かきます) | write |
| 話 | <span lang="ja">話す</span> (はなす) | <span lang="ja">話します</span> (はなします) | speak, talk |
| 買 | <span lang="ja">買う</span> (かう) | <span lang="ja">買います</span> (かいます) | buy |
| 行 | <span lang="ja">行く</span> (いく) | <span lang="ja">行きます</span> (いきます) | go |
| 来 | <span lang="ja">来る</span> (くる) | <span lang="ja">来ます</span> (きます) | come |

These are lesson-selected useful characters, **not** an official JLPT kanji list. The same kanji may have different readings in other words. For example, 来 is **く** in 来る but **き** in 来ます; learn both whole words.

## 2. Read the ending, not only the kanji

A dictionary-form verb is a useful form for looking up a word. It does **not** mean every sentence should end with it. In polite everyday conversation, you will often use the corresponding ます form.

- <span lang="ja">本を読む。</span> (ほんを よむ。) — “Read a book” / “(I) read a book,” in plain style, depending on context.
- <span lang="ja">本を読みます。</span> (ほんを よみます。) — “(I) read a book,” in polite style.
- <span lang="ja">水を飲む。</span> (みずを のむ。) — “(I) drink water,” plain style.
- <span lang="ja">水を飲みます。</span> (みずを のみます。) — “(I) drink water,” polite style.

**Important:** the dictionary form is not automatically an imperative. **本を読む** is not a polite instruction to someone else. Context and sentence style matter.

## 3. Three useful verb patterns

**Group 1 (godan):** many dictionary forms ending in う, く, す or む change their final sound to the matching **i-row** sound before ます: **読む → 読みます**, **書く → 書きます**, **話す → 話します**, **買う → 買います**, **行く → 行きます**, **飲む → 飲みます**.

**Group 2 (ichidan):** for today's **食べる → 食べます** and **見る → 見ます**, remove る and add ます. Not every verb ending in る belongs to Group 2; do not use that ending alone as a universal rule.

**Irregular:** **来る → 来ます** is read **くる → きます**. This change is not predicted by simply removing る. The other major irregular verb is **する → します** (“do”), written in kana here.

Compare **聞く → 聞きます** with **書く → 書きます**: both end in く and change to きます, but their kanji and meanings differ. Recognize the whole word rather than the ending alone.

## 4. Original fictional study-club activity card

> **土曜日の活動（どようびの かつどう） — Saturday activities**
>
> <span lang="ja">九時：図書館に行きます。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">九時半：本を読みます。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">十時：日本語を書きます。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">十一時：友だちと話します。</span>
>
> <span lang="ja">十二時：昼ごはんを食べます。水を飲みます。</span>

**Reading support:** 図書館 (としょかん) library; 九時半 (くじはん) 9:30; 日本語 (にほんご) Japanese language; 友だち (ともだち) friend; 昼ごはん (ひるごはん) lunch. The schedule means: “At nine, go to the library. At 9:30, read a book. At ten, write Japanese. At eleven, talk with a friend. At noon, eat lunch and drink water.” This is an invented learning scenario, not a real timetable.

Find the five activity verbs in the card. Their dictionary forms are **行く・読む・書く・話す・食べる・飲む** (six, because the final entry has two actions). Notice how a list of activities can use polite ます forms even though a dictionary displays the corresponding う/る forms.

## 5. Controlled retrieval and a common trap

Cover the polite-form column and supply the missing words: **買う → ?**, **見る → ?**, **来る → ?**. Check: **買います**, **見ます**, **来ます**. The last pair has a reading change, **くる → きます**.

A frequent error is **食べります**. For 食べる, the correct polite form is **食べます**: remove る before adding ます. By contrast, **帰る → 帰ります** (かえる → かえります, return) is a Group 1 verb even though it ends in る. That contrast is a reminder to learn each verb's group.

## 6. Guided writing and optional speaking

Write **two or three short sentences** about an imaginary study day. Include one action at a place, one object marked by **を**, and at least two polite verbs. Model:

> <span lang="ja">土曜日に図書館に行きます。本を読みます。友だちと日本語を話します。</span>
>
> (どようびに としょかんに いきます。ほんを よみます。ともだちと にほんごを はなします。)
>
> “On Saturday I go to the library. I read a book. I speak Japanese with a friend.”

**Self-review rubric:** (1) at least two actions; (2) correct whole-word kanji/kana; (3) polite ます endings; (4) を marks an object when needed; (5) sentences describe a coherent imaginary plan. You may type in kana before converting with a Japanese IME. Free writing is self-reviewed, not machine graded.

Optional speaking: read the model aloud slowly, then say the same plan using different activities. This is rehearsal only; no microphone or verified pronunciation feedback is provided.

## Review

Recognize a verb by its **kanji plus okurigana**, then match its dictionary form to its polite form. In the next reading lesson, use these skills to interpret a fresh verb list and identify explicit facts.
