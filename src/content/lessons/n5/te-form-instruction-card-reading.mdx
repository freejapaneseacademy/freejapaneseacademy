## Read to carry out a real sequence

At a fictional neighborhood community center, Mika and Haru are preparing a small cooking class. A printed **準備（じゅんび）カード** (preparation card) gives them a sequence of jobs. Your goal is not to translate every character: locate **people, actions, order and time**, and quote the exact words that prove your answer. This is an original teaching scenario, not a real food-safety procedure.

### Original instruction card

> **地域センターの料理教室（りょうりきょうしつ）— 土曜日の準備カード**  
> **担当（たんとう）：ミカさんとハルさん**  
> 午前九時に、ミカさんは手（て）を洗（あら）って、エプロンを着（き）ます。  
> 次（つぎ）に、ハルさんは野菜（やさい）を切（き）って、皿（さら）に入（い）れます。  
> 野菜を切ってから、ミカさんは水（みず）を鍋（なべ）に入れます。  
> 午前九時半（くじはん）に、二人（ふたり）はスープを作（つく）ります。  
> スープを作ってから、二人はテーブルを拭（ふ）きます。  
> 最後（さいご）に、午前十時に、参加者（さんかしゃ）にスープを出（だ）します。

### Read it once for the main idea

The card describes preparation, cooking, cleaning and serving. The two people are **ミカさん** and **ハルさん**. Try reading the Japanese without the translation first. Notice the clock times **午前九時** (9:00 a.m.), **午前九時半** (9:30 a.m.) and **午前十時** (10:00 a.m.).

### Sentence-by-sentence meaning

| Card wording | English meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 午前九時に、ミカさんは手を洗って、エプロンを着ます。 | At nine, Mika washes her hands and puts on an apron. |
| 次に、ハルさんは野菜を切って、皿に入れます。 | Next, Haru cuts vegetables and puts them on a plate. |
| 野菜を切ってから、ミカさんは水を鍋に入れます。 | After cutting the vegetables, Mika puts water into a pot. |
| 午前九時半に、二人はスープを作ります。 | At nine thirty, the two make soup. |
| スープを作ってから、二人はテーブルを拭きます。 | After making soup, the two wipe the table. |
| 最後に、午前十時に、参加者にスープを出します。 | Finally, at ten, they serve soup to the participants. |

**Reading note:** The card does not say that Mika alone cuts the vegetables; the preceding sentence explicitly assigns cutting to Haru. The subject of **野菜を切ってから** is not repeated, so avoid inventing a new actor. The card explicitly assigns the *following* water task to Mika.

## How the reading works

**〜て** joins actions in a natural sequence: **手を洗って、エプロンを着ます** (wash hands and put on an apron). The final verb **着ます** carries the polite sentence ending. The て-form itself is not a past-tense marker.

**〜てから** highlights that one action is completed before the next: **スープを作ってから、二人はテーブルを拭きます** (after making the soup, the two wipe the table). Contrast this with **作りますから**, where **から** following a polite clause can introduce a *reason* (“because ...”), not the same structure.

The forms in this card include **洗う → 洗って**, **切る → 切って**, **入れる → 入れて**, **作る → 作って**, **拭く → 拭いて** and **出す → 出して**. The card uses **出します** as the final polite predicate. Notice **切って** and **作って** both have a small **っ**, while **拭いて** does not.

## Trace the referents and evidence

When a sentence says **二人**, it means the two people already introduced: Mika and Haru. **参加者** refers to the class participants, not the two preparers. **次に** means “next”; **最後に** means “finally.” These expressions help you follow the order without guessing.

For example, ask: “Who wipes the table?” Locate **二人はテーブルを拭きます**. The evidence **二人は** proves the answer is *both*, not Haru alone. Ask: “What happens after the soup is made?” Quote **スープを作ってから、二人はテーブルを拭きます**. Do not confuse wiping the table with serving soup: serving is the final step.

## Guided reading: find, quote, explain

1. Find the first time. Quote **午前九時に** and explain that it anchors Mika's first two actions.
2. Find the first action pair. Quote **手を洗って、エプロンを着ます**. The action before **て** comes first.
3. Find the later cooking time. Quote **午前九時半に、二人はスープを作ります**.
4. Find the final audience. Quote **参加者にスープを出します**; **に** marks the recipients here.

**Try a deliberate wrong reading:** “Haru serves the soup at nine.” It conflicts with **最後に、午前十時に、参加者にスープを出します**. The text names a different time, and the serving subject is omitted rather than explicitly assigning the task to Haru. A good reader distinguishes what is stated from what is only possible.

## Independent reading and writing

Cover the English table. Write four short English answers: who cuts the vegetables, when the soup is made, who wipes the table, and who receives the soup. Under each answer, copy the **shortest Japanese evidence phrase** that proves it. Then write two Japanese sentences describing the sequence, for example:

> ミカさんは手を洗って、エプロンを着ます。  
> スープを作ってから、二人はテーブルを拭きます。

Compare your own sentences against the card. Check (1) the action order, (2) the correct て-form, (3) the person or group, and (4) the final polite predicate. This writing practice is **ungraded**; it is not automatically evaluated for Japanese accuracy.

For listening preparation, you may read the card aloud yourself, but this written reading lesson contains **no recorded narration or pronunciation assessment**. The chapter's L06 is the dedicated listening lesson.
