## A family photo at the welcome table

**Can-do:** Describe family members and simple relationships. Today you will recognize kanji as **whole words** on a fictional family chart. Readings are supplied because the same character can have different readings in other words.

> ミナ：これは私（わたし）の家族（かぞく）の写真（しゃしん）です。  
> ユキ：こちらの人（ひと）はだれですか。  
> ミナ：私の兄（あに）です。名前（なまえ）はたけしです。

**Meaning:** Mina: “This is a photo of my family.” Yuki: “Who is this person?” Mina: “He is my older brother. His name is Takeshi.” This is an invented scene. The name たけし is written in kana; no kanji name reading is assumed.

## 1. Six useful written words

| Written word | Reading | Meaning | Chart clue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 家族 | かぞく | family | The group in the photograph |
| 父 | ちち | my father | Family relation when speaking about one's own father |
| 母 | はは | my mother | Family relation when speaking about one's own mother |
| 兄 | あに | my older brother | Older male sibling, relative to the speaker |
| 弟 | おとうと | my younger brother | Younger male sibling, relative to the speaker |
| 写真 | しゃしん | photograph | A picture, not a person |

Read aloud slowly if you like: **かぞく・ちち・はは・あに・おとうと・しゃしん**. These are *written reading cues*, not verified recordings or pronunciation assessment.

**Notice the spelling:** 家族 uses voiced **ぞ**; 写真 starts **しゃ** with a small ゃ; 弟 has the long sequence **とう**. Do not guess a word's reading by assigning one sound to each kanji.

## 2. Read the original family chart

| Chart label | Person | Relationship to Mina |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 父（ちち） | けん | father |
| 母（はは） | えみ | mother |
| 兄（あに） | たけし | older brother |
| 弟（おとうと） | そうた | younger brother |

All names and relationships here are fictional. The chart says how each person is related **to Mina**; it does not establish anyone's occupation or nationality.

> ミナ：こちらは私の母（はは）です。  
> ユキ：お母（かあ）さんですか。  
> ミナ：はい。名前はえみです。

**Meaning:** Mina: “This is my mother.” Yuki: “Your mother?” Mina: “Yes. Her name is Emi.”

**Register matters:** When speaking to someone outside your family, **母（はは）** and **父（ちち）** commonly refer to your own parents. **お母さん（おかあさん）** and **お父さん（おとうさん）** are respectful everyday ways to address or refer to someone else's parents (and are also used within families). They are not interchangeable in every situation. **兄（あに）** is a reference to one's own older brother; **お兄さん（おにいさん）** is a respectful form for another person's older brother.

## 3. How の connects the words

**私の家族の写真** means “a photograph of my family.” Read from the linked parts: 私の家族 = my family; 家族の写真 = photograph of the family. This does **not** mean that the photograph belongs to each person in the family.

> こちらは私の兄です。 — This is my older brother.  
> その人はミナさんの弟です。 — That person is Mina's younger brother.

**こちら** is a polite way to introduce a person nearby. **その人** points to a person already identifiable in context; when introducing someone respectfully, こちら is often preferable. A frequent error is treating の as if it always means physical ownership. It also expresses relationships and descriptions.

## 4. Practice with evidence

**Controlled:** Cover the reading column and read 家族, 母, 父, 写真. Uncover it and check each kana. Next, distinguish 兄（あに） from 弟（おとうと）.

**Guided:** In the chart, who is Mina's older brother? Point to **兄：たけし**. Who is her mother? Point to **母：えみ**. What is the Japanese for “my family photo”? Say or type **私の家族の写真**.

**Independent:** Invent a fictional family with two people and write two short lines, such as **こちらは私の父です。名前はけんです。** Do not include private family details unless you choose to. Optionally read the lines aloud for self-rehearsal; this lesson does not grade speech.

**Takeaway:** Read whole words in context. The labels 父, 母, 兄 and 弟 describe a relationship **to a particular person**; the chart and the particle の help you identify whose relationship is meant.
