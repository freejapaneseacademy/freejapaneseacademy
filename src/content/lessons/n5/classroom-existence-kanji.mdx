## A classroom inventory

Imagine that a school staff member is checking a classroom before the next class. The staff member writes labels for the objects and people. These are fictional examples, not real school records. Your goal is to read **words in context**, not memorize a single reading for each kanji in isolation.

## Read the whole word

| Word | Reading | Meaning and use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">人</span> | ひと | person |
| <span lang="ja">先生</span> | せんせい | teacher |
| <span lang="ja">学生</span> | がくせい | student |
| <span lang="ja">本</span> | ほん | book |
| <span lang="ja">机</span> | つくえ | desk |
| <span lang="ja">教室</span> | きょうしつ | classroom |
| <span lang="ja">学校</span> | がっこう | school |
| <span lang="ja">中</span> | なか | inside |

**Reading notes:** 人 is **ひと** when it stands alone as “person”; the same character has other readings in compounds. 先生 is **せんせい**, while 学生 is **がくせい**. The small っ in 学校（がっこう） changes the rhythm: do not read it as がくこう. 教室 is **きょうしつ**, not きょうじつ. 本 is **ほん**, and 机 is **つくえ**. 中 is **なか** in かばんの中 (“inside the bag”).

## Original room notes

The staff member sees the following:

- <span lang="ja">教室に 先生が います。</span>（きょうしつに せんせいが います。） — There is a teacher in the classroom.
- <span lang="ja">机の上に 本が あります。</span>（つくえの うえに ほんが あります。） — There is a book on the desk.
- <span lang="ja">学校に 学生が います。</span>（がっこうに がくせいが います。） — There is a student at school.
- <span lang="ja">かばんの中に 本が あります。</span>（かばんの なかに ほんが あります。） — There is a book inside the bag.

The text does not say how many students are at school, who owns the bag, or whether the book on the desk is the same book as the one in the bag. Do not infer these facts.

## Notice the sentence pattern

**Place に + person or thing が + existence verb.** The location takes **に**; the person or thing being located takes **が**. Use **います** for people (先生, 学生, 人) and **あります** for inanimate things (本, 机). For example, <span lang="ja">教室に 本が あります。</span> means “There is a book in the classroom.” But <span lang="ja">教室に 先生が います。</span> means “There is a teacher in the classroom.”

**Common mistake:** <span lang="ja">先生が あります</span> is not the normal way to state that a teacher is present. Choose <span lang="ja">先生が います</span>. Similarly, avoid reading 学生 as 学校: the shared 学 does not make these words interchangeable.

## Practice in three steps

**Controlled recognition:** Cover the readings in the table. Read 先生, 学生, 本, 机 and 教室, then check your kana against the table. Trace the shapes with a finger or type the words; handwriting accuracy is not automatically assessed.

**Guided evidence search:** In the four room notes, find the exact words that tell you (1) a teacher is present, (2) a book is on the desk, and (3) a student is at school. Evidence: **先生が います**, **机の上に 本が あります**, and **学校に 学生が います**. Explain why the first and third use います.

**Independent transfer:** Invent a room with one teacher, one student and two books. Write three original sentences using the pattern. For example, <span lang="ja">教室に 学生が います。</span> (There is a student in the classroom). Check your kanji readings, に/が, and あります/います. Your writing is self-reviewed, not automatically scored.

## Knowledge check

Answer ten written questions about contextual kanji, word meanings and existence sentences. Feedback explains each answer. This check does not assess listening or pronunciation; those belong to the listening lesson.
