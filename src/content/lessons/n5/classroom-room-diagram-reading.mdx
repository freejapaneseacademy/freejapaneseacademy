## A room check before club time

**Can-do:** Say that a person or object exists in a place. This lesson asks you to read an **original fictional classroom diagram** and its Japanese notice. Your goal is to find stated facts and cite the words supporting them. This is not a real school floor plan.

## 1. Read the useful patterns

- <span lang="ja">教室（きょうしつ）に 先生（せんせい）が います。</span> — There is a teacher in the classroom.
- <span lang="ja">机（つくえ）の 上（うえ）に 本（ほん）が あります。</span> — There is a book on the desk.
- <span lang="ja">窓（まど）の 近く（ちかく）に 猫（ねこ）が います。</span> — There is a cat near the window.

**に** marks the location, **が** marks the person or thing whose presence is being stated. Use **います** for people and animals, and **あります** for inanimate objects in these examples. A sleeping cat still takes **います**; a book does not take it.

## 2. Original room diagram — さくら教室

Each cell names a zone in a fictional classroom. The diagram is **not to scale**; the notice below gives more precise relationships.

| Zone | Left (左・ひだり) | Center (中央・ちゅうおう) | Right (右・みぎ) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Back (後ろ・うしろ) | 窓（まど）・猫（ねこ） | 本棚（ほんだな）・辞書（じしょ） | ドア |
| Front (前・まえ) | 机（つくえ）・本（ほん） | 先生（せんせい） | いす・かばん |

**Word key:** 窓 = window; 猫 = cat; 本棚 = bookshelf; 辞書 = dictionary; ドア = door; 机 = desk; 本 = book; 先生 = teacher; いす = chair; かばん = bag.

## 3. The original classroom notice

> **さくら教室の メモ**
>
> **A.** <span lang="ja">教室の 前に 先生が います。</span>
>
> **B.** <span lang="ja">左の 机の 上に 本が あります。</span>
>
> **C.** <span lang="ja">後ろの 本棚に 辞書が あります。</span>
>
> **D.** <span lang="ja">窓の 近くに 猫が います。</span>
>
> **E.** <span lang="ja">右の いすの 下に かばんが あります。</span>
>
> **F.** <span lang="ja">ドアは 教室の 右の 後ろに あります。</span>

**English support:** A: A teacher is at the front of the classroom. B: A book is on the left desk. C: A dictionary is on the bookshelf at the back. D: A cat is near the window. E: A bag is under the right chair. F: The door is at the back right of the classroom.

Notice that the diagram identifies a **zone** while the notice identifies the more precise location **上** (on top), **下** (under) or **近く** (near). Do not infer that every item in a cell touches every other item.

## 4. Find the evidence, not just the answer

**Worked example 1:** Where is the dictionary? Look at the back-center zone, then quote **C: 後ろの 本棚に 辞書が あります。** This tells us the dictionary is on the back bookshelf. The sentence is more precise than the diagram alone.

**Worked example 2:** Which existence verb is used for the cat? Quote **D: 窓の 近くに 猫が います。** The animal takes **います**. It would be misleading to change it to **あります**.

**Worked example 3:** Is there a clock (時計・とけい)? Neither the diagram nor notice A–F mentions one. Answer **not stated**, rather than claiming that no clock exists.

## 5. Guided reading and referents

First, cover the English support. Find the teacher and cite **A**; find the cat and cite **D**; find the bag and cite **E**. Say which exact Japanese phrase proves each location.

Compare <span lang="ja">机の 上</span> (on the desk) and <span lang="ja">いすの 下</span> (under the chair). **の** connects the reference object and relative position. **に** marks the resulting location. Reversing 上 and 下 would change the fact.

If someone says <span lang="ja">そこに あります。</span> (“It is there”), you cannot locate **そこ** on this static diagram without knowing the speaker's viewpoint or gesture. The diagram has no speaker marked. Use the explicit notice instead.

## 6. Independent reading practice

Answer ten evidence questions below. You may reread the diagram and notice at any time. Each explanation quotes a relevant line after you submit. The quiz is formative, saved locally in your browser, and does not certify reading proficiency.

For optional ungraded writing, make one sentence using <span lang="ja">＿＿に ＿＿が あります／います。</span> and compare with the model. No machine grade is assigned to free writing.

## 7. Review

You can now scan a room diagram, identify people and objects, choose **います** or **あります** from written evidence, and distinguish stated from unstated information. Listening belongs to L06 and requires genuine audio; this written L05 does not pretend to test listening.
