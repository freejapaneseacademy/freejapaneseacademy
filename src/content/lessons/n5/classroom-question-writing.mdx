## A new student at the welcome desk

**Can-do:** Ask and answer who, what, which and where questions. Imagine two fictional students helping a new classmate find a teacher and the classroom. You will write **two to five connected Japanese sentences** as a short question-and-answer exchange. Do not use real names or identifying information.

## 1. Observe an original model

> A：あの人はだれですか。  
> B：田中先生です。  
> A：田中先生はどこですか。  
> B：教室（きょうしつ）です。

**Meaning:** A: Who is that person? B: (That's) Teacher Tanaka. A: Where is Teacher Tanaka? B: (In the) classroom.

The answer **教室です** is a short, context-dependent reply. For a complete location sentence, **田中先生は教室にいます** (“Teacher Tanaka is in the classroom”) is more explicit; **にいます** is an extension, not required in this lesson. In your own dialogue, you can instead ask **教室はどこですか** (“Where is the classroom?”) and answer **あそこです** (“Over there”).

## 2. Select the right question

| Form | Reading | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| だれ | だれ | who (person) | あの人はだれですか。 |
| 何 | なん | what (thing) | これは何（なん）ですか。 |
| どこ | どこ | where (place) | 教室はどこですか。 |
| どれ | どれ | which one (among options) | どれが先生の本ですか。 |
| も | も | also / too | 佐藤さんも学生です。 |

**Watch the meaning:** **どれ** stands alone as “which one”; **どの本** means “which book” and needs a following noun. **何** has context-dependent readings, including **なん** in **何ですか**; do not assume it is always read **なに**. The topic marker **は** is pronounced *wa*. **も** commonly replaces **は** in a statement like **佐藤さんも学生です** (“Sato is also a student”).

## 3. Controlled recombination

Choose the best word for each situation, then check the explanations.

1. Point to an unfamiliar person: あの人は＿＿ですか。 → **だれ** (a person).
2. Hold up an unknown object: これは＿＿ですか。 → **何（なん）** (a thing).
3. Look for the classroom: 教室は＿＿ですか。 → **どこ** (a location).
4. See three pens and ask which one belongs to Sato: ＿＿が佐藤さんのペンですか。 → **どれ** (a choice).
5. After learning that Emi is a student, add that Sato is a student too: 佐藤さん＿＿学生です。 → **も** (also).

**Common error:** **教室はだれですか** asks “Who is the classroom?” and does not fit a place. **教室はどこですか** is the intended location question. Likewise **どれですか** needs a visible or understood set of choices; for an unknown object, ask **これは何ですか**.

## 4. Guided writing: two classmates

Use these invented details:

- **田中先生** is the teacher.
- The **教室** is **あそこ** (“over there”).
- **エミさん** is a student; **佐藤さん** is also a student.

A supported four-line dialogue:

> A：あの人はだれですか。  
> B：田中先生です。  
> A：教室はどこですか。  
> B：あそこです。

A separate addition using **も** is **佐藤さんも学生です。** Use it only when an earlier student has already been identified: **エミさんは学生です。佐藤さんも学生です。** The second sentence adds a parallel fact; it does not mean Sato is the same person as Emi.

## 5. Independent composition and self-review

In the writing editor below, write **two to five connected Japanese sentences** in a fictional classroom situation. Include at least **one question and its matching answer**. Use two different question words if you can; add **も** only when a prior statement makes “also” meaningful. You may use kana with kanji where comfortable.

**Rubric (learner self-review, not an automatic grade):** (1) Is the situation understandable with two to five connected sentences? (2) Does each question word match the person, thing, place or choice requested? (3) Does the answer actually respond to its question? (4) Are **です**, **か** and any **も** used deliberately? A different natural answer can be valid; compare meanings rather than copying one fixed script.

The editor saves only when you choose **Save draft locally**. The text is not sent to a server or automatically graded. After writing, reveal the model and mark the four criteria yourself. Then complete the constrained recognition checks, which assess only taught distinctions.

## 6. Transfer and review

Try a second fictional situation: a student sees three books and asks **どれが先生の本ですか**. A friend answers **これです** (“This one”). Explain why **どれ** fits better than **どこ**. Tomorrow, without looking, write one **だれ** question and one **どこ** question. For optional speaking, read your own exchange aloud without recording; model-audio shadowing belongs to L08.
