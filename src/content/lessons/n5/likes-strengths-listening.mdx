## Two people, two interviews

At a fictional neighborhood hobby fair, a volunteer interviews a visitor about sports. In a second short interview, another visitor describes music and art. Your task is to find **what each person likes**, **what they can do**, and **what they are good at**. These are different ideas, even when the same hobby is involved.

The original Japanese dialogues are queued for synthesis with licensed AivisSpeech voices. **This draft must not be published until real WAV recordings, manifests, hashes and redistribution conditions are verified.** No native-speaker or independent pronunciation review is claimed.

## Before listening: three meaning clues

- **〜が好きです** (*... ga suki desu*) means “I like ...”. Example: **音楽が好きです** (*ongaku ga suki desu*, “I like music”).
- **〜が上手です** (*... ga jōzu desu*) means “is good at ...”. **あまり上手ではありません** (*amari jōzu dewa arimasen*) means “is not very good at ...”. The word **あまり** commonly appears with a negative predicate.
- **〜ができます** (*... ga dekimasu*) expresses ability, “can do ...”. **できますか** (*dekimasu ka*) asks whether someone can do it.

These are **preparation examples**, not a printed version of the recordings. You do not need to identify kanji by sound. Listen for contrasts: a person may like a sport without being good at it.

## Track A: Sports at the hobby fair

A woman asks a man about sports. Listen once for the topic. On the second play, decide which sport he enjoys, how he rates his skill, and whether he can do a different sport. The phrase **でも** (*demo*, “but”) often introduces a contrast.

## Track B: Music and art

A man interviews Yamada about instruments and another creative activity. Listen for **はい** (*hai*, “yes”) and **いいえ** (*iie*, “no”), but also listen to the full sentence that follows. The verb **弾けます** (*hikemasu*) means “can play” an instrument; **弾けません** (*hikemasen*) means “cannot play”.

## How to use the listening exercise

1. Play each recording without opening its transcript.
2. Answer the three questions for each track from the spoken evidence.
3. Submit and read the feedback, then reveal the Japanese transcript and English translation to check.
4. Replay a difficult turn. Optionally repeat the sentence aloud at your own pace.

The transcript is an accessibility alternative. If you reveal it before submitting, treat the attempt as **assisted practice**, not a blind listening result. If audio is unavailable, do not claim an unaided listening score.

## Common misunderstandings

**好きです** is a preference, not a statement of skill. **上手ではありません** does not mean “dislikes”. **できます** indicates ability, not necessarily frequent practice. For instruments, the potential form **弾けます** also expresses “can play”; this is a recognition preview, not a requirement to master potential conjugation here.

## Independent follow-up

Without looking at the transcript, say or write two true or imaginary hobby sentences: one using **〜が好きです** and one using **〜ができます** or **〜ができません**. For example, **私は料理が好きです。ピアノはできません。** (*Watashi wa ryōri ga suki desu. Piano wa dekimasen.* “I like cooking. I can't play the piano.”) Check whether you expressed preference and ability separately. This reflection is ungraded.
