## Optional speaking mission

You are making plans with a classmate. **Ask where they will go, how they will get there, and when they will return.** This is optional speaking practice: reading silently or typing the exchange is a fully valid alternative. Nothing here requests microphone permission, records speech, uploads your words, or grades pronunciation. The separate L06 travel-conversation lesson provides the chapter's real listening resource **only when its verified audio is available**; this page does not pretend that printed dialogue is audio.

After this lesson, you should be able to use two to five short conversational turns to talk about destinations and movement. The written knowledge check measures understanding of expressions, **not** speaking ability.

## 1. Five moves for a travel conversation

| Purpose | Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ask destination | どこへ行きますか。 | どこへ いきますか | Where are you going? |
| Say destination | 図書館に行きます。 | としょかんに いきます | I'm going to the library. |
| Ask transport | どうやって行きますか。 | どうやって いきますか | How will you get there? |
| Say means | バスで行きます。 | バスで いきます | I'll go by bus. |
| Ask return | いつ帰りますか。 | いつ かえりますか | When will you return? |

**Key distinction:** destination **に／へ** follows the place; transport **で** follows the vehicle. **図書館で行きます** is not a way to say “go to the library.” **図書館に行きます** is. For walking, say **歩いて行きます** (あるいて いきます), rather than mechanically adding で to 歩く.

## 2. Model A: a trip away from the speaker

> **あき：** 土曜日（どようび）、どこへ行きますか。  
> Aki: Where are you going on Saturday?
>
> **ゆう：** 駅（えき）の近（ちか）くの図書館（としょかん）に行きます。  
> Yuu: I'm going to the library near the station.
>
> **あき：** どうやって行きますか。  
> Aki: How will you get there?
>
> **ゆう：** 電車（でんしゃ）で駅へ行きます。それから、歩（ある）いて図書館に行きます。  
> Yuu: I'll go to the station by train. Then I'll walk to the library.
>
> **あき：** いつ帰（かえ）りますか。  
> Aki: When will you return?
>
> **ゆう：** 午後（ごご）四時（よじ）ごろ、家（いえ）に帰ります。  
> Yuu: I'll return home around 4 p.m.

**Notice:** ゆう leaves for a destination (**行きます**) and later returns to their home base (**帰ります**). **電車で** describes transport, **駅へ** a destination, and **家に** the return destination. **ごろ** means approximately.

## 3. Model B: a friend coming toward you

Imagine you are already at the library and are calling a friend who is on the way.

> **みお：** 今（いま）、どこにいますか。  
> Mio: Where are you now?
>
> **けん：** 駅にいます。  
> Ken: I'm at the station.
>
> **みお：** 図書館に来（き）ますか。  
> Mio: Are you coming to the library?
>
> **けん：** はい。歩いて行きます。もうすぐ着（つ）きます。  
> Ken: Yes. I'll walk there. I'll arrive soon.
>
> **みお：** わかりました。ここで待（ま）っています。  
> Mio: Understood. I'm waiting here.

**Viewpoint matters:** Mio says **来ますか** because the friend is moving **toward Mio's location**. Ken says **行きます** because he is moving away from where he is. These are not contradictory. **着きます** and **待っています** are helpful recognition-only extensions; you do not need to conjugate them for this lesson.

## 4. Build your own exchange

Choose one fictional setting. **Situation A:** Your classmate goes from home to the park by bus and returns at five. **Situation B:** You are at the station; a friend is coming to meet you on foot. **Situation C:** You go from school to a café by bicycle, then return home. Do not enter real addresses or private travel plans.

Use this scaffold, replacing the underlined ideas with your own:

1. **A:** 土曜日、どこへ行きますか。 (Where are you going on Saturday?)
2. **B:** ＿＿に行きます。 (I'm going to __.)
3. **A:** どうやって行きますか。 (How will you get there?)
4. **B:** ＿＿で行きます。 (I'll go by __.)
5. **A:** いつ帰りますか。 (When will you return?)

Add **午後五時ごろ、家に帰ります** as a sixth turn if you wish. A complete short interaction can also have fewer turns; clarity is more important than length.

### Role-switch ladder

**Pass 1 — Supported.** Read Model A once. Underline every place + に／へ and every vehicle + で. Pause at each question and supply a plausible answer.

**Pass 2 — Independent.** Without copying the model, perform a short exchange for Situation A, B or C. Use the editable practice area below if speaking is not convenient.

**Pass 3 — Switch.** Play the questioner, then the traveler. In Situation B, deliberately switch viewpoint: at the destination, ask **ここに来ますか**; from the station, reply **はい、そちらに行きます** (“Yes, I'm going over there”). The two verbs are appropriate to different speakers.

### Self-review, not an automatic assessment

| Check | Needs another try | Practice target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Destination | Missing or confused with transport | Place + に／へ |
| Means | Vehicle has a destination particle | Vehicle + で, or 歩いて |
| Viewpoint | 行く／来る／帰る used interchangeably | Verb fits direction or return |
| Conversation | Isolated phrase only | Question and relevant answer; both roles tried |

Read slowly, and repeat with a different destination. Japanese is usually polite here with **〜ます／〜ますか**. Avoid interpreting a self-check as verified accent, pitch or fluency.

## Recap

**どこへ行きますか** asks where. **どうやって行きますか** asks how. **で** marks a vehicle, **に／へ** a destination. **来ます** depends on the speaker's location, and **帰ります** describes returning to one's home or base. Try both roles, then decide whether you want to mark this optional lesson complete.
