## Your can-do: introduce and identify someone

At a language-exchange welcome desk, you can introduce yourself, ask whether someone is a student or teacher, and gently correct a mistaken guess. This lesson uses **polite** everyday language; it does not teach casual plain forms yet.

### 1. Notice the sentence frame

<span lang="ja">わたしは エマです。</span> — “I'm Emma.”

<span lang="ja">わたしは がくせいです。</span> — “I'm a student.”

The pattern is **topic は + noun + です**. **は** marks what the sentence is about and is pronounced *wa* when used as a particle. **です** makes this noun-predicate sentence polite; it does not map mechanically to every English form of “be.” Japanese normally does not require a separate word for “a” or “an.”

When the topic is clear, you can omit it: <span lang="ja">エマです。がくせいです。</span> — “I'm Emma. I'm a student.” The second sentence continues talking about Emma. This is a context-dependent omission, not a rule to omit every subject.

### 2. Ask rather than state

<span lang="ja">もりさんは せんせいです。</span> — “Mori is a teacher.”

<span lang="ja">もりさんは せんせいですか。</span> — “Is Mori a teacher?”

Adding **か** at the end creates a polite question. You do not need to rearrange the Japanese word order as English does. <span lang="ja">はい、せんせいです。</span> means “Yes, [Mori] is a teacher.”

### 3. Correct a guess without sounding abrupt

<span lang="ja">いいえ、せんせいじゃありません。</span> — “No, [I am] not a teacher.”

The polite negative of a noun predicate is **noun + じゃありません**. The fuller **ではありません** is also correct and more formal. Do not attach じゃありません directly after a completed です sentence: use <span lang="ja">がくせいじゃありません</span>, not <span lang="ja">がくせいですじゃありません</span>.

### 4. Original welcome-desk exchange

| Speaker | Japanese | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Staff | <span lang="ja">こんにちは。おなまえは？</span> | Hello. Your name? (elliptical polite-context prompt) |
| Emma | <span lang="ja">エマです。</span> | I'm Emma. |
| Staff | <span lang="ja">エマさんは せんせいですか。</span> | Emma, are you a teacher? |
| Emma | <span lang="ja">いいえ、せんせいじゃありません。がくせいです。</span> | No, I'm not a teacher. I'm a student. |
| Staff | <span lang="ja">そうですか。よろしくおねがいします。</span> | I see. Nice to meet you. |

**Usage note:** The shortened <span lang="ja">おなまえは？</span> is common in context but not a complete model of the **ですか** sentence pattern. **そうですか** often means “I see” in response to new information, not necessarily “Is that so?” as an information-seeking question.

### 5. Reading: two fictional registration cards

**Card A:** <span lang="ja">わたしは リーです。かいしゃいんです。せんせいじゃありません。</span>

“I am Lee. I am a company employee. I am not a teacher.”

**Card B:** <span lang="ja">わたしは エマです。アメリカじんです。がくせいです。</span>

“I am Emma. I am American. I am a student.”

Look for the exact phrase supporting a claim. <span lang="ja">かいしゃいんです</span> gives Lee's occupation, while <span lang="ja">アメリカじんです</span> gives Emma's nationality. **アメリカ** names the country; **アメリカじん** means an American person.

### 6. Controlled → guided → independent practice

First, identify the topic and predicate in <span lang="ja">わたしは がくせいです。</span>. Next, change the statement to a question: <span lang="ja">がくせいですか。</span>. Finally, imagine a visitor incorrectly assumes you are a teacher. Type or write a suitable correction, using the model <span lang="ja">いいえ、せんせいじゃありません。</span>. You may add a truthful role of your own, but do not auto-grade an open response by exact string matching.

**Self-check rubric (ungraded):** Did you write the topic particle as **は**, put **か** only on a question, and use **じゃありません** without **です** before it? Is the role you chose appropriate for your actual situation? Revisit any one mistake and try again.

### 7. Listening and speaking bridge

For optional oral rehearsal, read the original exchange aloud, pause after the staff's question, and answer with <span lang="ja">はい</span> or <span lang="ja">いいえ</span> plus the appropriate predicate. This is **text-based rehearsal**, not a recorded listening assessment; authentic first-meeting listening is taught in the chapter's L06 lesson. No pronunciation grade or native review is claimed.
