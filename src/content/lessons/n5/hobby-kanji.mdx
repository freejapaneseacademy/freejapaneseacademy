## A hobby-board introduction

At a fictional community club, members post brief introductions before a weekend activity exchange. Read the words as **whole words**, not as isolated kanji. You do not need to write every character from memory to succeed.

**Can-do:** Read a short Japanese hobby profile and tell which activity someone likes and which skill someone claims.

### Four original profile cards

> **A — あき**  
> 音楽（おんがく）が好（す）きです。歌（うた）を歌（うた）うのも好（す）きです。  
> *Aki likes music. Aki also likes singing songs.*

> **B — ゆう**  
> 本（ほん）を読（よ）むのが好（す）きです。でも、歌（うた）は上手（じょうず）ではありません。  
> *Yuu likes reading books, but is not good at singing.*

> **C — みお**  
> 日本語（にほんご）を書（か）くのが好（す）きです。ひらがなが上手（じょうず）です。  
> *Mio likes writing Japanese. Mio is good at hiragana.*

> **D — けん**  
> 音楽（おんがく）が好（す）きです。ピアノが上手（じょうず）です。  
> *Ken likes music and is good at piano.*

These are fictional self-descriptions, not claims that liking a hobby always means having skill at it.

## Six kanji in useful words

| Kanji | Word and reading | Meaning in this lesson | Where to find it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 音 | 音楽（おんがく） | music | A and D |
| 楽 | 音楽（おんがく） | music (second character) | A and D |
| 歌 | 歌（うた） | song; singing context | A and B |
| 書 | 書く（かく） | write | C |
| 読 | 読む（よむ） | read | B |
| 好 | 好き（すき） | liked; fond of | all four |

**Learn the word and its reading together.** 音楽 is **おんがく**, not おとたのしい; both characters contribute to the compound, but the compound has its own established reading. 楽 in 楽しい（たのしい, enjoyable）has a different reading. 歌 is **うた** in 歌を歌う and **うたう** in 歌う. The same visible kanji can appear in different grammatical words.

**Shape check:** 読 contains 言 on the left; 書 has a different overall structure and is used here in 書く. 好 has 女 on the left and 子 on the right. Visual cues help recognition, but they do not replace reading the whole word.

## What the grammar tells you

**Noun + が好きです** describes liking something: **音楽が好きです** = “I like music” when the subject is understood. To talk about liking an activity, attach **の** to a plain-form verb phrase: **本を読むのが好きです** = “I like reading books.” Here **の** turns the activity into something you can talk about liking.

**〜が上手です** describes being good at something. **ピアノが上手です** = “(Someone) is good at piano.” **歌は上手ではありません** contrasts singing with another topic: “As for singing, (they) are not good at it.” The negative is polite. Be tactful when describing other people's abilities; these fictional profiles are self-descriptions.

**好き** is not a verb meaning “to like”; it behaves like a な-adjective. Avoid **音楽を好きです** in this beginner pattern: use **音楽が好きです**. Likewise, liking music does not logically imply being good at playing it.

### Two evidence questions to try before the quiz

1. Who says they like **reading**? Point to **本を読むのが好きです** in card B.
2. Who explicitly says they are **good at piano**? Point to **ピアノが上手です** in card D.

Notice that **歌を歌う** is natural Japanese (“sing a song”); the noun and verb share the same character but have different readings.

## Guided recognition and writing

1. Find both characters of **音楽** in A. Read the whole word aloud as **おんがく**; do not guess each character independently.
2. Underline **読** in B and **書** in C. Explain the contrast between **読む** (read) and **書く** (write).
3. In B, circle **好き** and **上手ではありません**. One reports a preference; the other reports lack of skill.
4. Cover the English lines and identify the activity or skill in each card from the Japanese.
5. Try writing **音楽が好きです。** and **本を読むのが好きです。** with a Japanese IME; copying the kanji is optional. Check that **が** follows the thing or activity being liked.

## Independent profile task

Imagine a fictional new club member who likes reading and is good at writing hiragana. Write two connected Japanese sentences. Try before revealing this model:

> 本（ほん）を読（よ）むのが好（す）きです。ひらがなを書（か）くのが上手（じょうず）です。  
> *I like reading books. I am good at writing hiragana.*

**Self-check (ungraded):** Did you use **読む** and **書く** for the intended actions? Does **のが** follow each verb phrase? Did you distinguish liking from skill? Are your readings and kana correct? Can a reader understand the two statements without an English gloss?
