## First meeting: who is who?

**Can-do:** Introduce yourself and identify another person's basic details. At a language-school welcome event, you receive short profile cards. This lesson builds the vocabulary needed to read those cards before studying the full sentence patterns in L03. The names below are fictional.

A welcome card might say:

> なまえ：ミナ
>
> くに：にほん
>
> しごと：せんせい

The labels mean **name**, **country**, and **work/occupation**. Here, Mina is from Japan and is a teacher. The card does not say how old Mina is or which school she works at: avoid adding details that the Japanese does not provide.

## Words you can actually use

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning and context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| なまえ（名前） | なまえ | name; useful as a profile label |
| くに（国） | くに | country |
| しごと（仕事） | しごと | work or occupation |
| わたし（私） | わたし | I; often omitted when obvious |
| がくせい（学生） | がくせい | student, often at a school or university |
| せんせい（先生） | せんせい | teacher; also a respectful form of address |
| かいしゃいん（会社員） | かいしゃいん | company employee |
| にほん（日本） | にほん | Japan |
| アメリカ | アメリカ | United States (country name) |
| かんこく（韓国） | かんこく | South Korea |
| ～じん（～人） | ～じん | nationality/person from a country in these examples |
| ～さん | ～さん | polite suffix after a person's name |

Kanji in parentheses are recognition previews. Read the kana first; handwriting all the kanji is **not** an objective of this vocabulary lesson. アメリカ is written in katakana.

## Country is not the same as nationality

- **にほん** = Japan; **にほんじん** = Japanese person / Japanese nationality.
- **アメリカ** = the United States; **アメリカじん** = American person / American nationality.
- **かんこく** = South Korea; **かんこくじん** = Korean person / Korean nationality.

The suffix **じん** follows the country name in these examples. Do not attach じん to a personal name or an occupation. Country and nationality are related but answer different questions: a card's くに field may give にほん, while a sentence describing nationality may say にほんじんです. This lesson uses simplified fictional profiles; real identities and nationalities are not always inferable from a name or place of residence.

## Meet three fictional learners

**Card A:** なまえ：エマ / くに：アメリカ / しごと：がくせい

**Card B:** なまえ：キム / くに：かんこく / しごと：かいしゃいん

**Card C:** なまえ：さとう / くに：にほん / しごと：せんせい

Read each label before its value. Emma is a student from the United States. Kim is a company employee from South Korea. Sato is a teacher from Japan. These are explicit facts on the cards, not guesses from the names.

In conversation, **さとうさん** is a polite way to refer to Sato when their role is not the focus. **さとうせんせい** may be appropriate when Sato is your teacher; せんせい is not a generic suffix for everyone. Use ～さん with a name, not with a country: **アメリカさん** is not the intended way to say “American.”

## Short sentences: recognize the meaning

- **わたしはエマです。** — “I'm Emma.” The は is written *ha* but pronounced *wa* when it marks the topic.
- **エマさんはがくせいです。** — “Emma is a student.” The respectful name suffix is attached to エマ.
- **キムさんはかんこくじんです。** — “Kim is Korean.”
- **さとうさんはせんせいです。** — “Sato is a teacher.”

These examples use the polite ending **です**. They are statements, not questions. The next grammar lesson will explain the topic marker は, polite negatives, and the question marker か more systematically. Do not assume a speaker's nationality from a name alone: the sentence must actually state it.

## Practice: from recognition to retrieval

**Controlled:** Cover the English column and retrieve the meaning of せんせい, がくせい, かいしゃいん, くに and なまえ. Then reverse direction: given “student,” write がくせい.

**Guided:** Return to Cards A–C. Without looking at the English explanations, identify each person's name, country and occupation. Explain why にほん and にほんじん are not interchangeable in the same label.

**Independent:** Create a **fictional** profile card with three labels (なまえ, くに, しごと). Choose a fictional name and use a country and occupation from this lesson. Write one supported sentence, for example **エマさんはがくせいです。** Compare the spelling with the models. Do not enter actual private identifying information.
