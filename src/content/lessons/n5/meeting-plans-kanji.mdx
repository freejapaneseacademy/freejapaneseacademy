## A plan after class

A classmate leaves a note on the classroom board:

> **土曜日（どようび）、学校（がっこう）の前（まえ）で会（あ）いませんか。**  
> Would you like to meet in front of the school on Saturday?
>
> **はい。十二時（じゅうにじ）に会（あ）いましょう。**  
> Yes. Let's meet at twelve.

The note tells us **when**, **where**, and **what to do**. Your goal is to read the important written words, not to memorize every reading of each kanji. The new character set is **学・校・前・時・会・店**. The words and examples are academy-authored; this is not an official JLPT kanji list.

## 1. Six characters inside useful words

| Character | Word and reading | Meaning in this lesson | Notice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 学 | 学校（がっこう） | school | 学校 is a single word; the small っ is important |
| 校 | 学校（がっこう） | school | The second character of 学校 |
| 前 | 前（まえ） | in front of; before | 学校の前 = in front of the school |
| 時 | 十二時（じゅうにじ） | twelve o'clock | The time suffix is じ |
| 会 | 会います（あいます） | meet (someone) | 会いませんか invites someone to meet |
| 店 | 店（みせ） | shop | 店の前 = in front of the shop |

**Reading tip:** Learn the *whole words* first: **学校（がっこう）**, **会います（あいます）**, **店（みせ）**. Kanji can have more than one reading in other words; the readings above are the ones used in this scene. For example, **店** is **みせ** when used as this standalone noun.

## 2. Read place and time together

**学校の前（がっこうのまえ）** means *in front of the school*. The particle **の** connects the landmark and the location word.

- **店の前（みせのまえ）** — in front of the shop.
- **学校の前で会います（がっこうのまえであいます）** — meet in front of the school.
- **三時に会います（さんじにあいます）** — meet at three o'clock.
- **土曜日に会いませんか（どようびにあいませんか）** — would you like to meet on Saturday?

The **で** in **学校の前で会います** marks where the meeting action happens. The **に** in **三時に会います** marks a specific meeting time. This is different from **店にします**, where **にします** means *choose the shop*.

### A common misreading

**学校** is **がっこう**, not **がくこう** in ordinary speech. The small **っ** marks a held consonant. In **十二時**, **時** is **じ**; in **会います**, **会** is read **あ**. Do not assume one character always has only one pronunciation.

## 3. Original mini-message: the café plan

> **みお：土曜日（どようび）に会（あ）いませんか。**  
> Mio: Would you like to meet on Saturday?
>
> **ゆう：いいですね。何時（なんじ）ですか。**  
> Yu: Sounds good. At what time?
>
> **みお：二時（にじ）はどうですか。学校（がっこう）の前（まえ）で会（あ）いましょう。**  
> Mio: How about two o'clock? Let's meet in front of the school.
>
> **ゆう：はい。そのあと、店（みせ）に行（い）きましょう。**  
> Yu: Yes. After that, let's go to a shop.

**Evidence check:** The day is **土曜日**; the proposed time is **二時**; the meeting point is **学校の前**. The shop is a later destination, **not** the meeting point.

## 4. Read the sign, then make a decision

A fictional snack shop displays this notice:

> **店（みせ）は学校（がっこう）の前（まえ）です。**  
> The shop is in front of the school.

You and your classmate have two choices: the snack shop or the café.

- **この店にします。** — I'll choose this shop.
- **カフェにします。** — I'll choose the café.
- **この店で会いませんか。** — Would you like to meet at this shop?

In **店で会う**, **で** marks the meeting place. In **店にする**, **に** marks the chosen option. Reading the kanji helps you understand the nouns, while the particles and verbs explain the function.

## 5. Guided retrieval

Cover the answers, then try reading these aloud or writing their kana:

1. **学校** → **がっこう** (school)
2. **店** → **みせ** (shop)
3. **前** → **まえ** (front / before)
4. **三時** → **さんじ** (three o'clock)
5. **会います** → **あいます** (meet)
6. **学校の前で会いましょう。** → Let's meet in front of the school.

For optional handwriting, copy **学・校・前・時・会・店** slowly, checking the printed forms. This lesson does not claim to assess stroke order or handwriting quality.

## 6. Independent writing and optional speaking

Write **two to four short Japanese lines** inviting a friend to meet. Include a day, a time, and a location. You can invent all details; do not share your real meeting plans.

**Model:**

> 土曜日に会いませんか。  
> いいですね。二時に会いましょう。  
> 学校の前で会いましょう。  
> そのあと、店に行きましょう。

**Self-review:** Can you read **学校** as がっこう and **店** as みせ? Did you use **に** for the time and **で** for the meeting place? Is **ませんか** an invitation and **ましょう** a shared proposal? Is the place clear?

You may speak the model aloud, but no microphone, recording, or automatic pronunciation score is used here. For actual listening practice, continue to this chapter's L06 after its licensed audio is available.

## Quick recap

**学校（がっこう）・前（まえ）・時（じ）・会います（あいます）・店（みせ）** help you read invitations and meeting plans. Use the practice below to check readings and distinguish a meeting place from a later destination.
