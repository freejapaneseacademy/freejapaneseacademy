## A room you can describe

Imagine showing a new student around a small shared study room. Your task is to say **where** things are, not merely that they exist. Japanese uses a reference object plus a **spatial noun**: <span lang="ja">つくえの 上</span> (“the top of the desk / above the desk”).

### 1. The six location words

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">上（うえ）</span> | ue | on / above | <span lang="ja">つくえの 上</span> — on the desk |
| <span lang="ja">下（した）</span> | shita | under / below | <span lang="ja">いすの 下</span> — under the chair |
| <span lang="ja">前（まえ）</span> | mae | in front of | <span lang="ja">ドアの 前</span> — in front of the door |
| <span lang="ja">後ろ（うしろ）</span> | ushiro | behind | <span lang="ja">たなの 後ろ</span> — behind the shelf |
| <span lang="ja">中（なか）</span> | naka | inside | <span lang="ja">かばんの 中</span> — inside the bag |
| <span lang="ja">隣（となり）</span> | tonari | next to / beside | <span lang="ja">いすの 隣</span> — beside the chair |

The location is **relative to the reference noun**. <span lang="ja">つくえの 前</span> is the area in front of the desk; it is not automatically “in front of the room.” <span lang="ja">上</span> can mean above without contact; context determines whether “on” or “above” is the best translation. <span lang="ja">隣</span> generally describes neighboring positions; <span lang="ja">横（よこ）</span> is a broader word for “to the side,” which you do not need to master here.

### 2. Build a full sentence

**<span lang="ja">[reference noun] の [spatial noun] に [located thing/person] が あります／います。</span>**

- <span lang="ja">つくえの 上に ほんが あります。</span> — There is a book on the desk.
- <span lang="ja">つくえの 下に かばんが あります。</span> — There is a bag under the desk.
- <span lang="ja">ドアの 前に せんせいが います。</span> — The teacher is in front of the door.
- <span lang="ja">たなの 隣に いすが あります。</span> — There is a chair next to the shelf.
- <span lang="ja">かばんの 中に かぎが あります。</span> — There is a key inside the bag.
- <span lang="ja">いすの 後ろに ねこが います。</span> — There is a cat behind the chair.

**の** connects the reference object to its relative location. **に** marks the place of existence; **が** introduces the thing or person located there. Use **あります** for objects (including furniture) and **います** for people and animals. Plants ordinarily take **あります** in this existence construction.

### 3. Known things and questions

When the listener already knows which book you mean, you can make it the topic:

- <span lang="ja">ほんは どこに ありますか。</span> — Where is the book?
- <span lang="ja">ほんは つくえの 上に あります。</span> — The book is on the desk.
- <span lang="ja">せんせいは どこに いますか。</span> — Where is the teacher?
- <span lang="ja">せんせいは ドアの 前に います。</span> — The teacher is in front of the door.

This is not a rule that **は** must replace **が**. A question asking what is present naturally allows <span lang="ja">つくえの 上に なにが ありますか。</span> (“What is on the desk?”). A question about a particular book naturally uses **は**.

### 4. Original reading: the little study room

<span lang="ja">ここは べんきょうの へやです。つくえの 上に あおい ほんが あります。つくえの 下に くろい かばんが あります。かばんの 中に かぎが あります。たなの 隣に いすが あります。ドアの 前に せんせいが います。いすの 後ろに ねこが います。</span>

*This is a study room. A blue book is on the desk. A black bag is under the desk. A key is in the bag. A chair is next to the shelf. A teacher is in front of the door. A cat is behind the chair.*

Read the Japanese twice. Then answer from the **stated evidence**: Where is the key? <span lang="ja">かばんの 中に かぎが あります。</span> Where is the cat? <span lang="ja">いすの 後ろに ねこが います。</span> Nothing in this description says the cat is inside the bag.

### 5. A short room-tour conversation

**Aki:** <span lang="ja">ほんは どこに ありますか。</span> — Where is the book?

**Ren:** <span lang="ja">つくえの 上に あります。</span> — It's on the desk.

**Aki:** <span lang="ja">せんせいは どこに いますか。</span> — Where is the teacher?

**Ren:** <span lang="ja">ドアの 前に います。</span> — In front of the door.

**Aki:** <span lang="ja">かぎは つくえの 下に ありますか。</span> — Is the key under the desk?

**Ren:** <span lang="ja">いいえ。かばんの 中に あります。</span> — No. It's in the bag.

The last answer corrects the **key's** location, not the bag's location. This is written dialogue for reading practice; no recording or listening score is claimed in this L03 lesson.

### 6. Notice and repair errors

- <span lang="ja">つくえ 上に ほんが あります。</span> is missing the connecting **の**. Use <span lang="ja">つくえの 上に ほんが あります。</span>
- <span lang="ja">ドアの 前が せんせいに います。</span> reverses the particles. Use <span lang="ja">ドアの 前に せんせいが います。</span>
- <span lang="ja">いすの 後ろに ねこが あります。</span> uses the object existence verb for an animal. Use **います**.
- <span lang="ja">かばんの 中</span> means *inside the bag*, not *beside the bag*. For “beside,” use <span lang="ja">かばんの 隣</span> when the relationship is appropriate.

### 7. From supported practice to your own description

**Controlled:** Complete <span lang="ja">つくえ ___ 下 ___ かばん ___ あります。</span> Answer: <span lang="ja">の・に・が</span>.

**Guided:** Imagine a desk, a shelf and a teacher. Make one sentence with **上**, one with **隣**, and one with **前**. Decide whether the subject requires **あります** or **います**.

**Independent writing:** Write **four connected Japanese sentences** about a fictional room. Describe two pieces of furniture or belongings, one person or animal, and one item inside or behind something. Model: <span lang="ja">へやに つくえが あります。つくえの 上に ほんが あります。ドアの 前に せんせいが います。かばんの 中に かぎが あります。</span> Self-check: (1) every spatial expression has a reference noun + の, (2) location takes に, (3) located subject takes が unless deliberately topicalized, and (4) あります／います agrees with the subject. Different accurate rooms and natural sentence orders are valid; free writing is **not automatically graded**.

**Optional speaking:** Read the room-tour dialogue aloud and change two locations. You may rehearse without recording yourself. No pronunciation evaluation or microphone permission is required.
