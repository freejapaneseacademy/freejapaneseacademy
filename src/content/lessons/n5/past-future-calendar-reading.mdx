## A classmate's week

**Can-do:** Say what happened yesterday and what is planned. You are helping a fictional student, はる (Haru), check a calendar before arranging a study meeting. Read the Japanese note first, then check the English support. Every scored question below is answerable from the note alone.

## 1. Read the calendar note

| Time heading | Haru's original Japanese note | English support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">昨日（きのう）</span> — yesterday | <span lang="ja">図書館で日本語を勉強しました。</span> | I studied Japanese at the library. |
| <span lang="ja">昨日（きのう）</span> | <span lang="ja">映画を見ませんでした。</span> | I did not watch a film. |
| <span lang="ja">今朝（けさ）</span> — this morning | <span lang="ja">七時に起きました。</span> | I got up at seven. |
| <span lang="ja">今日（きょう）</span> — today | <span lang="ja">学校で友だちに会います。</span> | I am meeting a friend at school. |
| <span lang="ja">今日（きょう）</span> | <span lang="ja">午後三時に友だちに会います。</span> | I am meeting a friend at 3 p.m. |
| <span lang="ja">明日（あした）</span> — tomorrow | <span lang="ja">家で本を読みます。</span> | I will read a book at home. |
| <span lang="ja">来週（らいしゅう）</span> — next week | <span lang="ja">土曜日に公園に行きます。</span> | I will go to the park on Saturday. |

The two **今日** lines describe the same planned meeting. The headings are part of the note: combine a heading with its sentence when citing evidence.

## 3. How to read a schedule accurately

Find the **time heading** first: **昨日** and **今朝** anchor completed actions; **明日** and **来週** signal plans in this note. **今日** needs the sentence and context to tell you whether the action has already happened. The polite past affirmative ends in **ました**, while the polite past negative ends in **ませんでした**. The nonpast **ます** can express a future plan; Japanese has no separate “will” ending.

Particles matter: **図書館で** marks where studying took place; **家で** marks where reading takes place; **公園に行きます** marks the destination; **七時に** and **土曜日に** mark specific times. **友だちに会います** uses **に** for the person met. Do not interpret all occurrences of **に** as destinations.

**Common mistake:** **映画を見ませんでした** does not mean “I will not watch a film.” It means “I did not watch a film.” The past negative is essential.

## 4. Evidence before inference

**Question:** Where did Haru study yesterday? **Evidence:** the **昨日** heading plus <span lang="ja">図書館で日本語を勉強しました。</span>. Answer: at the library.

**Question:** What time will Haru meet a friend? **Evidence:** <span lang="ja">午後三時に友だちに会います。</span>. Answer: 3 p.m.

**Question:** Who is the friend? The note does **not** give the friend's name. Answer: **not stated**. Never fill in missing information just because a plausible guess comes to mind.

## 5. Practise in three stages

**Controlled:** Locate the headings **昨日**, **今日**, **明日**, **来週** and read them aloud or silently using the kana above. Underline every **ました**, **ませんでした** and **ます** ending.

**Guided:** Complete these from the note: “Yesterday Haru did not ___”; “Tomorrow Haru will ___”; “Next week Haru will go to ___ on ___.” Check against the exact Japanese lines: **映画を見ませんでした**, **本を読みます**, **土曜日に公園に行きます**.

**Independent:** Write two short Japanese calendar entries for a *fictional* student: one completed action yesterday and one plan tomorrow. Model: <span lang="ja">昨日、家で日本語を勉強しました。明日、図書館で本を読みます。</span> — “Yesterday I studied Japanese at home. Tomorrow I will read a book at the library.” Check the time words, particles and endings; alternative natural sentences are welcome.
