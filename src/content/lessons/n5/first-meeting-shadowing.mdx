## Your first spoken introductions

**Optional speaking lab.** You are arriving at a fictional language exchange. First greet someone, then ask about their role. The chapter Can-do is to **introduce yourself and identify another person's basic details**. You may complete the rest of the course without recording or completing this optional speaking task.

You will listen to two original conversations from Chapter 01 Lesson 06, practise each turn aloud or silently, and swap roles. A self-check rubric helps you notice your own improvement; **no automatic pronunciation score** is calculated.

## 1. Small phrases with big functions

| Japanese | Reading support | Natural English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| こんにちは。 | こんにちは | Hello (daytime greeting). |
| 私はエマです。 | わたしは エマです | I'm Emma. |
| 田中さんは先生ですか。 | たなかさんは せんせいですか | Tanaka, are you a teacher? |
| はい。日本語の先生です。 | はい。にほんごの せんせいです | Yes. I'm a Japanese-language teacher. |
| すみません。お名前は？ | すみません。おなまえは | Excuse me. Your name? |
| いいえ。先生じゃありません。学生です。 | いいえ。せんせいじゃありません。がくせいです | No. I'm not a teacher. I'm a student. |

**Pronunciation and function:** The topic particle **は** is written *ha* but pronounced *wa*. A polite statement ends in **です**; adding **か** makes it a question. **じゃありません** politely negates a noun predicate. After **いいえ**, state the correction clearly rather than only saying “no.” The short **お名前は？** is an elliptical, polite-in-context question; a fuller form is **お名前は何ですか。** (What is your name?).

Do not confuse **日本語の先生** (a teacher of Japanese) with **日本人の先生** (a Japanese teacher by nationality). Neither phrase alone establishes the other's meaning.

## 2. Model conversation A — welcome table

These lines match the *original* L06 model audio. Play the recording first, then speak after each turn. Do not claim your timing or pronunciation has been independently checked.

> **Woman:** こんにちは。わたしは、エマです。  
> **Man:** こんにちは。わたしは、たなかです。  
> **Woman:** たなかさんは、せんせいですか。  
> **Man:** はい。にほんごのせんせいです。

**Meaning:** “Hello, I'm Emma.” / “Hello, I'm Tanaka.” / “Tanaka, are you a teacher?” / “Yes, I'm a Japanese-language teacher.”

**Controlled rehearsal:** Say only the woman's two turns. Pause after **エマです**. In the question, make **か** audible without turning the line into a statement. Then rehearse the man's replies. Use the play/pause control in the practice studio to compare the *model*, not to produce an automatic grade.

## 3. Model conversation B — registration desk

> **Man:** すみません。おなまえは？  
> **Woman:** リーです。ちゅうごくじんです。  
> **Man:** リーさんは、せんせいですか。  
> **Woman:** いいえ。せんせいじゃありません。がくせいです。

**Meaning:** “Excuse me. Your name?” / “I'm Lee. I'm Chinese.” / “Lee, are you a teacher?” / “No. I'm not a teacher. I'm a student.”

**Guided rehearsal:** First play the whole recording. Then take the woman's role: say **リーです**, state the nationality, and correct the mistaken occupation with **いいえ** and **じゃありません**. Swap to the man's role. Listen for the difference between a question **せんせいですか** and a negative statement **せんせいじゃありません**.

The nationality **ちゅうごくじん** (Chinese person) is not an occupation. **がくせい** (student) is a role; **せんせい** (teacher) is another. The dialogue never says that Lee is from a particular city.

## 4. Make the exchange your own

Use **fictional details** if you do not wish to disclose personal information. Here is a new practice exchange, *not* part of the recorded L06 audio:

> A: はじめまして。わたしは、ミナです。  
> B: はじめまして。わたしは、ケンです。  
> A: ケンさんは、がくせいですか。  
> B: いいえ。がくせいじゃありません。かいしゃいんです。  
> A: よろしくおねがいします。  
> B: よろしくおねがいします。

**Meaning:** “Nice to meet you. I'm Mina.” / “Nice to meet you. I'm Ken.” / “Ken, are you a student?” / “No, I'm not a student. I'm a company employee.” / “Nice to meet you.” / “Likewise.”

**Independent practice:** Replace *Mina*, *Ken* and the occupation with fictional details you can say comfortably. Keep the polite question and negative correction. Rehearse once with the text, then try once without reading. If you have no microphone, speaking quietly, mouthing or mentally rehearsing the turns is equally acceptable.

## 5. Self-review and written checks

The optional practice studio below replays the two approved chapter recordings, lets you reveal their scripts, and provides a learner-controlled microphone option where supported. Recording never starts automatically. The microphone preview stays in your browser and is not uploaded or scored; stop and discard it when finished.

Use the four-criterion rubric: **intelligible name**, **clear question ending**, **correct negative correction**, and **comfortable turn-taking**. A self-tick is your own reflection, not a teacher's pronunciation verdict. Complete the eight written recognition checks to verify the language patterns you rehearsed. The written score is separate from spoken performance and from lesson completion.

**Common repairs:** If you say **先生です** after **いいえ**, you have contradicted the negative answer. Say **先生じゃありません。学生です** instead. If you want to ask whether someone is a student, include **か**: **学生ですか。** Avoid asserting a person's nationality from their country field alone.
