## Mission: prepare an orientation profile

At a fictional community orientation, you need to introduce yourself, read other participants' short profiles, and understand two conversations. This original academy checkpoint reviews the chapter's Can-do: **write and understand a short self-introduction**. It is not an official JLPT examination.

### 1. Retrieve the useful patterns

| Japanese | Meaning and function |
| --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">わたしは小林あきです。</span> | I am Aki Kobayashi. **は** marks the topic (pronounced *wa* as a particle). |
| <span lang="ja">十九歳（じゅうきゅうさい）です。</span> | I am nineteen years old. The age counter is **歳**. |
| <span lang="ja">出身（しゅっしん）は京都（きょうと）です。</span> | I am from Kyoto. **出身** means place of origin. |
| <span lang="ja">わたしも大学生（だいがくせい）です。</span> | I am also a university student. **も** adds a shared fact. |
| <span lang="ja">あきさんの出身</span> | Aki's hometown. **の** links nouns. |
| <span lang="ja">会社員（かいしゃいん）じゃありません。</span> | I am not a company employee. **じゃありません** is polite conversational negation. |

In more formal contexts, <span lang="ja">ではありません</span> is another polite negative. A question can end in <span lang="ja">ですか</span>, as in <span lang="ja">大学生ですか。</span> (Are you a university student?). Do not add **か** to a statement unless you intend a question.

**A crucial distinction:** <span lang="ja">出身は名古屋です。</span> means “I am from Nagoya,” while <span lang="ja">今（いま）は東京に住（す）んでいます。</span> means “I live in Tokyo now.” These can both be true. You need only recognize **住んでいます** here, not produce its grammar.

### 2. Read four original profile cards

Each card is a fictional written source. Read the Japanese first and point to the words that support your answer.

| Card | Profile evidence | Reading hint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | <span lang="ja">はるかさんは二十歳です。出身は大阪です。</span> | **二十歳（はたち）** is the special common reading for age twenty. |
| B | <span lang="ja">たろうさんは大学生です。出身は福岡です。</span> | **福岡（ふくおか）** is the hometown, not a school name. |
| C | <span lang="ja">えみさんは会社員です。先生じゃありません。</span> | A negative rejects “teacher”; **会社員** gives the actual occupation. |
| D | <span lang="ja">ミカさんは大学生です。リナさんも大学生です。</span> | **も** identifies a shared school status, not necessarily a shared age. |

**Evidence habit:** For age, find **歳**; for hometown, find **出身**; for a job, look for **です** and check whether a preceding **じゃありません** negates a guess.

### 3. Fix common mistakes

- <span lang="ja">わたしの大学生です。</span> does not mean “I am a university student.” Say <span lang="ja">わたしは大学生です。</span> **の** connects nouns, not a topic to its predicate.
- <span lang="ja">会社員です。</span> affirms “company employee.” If you mean “not a company employee,” say <span lang="ja">会社員じゃありません。</span>
- <span lang="ja">わたしも大学生です。</span> is suitable after someone else says they are a university student. **も** implies an additional matching fact; it is not interchangeable with **は** in every context.
- Do not treat someone's present city as their hometown. The dialogue explicitly distinguishes <span lang="ja">出身</span> from <span lang="ja">今は…住んでいます</span>.

### 4. Listening transfer: two paired introductions

The chapter's original L06 has **Track A**, in which Aki and Ken meet at orientation, and **Track B**, in which Sato corrects a visitor's assumptions about work and hometown. Listen without a transcript first, then replay for the details. Track A includes two different hometowns, Kyoto and Osaka. Track B contrasts Nagoya as hometown with Tokyo as current residence.

The four listening questions below become available only when **both actual recordings are playable**. Written evidence is shown after scoring; it is not a substitute for hearing the recording. Revealing the transcript before submission labels the attempt *assisted*. If either recording is missing, finish the ten written questions and return for genuine listening later.

### 5. Guided self-introduction (ungraded writing)

Write **three or four connected Japanese sentences** for a fictional participant. Include a name, an age, a hometown, and either a school or work role. Optionally add a truthful shared fact using **も** after another participant's introduction.

Model: <span lang="ja">わたしはミカです。二十歳です。出身は大阪です。大学生です。</span>

Meaning: I am Mika. I am twenty. I am from Osaka. I am a university student.

**Self-review rubric:** (1) Is **は** used for the topic? (2) Is the age counter correct? (3) Does **出身** name the place of origin? (4) Is **です** or **じゃありません** consistent with the intended meaning? (5) Is **も** used only when something is shared? Natural alternatives are possible; no automatic writing grade is claimed.

### 6. Optional speaking and spaced review

Read your self-introduction aloud once slowly, then at a conversational pace. When genuine chapter audio is available, optionally shadow one short turn. This checkpoint does not activate a microphone or score pronunciation. After submitting, review any incorrect items tomorrow and again several days later; focus on the Japanese evidence and the explanation rather than memorizing choice positions.
