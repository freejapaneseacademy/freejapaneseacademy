## A travel plan needs two kinds of information

**Can-do:** talk about destinations and movement. Imagine that Haru and Mei are planning a Saturday outing. One question is **where** they are going; another is **how** they will travel. The words for places and the words for vehicles answer different questions.

This is a **written vocabulary lesson**. Kana readings are supplied for recognition and optional aloud rehearsal; there are no recordings or verified pronunciation scores.

## 1. Destination words: where?

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning and use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">駅</span> | えき | station (often a railway station) |
| <span lang="ja">学校</span> | がっこう | school |
| <span lang="ja">図書館</span> | としょかん | library (a public or institutional library building) |
| <span lang="ja">公園</span> | こうえん | park |
| <span lang="ja">スーパー</span> | スーパー | supermarket |
| <span lang="ja">家</span> | いえ／うち | house/home; うち commonly means one's home |
| <span lang="ja">東京</span> | とうきょう | Tokyo, a place name |
| <span lang="ja">空港</span> | くうこう | airport |

**Notice the writing:** スーパー is a loanword in katakana; the long mark ー belongs to its spelling. 駅 and 公園 use kanji; you can use their kana readings now and study character forms more closely in L04.

## 2. Transport words: how?

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="ja">電車</span> | でんしゃ | train |
| <span lang="ja">バス</span> | バス | bus |
| <span lang="ja">自転車</span> | じてんしゃ | bicycle |
| <span lang="ja">車</span> | くるま | car |
| <span lang="ja">地下鉄</span> | ちかてつ | subway / metro |
| <span lang="ja">飛行機</span> | ひこうき | airplane |
| <span lang="ja">徒歩</span> | とほ | on foot (a noun used in descriptions such as 徒歩で) |

**歩いて (あるいて)** also means “on foot / by walking,” but it is a verb form, not a vehicle name. For now, compare **バスで行きます** (“go by bus”) with **歩いて行きます** (“go on foot”). Do not put で after 歩いて.

## 3. A short Saturday exchange

> <span lang="ja">メイ：土曜日にどこへ行きますか。</span>  
> めい：どようびに どこへ いきますか。  
> Mei: “Where are you going on Saturday?”
>
> <span lang="ja">ハル：図書館へ行きます。</span>  
> はる：としょかんへ いきます。  
> Haru: “I'm going to the library.”
>
> <span lang="ja">メイ：何で行きますか。</span>  
> めい：なんで いきますか。  
> Mei: “How are you going?”
>
> <span lang="ja">ハル：バスで行きます。午後、家に帰ります。</span>  
> はる：バスで いきます。ごご、うちに かえります。  
> Haru: “I'll go by bus. In the afternoon, I'll return home.”

In this context **何で (なんで)** asks about means of transport; in other contexts なんで can mean “why.” The surrounding travel discussion disambiguates it. A clearer transport question is **何で行きますか** in this travel setting, or **どうやって行きますか** (“How will you get there?”).

Here **へ** marks the direction or destination and is pronounced **え**, not “he.” **で** marks the means of travel; **に** in 家に帰ります marks the destination of returning. L03 will teach these particles systematically. This lesson concentrates on the words that fill the patterns.

## 4. What sounds similar but means something different?

**駅 (えき)** is a station; **空港 (くうこう)** is an airport. **電車 (でんしゃ)** is a train, whereas **地下鉄 (ちかてつ)** specifically refers to a subway/metro. A subway can be a kind of train, but the terms are not always interchangeable when a travel plan specifies a particular service.

**図書館 (としょかん)** is a library building or institution; **図書室 (としょしつ)** usually names a library *room*, such as one in a school. **家 (いえ)** can describe a house as a building; **うち** often emphasizes home or one's household. Both readings occur in natural Japanese, but you cannot mechanically substitute them in every expression.

**Common mistake:** <span lang="ja">電車へ行きます</span> normally suggests going *toward the train* rather than travelling *by train*. For the transport meaning, say <span lang="ja">電車で行きます</span> (でんしゃで いきます).

## 5. Guided retrieval: identify place or means

Read each Japanese sentence and label the highlighted noun **destination** or **transport**.

1. <span lang="ja">公園へ行きます。</span> (こうえんへ いきます。 — “I go to the park.”) **公園 = destination**.
2. <span lang="ja">自転車で行きます。</span> (じてんしゃで いきます。 — “I go by bicycle.”) **自転車 = transport**.
3. <span lang="ja">空港へ行きます。</span> (くうこうへ いきます。 — “I go to the airport.”) **空港 = destination**.
4. <span lang="ja">地下鉄で行きます。</span> (ちかてつで いきます。 — “I go by subway.”) **地下鉄 = transport**.

Cover the English translations and say the category first. Then reveal the English and check your understanding.

## 6. Make your own travel plan

Complete these sentence frames with a destination and a vehicle from the tables.

- <span lang="ja">土曜日に＿＿へ行きます。</span> (どようびに ＿＿へ いきます。 — “On Saturday I will go to ___.”)
- <span lang="ja">＿＿で行きます。</span> (＿＿で いきます。 — “I will go by ___.”)
- <span lang="ja">午後、家に帰ります。</span> (ごご、うちに かえります。 — “In the afternoon, I will return home.”)

**Model:** <span lang="ja">土曜日に公園へ行きます。自転車で行きます。午後、家に帰ります。</span> (“On Saturday I'll go to the park. I'll go by bicycle. In the afternoon I'll return home.”)

Self-check: Did you choose a place for **へ**, a means for **で**, and use **帰ります** for the return? You can practise reading the model aloud if you wish, but this written activity does not evaluate speaking.

## Review and next step

Remember the contrast **公園へ** (“toward/to the park”) versus **バスで** (“by bus”). Readings help you identify the words, while a sentence tells you how each word functions. The quiz below checks written recognition and meaning; L03 develops destination and means particles in more depth.
