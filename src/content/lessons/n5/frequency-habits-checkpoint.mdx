## Mission: tell someone how often you do things

Imagine you are completing a community habit survey. You must say what you **always**, **often**, **sometimes**, **rarely** and **never** do, then identify another person's habits from a journal and a recording. This is an original academy checkpoint, not an official JLPT examination.

### 1. Frequency and polarity

| Expression | Example (Japanese) | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| いつも | いつも夜に本を読みます。 | いつも よるに ほんを よみます | I always read books at night. |
| よく | よく公園を歩きます。 | よく こうえんを あるきます | I often walk in the park. |
| ときどき | ときどき家でヨガをします。 | ときどき いえで ヨガを します | I sometimes do yoga at home. |
| あまり〜ません | 朝はあまりテレビを見ません。 | あさは あまり テレビを みません | I don't watch much TV in the morning. |
| ぜんぜん〜ません | ぜんぜん走りません。 | ぜんぜん はしりません | I never run. |

These adverbs are **approximate descriptions**, not fixed percentages. For this beginner lesson, **あまり** and **ぜんぜん** require a negative ending: **あまり読みません** and **ぜんぜん走りません**. Do not translate **ぜんぜん走ります** as “I never run”; that loses the negative. **よく** means “often” in the frequency examples, though it has other uses in Japanese.

Notice the particles: **図書館で勉強します** uses **で** for the location of an action; **本を読みます** uses **を** for the object. **公園を歩きます** uses **を** for the path traversed. **夜に** identifies the time of day.

### 2. Original reading: Mika's habit journal

The following fictional journal was written specifically for this checkpoint; it is **not** a transcript of the chapter recording.

> <span lang="ja">私はみかです。いつも夜に本を読みます。よく学校で日本語を勉強します。ときどき図書館で勉強します。朝はあまりテレビを見ません。毎週、公園を歩きます。でも、ぜんぜん走りません。日曜日はときどき家でヨガをします。</span>

**Translation:** I'm Mika. I always read books at night. I often study Japanese at school. Sometimes I study at the library. I don't watch much TV in the morning. Every week I walk in the park. But I never run. On Sundays I sometimes do yoga at home.

Find the **verb** as well as the adverb. **ときどき図書館で勉強します** answers where Mika sometimes studies; **朝はあまりテレビを見ません** states a *negative* morning habit; **ぜんぜん走りません** does not contradict walking because walking and running are different activities. The journal does not say Mika never exercises.

### 3. Chapter listening: two habit surveys

**Track A:** a library interviewer asks about books and studying. The respondent always reads at night, sometimes studies at the library, and does not study much in the morning.

**Track B:** an exercise interviewer asks about walking and running. The respondent often walks in a park, never runs and sometimes does yoga at home.

Listen once **without** reading the transcript; answer from the spoken details, then replay to check frequency words and verb endings. A transcript can be revealed for assisted study, but that is a different attempt category. Listening questions unlock only when both actual chapter recordings can play. Printed text is not accepted as proof of listening.

### 4. Guided and independent writing

Build one sentence from each pair: **よく + 図書館で勉強します**, **あまり + テレビを見ません**, and **ときどき + 家でヨガをします**. Notice that the negative verb remains negative after **あまり**.

Now write **four connected Japanese sentences** about your own or a fictional person's habits: one always/often habit, one sometimes habit, one thing not done much, and one thing never done. A model:

> <span lang="ja">私はよく本を読みます。ときどき友達と公園を歩きます。朝はあまりテレビを見ません。ぜんぜん走りません。</span>

**Self-review rubric (not automatically scored):** Check (1) the adverb matches the intended frequency, (2) **あまり** and **ぜんぜん** use negative verbs, (3) the action and location particles are appropriate, and (4) all four sentences are intelligible and connected. Rewrite any sentence that fails a criterion. Optional speaking: ask **よく本を読みますか。** and answer with **はい、よく読みます。** or **いいえ、あまり読みません。** Rehearsal is not a pronunciation grade; no recording begins automatically.

### 5. Check and revisit

Answer six grammar and four reading questions, then four listening questions if the genuine audio is playable. After submission, read the evidence and choice-specific feedback. For missed questions, rewrite one correct example and revisit tomorrow; this supports spaced review without claiming automatic SRS scheduling.
