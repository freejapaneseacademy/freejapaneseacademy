## Mission: explain a room to a visitor

A visitor wants to know where the furniture, belongings and a cat are. You will first interpret written room notices, then listen to two *different* guided tours. A correct location always depends on a reference object: “behind” is not useful unless you know **behind what**.

### 1. Review the location pattern

| Spatial noun | Reading | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 上 | うえ | on / above | 机の上に本があります。 — There is a book on the desk. |
| 下 | した | under / below | いすの下にかばんがあります。 — There is a bag under the chair. |
| 前 | まえ | in front | 机の前にいすがあります。 — There is a chair in front of the desk. |
| 後ろ | うしろ | behind | いすの後ろに猫がいます。 — There is a cat behind the chair. |
| 中 | なか | inside | 棚の中にコップがあります。 — There is a cup inside the shelf. |
| 隣 | となり | next to | 窓の隣に本棚があります。 — There is a bookshelf beside the window. |

The useful pattern is **reference noun の position に item が あります／います**. The first **の** connects the reference object to the spatial noun; **に** marks the location; **が** introduces the item. Compare <span lang="ja">机の上に本があります。</span> with <span lang="ja">机の下に本があります。</span>: only the spatial noun changes, but the meaning reverses.

**あります** is normally used for inanimate objects, such as books, bags, clocks and furniture. **います** is used for people and animals, such as <span lang="ja">猫（ねこ）</span>. When an item is already under discussion, a topic sentence like <span lang="ja">かばんは机の下にあります。</span> is natural. Do not automatically replace every **が** with **は**.

### 2. Original reading: a small lounge

These four notices describe **one fictional lounge**. They are independent of the two audio-tour rooms. Read them before selecting the four reading answers.

| Notice | Japanese | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | <span lang="ja">机の上にランプがあります。</span> | A lamp is on the desk. |
| B | <span lang="ja">いすの下にかばんがあります。</span> | A bag is under the chair. |
| C | <span lang="ja">窓の隣に本棚があります。</span> | A bookshelf is beside the window. |
| D | <span lang="ja">ソファの後ろに猫がいます。</span> | A cat is behind the sofa. |

For example, “Where is the bookshelf?” is answered by **C: 窓の隣に本棚があります。** Do not infer a location for a clock: this notice never mentions one. A textual room description can be read without assuming any unshown floor-plan coordinates.

### 3. Listen: two other rooms

The chapter's L06 **Track A** describes a study room. Listen for the book on the desk and the bag under it. **Track B** describes a guest room: listen for a cup inside a shelf and a cat behind a chair. These are separate rooms; never combine their facts with the lounge notice.

First listen without opening a transcript. Answer the four listening questions from sound alone. Afterward, reveal the transcripts and check the evidence. Opening either transcript labels the attempt *transcript-assisted*. A written transcript does not substitute for authentic playable audio, so the listening questions remain locked until both recordings are playable.

### 4. Guided writing and self-review

Write **four connected sentences** describing a room you imagine. Include one item **on** something, one **under** something, one **beside** something, and one animal or person **behind** something. Try without copying the model:

> <span lang="ja">部屋に机があります。机の上に本があります。いすの下にかばんがあります。いすの後ろに猫がいます。</span>

Check your writing against four criteria: (1) each position has a reference noun and **の**; (2) **に** marks the place; (3) **あります** describes objects and **います** describes people or animals; (4) your four sentences form a consistent room. This task is self-assessed, not automatically scored. For an additional challenge, add a fifth sentence using **隣**.

**Optional speaking:** Ask <span lang="ja">本はどこにありますか。</span> and answer <span lang="ja">机の上にあります。</span> Rehearse with a partner or shadow the chapter audio only after the licensed recordings are available. Recording yourself is optional; no microphone is activated here and no pronunciation grade is inferred.

### 5. Review and retry

The checkpoint has six grammar, four reading and four audio-dependent listening questions. After submitting, explain every error using the evidence shown in feedback. Return to a weak spatial noun later rather than memorizing the choice position. Your practice attempt is stored on this device when browser storage is available; it is not an official JLPT score.
