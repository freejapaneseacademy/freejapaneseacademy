## Reading mission: two family introductions

At a fictional community Japanese class, two learners share simple family notes. Read the **Japanese** before consulting the English support. All names and family situations are invented for this exercise. A chart lists only the people mentioned; it does **not** establish that these are the only relatives.

### Chart A — はるの かぞく (Haru's family)

| Japanese note | English meaning |
| --- | --- |
| はる：わたしは はるです。 | Haru: I am Haru. |
| みきは わたしの あねです。 | Miki is my older sister. |
| けんは わたしの おとうとです。 | Ken is my younger brother. |
| あきは わたしの ははです。 | Aki is my mother. |
| みきは はるの あねです。 | Miki is Haru's older sister. |
| けんは はるの おとうとです。 | Ken is Haru's younger brother. |

**Reading aid:** 姉（あね） is used for one's own older sister when speaking to outsiders; 弟（おとうと） is younger brother; 母（はは） is one's own mother. Here the chart spells them in kana to reduce decoding load. **わたしの** means “my”; **はるの** means “Haru's.”

### Chart B — りなの かぞく (Rina's family)

| Japanese note | English meaning |
| --- | --- |
| りな：こちらは わたしの おかあさん、なおこです。 | Rina: This is my mother, Naoko. (Polite introduction.) |
| こちらは りなの おかあさんです。 | This is Rina's mother. |
| たけしは りなの ちちです。 | Takeshi is Rina's father. |
| ゆうたは りなの あにです。 | Yuta is Rina's older brother. |
| ゆうたです。その人は りなの あにです。 | That's Yuta. That person is Rina's older brother. |

**Reading aid:** 父（ちち） is one's own father when speaking to outsiders; 兄（あに） is one's own older brother. **おかあさん** is appropriate when addressing or referring respectfully to another person's mother; here Rina is introducing her mother in a polite conversational style, although **母（はは）** would be the more conventional humble term when describing one's own family to outsiders. That contrast is worth noticing, not mechanically translating every family word as interchangeable.

## Step 1 — Follow the possession marker の

In **はるの あね**, の links Haru and the older sister: “Haru's older sister.” In **りなの ちち**, it links Rina and her father. It does not by itself tell you whether the family is large or whether the relationship is biological, adoptive, or otherwise; the chart only states the relationship.

Compare **わたしの はは** (“my mother,” said about one's own family) with **りなの おかあさん** (“Rina's mother,” respectful reference). These expressions are not perfect synonyms in every social setting.

## Step 2 — Resolve references, not guesses

**こちら** is a polite way to present someone nearby: **こちらは りなの おかあさんです**. In Chart B, **その人** (“that person”) refers to Yuta because the preceding sentence names him. Outside that context, その人 would not identify a unique person. It is not a family-role word.

## Worked evidence example

**Question:** Who is Ken to Haru? **Answer:** Haru's younger brother. **Evidence:** 「けんは はるの おとうとです。」 This is stronger evidence than merely seeing Ken listed near Haru. By contrast, neither chart tells us Yuta's age, where Aki works, or how many other siblings Rina has.

## Guided reading

Cover the English column. Point to **の** in three family expressions and identify the person before it. Then underline **あね**, **おとうと**, **ちち**, and **あに**. For Chart B, trace **その人** back to the sentence naming Yuta. If a question asks about an age, say “not stated” rather than inventing a number.

## Independent check

Answer ten questions using the Japanese chart as your evidence. The feedback will show the supporting phrase. Afterward, try writing two original lines: one using **Xの あね／あに** and one using **こちらは…です**. Compare your work with the optional model below. This written practice does not assess listening or pronunciation, and the optional writing is self-reviewed rather than automatically graded.

**Takeaway:** Family charts encode specific relationships. Cite the exact phrase; don't fill gaps with assumptions.
