## A day of actions, written clearly

**Can-do:** Recognize dictionary forms and basic verb categories, then write two to five connected sentences about a fictional day. A dictionary lists **読む（よむ）** “read,” but a polite plan often uses **読みます（よみます）**. The meaning remains “read”; the ending changes the style. This lesson is written practice, not a pronunciation assessment.

### 1. Read whole verbs before changing endings

| Dictionary form | Polite form | Group | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 読む（よむ） | 読みます（よみます） | 1 | read |
| 書く（かく） | 書きます（かきます） | 1 | write |
| 話す（はなす） | 話します（はなします） | 1 | speak |
| 飲む（のむ） | 飲みます（のみます） | 1 | drink |
| 行く（いく） | 行きます（いきます） | 1 | go |
| 食べる（たべる） | 食べます（たべます） | 2 | eat |
| 見る（みる） | 見ます（みます） | 2 | watch, see |
| する | します | irregular | do |
| 来る（くる） | 来ます（きます） | irregular | come |

In Group 1, change the final dictionary-form **u-row** sound to its **i-row** partner before ます: 読**む** → 読**み**ます; 書**く** → 書**き**ます. For these Group 2 examples, remove る and add ます: 食べる → 食べます. Do **not** assume every verb ending in る is Group 2: **帰る（かえる） → 帰ります（かえります）**, “return,” is Group 1. **来る** changes reading from くる to きます.

The dictionary form is a **plain nonpast form**, not a command. **本を読む。** can mean “(I) read/will read a book,” depending on context; **本を読みます。** is its polite counterpart. For a consistent polite diary or plan, use ます forms throughout.

### 2. Follow an original fictional activity plan

> **土曜日（どようび）の勉強会（べんきょうかい） — Saturday study gathering**
>
> 午前九時に図書館（としょかん）に行きます。
>
> 図書館で日本語（にほんご）の本を読みます。
>
> 十時にノートに漢字（かんじ）を書きます。
>
> 十一時に友だちと話します。
>
> 昼（ひる）におにぎりを食べます。水を飲みます。

Translation: “At nine in the morning I go to the library. At the library I read a Japanese book. At ten I write kanji in a notebook. At eleven I talk with a friend. At lunchtime I eat a rice ball. I drink water.” The scene and times are invented.

**Read for structure:** **に** marks a specific time or a destination with 行きます; **で** marks where an activity happens; **を** marks what is read, written, eaten or drunk; **と** marks the person you talk with. In **ノートに漢字を書きます**, ノートに identifies the surface where the characters are written.

### 3. Move between a vocabulary list and a paragraph

A vocabulary card might say **書く — to write**. In your own polite paragraph, choose **書きます**. A word list can mix dictionary forms, but connected prose should use an intentional, consistent style.

**Guided change A:** **本を読む。** → **本を読みます。** The object 本を stays unchanged.

**Guided change B:** **水を飲む。** → **水を飲みます。** Do not write **飲むます**; ます replaces the dictionary-form ending pattern.

**Guided change C:** **友だちが来る。** → **友だちが来ます。** Here 来ます is pronounced **きます**, not くます.

**Common mistake:** **食べります** is not the polite form of 食べる. It is **食べます**. Conversely, **帰ます** is incorrect for Group 1 帰る: write **帰ります**.

### 4. Independent composition

Write **two to five connected Japanese sentences** describing a *fictional* after-school plan. Include at least **three different action verbs**, one location or time, and at least one appropriate object marked with を. Use consistent polite ます endings. Kana is acceptable for unfamiliar kanji.

**One possible model:**

> 月曜日（げつようび）の午後（ごご）四時に家（いえ）に帰ります。家で本を読みます。五時に日本語を書きます。夜（よる）に音楽（おんがく）を聞きます。

“On Monday at four in the afternoon I return home. I read a book at home. At five I write Japanese. At night I listen to music.”

Compare the model with your own writing **after** you draft. Other correct activities, times and objects are possible. The workshop below stores a draft locally when available and does **not** automatically grade free composition.

### Five-criterion self-review

1. I wrote two to five connected sentences about one coherent fictional plan.
2. I used at least three different action verbs with correct ます forms.
3. I marked objects with を and locations/times with appropriate particles.
4. I can identify at least one Group 1 and one Group 2 dictionary-form pair from my sentences or the table.
5. I checked kana/kanji readings and did not confuse 来る → 来ます or 食べる → 食べます.

### 5. Check, revise and optionally speak

Complete the eight recognition questions below, then return to your draft and revise any mismatched verb endings. The score covers **recognition only**, not your creative Japanese. Optional speaking: read your revised plan slowly aloud, then replace one action with another. No microphone recording, speech scoring or verified pronunciation is provided. The chapter's L06 handles authentic listening separately.

**Takeaway:** Use dictionary forms to recognize and classify verbs; use the corresponding polite forms when writing a consistent beginner plan.
